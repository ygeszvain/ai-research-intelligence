Deep 01 · npj Digital Medicine

Artificial intelligence tools in sepsis prediction: a systematic review and meta-analysis

Aadith Ashok et al. · Aug 31, 2026 · DOI 10.1038/s41746-026-03168-2

Thesis: ML improves average sepsis discrimination, but translation is constrained by low PPV, extreme heterogeneity, bias, and absent patient-outcome evidence.

Verified results

  • 53 studies; >7M admissions.
  • AUROC 0.88 (0.86–0.90).
  • Mean difference vs traditional comparators +0.119, p<0.001.
  • Sensitivity 77.2%; specificity 84.7%; PPV 34.2%.
  • I² >95%; prediction interval −0.06 to 0.30.

Adversarial critique

  • AUROC obscures false-alert burden.
  • Extreme heterogeneity weakens transportability.
  • Most studies had high risk of bias.
  • No demonstrated patient-outcome improvement.

Enterprise inference: every predictive-AI release gate should include discrimination + calibration + prevalence-sensitive utility + subgroup/site transfer + alert/workflow burden + cost + downstream outcome.

Reversible experiment: shadow-score 500–2,000 historical cases; pre-register PPV, calibration, subgroup slices, false-alert budget, latency and cost; no user-visible decisions.

Deep 02 · Translational Psychiatry

Artificial intelligence-assisted screening of major depressive disorder using routine laboratory tests across multicenter cohorts

Yuyang Sha et al. · Aug 31, 2026 · DOI 10.1038/s41398-026-04413-8

Thesis: MD³S tests whether cheap, routinely collected structured data can support scalable MDD screening across a large population cohort and independent hospitals.

Verified evidence

  • >180,000 UK Biobank records.
  • Hematological, biochemical, physiological inputs.
  • Internal accuracy 86.57%.
  • Two independent external hospital cohorts.

Uncertainty

  • Not all external metrics are exposed in accessible early-access HTML.
  • Retrospective screening ≠ prospective diagnosis/outcome benefit.
  • Population/site transfer remains a core risk.

Enterprise inference: model registries should record validation site, population, prevalence, feature availability, and intended use—not only one global score.

Reversible experiment: test whether low-cost structured telemetry can triage cases before expensive model/tool calls on historical data; compare recall, precision, cost avoided and site stability.

Multidimensional trust perceptions of AI medical conversational agents — Scientific Reports, Aug 29, DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-68299-8. A 15-item five-factor scale explained 76.07% of variance. Ordinal-logit use-frequency model: χ²(5)=71.963, p<0.001, McFadden pseudo-R²=0.125; interactional trust OR 3.096 (95% CI 2.257–4.246). Why Watchlist: exact publication time relative to the Monday cutoff is unavailable; self-reported use is not appropriate reliance. Trigger: enterprise replication with observed behavior.

Cross-Paper Synthesis

Pattern: measurement validity dominates. AUROC can hide poor precision; external validation can still be retrospective; trust can be high while reliance is inappropriate.
Architecture implication: add an evidence gate between model output and operational action, with prevalence/site metadata, calibration, drift, policy, cost, human review and rollback.

Top 3 Future Bets

1. Prevalence-aware evaluation gate.
Why: prevents AUROC-driven false confidence. Change recommendation if operational PPV/calibration adds no predictive value over current gates. Next: add metrics to one offline benchmark.
2. External-transfer model registry.
Why: makes generalization evidence inspectable. Change recommendation if site metadata cannot predict failure across your use cases. Next: annotate one existing model’s validation lineage.
3. Shadow deployment telemetry.
Why: connects model scores to real workflow cost and outcomes. Change recommendation if replay cannot reproduce live failure modes. Next: instrument one read-only workflow.

Knowledge Reinforcement

Key concepts: prevalence-sensitive precision; prediction interval; external validation; transportability; appropriate reliance.
Recall: Why can AUROC 0.88 still produce operational pain? What does I²>95% imply? Why is external validation stronger but not sufficient? Why should trust not equal authorization? Which metadata belongs in a model registry?
90-second teach-back: Explain why a model can improve average discrimination yet still be a bad production decision. Use PPV, heterogeneity, external validation, and workflow burden, then describe one reversible shadow test.
