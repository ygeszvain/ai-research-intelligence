Scope, window, and evidence policy

Research window. The exact window was Friday, October 9, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT through Saturday, October 10, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT (America/Chicago; 09:00 UTC to 09:00 UTC). No new Saturday arXiv announcement batch was available, so discovery expanded to the prior 72 hours and screened the complete Friday artificial-intelligence batch plus relevant cross-lists, primary papers, repositories, data releases, and license pages. All five selections were submitted October 8, were announced Friday, and were absent from the 61-report archive through October 9.

Selection. The screen emphasized controlled counterfactuals, causal dependence on rules or evidence, long-running learning under fixed budgets, negative and null results, artifact availability, and reversible enterprise experiments. Three papers cleared deep review: Verdict Without the Rule, Cited but Not Consulted, and Can AI Agents Learn Their Way to the Top? Recursive Self-Improvement through Multi-Agent Self-Supervision and Memento 3 enter the Watchlist because both show meaningful learning signals but remain too coupled to self-judgment, narrow tasks, or incomplete external validation for an implementation recommendation.

Anti-duplication. Yesterday's six identifiers—arXiv:2610.09469, 2610.11773, 2610.12292, 2610.12360, 2610.12375, and 2610.12466—were excluded, as were all identifiers in the full archive. Earlier briefs covered evaluation contracts, executable claim ledgers, persistent task state, agent-harness qualification, rollback, and outcome verification. Today's incremental question is narrower: does the decision actually depend on the rule, citation, evidence, or learned policy that the system claims to use?

Evidence labels. Paper Fact means directly reported in a reviewed paper or verified first-party artifact. Independent Analysis means this briefing's comparison, calculation, or critique. Enterprise Inference translates research into a bounded future option. Uncertainty marks missing causal, adversarial, external-validity, artifact, security, privacy, governance, economic, or production evidence. Benchmark performance never authorizes production deployment.

Executive signal

Reliable enterprise AI must prove dependency, not merely produce a correct answer or a plausible explanation. A verdict should change when its governing rule changes; a cited conclusion should react when its authority changes; and a learned policy should retain verified gains on held-out evaluation with a recoverable champion.

The three deep papers support complementary parts of that rule:

  1. Verdict Without the Rule evaluates five open models across 20 compliance domains and 64,485 case-condition generations. Aggregate output-change sensitivity is only .013–.094 despite roughly 90–92% baseline accuracy for the general models. Deleting the rule changes verdicts only 5.3% of the time in pooled results, while a rule-necessity control yields .73–1.00 sensitivity on cases constructed so the rule must determine the answer. Accuracy and rule dependence are therefore distinct.
  2. Cited but Not Consulted requires seven open-weight models to name legal authority, then swaps that authority while holding the fact pattern fixed as closely as possible. Correct authority naming reaches 66.7–100%, but ECHR verdict-swap sensitivity is only 31.0–58.6%; CaseHOLD holdings change in 0–21.7% of detected citation cases. The paper also reports substantial uncertainty and several confounds, so its strongest contribution is the audit design—not a universal prevalence estimate.
  3. AAArena evaluates seven model–harness configurations on 12 authentic adversarial games and 1,920 archived human programs. The strongest configuration earns six rank-1 finishes; no configuration tops the other six ladders. Tripling interaction budget on four hard games yields no new rank-1 result, while opponent selection, dense replay feedback, and retained-champion rollback matter. The public repository is substantial, but full-pool evaluation remains hosted and authenticated.

Decision posture. Prototype counterfactual rule/evidence audits and champion-retaining improvement loops in isolated shadow environments. Prepare shared decision-dependency, evaluator-separation, replay, and rollback foundations. Monitor recursive self-training and population world models. Do not use model-generated citations, explanations, or self-evaluation as authorization evidence without independent behavioral tests.

Top 3 Future Bets

  1. Rule-dependence qualification gate — 93.0/100. Why it matters: accuracy can remain high even when verdicts barely respond to the rule that supposedly governs them. Evidence that would change the recommendation: internal rule-necessity cases show high output-change sensitivity without harming accuracy, or low sensitivity is explained by demonstrably redundant evidence. Next reversible action: create 40 paired internal cases with deletion, swap, negation, and rule-necessity controls; run the gate offline against current systems.
  2. Evidence-dependence and citation counterfactual audit — 91.1/100. Why it matters: a correct citation can decorate a conclusion that was reached independently of the cited authority. Evidence that would change the recommendation: source substitutions reliably produce semantically appropriate changes across models and domains, with low paraphrase instability and human-confirmed counterfactual validity. Next reversible action: swap one material source at a time in 30 low-risk research or policy cases and compare verdict, cited rationale, and uncertainty state under blinded review.
  3. Champion-retaining policy-improvement sandbox — 89.5/100. Why it matters: long-running agents can regress after promising revisions, and extra interaction alone does not reliably recover. Evidence that would change the recommendation: held-out internal tasks show no stable gain over a fixed baseline, rollback rarely prevents regression, or costs exceed the value of recovered performance. Next reversible action: give an isolated agent a frozen simulator, a 20-evaluation budget, immutable experiment records, and automatic restoration of the best externally scored policy.

Deep paper 1 — High accuracy can coexist with rule independence

Identity and status. Verdict Without the Rule: Diagnosing and Auditing Regulatory Rule Sensitivity in LLM Compliance Systems; Saisab Sadhu, Aadit Sengupta, Vinay Kumar Sankarapu, and Pratinav Seth; arXiv:2610.12313v1; submitted October 8, 2026; preprint. The paper uses the arXiv non-exclusive distribution license. No verified public code repository was linked at review time. (paper)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

The paper introduces Output Change Sensitivity (OCS), a black-box measure of whether a compliance verdict changes after the supplied rule is deleted, swapped for a rule from another domain, or negated. It pairs OCS with an activation-space Internal Change Score (ICS-Δ) for general models. OCS does not measure correctness; it measures behavioral dependence on a perturbation.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Prior compliance evaluation largely asks whether a classifier matches a label or follows a policy in the observed input. OCS instead asks whether the same system's answer reacts to a controlled change in the purported governing rule. That distinguishes getting the answer right from using the intended basis. ICS-Δ adds a representation-level comparison but, importantly, the paper does not treat it as a substitute for behavioral evidence.

The design is strong in scale, perturbation diversity, per-domain reporting, held-out layer selection, multiple-comparison correction, and inclusion of a rule-necessity control. It evaluates five models over 20 domains, samples 200 cases per domain and model, uses a fixed seed, and reports 12,897 generations per model—64,485 total—with more than 99.5% parseability.

The causal claim remains bounded. Rule deletion and cross-domain swap can produce unnatural prompts; negation applies only when mandatory modals are present, with domain coverage from 0% to 60.7%. The 300-character truncation can remove decisive clauses. OmniCompliance's scenarios may leak the label, while the rule-necessity control is intentionally constructed to do the opposite. Transfer to LegalBench and ContractNLI yields OCS three to four times higher, demonstrating that the metric is task-distribution sensitive rather than a universal model trait. No deployment-time latency, security, privacy, or policy-governance evaluation is provided.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper specifies models, domains, sample sizes, seed, prompts, parsing, interventions, statistical tests, and layer selection. No verified repository or exact runnable artifact was found, limiting replication. Enterprise readiness: high for a small offline qualification suite; medium for use in model/prompt admission; low for runtime enforcement or automated policy approval. Research Utility Score: 90/100 (methodological rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 12/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 15/15).

Deep paper 2 — Citation presence is not citation dependence

Identity and status. Cited but Not Consulted: A Counterfactual Audit of Legal Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness; Saisab Sadhu, Shreeyans Arora, and Pratinav Seth; arXiv:2610.12361v1; submitted October 8, 2026; preprint. The paper uses the arXiv non-exclusive distribution license. No verified public code repository was linked at review time. (paper)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

The audit forces a model to identify a legal authority, records whether it commits to a verdict before or after naming that authority, then replaces the authority while preserving the surrounding fact pattern. It covers ECHR, SCOTUS, CaseHOLD, and ContractNLI with seven open-weight models from 8B to 70B parameters.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Prior legal-LLM work emphasizes citation correctness, hallucinated authorities, outcome accuracy, or whether a rationale entails an answer. This paper instead tests behavioral dependence: if the cited authority is load-bearing, substituting it should alter the holding, reasoning, or uncertainty in a legally coherent direction. Internal commitment tracking and injection tests add process evidence without treating chain-of-thought as ground truth.

Strengths include two structurally independent authority pairs for ECHR and SCOTUS, balanced sampling, a fixed seed, multiple model families, bootstrap confidence intervals from 1,000 resamples, paraphrase controls, a null condition, and explicit reporting of weaker interpretations. The authors distinguish injection compliance from verdict change and acknowledge that an unchanged answer can sometimes be correct when facts independently determine the outcome.

Limitations are material. Each model–pair cell uses 30 cases, so rank differences are usually not significant. The swap can change both authority and the legal question, weakening causal isolation. CaseHOLD substitutions may leave redundant cues. ContractNLI's clean subset has only 10–12 examples. Legal-Delta-14B is a best-effort LoRA reproduction rather than a verified original checkpoint. The study evaluates open models and benchmark legal tasks, not governed enterprise policy systems or licensed legal advice. No public runnable artifact was verified.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper reports model list, prompts, benchmark construction, seeds, sample counts, matching rules, and resampling protocol. Missing code and processed counterfactual pairs constrain independent replication. Enterprise readiness: high for designing a source-dependence test set; medium for low-risk knowledge and policy qualification; low for automated legal approval or live source injection. Research Utility Score: 88/100 (methodological rigor 23/25; quantitative traceability 23/25; reproducibility/artifacts 12/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 15/15).

Deep paper 3 — Long-running learning needs a retained champion and an external ladder

Identity and status. Can AI Agents Learn Their Way to the Top? Evaluating Heuristic Learning in a Long-Running Game Agent Competition; Kaisen Yang and 26 coauthors; Tsinghua University; arXiv:2610.12341v1; submitted October 8, 2026; preprint under CC BY 4.0. The verified public AAArena repository is MIT-licensed and includes environments, configs, results, tests, validation, runners, and 909 filtered player packages; full-pool evaluation requires hosted credentials and game resources retain their individual licenses. (paper · artifact)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

AAArena formalizes Adversarial Heuristic Learning: a fixed-weight language model revises executable game policies from match experience, replay analysis, and opponent selection. The benchmark uses 12 authentic adversarial games, 1,920 archived human programs, and rank/Elo evaluation under fixed interaction budgets.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Classic game benchmarks often evaluate a frozen policy, a fixed training algorithm, or self-play under simulator access. AAArena makes the language-model agent the learning engine: it reads rules, selects opponents, analyzes replays, modifies source code, and keeps an experiment record. Compared with software-agent benchmarks, the output is not merely a patch that passes tests; it is a policy whose value depends on performance against a large, rated population.

The benchmark's strongest design choices are fixed small/full evaluation budgets, real archived opponents, per-game ranking, repeat runs with the median best retained policy, dense trajectory disclosure, and ablations for feedback and opponent selection. Rule descriptions span 1,127–6,368 AST nodes and 219–1,008 extracted rule atoms, but these are representation-dependent description sizes—not state-space or optimal-play complexity.

The key validity gap is evaluator reuse. Full-pool evaluation feedback participates in optimization, so final rank is not an unseen-opponent generalization estimate. Only 909 filtered player packages are public; the complete 1,920-program pool and hosted evaluator require tokens. Human ratings are treated as the ladder without an independent revalidation study. Model and harness effects are entangled. Gold medals are maxima across games and do not quantify cost, variance, or safety. Authentic games improve ecological richness but do not establish transfer to enterprise workflows with privacy, authorization, side effects, or human review.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The repository exposes 57 commits at verification, source, environments, configs, docs, results, scripts, tests, validation, an MIT license, model/harness runners, and sandbox guidance. Full replication still depends on hosted pool access, provider APIs, and separately licensed game resources. Enterprise readiness: high for champion retention, experiment ledgers, rollback, and held-out evaluation design; medium for isolated policy-optimization sandboxes; low for autonomous online self-modification. Research Utility Score: 93/100 (methodological rigor 23/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 20/20; enterprise relevance 13/15; prior-work advance 13/15).

Watchlist

Recursive Self-Improvement through Multi-Agent Self-Supervision

Identity. Hyunin Lee, Jinglue Xu, Jeffrey Seely, Donghyun Lee, Somayeh Sojoudi, Matei Zaharia, and Yujin Tang; arXiv:2610.12176v1; October 8, 2026; CC BY 4.0. A verified public repository contains 14 commits, workflow search, post-training, tasks, configs, evaluation adapters, tests, and documentation, but no visible top-level license file; the repository also states that several public-benchmark adapters and paper ablations are absent and that the released search driver differs from Algorithm 1. (paper · artifact)

Paper Fact. MASS alternates evolutionary multi-agent workflow search with SFT on self-generated trajectories using Qwen3.6-27B. After two cycles, the paper reports 1.2–1.6× higher score per output token on four research benchmarks, test-task win rate rising from 53.9% for generation 1 over base to 69.9% for generation 2 over base, and external-reference evaluator agreement rising .73→.91→.93. Yet Terminal-Bench falls from 37.5%±2.2 to 35.2%±1.7 and SWE-bench Verified from 62.6%±1.4 to 61.1%±0.6. On MLR-Bench, aggregate gains accompany more delivered papers, while the mean score among delivered papers falls 3.07→2.88; paired exact sign tests across tasks are not significant.

Why Watchlist. The executor, optimizer, and in-loop evaluator share one model family, creating correlated error and self-confirmation risk. The best workflow is self-judged and explicitly not a certified global optimum. Gains depend on aggregation unit and do not transfer uniformly to coding or terminal tasks. The artifact is strong but not protocol-identical. Enterprise trigger: independently judged held-out tasks show repeatable gains across at least two internal task families, adverse transfer is bounded, evaluator disagreement is audited, data rights permit self-training, and rollback to the pre-cycle model is proven.

Memento 3: Model-Based Recursive Self-Improvement through Reflective Rulebooks

Identity. Haoyu Zhao, Zhengxu Yu, Zhiyuan He, Meng Fang, Rasul Tutunov, Haitham Bou-Ammar, Weilin Luo, and Jun Wang; arXiv:2610.11794v1; October 8, 2026; CC BY 4.0. No verified public implementation repository was linked in the paper. (paper)

Paper Fact. A frozen LLM maintains a natural-language rulebook and executable world model, accepts revisions only after semantic-fidelity judgment and exact replay against observed transitions, and can keep multiple hypotheses in parallel. On the 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games, the single-model agent clears every level, reports mean RHAE 100, and uses 7,518 actions—44% of the 17,135 human action count. In Pong, its learned controller wins 21:0 in each of three evaluated episodes with different openings and no additional LLM calls during execution.

Why Watchlist. Exact replay proves consistency with observed transitions, not correctness on unseen states. The paper explicitly notes that several hypotheses can fit the same history and that committing to one can steer exploration away from falsifying evidence. ARC-AGI-3's 25 public games and three Pong episodes are narrow; the semantic-fidelity judge is itself model-based; no code, hidden-game evaluation, cost accounting, adversarial testing, or enterprise controls were verified. Enterprise trigger: a released implementation and hidden-environment replication show that a hypothesis population improves unseen prediction and task success over single-model and no-rulebook baselines, with bounded cost, deterministic rollback, provenance, and security review.

Independent Analysis. These papers converge on a decision-dependency architecture. Enterprise systems should preserve the claimed basis for a decision as inspectable state, then test whether changing that basis changes the behavior in the expected direction. Learning systems need the same discipline: every revision remains provisional until an external evaluator confirms improvement on traffic withheld from the optimizer.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

Independent Analysis. Over the next 12–24 months, the recommended options would shift enterprise AI from explanation-centric governance to testable decision dependencies:

  • Rule and evidence provenance plane. Assign immutable IDs and versions to rules, sources, retrieved passages, model/harness configurations, and policy owners. Final claims refer to those IDs, not only rendered text.
  • Decision-dependency service. Record which rules and sources were available, cited, and used; preserve pre-evidence commitments; generate authorized delete/swap/negate counterfactuals; compare semantic changes; and retain reviewer judgments.
  • Independent evaluation layer. Separate optimizer from evaluator; reserve task, source, opponent, and environment partitions; validate judge agreement; report uncertainty and adverse-transfer metrics; prevent evaluation traffic from silently becoming training data.
  • Champion registry and rollback. Treat every learned workflow, memory, rulebook, prompt, model, or executable policy as a versioned candidate. Promote only after held-out improvement and security checks; retain the last accepted champion and one-click restoration.
  • Agent orchestration and model gateway. Route by qualification metadata—domain, model, harness, prompt, evidence contract, cost, and safety limits—not by a global leaderboard. Gate self-modification behind experiment identities and bounded budgets.
  • Human-review and policy controls. Route unresolved high-impact dependencies, counterfactual disagreements, and regression cases to named reviewers. Store override reasons and require policy-owner approval before a research score affects production authority.
  • Observability and cost telemetry. Capture decisions, source changes, replay outcomes, evaluator disagreements, revision lineage, compute, latency, and rollback frequency so that apparent improvement cannot hide rising cost or risk.

Shared foundations are model gateways; context and memory services; identity and authorization; policy enforcement; data provenance; knowledge systems; agent orchestration; evaluation infrastructure; experiment registries; immutable traces; cost telemetry; human-review queues; and reversible feature flags. No particular enterprise stack is assumed. Implementation decisions require internal domain criticality, policy ownership, source licensing, acceptable false-change and false-stability costs, reviewer capacity, model/version inventory, data-retention rules, evaluation independence, latency budgets, and representative historical cases.

Knowledge reinforcement

  1. Question: Why is high compliance accuracy insufficient evidence of rule grounding?
    Answer: A system can infer labels from scenario cues or prior knowledge. Verdict Without the Rule shows high baseline accuracy with very low output-change sensitivity when the rule is deleted, swapped, or negated.
  2. Question: What distinguishes citation correctness from citation dependence?
    Answer: Correctness asks whether the named source is appropriate. Dependence asks whether changing that source changes the conclusion or reasoning in the expected direction.
  3. Question: Why is OCS not a correctness metric?
    Answer: It measures whether output changes under a perturbation. A change can be wrong, and no change can be justified when other evidence is sufficient.
  4. Question: What does AAArena add beyond a one-shot software benchmark?
    Answer: It evaluates long-running policy revision against a rated opponent population under fixed interaction budgets, with replays, opponent selection, experiment records, and retained champions.
  5. Question: What is the safest reusable pattern across today's learning papers?
    Answer: Keep every revision provisional, score it with an evaluator separated from the optimizer, retain the last accepted champion, and make rollback deterministic.
  6. Question: Why are MASS and Memento 3 on the Watchlist rather than implementation priorities?
    Answer: MASS has self-judgment and adverse-transfer concerns; Memento 3 has narrow public-game evidence, no verified implementation, and replay consistency that does not prove unseen-state correctness.
  7. Question: What internal context is required before implementation?
    Answer: Domain criticality, authoritative rules and sources, policy ownership, false-change costs, source rights, representative cases, model/harness inventory, reviewer capacity, identity and authorization, retention rules, latency budgets, and independent evaluation capacity.
