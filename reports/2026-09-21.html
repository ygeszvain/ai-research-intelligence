Scope, window, and evidence policy

Research window. Friday, September 18, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT through Monday, September 21, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT (72 hours; America/Chicago; 09:00 UTC to 09:00 UTC), plus the complete September 21 arXiv announcement batch. Three selected papers were submitted 5–8 hours before the strict window but first appeared in the Monday announcement; the practical discovery window is therefore disclosed as approximately 80 hours. Screening covered 222 records across cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.IR and cs.SE, adjacent cs.CR/cs.PL work, current primary pages from major publishers and conference repositories, full PDFs, TeX source bundles, and linked or claimed artifacts. Five papers were retained: three cleared deep analysis and two remain Watchlist-only.

Anti-duplication. Exact identifiers and titles were checked against the 42-report archive through September 20 and the local source corpus. None of today's five papers appeared previously. The prior brief examined support boundaries, accountable source discovery and false consensus. Today's increment is operationally downstream: an AI system is not controlled merely because one prediction is confident, one turn is correct, or one cancellation is acknowledged. Control requires evidence that the serving slice can be certified, the complete trajectory succeeds, and retired authority cannot still produce a durable effect.

Evidence policy. Paper Fact denotes a statement directly reported in a paper or verified first-party artifact. Independent Analysis denotes this briefing's calculation, comparison or critique. Enterprise Inference translates evidence into a bounded future option. Uncertainty marks missing causal, external-validity, artifact, licensing, economic, privacy, security, governance, operational or adoption evidence. Available Guardrails and When Better Turns... use CC BY 4.0; Authorization Revocation uses arXiv's non-exclusive distribution license. The workflow paper reports REALM Workshop at EMNLP 2026 acceptance. No benchmark result is treated as production approval. Proposed experiments are isolated, reversible, measurable and non-destructive.

Executive signal

Reliable enterprise AI needs three distinct proofs: that the action is statistically supportable, that the workflow succeeds from its own state, and that withdrawn authority can no longer create a durable effect. Confidence, turn accuracy and cancellation acknowledgement are not substitutes.

Three deep papers establish complementary controls:

  1. Available Guardrails separates certificate validity from certificate availability. Fine-grained safety targets can exhaust calibration evidence, and distribution shift can leave a formally valid gate unsafe for current traffic.
  2. When Better Turns Do Not Make Better Agents shows that supervised fine-tuning improves gold-history next-turn scores while complete tool workflows still fail. The best strict completion is 8/77; a holistic judge accepts 0/77 for every evaluated model.
  3. Authorization Revocation for Long-Running AI Agents formalizes root-scoped quiescence. Cancellation and token revocation do not settle already accepted work; an authorization cut, sink-local fence, exact carrier accounting and fail-closed evidence are required.

The Watchlist adds two useful but incomplete operating ideas. EnterpriseVal proposes a frozen use-case evaluation charter with value and risk gates, but its bank pilot omits sample sizes, confidence intervals, agreement statistics and harness parity. Efficient Benchmarking in Production shows that fixed question subsets can reduce recurring evaluation load, but the benchmark, question-level outcomes and implementation are not released, and the study does not test release-threshold errors.

Decision posture. Prototype closed-loop workflow qualification, a certified selective-action gate and a provider-free revocation shadow harness. Prepare configuration lineage, protected reporting-unit semantics and a full-benchmark sentinel. Monitor enterprise value modeling and automatic partition optimization until internal evidence and independent replication exist. Deprioritize any release policy that equates next-turn improvement with agent success or cancellation acknowledgement with effect closure.

Deep paper 1 — Safety gates need an evidence budget

Identity and status. Available Guardrails: Certifying Selective Prediction across ML Systems, Parivesh Priye, Yufeng Wang, Haibin Ling and Michael Chaykowsky; submitted September 18, 2026; arXiv:2609.22048v1; cs.LG; 26-page CC BY 4.0 manuscript. The TeX uses an ICLR 2027 conference template, but the arXiv record does not claim acceptance. The paper states that supplementary material contains code, preregistration records and machine-readable results; the downloaded arXiv source package contained the manuscript and figures but not those claimed experimental artifacts, and no public repository surfaced during verification. (paper)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

A selective predictor acts only when its estimated reliability crosses a threshold. Existing work focuses on whether a returned certificate is valid. This paper asks whether finite calibration data can produce a certificate at all for every declared reporting unit. It derives exact-binomial availability, optimizes contiguous partitions under a frozen reliability order, uses construction/selection/certification splits to reduce selection bias, and tests the safety–granularity–coverage frontier across synthetic designs, intent routing and four additional task families.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Conformal risk control and exact-binomial methods establish validity once a policy is selected. Fair or group risk control exposes heterogeneous risk. This paper's advance is to make availability an explicit deployment resource and optimize the reporting partition before a fresh certification sample. The preregistered synthetic confirmations, disjoint roles for planning and certification data, explicit strong baselines, per-model effects, controlled shift study and semantic constraints are unusually disciplined.

The study also demonstrates why optimization claims must be read cautiously. The structured candidate family contributes only 0.0027 mean coverage over held-out random-five candidates in the matched-pool mechanism study; most benefit comes from independent selection. In real intent data, the held-out planner does not dominate the random-order comparator. Cross-domain studies use one frozen predictor each, so resplits measure allocation variability rather than training variability. The BFCL experiment is single-turn tool calling, not an autonomous agent. The skin-lesion result is benchmark classification, not clinical efficacy. MovieLens allows users/items across random splits and excludes cold-start guarantees. The paper acknowledges that adaptive candidate choice and unmitigated drift fall outside the certificate.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The mathematical contract, datasets, split roles, training recipes, seeds and reporting tables are unusually complete. However, the arXiv package available during review did not include the code and machine-readable artifacts claimed in the reproducibility statement; therefore deterministic reproduction was not independently attempted. Enterprise readiness: high for offline frontier analysis and evidence-budget planning; medium for a shadow selective-action gate after semantic/legal group review and fresh calibration; low for autonomous production gating under uncontrolled shift. Research Utility Score: 92/100 (methodological rigor 25/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 14/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 14/15).

Deep paper 2 — Better turns can still produce failed workflows

Identity and status. When Better Turns Do Not Make Better Agents: Diagnosing the Gap Between Next-Turn Metrics and Workflow Success, Md Tahmid Rahman Laskar and 13 collaborators at Dialpad; submitted September 18, 2026; arXiv:2609.21187v1; cs.CL; seven-page CC BY 4.0 manuscript accepted to the REALM Workshop at EMNLP 2026. No public code or evaluation data is linked; the workflows, policies and tools are proprietary, although sanitized data-generation and judge prompts appear in the appendix. (paper)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

The study holds models and workflows fixed while changing the evaluation protocol. Qwen3-4B/14B and Gemma-3-4B/12B are evaluated before and after five-epoch supervised fine-tuning. Gold-history protocols reset every turn to the correct state; closed-loop replay makes each model continue from its own prior decisions. The held-out set contains 84 validated conversations, 542 next-action decisions and 77 tool-requiring workflows.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Task-oriented dialogue metrics and next-action benchmarks are cheap and reproducible but prevent early errors from contaminating later context. Interactive benchmarks such as τ-bench and τ²-bench evaluate trajectories, but this paper directly compares protocols on identical models and workflows. The protocol ladder—lexical similarity, judged turn success, deterministic tool matching, closed-loop completion and holistic success—shows exactly where the apparent improvement collapses.

The inference boundary is narrow. The training data is synthetically generated and filtered, with 5,834 training examples and 664 validation examples. The held-out sample is one customer-support domain; only two open model families are tested. Exact tool matching assumes a single reference trajectory even when several actions may be valid. The deterministic simulator can underrepresent ambiguous users, unexpected tool state and recovery. The holistic judge has no human agreement analysis. Most importantly, the paper demonstrates a failure of proxy validity, not a universal claim that fine-tuning cannot improve agents.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Model identities, training size, evaluation counts, prompts and headline tables are public, but proprietary policies, tool definitions, trajectories and no code prevent direct replication. Enterprise readiness: high for adding closed-loop and holistic slices to evaluation; medium for release gating once valid alternative paths and judge calibration are addressed; low for transferring reported failure rates. Research Utility Score: 89/100 (methodological rigor 23/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 12/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 15/15).

Deep paper 3 — Cancellation is not authorization closure

Identity and status. Authorization Revocation for Long-Running AI Agents: Root-Scoped Quiescence under Delegation and Asynchronous Execution, Genliang Zhu and Chu Wang; submitted September 18, 2026; arXiv:2609.21284v1; cs.PL/cs.AI/cs.CR; 39-page manuscript under arXiv's non-exclusive distribution license with complete proof appendix. The paper describes a registered executable artifact, runner, independent checker and adversarial traces, but the arXiv source package available during review contained only LaTeX/BibTeX; no public code repository or artifact URI was found. (paper)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

Long-running agents create authority carriers that outlive the initiating process: credentials, delegated tasks, queues, callbacks, reservations and provider-side operations. The paper defines root-scoped authorization quiescence. A durable cut retires one root epoch; registered sinks fence that epoch; every pre-fence acceptance receives an exact terminal receipt; continuing work must atomically rebind to an independently sufficient current root; cross-provider channel tokens must balance; missing evidence remains Indeterminate rather than becoming success.

Cancellation acknowledgement does not stop accepted work 2/2 cancellation-only cases acknowledge cancellation and accept the late epoch-1 effect Never equate requested cancellation with durable-effect prevention Provider-free inert effects, not live external services
Cutting authority without fencing the sink is incomplete 1/1 cut-only case blocks new authority expansion but admits already scheduled old-root effect Authorization issuer and effect sink need coordinated barriers Assumes every protected commit crosses a registered sink
Cut plus fence changes the outcome 2/2 cut-plus-fence, 1/1 restart and 1/1 stale-process late effects are rejected Test the linearization point at the durable boundary Four mechanism rejections are a small deterministic corpus
Fail-closed evidence distinguishes unknown from safe Root totals: six Quiescent, three Not-Quiescent and two Indeterminate; all insufficient-evidence cases remain Indeterminate Preserve uncertainty as a machine state Manifest completeness is a trusted premise
Independent checking catches semantic tampering Runner and checker agree 17/17; checker rejects 44/44 consistently rehashed semantic regressions Verify semantics, not only hashes Checker observes serialized evidence, not unexecuted external facts
Precise rebind preserves unrelated authority One alternate-root case advances root B to epoch 2 and rebinds exactly; conjunctive case rejects an incomplete witness Avoid over-revoking shared work Correct support antichains and registries are hard to maintain

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Cancellation APIs express intent; OAuth revocation closes a named credential; task frameworks expose lifecycle state; distributed termination detects process/message quiescence; graph revocation determines surviving authorization. The paper composes a narrower but practically important claim: after retiring a specific root epoch, no registered carrier or in-flight transfer can use that retired atom to create another protected acceptance within a bound manifest. It distinguishes quiescence from global idleness, business completion, rollback and physical cessation.

The formal discipline is strong: explicit threat model, exact support witnesses, linearized cut, local sink fences, crash-safe evidence, replay identity, counterexamples, proof obligations, a registered case corpus and a separately implemented checker. The assurance boundary is equally material. Complete carrier coverage, complete mediation, authentic leaves, correct adapter semantics, exact channel accounting, crash-safe storage and protected sinks are premises. An omitted queue, bypassable tool, unregistered credential or false provider receipt can invalidate the positive certificate. The experiment never invokes a real MCP, A2A, OAuth, cloud queue or payment provider. It measures a file-backed local semantics, not cross-provider production behavior, performance or usability.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper gives detailed state transitions, case definitions, registered outcomes and proof appendix, but the executable artifact is not publicly obtainable from the paper or source bundle reviewed. Enterprise readiness: medium-high for threat modeling and an inert shadow harness; low for a production certificate service without provider adapters, complete carrier discovery and independent implementation. Research Utility Score: 90/100 (methodological rigor 25/25; quantitative traceability 23/25; reproducibility/artifacts 12/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 15/15).

The manuscript footer states “Proceedings of the 10th MLSys Conference, 2027,” while the current arXiv record does not provide a journal reference; venue status therefore remains unverified. (paper)

Why Watchlist. The bank pilot is candidly incomplete: sample sizes, confidence intervals and agreement were not recorded; graders were not always blind; model harness parity is undocumented; baselines are estimated; one workflow has no results; agentic extensions are proposed but untested. Credit-memo citation precision is reported at 88% and hallucination at 1.6% for Gemini 2.5, but without denominators or intervals. Watch trigger: preregistered thresholds, reported denominators/CIs/agreement, blinded human grading, observed time baselines, parity-controlled model comparison and a public reference implementation.

Efficient Benchmarking in Production — credible deployment evidence, opaque reproduction

Efficient Benchmarking in Production: A Study of an Evolving LLM Agent, Yining She and Lei Lin; arXiv:2609.21267v1; submitted September 18, 2026; 28-page CC BY 4.0 manuscript. The study uses 574 complete runs of a 519-question production analytics-agent benchmark over 52 days, split chronologically 287/287. Multidimensional 2PL adaptive testing reaches 1.03 percentage-point MAE at 200 questions (38.5% of a full run) and 0.992 Spearman at 300. The team deployed difficulty-stratified fixed subsets for operational simplicity; without recalibration, the subset beats random sampling at 11/12 budgets across 299 runs from five additional families. (paper)

Why Watchlist. This is valuable first-hand production evidence, but one organization, one central benchmark and anonymized agent families limit external validity. The benchmark questions, question-level outcome matrix and implementation are not released. MAE and rank correlation do not quantify missed regressions, false release approvals or agreement at operational thresholds. Short-window stability may reflect a mature, slowly changing agent. Watch trigger: an internal replication with release-threshold error metrics, periodic full-run sentinels, material-change invalidation rules and a clear cost-versus-missed-regression curve.

Cross-paper synthesis

Independent Analysis. The five papers describe a three-boundary assurance chain:

  1. Before acting — statistical admission. Can the system certify the requested precision for this declared unit, population and granularity with current evidence? If not, abstain or collect labels.
  2. While acting — trajectory qualification. Does the workflow succeed from its own evolving state, with correct tool calls, policy compliance and recovery, rather than from a reset gold history?
  3. After authority changes — effect closure. Can retired authority still reach a protected sink through already accepted work, delegated tasks, queues or retries? If evidence is missing, remain Indeterminate.

The common failure is proxy substitution: confidence for certification, turn correctness for workflow completion, or cancellation status for effect closure. Each proxy is locally useful but becomes unsafe when promoted to a stronger claim. The shared remedy is a versioned contract that binds evidence to the exact prediction population, workflow configuration, authority epoch, semantic unit and durable effect boundary.

The papers also reveal a recurring economics problem. Finer guarantees consume more calibration data; closed-loop replay costs more than next-turn scoring; root-scoped closure requires instrumentation at every carrier and sink. These are not reasons to omit the controls. They are reasons to reserve stronger automation for consequences whose value justifies the evidence and observability cost.

Knowledge reinforcement

  1. Why can a valid gate be unavailable? A guarantee may be statistically correct when issued, yet finite calibration data may be too sparse to issue it for every required unit at the target precision and familywise error level.
  2. Why is gold history a weaker test than closed-loop replay? Gold history repairs prior mistakes before every turn. It measures local action prediction from a correct state, not whether the agent can construct, preserve and recover that state.
  3. What does a cancellation acknowledgement prove? Only that a cancellation request was acknowledged under the provider's lifecycle semantics. It does not settle already accepted work, delegated carriers or future durable effects.
  4. Why keep Indeterminate separate from failure? Not-Quiescent means known live authority or work; Indeterminate means required evidence is missing or conflicting. Collapsing the latter into success creates false closure.
  5. Why can semantic group protection reduce automation coverage? Preventing merges preserves legal, fairness and operational meaning but leaves less support per certifiable unit. The cost is real and should be budgeted, not hidden.
  6. Why must a benchmark subset retain a full-run sentinel? The agent, tools and item difficulties evolve. A subset can preserve average score while missing the exact regressions that determine a release.

Closing judgment

Today's strongest signal is architectural, not algorithmic. Enterprise AI needs to qualify permission to act, ability to finish, and ability to stop as separate properties. A calibrated selective gate cannot prove the workflow succeeds. A successful local action cannot prove the trajectory is safe. A cancellation status cannot prove retired authority is unable to commit. The near-term opportunity is to make those boundaries executable in shadow mode—using frozen configuration identities, independent evidence, complete traces and fail-closed uncertainty—before considering production automation.
