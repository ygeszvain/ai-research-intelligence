Scope, window, and evidence policy

Research window. Friday, September 11, 2026 at 4:00 PM CDT through Sunday, September 13, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT (36 hours; America/Chicago). arXiv's current computer-science listing still ended with the Friday, September 11 announcement cycle; no Saturday or Sunday cycle had appeared. Discovery covered current arXiv cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.SE, cs.CR, cs.IR, cs.OS, cs.HC, and stat.ML records, plus ACL Anthology, OpenReview, PMLR, Nature-family research pages, first-party repositories, releases, datasets, and cited prior work. The three selected records were announced in the September 11 update and carry September 10 v1 submission timestamps. The original window was sufficient; no seven-day expansion was used.

Anti-duplication. Exact titles, identifiers, principal claims, and enterprise implications were checked against the 34-report archive through September 12. None of the three deep papers or two Watchlist papers had appeared previously. Papers already used in the September 11 and September 12 briefs were excluded even where today's synthesis is adjacent.

Evidence policy. Paper Fact means directly reported by a paper or verified first-party artifact. Independent Analysis is this briefing's synthesis, calculation, or critique. Enterprise Inference translates evidence into a bounded future option. Uncertainty marks missing causal, generalization, artifact, economic, security, governance, or operational evidence. Preprints are not treated as peer reviewed. Reported model names, dates, licenses, and metrics are preserved as stated in the source. Benchmark performance is not production readiness. Every proposed experiment is isolated, reversible, measurable, and non-destructive.

Executive signal

A correct answer is not a complete evidence process, a high-performing skill is not a governed capability, and a benchmark score is not comparable until its evidence contract is preserved.

The three deep papers expose one enterprise design requirement from different directions. Mr.LHDR shows that final-answer accuracy can conceal missing prerequisite conclusions in long, multimodal investigations. COBRA-Skills shows that reusable procedures can be improved under an evaluation budget, but it also turns the optimization set into a governance boundary whose feedback can overfit or encode unsafe behavior. Benchmark Radar shows that benchmark discovery itself needs durable source identity, explicit missingness, versioned artifacts, and comparison eligibility rather than a flat leaderboard. (Mr.LHDR, COBRA-Skills, Benchmark Radar)

Decision posture. Prototype dependency-aware evaluation and evidence-preserving benchmark discovery now. Test budgeted skill optimization only in a quarantined shadow loop with held-out tasks, immutable trajectories, rollback, security review, and no production write authority. Do not use process scores as proof of provenance, optimize skills on production outcomes without an independent holdout, or compare numbers whose units, versions, prompts, tools, attempts, and evaluators are not aligned.

Top 3 Future Bets

  1. Benchmark evidence and suitability registry — 91.9/100. Why it matters: teams can select a benchmark quickly while still making an invalid comparison or losing the artifact, version, unit, and evaluation setting that make the score interpretable. Evidence that would change the recommendation: an internal audit showing the current benchmark catalog already preserves source-level identities, versions, units, missingness, artifacts, and protocol eligibility with negligible discovery failures. Next reversible action: ingest 25 active evaluations into a read-only registry and ask independent evaluators to reproduce five benchmark-selection decisions from the recorded evidence.
  2. Dependency-aware research completion gate — 91.4/100. Why it matters: a plausible final answer may be supported by an incomplete or dependency-inconsistent chain, especially when an early visual identification controls many later steps. Evidence that would change the recommendation: representative internal research tasks showing that prerequisite-gated process scoring adds no predictive value over answer, citation, and factuality checks, or creates unacceptable evaluator disagreement. Next reversible action: annotate ten high-value research questions with small evidence DAGs and score archived outputs without changing user-facing behavior.
  3. Evaluation contract and version lineage — 90.4/100. Why it matters: every result depends on the benchmark version, model route, prompt, tools, attempts, evaluator, unit, and date; silently changing one can invalidate a release decision. Evidence that would change the recommendation: existing internal experiment infrastructure already enforces immutable contracts and can reproduce historical score eligibility end to end. Next reversible action: wrap one existing evaluation in a signed manifest, rerun it twice, and require exact reconciliation of inputs, configuration, artifacts, and declared metric semantics.

Deep paper 1 — Final-answer accuracy hides broken research chains

Identity and status. Mr.LHDR: A Benchmark for Multimodal Real-World Long-Horizon Deep Research Agents, Minghao Guo et al.; submitted September 10, 2026; arXiv:2609.11318; cs.AI; preprint; arXiv perpetual non-exclusive license. The first-party repository is public under Apache-2.0 and links a Hugging Face dataset plus evaluation scripts. At review time the repository showed two commits and no tagged release. (paper, full text, artifact)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

Mr.LHDR contains 102 open-web questions across eight categories. Each item is authored from a hidden node–relation graph, includes a short stable answer, and requires at least one non-text element that changes the reasoning state. The benchmark contains 1,231 annotated intermediate conclusions, averaging 12.1 necessary conclusions and dependency depth 10.4. It reports Overall Accuracy (OA), Strict Accuracy (SA), Checklist Score (CS), and Dependency-Aware Checklist Score (DACS). DACS withholds credit for a correctly stated downstream conclusion when any annotated prerequisite is absent.

SA requires the final answer and every checklist conclusion to be correct. CS measures stated checklist coverage without dependency gating. DACS recursively requires predecessors, so DACS ≤ CS. The paper explicitly says these response-level metrics neither observe latent reasoning nor prove that a stated conclusion came from retrieval. They measure dependency-consistent stated conclusions, not provenance.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

The strongest design choice is structural supervision. BrowseComp emphasizes hard-to-find short answers; MM-BrowseComp adds multimodal evidence but averages about three checklist items; report benchmarks evaluate long-form output; fixed-corpus process benchmarks expose different traces. Mr.LHDR combines open-web search, necessary non-text evidence, short verifiable answers, hidden prerequisite DAGs, and prerequisite-gated scoring. Human annotators build graphs before questions, audit every key relation and source, test multimodal necessity, and reject direct-search shortcuts.

The benchmark remains small and intentionally constructed. Six AI researchers created and audited the items, so authoring conventions can leak across categories despite shortcut checks. The graph is one verified necessary explanation, not proof that every valid solution must traverse the same structure. Requiring conclusions to appear in the submitted response may reward verbose trace disclosure and penalize systems designed to return a concise answer plus citations. Open-web facts and sources can drift, while no complete temporal maintenance study is reported.

System comparisons are configuration-specific. Twenty-five systems span tool-augmented models, tool-free models, dedicated research services, and one open-source framework; the frameworks are not controlled versions of the same underlying model. Tool failures, provider search quality, context limits, and answer formatting can all affect scores. The authors correctly avoid claiming statistically separable rankings where confidence intervals overlap. A deployable enterprise evaluation would pair DACS-like scoring with source identity, retrieval logs, tool success, cost, latency, data-access policy, and human adjudication.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The repository exposes setup, run, judge, and score commands and an Apache-2.0 license; the data are downloadable separately. The paper provides prompts, metrics, system routes, bootstrap uncertainty, judge agreement, category splits, and ablations. Missing release tags, containers, frozen provider responses, total cost, and a permanent dataset snapshot still limit exact replication. Enterprise readiness: high for adapting the metric and building a small internal evaluation; medium for using the public benchmark as one research-agent diagnostic; low for treating any score as a production gate without internal tasks and provenance telemetry. Research Utility Score: 94/100 (rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 18/20; enterprise relevance 14/15; prior-work advance 14/15).

Deep paper 2 — Skill learning needs a budget and an admission boundary

Identity and status. COBRA-Skills: Contextual Bandit-Guided Evolution for Agent Skill Optimization, Pingchen Lu et al.; submitted September 10, 2026; arXiv:2609.11682; cs.AI; preprint; arXiv perpetual non-exclusive license. The public repository carries Apache-2.0 for original code, six benchmark adapters, fixed split manifests, configuration, tests, and third-party notices. Some benchmark payloads are excluded or require upstream terms; no frozen experiment outputs or tagged release were visible. (paper, full text, artifact)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

COBRA-Skills treats reusable text skills as arms in a dynamically evolving population. A neural reward predictor estimates performance from fixed embeddings; a LinearUCB bonus favors uncertain regions; the highest-priority candidate receives the next target-agent evaluation. Scheduled evolution prunes low-priority candidates and regenerates, mutates, or crosses over replacements using execution evidence. The experiments use 30 rounds, a population of 10, three pruned skills at an update, 50 optimization examples per benchmark, and a held-out 100-example test set.

Evaluation breadth 6 benchmarks × 3 target models; 3 independent runs; mean accuracy ± SE Tests QA, spreadsheets, documents, math, social inference, and embodied action Six small benchmark slices are not enterprise workflows or adversarial production data
Average improvement +13.1, +26.9, and +22.5 percentage points over no-skill baselines for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, GPT-5.4-Nano, and Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it Reusable optimized skills can materially improve these agents Attribution to the bandit alone; skill generation and evolution also contribute

The headline cost uses fixed per-million-token prices and counts model calls under the paper's protocol. It is not a total-cost-of-ownership estimate. The test set is held out from optimization, which is essential, but the same benchmark family supplies both sets and all hyperparameters are fixed after development; repeated benchmark-level research choices can still induce meta-overfitting.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Prior work generates skills from traces, optimizes instructions with repeated execution, retrieves skill libraries, or performs multi-round self-evolution. COBRA's advance is allocation: treat expensive target executions as a scarce sequential budget, learn which skill candidates are promising or underexplored, and evolve the search space rather than sampling a fixed pool. The comparison includes no-skill, one-shot grounded skill, Trace2Skill, and SkillOpt baselines; the ablations compare selection, evolution, and unstructured best-of-budget search.

The paper reports three runs and standard errors, but no multiple-comparison correction or paired statistical test across 54 model–benchmark cells. The primary average weights heterogeneous tasks equally, regardless of business value, failure severity, or evaluation reliability. SocialMaze has a soft score while the others use binary accuracy. One teacher (GPT-5.5), one embedding model, and a largely fixed prompt/evolution family create common dependencies. The paper has no explicit limitations section, no harmful-skill evaluation, no prompt-injection study, no privacy analysis, and no measurement of skill conflicts, stale skills, or rollback.

Optimization feedback is an attack and governance surface. Execution trajectories may contain secrets, untrusted retrieved content, or reward-hacking strategies. A skill that raises test accuracy could still broaden tool authority, evade logging, overfit evaluator quirks, or transfer poorly after a model/harness update. Production use therefore needs typed scope, source lineage, permission constraints, sandbox execution, holdouts isolated from the optimizer, canarying, signed versions, conflict tests, expiry, human approval, and instant rollback.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper and repository provide algorithm details, prompts, manifests, configuration, runnable commands, benchmark adapters, test instructions, sandbox notes, and licensing boundaries. Exact result reproduction remains costly and incomplete without frozen outputs, tagged environments, model endpoints/checkpoints, and all benchmark payloads. Enterprise readiness: high for a sandboxed shadow optimizer on synthetic tasks; medium for manually reviewed skill candidates in a low-risk developer workflow; low for autonomous skill promotion into production. Research Utility Score: 90/100 (rigor 22/25; quantitative traceability 23/25; reproducibility/artifacts 17/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 13/15).

Deep paper 3 — Benchmark discovery is an evidence system, not a leaderboard

Identity and status. Benchmark Radar: A Living Database and Search Engine for AI Benchmarks and Evaluation, Koutian Wu et al.; submitted September 10, 2026; arXiv:2609.11115; cs.AI; preprint; arXiv perpetual non-exclusive license. The software repository is MIT licensed; the technical report and original editorial content are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 with explicit commercial restrictions. The verified v0.11.0 release pins commit 8f46bbf, provides frozen paper data, a rebuilt PDF, checksums, and reports 1,314 passing tests. (paper, full text, repository, v0.11.0 release)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

Benchmark Radar separates a benchmark catalog from daily discovery observations. Source-specific records retain their identity rather than being merged into a fictitious global benchmark count. Search uses BM25F with transparent field and phrase boosts; retrieval returns candidates with matched fields, missing query terms, sources, and score components. Numeric comparisons are admitted only when the unit, direction, value range, and relevant settings support the calculation.

The most important negative result is eligibility. Equal-looking numbers do not imply a shared scale, ceiling, test version, prompt, tools, number of attempts, or evaluator. The system preserves these uncertainties rather than filling them in. This is a design pattern for enterprise evaluation registries, not evidence that every catalog entry is suitable.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Existing leaderboards and catalogs organize scores, but Benchmark Radar emphasizes provenance, source-specific identity, daily discovery, explicit missingness, score history, and a reproducible census. The full-population audit avoids filtering out unscored or inconvenient records. A frozen release links the manuscript's counts to exact files and hashes. This is stronger evidence hygiene than a live dashboard whose data silently changes beneath a paper.

Coverage is still shaped by four seed catalogs and operational connectors. LLM Stats contributes 687 of 1,283 records, OpenCompass 461, Artificial Analysis 25, and model reports 110; these are not a probability sample of AI evaluation. GitHub Search hit a 300-row cap in the audited discovery run. Exact-identifier linking misses artifacts with absent or inconsistent identifiers, and only 59 of 6,546 artifacts had observations from multiple sources. The paper audits catalog structure, not semantic correctness of every description or numeric claim.

Search quality is not experimentally evaluated. BM25F is transparent and reproducible, but recall for new terminology, multilingual phrasing, or domain synonyms remains unknown. The worked example demonstrates a workflow, not an outcome study. Enterprise use would need internal benchmark ownership, access classification, license policy, vendor/model aliases, deprecation state, evaluation-run lineage, sensitive dataset controls, and reviewer sign-off. The content license also limits commercial integration even though the software is MIT.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

This is the strongest artifact package in today's set: public code, tests, immutable release, checksums, paper data, data contracts, citation metadata, CLI, and explicit content/software licenses. Enterprise readiness: high for borrowing the evidence model or testing the software in a read-only catalog; medium for adopting the public dataset after legal and coverage review; low for allowing the catalog to approve benchmarks or model releases automatically. Research Utility Score: 93/100 (rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 20/20; enterprise relevance 13/15; prior-work advance 12/15).

Watchlist

Scientific figure reasoning needs complete evidence acquisition, not just cropping

Sci-MMR: Benchmarking Multi-Step Evidence-Grounded Scientific Reasoning in Multimodal Agents, Jiaqiang Li et al.; submitted September 10, 2026; arXiv:2609.11243; cs.AI; preprint. It constructs 235 tasks from 170 peer-reviewed papers across four disciplines and 35 domains, with an average of nine figure panels per task. Across eight multimodal models, answer accuracy exceeds complete-evidence recovery by more than 20 points. Under direct visual reasoning, models locate at least one relevant region in 62.1% of runs but recover the complete evidence set in only 12.7%. Evidence-related failures account for 57.2% of errors; cropping raises accuracy only 4.5 points, whereas gold evidence improves it by 19.1–37.0 points. Independent reviewer agreement is reported for regions, evidence, claims, edges, questions, and answers (κ 0.77–0.87). (paper, full text)

Why Watchlist. The controlled caption, direct-visual, evidence-hint, and tool-use settings separate acquisition from integration unusually well. But an argument graph is one reconstruction, process metrics score stated evidence rather than latent reasoning, and LLM judging retains bias. No code or dataset artifact was verified from the reviewed record. Monitor for a stable artifact, license, leakage analysis, source-paper split policy, and independent reproduction before using it as a gate. Research Utility Score: 85/100 (rigor 24; traceability 23; artifacts 10; enterprise relevance 14; advance 14).

A reverse reasoning path may help when agents disagree—but only in closed label spaces

When Agents Disagree: Bayesian Backward Reasoning as a Label-Free Anchor for Multi-Agent Collective Decision-Making, Ken Chen, Wei Wang, Sachith Seneviratne, Hansani Weeratunge, and Saman Halgamuge; submitted September 10, 2026; arXiv:2609.11709; cs.AI; preprint. It builds a shared reverse posterior from an explicit likelihood and prior, ranks forward agents by Jensen–Shannon divergence, and tests hard selection, soft weighting, and log-linear fusion on a fixed 2,000-case slice of synthetic DDXPlus diagnostic data with 49 labels, five prompt agents, and five backbones. The paper reports consistent gains, especially on disagreement cases, while acknowledging that the reverse posterior is a biased consistency signal rather than ground truth. (paper, full text)

Why Watchlist. The differently factorized anchor is a useful challenge to majority vote and another forward LLM judge. Yet evidence is limited to one synthetic closed-label medical dataset, same-backbone prompt pools, one reverse construction, and an extra two LLM elicitation steps per case. The method cannot directly handle open-ended generation and no code artifact was verified. Monitor for mixed-backbone, multi-domain, calibration, adversarial-correlation, cost, and open-ended studies. Research Utility Score: 80/100 (rigor 21/25; quantitative traceability 22/25; reproducibility/artifacts 7/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 15/15).

Cross-paper synthesis

The reviewed evidence supports eight operating rules:

  1. Score the evidence process separately from the answer. OA, SA, CS, and DACS measure different failure surfaces; none proves provenance.
  2. Represent prerequisite structure explicitly for high-value research. A downstream claim should not receive full credit when its required upstream evidence is absent.
  3. Make multimodal acquisition observable. “Used vision” is too coarse; record which regions, documents, charts, or frames were located, extracted, and integrated.
  4. Treat skills as governed executable policy. Every learned procedure needs scope, lineage, permissions, tests, owner, expiry, conflict handling, and rollback.
  5. Keep optimizer and holdout authority separate. Optimization examples and reward signals are not admissible release evidence; independent tasks and evaluators must decide promotion.
  6. Preserve benchmark identity and missingness. Do not merge related source records or silently coerce missing dates, units, scores, artifacts, or organizations.
  7. Comparison is an admitted operation. Matching numbers are insufficient; versions, prompts, tools, attempts, evaluators, units, direction, and dates must be compatible.
  8. Disagreement diversity must be structural. More forward agents can share the same error; a reverse path may add signal, but cross-path agreement is not a correctness certificate.

Independent Analysis. Together these papers define an evidence-control loop: structure the claim and its prerequisites, collect modality-specific evidence with stable identity, evaluate completion under an immutable contract, learn reusable procedures only in quarantine, and admit benchmark comparisons or skill promotions through independent gates. The shared foundation is not a particular model vendor. It is typed provenance, versioned evaluation, experiment isolation, policy-aware identity, reversible promotion, and an observable chain from evidence to decision.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

Independent Analysis. Over the next 12–24 months, the reviewed signals favor an enterprise architecture with a distinct evidence control plane rather than more logic inside a single agent prompt.

  1. Model gateway: preserve immutable model/provider/route identities, tool availability, prompt versions, token/cost telemetry, and evaluation compatibility.
  2. Context and knowledge services: assign stable source and region IDs, carry tenant/user authorization into retrieval, snapshot time-sensitive evidence, and support deletion/invalidation.
  3. Agent orchestration: separate trajectory execution from skill generation and from promotion authority; expose tool success, modality acquisition, and dependency state.
  4. Evaluation infrastructure: support answer, citation, evidence coverage, prerequisite consistency, safety, cost, latency, and subgroup slices under signed contracts.
  5. Benchmark registry: retain source-specific records, artifacts, licenses, units, direction, dates, and missingness; search proposes candidates but cannot approve suitability.
  6. Skill registry: store lineage, scope, permissions, compatibility, tests, owners, expiry, canary state, incidents, and rollback pointers.
  7. Security and policy enforcement: isolate optimizer data and credentials, redact trajectories, validate learned procedures, and deny capabilities not declared in the skill contract.
  8. Observability: join evidence objects, claims, model/tool events, evaluation results, human decisions, costs, and incidents through stable IDs.
  9. Human-review layer: adjudicate high-impact chain breaks, benchmark suitability, skill promotions, exceptions, appeals, and rollback decisions.
  10. Experiment infrastructure: provide frozen fixtures, independent holdouts, multi-seed replay, model-update rehearsals, and non-destructive shadow execution.

Shared foundations unlock multiple enhancements: model and artifact identity; data provenance; policy-aware IAM; evaluation manifests; source/region addressing; experiment isolation; cost telemetry; human approval; and reversible promotion. Internal stack, data classification, workflow criticality, legal obligations, latency targets, and current evaluation maturity must be supplied before implementation choices are made.

Knowledge reinforcement

  1. Why can OA exceed SA? OA checks the final answer; SA also requires every necessary intermediate conclusion, so a guessed or incompletely supported answer may pass OA and fail SA.
  2. What does DACS add to checklist coverage? It withholds downstream credit when annotated prerequisites are missing, distinguishing isolated correct statements from a dependency-consistent chain.
  3. Does DACS prove retrieval provenance? No. It scores what the response states, not whether the system actually retrieved or used the cited evidence.
  4. Why is a held-out test not enough for production skill promotion? The optimizer can still exploit benchmark-family quirks; production admission also needs security, permissions, privacy, drift, cost, model-version, and rollback evidence.
  5. Why preserve source-specific benchmark records? Two catalogs may describe related tests under different versions, units, artifacts, or settings; premature merging destroys evidence identity.
  6. When are two percentage-looking scores comparable? Only after unit, direction, benchmark version, prompt, tools, attempts, evaluator, and date/settings are compatible.
  7. What is the near-term use of Sci-MMR? Separate multimodal evidence acquisition, extraction, and integration in diagnostics; do not treat its score as enterprise readiness.
  8. What is the limit of reverse-path agreement? It can supply a different error factorization in a closed label space, but agreement remains a consistency signal—not a certificate.
