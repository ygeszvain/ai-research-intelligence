Executive decision

Central signal — Independent Analysis. This release makes one control-plane lesson unusually concrete: measure the whole effect, authorize every side effect, and recalibrate when the operating policy changes. A faster workflow can merely defer defects; a successful agent task can conceal an unauthorized disclosure; and a fixed predictor can become miscalibrated solely because the policy choosing its inputs changed.

The strongest decision signal is not a new model. It is a better definition of success. Counting the Bandages shows that the same 246 developer issues support materially different time estimates depending on the estimand, that review alone bounds one advertised 100× workflow to at most 3.56× under the paper's own assumptions, and that an AI reviewer told a false low error rate checked 7.2 fewer of nine figures and let errors escape in 22.5% of briefs rather than none. The result is a warning about productivity measurement, not a universal estimate of AI value.

The security signal is immediately testable. Log-To-Leak induces a post-task call to an attacker-controlled MCP tool while often preserving the user-visible task. Across 18 valid model–server pairs, attack success is 62.64–100%; three evaluated defenses leave more than 55% residual attack success in the focal GitHub/GPT-4o setting. Task completion is therefore an unsafe proxy for authorized execution.

The uncertainty result supplies the statistical analogue. Calibrated for Which Policy? leaves the world model fixed but changes the state–action distribution. Under the strongest controlled shift, ordinary nominal-90% conformal coverage falls to 55.0%; oracle and learned-ratio weighting recover 91.0% and 90.9%. The evidence is rigorous but narrow: 192 physical transitions, one-step marginal guarantees, controlled support, and shared conditional dynamics.

Decision posture: prototype effect-inclusive productivity ledgers and host-controlled secondary-call authorization now, in read-only or simulated settings. Prepare policy-change calibration and common evidence/action telemetry foundations. Monitor deterministic-first routing and contextual PII masking until enterprise-shaped data, transfer evidence, and operating costs are known. Do not approve production deployment from these papers alone.

Deep paper 1 — Counting the Bandages

Citation: Solomon Shalom Lijo. Counting the Bandages: On measuring the time that AI saves. arXiv:2610.35942v1, submitted October 23, 2026. License: CC BY 4.0. Artifacts: paper-linked code and data and Zenodo record.

What the paper actually does

The paper distinguishes a stopwatch view of productivity from a ledger view. The stopwatch ends when the nominal task ends. The ledger follows both assisted and unassisted workflows until the output is used, then records time saved alongside excess escaped defects. Its decision statistic is a break-even defect cost: how costly an additional escaped defect would have to be before an apparent time saving ceases to be beneficial.

The contribution has four layers. First, it supplies model-free outcome definitions. Second, a stylized checking model predicts that a calibrated reviewer increases checking effort with the logarithm of the drafter's error rate and that optimism buys visible minutes by allowing more downstream cost. Third, it reanalyzes published productivity studies using partial identification rather than a single imported assumption. Fourth, it preregisters a small experiment in which Claude Sonnet 5.5 reviews Claude Haiku 5.5 drafts built from post-training-cutoff SEC filings.

The reanalysis is deliberately unsettling. The METR public data contain 246 issues, but the estimated slowdown is 19% or 24% depending on the estimand, and 34% before adjusting a chance imbalance. The fraction of issues helped is only partially identified at 3–82%. In GDPval's review-every-output workflow, experts take 404 minutes, review takes 109 minutes, generation about 4.5 minutes, and GPT-5 wins against expert output 39% of the time. Allowing failed-draft fix cost to range from zero to a full redo produces a one-try speedup interval of 1.12–3.56×. The 100× announcement measured model time rather than the reviewed workflow.

The experiment isolates checking effort. In the truthful-error-rate arm, review effort rises by 2.2 lookups per unit increase in log error probability, but 96% of reviews choose either zero or all nine lookups. In the over-trust arm, the true draft error rate is 30% but the reviewer is told 3%. It performs 7.2 fewer lookups and allows errors through in 22.5% of 40 briefs versus zero in the truthful condition. Every review that inspected a line exposing the wrong figure corrected it; all 16 escapes occur when no revealing line was checked. The failure therefore runs through effort, not a reduced ability to recognize errors once seen.

Prior-work comparison

The paper does not claim to invent cost-effectiveness analysis, optimal search, or human–AI complementarity. It maps the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio to AI time-saved claims, specializes classic search results to checking, and applies partial-identification tools to “share helped” and “share reliable.” Its novelty is the integrated ledger and reporting standard, plus a controlled AI-reviewer demonstration of how false reliability beliefs change effort.

Methodological critique

  1. The model is intentionally stylized. It assumes one defect per draft, exponential search, risk neutrality, fixed checking budget, and no false alarms. Real review finds correlated defect clusters and can introduce new defects.
  2. The audit is not omniscient. Differential audit sensitivity across defect types can bias the excess-defect comparison; equal miss rates attenuate it.
  3. Downstream defect cost is not measured. The break-even statistic makes this explicit, but enterprise adoption still requires a local loss function.
  4. The METR bound lacks sampling uncertainty. The 3–82% identified interval is a plug-in result, not a confidence set around the identified set.
  5. The reviewer experiment is narrow. One reviewer model, one structured finance task, seeded defects, explicitly stated costs, and power for large effects do not establish human or cross-domain behavior.
  6. Model-family dependence is unresolved. Both reviewer and drafter are Claude models; Claude also assisted paper preparation. This is disclosed but increases the value of independent reproduction.
  7. The framework can be gamed if outcomes stop too early. Repair and escaped defects require a follow-up horizon and ownership definitions; otherwise the “ledger” can become another incomplete KPI.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Paper Fact: the paper provides formulas, code/data links, a Zenodo archive, preregistered hypotheses, exact denominators, bootstrap procedures, and a CC BY 4.0 license. Uncertainty: artifact execution and provenance were not independently rerun in this briefing; external validity is limited. Enterprise Inference: an organization can adopt the measurement schema before changing any production system. The minimum useful ledger joins baseline and assisted completion time, review effort, repairs, escaped defects, and follow-up outcomes at the unit of work. A production incentive or investment decision additionally needs internal workflow boundaries, defect taxonomy, loss estimates, audit sensitivity, privacy/retention rules, and worker-level clustering.

Deep paper 2 — Log-To-Leak

Citation: Yuepeng Hu, Chongyu Fan, Sirat Samyoun, and Jian Du. Log-To-Leak: Prompt Injection Attacks on Tool-Using LLM Agents via the Model Context Protocol. arXiv:2610.36108v1, submitted October 26, 2026; AGENT-SEC '26, DOI 10.1145/3847320.3847399. License: CC BY 4.0. Artifact status: the paper says code will be released without live exfiltration infrastructure, secrets, or real-user data; no repository is linked on the official arXiv record.

What the paper actually does

The attack lives in natural-language MCP tool metadata. An attacker publishes a logging tool and an attacker-controlled server whose description instructs the agent to make an additional call after the final legitimate call. The template composes four elements: a temporal trigger, binding to the logging tool, a stated operational justification, and pressure framing the action as mandatory. The payload attempts to carry the user query, MCP server name, server response, and agent response.

The evaluation separates three questions often conflated in agent security: did the unauthorized call happen, were the leaked fields accurate, and did the legitimate task still complete? It tests GPT-4o, GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4, and GPT-OSS-120B across GitHub, Mapbox, PayPal, Playwright, and YFinance MCP servers. The task set contains 555 queries: 160 each for GitHub, Mapbox, and PayPal, plus 35 Playwright and 40 YFinance queries from MCP-Universe. Eighteen model–server pairs produce valid tool execution.

Across those pairs, Log-To-Leak reaches 62.64–100% attack success and 76.55–95.64% logging accuracy conditional on a logging call. In 16 pairs, absolute target-task completion changes are at most 6.63 percentage points. The two exceptions are GPT-5 on GitHub (−21.50 points) and Mapbox (−18.38). The attack can therefore remain invisible to a success-only monitor.

The focal GitHub/GPT-4o comparison is especially useful. Log-To-Leak reaches 62.64% ASR and 94.80% conditional logging accuracy while target-task and malicious-server legitimate completion do not change; it adds about 8.2k tokens. Adapted Combined Attack and TopicAttack baselines each stay below 4.5% ASR and reduce target-task completion by 11.25 and 13.75 points.

Defense results show why metadata classification alone is brittle. A hidden-state/gradient instruction detector flags 91/100 injected descriptions but also 136/159 benign descriptions, an 85.53% benign flag rate. A GPT-4o metadata auditor allows descriptions that still yield 55.38% residual ASR; IPIGuard leaves 60.47%. The paper argues for host-side dependency allowlists, cross-server argument data-flow checks, and user or policy confirmation for logging-like calls.

Prior-work comparison

Indirect prompt injection and MCP tool poisoning already demonstrate malicious selection, cross-tool actions, and data exfiltration. Log-To-Leak narrows the behavior: an additive call after the last legitimate call, with separate metrics for call induction, field fidelity, primary task completion, legitimate attacker-server calls, and token use. That measurement decomposition—especially the refusal to treat primary-task success as evidence of safety—is the practical advance.

Methodological critique

  1. The attacker must get a server enabled. Installation review, allowlists, tenant policy, and network controls can materially shrink exposure.
  2. The test matrix is finite. Four models, five servers, and one deployed configuration each for Cursor and Claude Desktop do not establish ecosystem prevalence.
  3. Design and defense concentrate on one pair. Injection-position selection, ablations, baselines, and defense evaluation use GitHub MCP with GPT-4o, creating a legitimate transfer question.
  4. Generated prompts dominate three servers. GPT-4o generated 480/555 tasks; realism and task difficulty may not match production distributions.
  5. Conditional LA can look high at low ASR. The paper correctly separates them, but readers must not interpret LA as end-to-end leak probability.
  6. Attack cost is material. The focal run adds roughly 8.2k tokens. Latency, billing anomalies, rate limits, and trace inspection could become defenses, but are not evaluated as such.
  7. Safe artifact availability is pending. The paper describes reproducibility detail but withholds live exfiltration infrastructure and has no linked repository at review time.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Paper Fact: official full text gives task counts, models, servers, metrics, prompt construction, ablations, defense configurations, ethics, and a CC BY 4.0 license. Uncertainty: a safe code release is promised rather than linked; provider versions and MCP clients evolve quickly. Enterprise Inference: the most defensible near-term control is at call time: treat tool metadata as untrusted, bind permitted tools and data fields to the user's task, block undeclared cross-server flows, and require confirmation or policy approval for post-task/logging-like side effects. Adoption needs the internal MCP/tool inventory, data classifications, side-effect taxonomy, identity propagation, false-positive tolerance, incident response, and vendor enforcement points.

Deep paper 3 — Calibrated for Which Policy?

Citation: Ayush Mittal and Dhruv Gupta. Calibrated for Which Policy? Conformal Uncertainty for Fixed World Models Under Intervention. arXiv:2610.35877v1, submitted August 20, 2026 and surfaced in the current new/recent listing. License: CC BY 4.0. Artifact: commit-pinned Lean 4 formalization; experimental model and trajectories use released JEPA-WMs/DINO-WM assets.

What the paper actually does

The paper studies a fixed world model under a changed deployment policy. If source and target policies induce different state–action query distributions but preserve the conditional physical mechanism, uncertainty calibrated on source data is calibrated under the wrong measure. For one-step prediction, the required likelihood ratio depends only on pre-outcome covariates. In policy-controlled dynamics it factors into state-occupancy and action-propensity ratios.

Weighted conformal prediction transports marginal coverage when that ratio is known. When the ratio is estimated with a classifier distinguishing source from target covariates, the paper bounds target coverage by nominal coverage minus total-variation error between the true target law and the learned surrogate. A second bound connects population log-loss regret to ratio error. A cancellation improves dependence from a generic inverse-square clipping factor to an inverse-linear factor in the target prior and clipping parameter.

The experiment freezes a released DINO-WM predictor and uses a pool of 192 physical Franka robot transitions. Controlled exponential tilts create four target policies indexed by shift strength. Across 50 repetitions, each uses source calibration size 500; learned weights come from an independently fitted domain classifier. At nominal 90% and the strongest shift, unweighted coverage falls to 55.0%. Oracle weighting gives 91.0%; learned weighting gives 90.9%, with an effective sample size of 73.2 and a modestly increased radius of 1.281 versus 1.183 without shift.

The learned surrogate lies 0.7–0.9% total variation from the target across the grid. Naively clipping weights at five looks statistically attractive—effective sample size rises from 73 to 125 at the strongest shift—but changes the law being calibrated: total variation rises from 0.9% to 18.7% and coverage falls to 83.8%. This is a useful operational warning against treating effective sample size as the objective.

Prior-work comparison

Weighted conformal prediction under covariate shift, propensity correction, off-policy evaluation, and domain-classifier density-ratio estimation all predate this work. The contribution identifies the policy intervention's ratio for a world-model setting, composes estimated-ratio error with coverage, sharpens one regret bound, tests the failure on physical transitions, and machine-checks the finite-support algebra. It does not solve general adaptive or trajectory-level uncertainty.

Methodological critique

  1. The physical pool is small and reused. There are 192 transitions; target laws are controlled reweightings over this finite support, not naturally arising policy deployments.
  2. The guarantee is one-step and marginal. It does not ensure conditional coverage, multistep trajectory safety, or simultaneous reliability over a plan.
  3. Shared dynamics are assumed. Sensor changes, actuator wear, environment drift, generated dynamics, and feedback-induced mechanism changes violate the core condition.
  4. Support overlap is essential. Target mass outside source support cannot be repaired by weighting; this is a stop condition, not a tuning problem.
  5. Ratio fitting is unusually favorable. The controlled exponential tilt yields a correctly specified linear logit and allows exact population regret/TV computation over the pool.
  6. Effective sample size falls sharply. At the strongest shift, ESS near 73 means intervals and decisions may become operationally conservative even when coverage is restored.
  7. Formal verification checks derivations, not applicability. Lean reduces algebraic risk but cannot validate covariate choice, support, policy logging, or conditional invariance.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Paper Fact: the paper provides complete algorithm steps, all coverage tables, 50-repetition intervals, public upstream assets, a commit-pinned Lean project, and CC BY 4.0 licensing. Uncertainty: the briefing did not rerun robot experiments; no general deployment study tests learned ratios under model misspecification or evolving support. Enterprise Inference: treat a policy, routing, or planner change as a calibration-change event. Store source and target policy identifiers, pre-outcome covariates, action propensities, support diagnostics, conformal scores, ESS, and mismatch estimates. Adoption requires internal model use cases where conditional invariance is plausible, policy logs, safety consequences, acceptable coverage/radius tradeoffs, and a fallback when overlap fails.

ReCoPII — contextual PII masking from relation-grounded synthetic data

Citation: Taehwan Lee. ReCoPII: A Relation-Grounded Framework for Building Contextual PII Masking Datasets. arXiv:2610.35925v1, submitted October 14, 2026 and surfaced in the current listing. License: arXiv perpetual non-exclusive license; no code/data license or public repository was verified.

The paper addresses over-masking: the same surface type can be private in one relationship and preservable in another. It plans ontology-constrained relations, asks an LLM to write placeholder-bearing insertions, fills values and annotations deterministically, and uses a separate LLM reviewer before deriving MASK/KEEP labels from relation edges. ReCoPII-Ko contains 1,857 Korean documents, 25,656 relation edges, 17,104 MASK and 6,780 KEEP entities across six domains.

The strongest evidence is human, not merely synthetic-label accuracy. Four project-member annotators review 300 paired documents, yielding 1,200 pair reviews. Agreement with sampled KEEP labels is 88.42% for ReCoPII versus 26.27% for matched SPY-style augmentation; MASK agreement is 96.29% versus 95.75%. On the human-verified integrated test, four detectors meeting the validation leakage budget over-mask no more than 5.04% and leak no more than 4.18%. The paper also reports that its detector is statistically comparable to XLM-R-large on KDPII with about one quarter of the parameters: 86.71±1.47 versus 85.32±1.24, difference interval [−0.37, 3.04].

Why Watchlist: the human audit is unusually careful, source groups are kept across split boundaries, and the system distinguishes generated labels from human judgments. But all documents are Korean, much supervision is synthetic, annotators are project members, RQ2-B uses an earlier snapshot/split, model choices were partly informed by earlier test errors, and no usable code/data artifact or license was verified. A future enterprise option is a relation-aware privacy decision layer, but only after local policy owners construct adjudicated, multilingual, real-document tests and define false-release loss.

KORA — deterministic-first execution control

Citation: Albert Heekwan Kim. KORA: Deterministic-First Execution Control for AI Workloads. arXiv:2610.35869v1, submitted May 24, 2026 and surfaced in the current listing. License: CC BY 4.0. Artifact: paper-linked code and benchmark repository.

KORA puts a route/validate/escalate controller before model inference. In a 100-task deterministic-heavy benchmark, it avoids 80 simulated model calls and reports zero mismatches in the deterministic subset. A separate synthetic customer-support workload demonstrates local/no-network route telemetry.

Why Watchlist: the architectural instinct is sound and the artifact is reproducible, but the benchmark is designed to contain 80 deterministic tasks; calls are simulated, workloads synthetic, and there is no production traffic, provider cost, latency distribution, energy measurement, user study, or real-provider outcome. The paper itself explicitly refuses those interpretations. An enterprise should test whether an actual request distribution contains stable, verifiable deterministic slices before building a routing layer.

Cross-paper synthesis

Three ways a metric becomes detached from reality

  1. The workflow boundary is too short. Clock time ends before review, repair, or escaped defects. The fix is an outcome ledger and an explicit downstream-cost threshold.
  2. The security boundary is too shallow. “Task completed” ignores extra calls after the final legitimate action. The fix is per-action purpose and authorization, including cross-server flow controls.
  3. The statistical boundary is too static. Calibration is evaluated under the collection policy while a new controller changes the query distribution. The fix is policy-versioned calibration with support and density-ratio checks.

These are not interchangeable failures, but they share a governance pattern: the system that produces an output should not unilaterally define the accounting boundary, authorize its side effects, or certify that its uncertainty remains valid.

Prototype Soon — Host-controlled secondary-call authorization

  • Research signal and supporting paper: Log-To-Leak reaches 62.64–100% ASR across 18 valid model–server pairs while 16 preserve target-task completion within 6.63 points. Evidence label: Paper Fact for results; Enterprise Inference for capability.
  • Problem / opportunity: user-visible success does not reveal unauthorized logging or cross-server disclosure.
  • Future capability: the host evaluates every tool call against declared task purpose, approved dependencies, data-field policy, timing/sequence, and side-effect class; it denies or confirms unbound post-task and cross-server calls.
  • Placement / value: orchestration, agent layer, model gateway, security/governance; risk reduction, reliability, customer trust, incident containment. Technical value is enforcement independent of model obedience.
  • Maturity / confidence / horizon / effort: strong controlled multi-model evidence; no production prevalence estimate. Confidence: High. Horizon: Now. Effort: M.
  • Dependencies: tool registry, action schema, identity propagation, data classification, policy engine, immutable traces, user-confirmation UX.
  • Risks: false positives break legitimate multi-tool work; covert flows inside allowed arguments; policy drift; approval fatigue; incomplete vendor enforcement.
  • Build / buy / partner: Build the policy and purpose-binding layer; buy/partner for gateway integration and content inspection. Enterprise-specific action semantics are unlikely to be safely outsourced in full.
  • Minimum reversible experiment: isolated 100-task replay across actual candidate servers/models; inject poisoned metadata, deny unbound calls, and use synthetic data only.
  • Success metrics / stop conditions: ≥95% blocked unauthorized calls, ≤2% absolute legitimate-task loss, complete decision trace, no production credentials. Stop if the host cannot observe arguments or enforcement requires broad production access.
  • Roadmap trigger / internal context required: independent reproduction on the intended client stack plus acceptable false-positive and latency budgets. Need server inventory, task graphs, sensitive-field policy, and accountable approval owners.

Prototype Soon — Effect-inclusive AI productivity ledger

  • Research signal and supporting paper: Counting the Bandages' METR, GDPval, and reviewer analyses show large estimand sensitivity and under-checking risk. Evidence label: Paper Fact for study results; Enterprise Inference for adoption.
  • Problem / opportunity: stopwatch metrics can reward shifted work and escaped defects.
  • Future capability: join assisted and control workflow time with review, repair, incident/rework, quality, and follow-up outcomes; report time saved, excess escaped defects, uncertainty, and break-even defect cost.
  • Placement / value: evaluation, observability, human-review layer, applications; productivity, quality, cost, risk reduction, investment discipline. Technical value is a reusable causal outcome schema.
  • Maturity / confidence / horizon / effort: mature measurement concepts; new AI-specific integration but narrow model-review experiment. Confidence: High. Horizon: Now. Effort: S–M.
  • Dependencies: unit-of-work IDs, baselines, randomized or credible quasi-experimental design, defect taxonomy, audit process, follow-up horizon, privacy safeguards.
  • Risks: surveillance concerns, selective logging, unobserved downstream cost, metric gaming, spillovers between workers, underpowered subgroup claims.
  • Build / buy / partner: Build definitions and analysis; buy commodity telemetry where useful. The loss function and workflow boundary are enterprise-specific.
  • Minimum reversible experiment: rescore one completed pilot from immutable logs; do not alter incentives or staffing.
  • Success metrics / stop conditions: ≥90% linkage of work units to review/repair outcomes, prespecified estimand, uncertainty intervals, explicit missingness, decision sensitivity to defect cost. Stop if outcomes cannot be linked without disproportionate privacy risk.
  • Roadmap trigger / internal context required: replicated positive value after realistic defect costs across at least two workflows. Need baselines, downstream owners, error severity, labor accounting, and works-council/HR review where applicable.

Prototype Soon — Action/effect trace and purpose binding

  • Research signal and supporting papers: Counting the Bandages requires follow-through from generation to escaped defect; Log-To-Leak requires visibility after the nominal task. Evidence label: Independent Analysis.
  • Problem / opportunity: fragmented traces cannot explain why a call occurred or what cost/outcome followed.
  • Future capability: immutable event IDs link user intent, retrieved evidence, model decisions, policy checks, tool calls, human approvals, outputs, reviews, repairs, and delayed outcomes; each action carries a declared purpose and causal parent.
  • Placement / value: orchestration, observability, evaluation, security/governance; reliability, debugging speed, cost visibility, auditability, risk reduction. Technical value is a shared substrate for three backlog items.
  • Maturity / confidence / horizon / effort: telemetry patterns are mature; semantic purpose binding is less standardized. Confidence: Moderate. Horizon: Now. Effort: M–L.
  • Dependencies: common event schema, stable identifiers, clock synchronization, access controls, retention/deletion rules, sampling strategy, lineage store.
  • Risks: sensitive prompt/data capture, high volume/cost, purpose labels that become decorative, cross-system correlation risk, retention conflict.
  • Build / buy / partner: Hybrid. Buy observability transport/storage; build the enterprise event, purpose, and outcome semantics.
  • Minimum reversible experiment: instrument one sandbox workflow for 30 days with redacted payloads and compare missing causal links against current logs.
  • Success metrics / stop conditions: ≥95% trace completeness, <5% latency overhead, demonstrable least-privilege access, successful deletion test. Stop if sensitive payload minimization or access isolation fails.
  • Roadmap trigger / internal context required: two independent use cases derive measurable audit or evaluation value. Need data classes, retention policy, SIEM/APM landscape, and audit consumers.

Prepare Foundations — Model/tool metadata quarantine and data-flow policy

  • Research signal and supporting paper: Log-To-Leak exploits natural-language tool descriptions; metadata detectors either miss attacks or reject most benign descriptions. Evidence label: Paper Fact and Enterprise Inference.
  • Problem / opportunity: documentation and executable intent are mixed in the same model context.
  • Future capability: sign and version tool manifests; separate untrusted descriptions from host-enforced schemas; statically and dynamically restrict source-to-destination field flows; sandbox newly added servers.
  • Placement / value: model gateway, orchestration, security/governance, developer platform; risk reduction, supply-chain control, change management, developer speed through safer onboarding.
  • Maturity / confidence / horizon / effort: control patterns are familiar, but MCP-specific interoperability is evolving. Confidence: High. Horizon: 3–6 months. Effort: L.
  • Dependencies: registry/PKI, software supply-chain process, policy-as-code, manifest diffing, server provenance, DLP semantics, exception workflow.
  • Risks: unsigned legacy tools, semantic data flow invisible to static rules, centralized bottleneck, stale manifests, vendor divergence.
  • Build / buy / partner: Partner for protocol/runtime integration; build signing policy and data-flow rules.
  • Minimum reversible experiment: mirror tool manifests into a quarantine registry and generate allow/deny recommendations without enforcing them.
  • Success metrics / stop conditions: 100% inventory coverage in pilot, reproducible diffs, <5% high-severity false alarms after adjudication. Stop if tool identity/version cannot be established.
  • Roadmap trigger / internal context required: successful shadow-mode policy on representative multi-tool workflows and vendor commitment to enforcement hooks. Need procurement/onboarding process and tool ownership.

Prepare Foundations — Defect audit and reviewer-effort telemetry

  • Research signal and supporting paper: all 16 reviewer escapes in the over-trust experiment follow absence of a revealing lookup; the paper estimates roughly 3/q clean audits to certify a sufficiently low error rate at 95% confidence. Evidence label: Paper Fact for experiment/formula; Enterprise Inference for service.
  • Problem / opportunity: organizations observe final acceptance but not how much checking occurred or what audits miss.
  • Future capability: sample outputs by task/model/version, record evidence inspections and corrections, estimate audit sensitivity, certify task-specific error ceilings, and maintain a review-effort frontier.
  • Placement / value: evaluation, observability, human-review layer; quality, reliability, risk reduction, reviewer capacity planning.
  • Maturity / confidence / horizon / effort: audit sampling is mature; task-specific defect discovery and effort instrumentation need validation. Confidence: Moderate. Horizon: 3–6 months. Effort: M.
  • Dependencies: gold or adjudicated audits, task strata, reviewer tooling, blind sampling, disagreement protocol, calibrated severity weights.
  • Risks: checklist theater, audit fatigue, correlated misses, reviewer gaming, sampling bias, inappropriate autonomy claims from clean audits.
  • Build / buy / partner: Build the audit design and labels; buy annotation workflow tooling.
  • Minimum reversible experiment: dual-blind audit of 200 archived outputs, with inspection actions instrumented but no production gate.
  • Success metrics / stop conditions: inter-reviewer agreement, audit sensitivity estimate, stable defect taxonomy, useful confidence bounds. Stop if reviewers cannot agree on material defects or samples are unrepresentative.
  • Roadmap trigger / internal context required: audit results predict downstream rework/incident outcomes and capacity is sustainable. Need review workforce, error costs, autonomy threshold, and regulated QA obligations.

Prepare Foundations — Policy-change calibration registry

  • Research signal and supporting paper: Calibrated for Which Policy? shows ordinary 90% coverage falling to 55.0% under a controlled policy shift while learned weighting restores 90.9%. Evidence label: Paper Fact and Enterprise Inference.
  • Problem / opportunity: routing, planner, or eligibility-policy changes silently invalidate uncertainty thresholds.
  • Future capability: version every decision policy with the model; log pre-outcome covariates and action propensities; on change, run support, density-ratio, ESS, coverage, and mechanism-invariance checks before reusing thresholds.
  • Placement / value: model gateway, evaluation, model/inference, MLOps platform; reliability, safety, change control, faster qualification of model reuse.
  • Maturity / confidence / horizon / effort: strong theory and controlled physical result; limited open-world evidence. Confidence: Moderate. Horizon: 6–12 months. Effort: L–XL.
  • Dependencies: policy IDs, propensity/decision logs, calibration datasets, overlap diagnostics, conformal implementation, fallback policy, statistical ownership.
  • Risks: unobserved confounding, support failure, mechanism drift, unstable weights, misleading marginal coverage, large prediction sets, multistep compounding.
  • Build / buy / partner: Build the registry/qualification logic; partner with statistical experts for high-consequence use.
  • Minimum reversible experiment: replay one historical routing-policy change with frozen outcomes and compare source, oracle-if-known, and learned-weight coverage.
  • Success metrics / stop conditions: nominal coverage within tolerance, adequate ESS, stable ratio estimates, explicit abstention on support failure. Stop if conditional dynamics or overlap cannot be defended.
  • Roadmap trigger / internal context required: success on multiple naturally occurring policy changes with predeclared thresholds. Need high-consequence model use case, policy logs, coverage target, and fallback workflow.

Monitor for Maturity — Relation-aware contextual PII masking

  • Research signal and supporting paper: ReCoPII human annotators agree with 88.42% of sampled KEEP labels versus 26.27% for SPY-style data; integrated systems meeting budget report ≤5.04% over-mask and ≤4.18% leakage on human-verified records. Evidence label: Paper Fact and Enterprise Inference.
  • Problem / opportunity: type-only masking destroys useful organizational contact or reference information while contextual release can expose personal data.
  • Future capability: a conservative detector proposes candidates; a relation/context layer emits traceable MASK/KEEP decisions; low-confidence or high-risk cases remain masked or go to human review.
  • Placement / value: data/knowledge infrastructure, security/governance, human-review layer; data utility, privacy risk reduction, customer experience, analyst productivity.
  • Maturity / confidence / horizon / effort: careful single-language synthetic-data study, no verified public implementation. Confidence: Moderate. Horizon: 12+ months. Effort: XL.
  • Dependencies: local privacy ontology, adjudicated multilingual corpus, DPO/legal policy, privacy threat model, encrypted evaluation, human escalation, leak-budget definition.
  • Risks: false release, regulatory inconsistency, synthetic artifacts, cultural/language transfer, relation extraction errors, memorization, explainability that overstates certainty.
  • Build / buy / partner: Partner for domain/language expertise; build policy labels; buy only after independent validation.
  • Minimum reversible experiment: offline benchmark on de-identified or synthetic enterprise-shaped documents; never unmask production data.
  • Success metrics / stop conditions: zero severe releases in adjudicated high-risk subset, prespecified leakage bound, material over-mask reduction, subgroup/language stability. Stop on any unsupported release pathway or unavailable artifact/license.
  • Roadmap trigger / internal context required: independent reproduction, public artifact terms, and local adjudication across representative domains. Need jurisdictions, document languages, data-subject roles, and acceptable leakage loss.

Monitor for Maturity — Deterministic-first inference router

  • Research signal and supporting paper: KORA avoids 80/100 simulated calls with zero deterministic-subset mismatches in a workload constructed with 80 deterministic tasks. Evidence label: Paper Fact and Uncertainty.
  • Problem / opportunity: model-first systems spend cost and introduce variability on rule-, lookup-, schema-, or transformation-bound work.
  • Future capability: classify requests into validated deterministic routes, model-needed routes, or abstention; execute known logic first and escalate on missing route or validation failure.
  • Placement / value: orchestration, model gateway, developer platform; cost, latency, reliability, policy enforcement, observability.
  • Maturity / confidence / horizon / effort: reproducible artifact but synthetic, circularly deterministic-heavy evidence. Confidence: Low–Moderate. Horizon: 6–12 months. Effort: M–L.
  • Dependencies: traffic taxonomy, maintained rules/services, validators, escalation, drift monitoring, route ownership, golden tests.
  • Risks: routing false negatives, stale policy code, duplicated logic, maintenance cost exceeding model savings, latency from extra layer, hidden ambiguity.
  • Build / buy / partner: Build a thin experiment around existing rules; defer platform purchase.
  • Minimum reversible experiment: shadow-route 10,000 historical requests; compare against actual outcomes without serving deterministic responses.
  • Success metrics / stop conditions: ≥20% safely deterministic share, near-zero critical mismatches, net latency/cost benefit after maintenance. Stop if route errors concentrate in high-consequence groups or savings vanish under real traffic.
  • Roadmap trigger / internal context required: representative shadow evidence and stable route ownership for two quarters. Need request distribution, current costs/latency, rules of record, and error severity.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications — Independent Analysis

Over 12–24 months, the highest-leverage change is a separable AI control plane between applications/agents and models/tools. It does not assume a particular vendor or current stack. Its shared foundations are:

  1. Identity and authorization: workload, user, agent, model, tool, policy, and reviewer identities; least-privilege credentials; per-call authorization; signed tool manifests.
  2. Purpose-bound orchestration: declared task graph, allowed dependencies and fields, post-task side-effect rules, user/policy confirmation, deterministic and model routes.
  3. Evidence and outcome lineage: request/evidence/action/output/review/repair/outcome links, model and prompt versions, policy IDs, data provenance, and defect ledgers.
  4. Evaluation and experiment infrastructure: randomized/shadow experiments, prespecified estimands, audit sampling, coverage tests, subgroup analysis, counterfactual replay, stop conditions.
  5. Policy-relative context services: current and prior routing/planner policies, propensities, support and shift diagnostics, calibration sets, abstention/fallback behavior.
  6. Observability and cost telemetry: tool-call traces, cross-server flows, reviewer effort, inference tokens/latency, deterministic-route outcomes, delayed rework and incident cost.
  7. Human-review controls: risk-tiered queues, blind audits, disagreement/adjudication, hardware or policy confirmation for high-consequence actions, and fatigue monitoring.
  8. Privacy and governance: payload minimization, retention/deletion, tenant isolation, role-based access, artifact/license inventory, incident response, and controlled sandboxing.

The sequencing matters. Instrumentation and identity precede automated enforcement; shadow policies precede blocking; offline calibration precedes policy reuse; local audits precede autonomy; and every prototype remains isolated, reversible, measurable, and non-destructive. Internal architecture, vendor contracts, model/data platforms, security posture, and maturity have not been verified, so no target-state implementation is implied.

Knowledge reinforcement

Five questions leaders should now ask

  1. When we report “time saved,” where does our clock stop, and who pays for errors found later?
  2. Can an agent make any call after the user-visible task is complete, or move data between servers without separate authorization?
  3. Which model thresholds were calibrated under a policy different from the one currently choosing inputs?
  4. Do our traces connect model/tool actions to human review, repair, incidents, and cost—or merely record prompts and responses?
  5. Which proposed AI capability has a reversible experiment, measurable success threshold, and explicit stop condition before any roadmap commitment?

Compact recall

  • Ledger, not stopwatch: speed is a workflow property; pair it with escaped defects.
  • Action, not metadata: classify text if useful, but enforce permission at the call boundary.
  • Policy-relative calibration: a fixed model can become miscalibrated when a controller changes its inputs.
  • Artifact humility: code helps reproduction; it does not establish production economics or safety.
  • Foundations first: identity, lineage, evaluation, observability, and human review unlock multiple future enhancements.
