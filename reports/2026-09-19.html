Scope, window, and evidence policy

Research window. Friday, September 18, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT through Saturday, September 19, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT (24 hours; America/Chicago). Discovery covered the September 18 arXiv announcement cycle—215 cs.AI records—plus current cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.SE, cs.IR, cs.HC, cs.DC, cs.PF and stat.ML listings; primary paper pages and full texts; and linked repositories or preregistrations. The normal window was sufficient; no expansion was used.

Anti-duplication. Exact identifiers, titles, claims and proposed controls were checked against the 40-report archive through September 18. None of today's five selected papers appeared previously. Recent briefs already covered memory upgrade rehearsal, evidence identity, adaptive RAG, tail-aware scheduling, benchmark contracts, transaction-safe tool use, trajectory safety and closed-world tool resolution. Today's increment is different: the same AI feature changes value and risk with the model, resource budget, task stage and interaction history. Enterprise qualification therefore needs conditional policies, not a universal “best” harness, retriever or personalization mode.

Evidence policy. Paper Fact means directly reported by a paper or verified first-party artifact. Independent Analysis is this briefing's calculation, comparison or methodological critique. Enterprise Inference is a bounded translation into a future option. Uncertainty marks missing causal, external-validity, artifact, economic, security, privacy, governance, operational or adoption evidence. The coding-harness and personalization papers are preprints. RAFT is accepted to the EMNLP 2026 Industry Track and links a public Microsoft repository. The financial-document and resource-dynamics papers are preprints. Benchmark performance is not deployment readiness. Every proposed experiment is isolated, reversible, measurable and non-destructive.

Executive signal

AI architecture choices are conditional policies. Qualify them against the model, budget, task stage and human relationship they actually govern.

Three deep papers reach this conclusion from different directions:

  1. Harness design varies planning, action space and context management across four models, two benchmarks and four context budgets. Context management is most valuable when budgets are tight; planning scaffolds a weak model but becomes a cost reducer for stronger ones; structured tools help bash-weak models while bash-only can help capable ones.
  2. RAFT treats troubleshooting as an evolving case rather than a static query. Timeline-entry retrieval improves Case Hit over vanilla RAG by 12.19–16.79 percentage points on the synthetic benchmark and shows similar directional gains on 30 real Apache Jira duplicate groups.
  3. Longitudinal personalization finds that five days of repeated AI advice, not personalization alone, drives many changes in perceived competence, usefulness and closeness. Memory- and survey-based personalization produce different disclosure and regret patterns; implementation form matters.

The Watchlist adds two compatible signals. A decomposed perception/layout/validation score with a conformal gate turns weak VLM confidence into high-coverage selective document extraction, but relies on historical labels and familiar layouts. Agent-container experiments show large task-dependent resource effects, but the infrastructure evidence is narrow and lacks a mature production artifact.

Decision posture. Prototype a model-and-budget-aware harness qualification matrix, stateful case-trajectory retrieval in a bounded support corpus, and a longitudinal personalization audit with inspect/redact controls. Prepare context-compaction telemetry, retrieval coverage metrics and governed memory provenance. Monitor document-confidence gating and resource-aware scheduling until independent reproduction, drift tests and internal economics are available. Deprioritize any universal harness profile or personalization default.

Deep paper 1 — Harness components are policies, not universally beneficial features

Identity and status. An Empirical Study of Harness Design for Coding Agents, Run-Ze Fan, Zihao Zhang, Simin Ma, Yebowen Hu, Shouju Wang, Kaiqiang Song, Fei Liu, Hamed Zamani and Xiaoyang Wang; submitted September 17, announced September 18, 2026; arXiv:2609.20804v1; cs.AI/cs.CL/cs.LG/cs.SE; 43-page preprint under arXiv's non-exclusive distribution license. No repository is linked from the paper page. (paper, full text)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

The authors build a fixed ReAct-style execution loop and vary three components: persistent planning, action space, and context management. Five context tiers compose elision, recoverable external storage and summarization. Four context budgets—32k, 64k, 96k and 128k—are evaluated on SWE-Bench Verified (500 issues) and Terminal-Bench 2.1 (89 tasks) with Nemotron-3 30B/120B/550B and Mistral-Medium-3.5-128B. Planning and action-space ablations use the 128k/T4 baseline. The result is 176 matched settings. Paired success comparisons use exact two-sided McNemar tests with Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery control at 0.05.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Prior coding-agent studies usually compare complete systems, confounding model, planning, tools and memory. This paper's useful advance is controlled component-level comparison across both a model-capability axis and an explicit context-budget sweep. The paired task outcomes and multiple-testing correction are stronger than unpaired leaderboard deltas. Trajectory analysis explains whether interventions change stopping points, action granularity or merely trajectory length.

The design is still not a full factorial experiment. Planning and action-space effects are tested only at T4/128k, so interactions with other context tiers and budgets remain unknown. The four models are concentrated in two families and served locally under one implementation. A “structured tools versus bash” contrast changes typed schemas, instructions, state tracking, read-before-write checks and automatic diagnostics together; attributing the effect to tool count would be wrong. Temperature zero does not make hosted or GPU inference perfectly deterministic, yet no seed/repeat variance is reported. Cost uses token prices, not wall-clock labor, hardware, sandbox or incident cost. The paper publishes prompts and detailed tables but no linked repository, container digest or immutable trajectories.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The benchmarks, models, prompts, thresholds and statistical tests are documented. Reproduction is feasible in principle but expensive: 176 settings over 589 tasks imply a large run volume, and the absent artifact raises implementation ambiguity. Enterprise readiness: high for a small internal factorial harness evaluation; medium for adaptive runtime selection after repeated internal evidence; low for copying T4 or bash-only as a default. Research Utility Score: 93/100 (methodological rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 15/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 15/15).

Deep paper 2 — Troubleshooting retrieval should follow the case state

Identity and status. RAFT: A Stateful Retrieval-Augmented Framework for Troubleshooting Agents, Mingxuan Zhang, Xiaowen Wang, Anupma Sharan, Zhengyi Chen, Chenyu Diana Zhang, Shanshan Yang and Chittibabu Pacharu; Microsoft; submitted September 17, announced September 18, 2026; arXiv:2609.20754v1; accepted to EMNLP 2026 Industry Track. The paper links an open Microsoft repository containing the implementation, benchmark and Apache Jira evaluation set. (paper, full text, artifact)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

RAFT converts each historical case into a directed chain of timeline entries, retrieves at the entry level against the evolving active case, and returns the parent trajectory anchored at the matched state. A configurable case-level graph is optional. The main synthetic corpus contains 826 Windows Server support cases across seven categories, averaging 10.4 messages and 2,767 tokens. One case per root-cause group is held out. Queries are built at 0%, 30% and 60% of the investigation. All systems share text-embedding-3-large, a GPT-5.2 indexing model, GPT-5.4 evaluation, a 6,000-token return budget and—except chunk-returning Fast-GraphRAG—a five-case cap.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Traditional RAG chunks documents; GraphRAG adds entities and relations; case-based support systems often retrieve entire tickets. RAFT's advance is to represent a case as stateful evidence and return coherent trajectories anchored at the matching investigation stage. Direct retrieval-layer evaluation isolates this mechanism from downstream agent variance. Equal embedding/evaluator models, fixed budgets, grouped holdouts and clustered bootstrap intervals make the comparison unusually interpretable.

The central benchmark is synthetic and moderate in scale; production tickets can be much longer, noisier, multilingual and access-controlled. Synthetic cases share a generation process and a common source wiki, which may make timeline structure easier to exploit. GPT-5.4 judges root-cause and resolution-claim entailment; no reported human calibration establishes judge error at each stage. The Jira transfer study has only 30 groups, no interval and duplicate labels rather than root-cause/resolution outcomes. RAFT does not measure final diagnosis accuracy, time to resolution, engineer productivity, privacy leakage, access-control errors or stale-case harm. Default GraphRAG settings may not be competitive tuning.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The public artifact is a meaningful advantage: implementation, benchmark and Jira evaluation set are linked. Exact reproduction still depends on future model/API versions and costs. Enterprise readiness: high for offline retrieval evaluation on redacted cases; medium for support-engineer shadow suggestions with access controls; low for autonomous diagnosis or remediation. Research Utility Score: 92/100 (methodological rigor 23/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 19/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 11/15).

Deep paper 3 — Repetition and personalization are different interventions

Identity and status. Tailored to you: longitudinal effects of personalising language models, Canfer Akbulut, Justine Breuch, Arianna Manzini, Lujain Ibrahim, Matija Franklin, Roma Patel, Iason Gabriel, Kristian Lum and Laura Weidinger; Google DeepMind; submitted September 17, announced September 18, 2026; arXiv:2609.20077v1; cs.AI; CC BY 4.0 preprint. The paper links an AsPredicted preregistration and reproduces prompts and measures, but does not link a public conversation dataset or analysis repository. (paper, full text, preregistration)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

The preregistered randomized trial assigns 992 analytic participants to a no-personalization control, memory-based personalization using cumulative conversation summaries, or survey-based personalization using an intake persona. Each participant completes five 15–20 minute relationship-advice sessions: 4,960 conversations total. Mixed-effects models analyze daily measures; baseline-adjusted models analyze pre/post outcomes. Preregistered outcomes receive Benjamini–Hochberg correction, and pairwise estimated marginal means use Holm–Bonferroni correction. Power planning targeted 380 participants per arm and 90% power for a smallest effect of d=0.30.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Most personalization evaluations are single-session preference or task-accuracy studies. This paper adds longitudinal randomized exposure and separates memory-based from survey-based personalization. The large sample, preregistration, attention checks, attrition test, repeated measures and corrected confirmatory analyses are strengths. It reports nulls rather than forcing a universal harm narrative.

The trial has no no-AI control, so session effects combine repeated model exposure, repeated measurement, topic sequence and study demand. Participants who completed all five sessions form the analytic sample; attrition was balanced by condition but completer selection may limit population inference. The control and personalized system prompts differ, creating a first-session confound acknowledged by the authors. Relationship advice is a sensitive but narrow domain. All personalization is prompt-context injection; fine-tuning, inferred attributes, ambient enterprise data, voice and multimodal use may behave differently. Several decision-relevant results are exploratory or marginal after correction. Conversation data are sensitive and not publicly linked, limiting independent analysis.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Prompts, questionnaires, design and preregistration are available, but exact data and analysis code were not verified publicly. Enterprise readiness: high for adding longitudinal disclosure/reliance measures and inspect/delete controls; medium for consented internal studies with synthetic data; low for product-wide policy thresholds or high-stakes personalization. Watchlist

Multi-signal confidence can support selective document automation—if the calibration population holds

Perception, Layout, and Validation: Calibrated Confidence for Reliable Straight-Through Processing of Financial Documents, Yichao Jin et al.; OCBC, Singapore; arXiv:2609.20110v1; submitted September 17, 2026; CC BY 4.0 preprint. (paper, full text)

Paper Facts. Two VLM families are evaluated on three public datasets. Native verbalized confidence and five-sample self-consistency yield AUROC 0.54–0.74 for extraction correctness. A LightGBM fusion of perception, retrieval-conditioned layout and validation features reaches 0.90–0.99; cross-model agreement adds a further tier. At a target error of at most 10%, native signals auto-approve 0.1–7.0% of fields, while the proposed pipeline auto-approves 49–72%. A Learn-Then-Test gate selects the acceptance threshold on held-out calibration data.

Why Watchlist. The decomposition, public datasets, two VLM families, reason codes and selective-risk framing are decision-useful. But the reported 10% accepted-field error target may be too permissive for financial identifiers or amounts; guarantees rely on exchangeability and representative calibration; templates are repeatedly sampled; historical correctness labels are required; no deployment drift, privacy, latency or review-cost study is reported. Watch trigger: promote after internal time-split and counterparty-disjoint evaluation with field-specific risk budgets, monitored drift and audited calibration sustains useful coverage without critical-field false acceptance. Research Utility Score: 88/100.

Resource-aware scheduling is promising, but today's evidence is too narrow for a platform commitment

Not All AI Agents Are Equal: Characterizing Resource and Performance Dynamics, Wonmi Choi, Minuk Park, Zhixiong Niu, Yongqiang Xiong, Chuck Yoo and Gyeongsik Yang; Korea University and Microsoft Research Asia; arXiv:2609.19947v1; submitted September 17, 2026; preprint. (paper, full text)

Paper Facts. The study profiles retrieval-augmented QA, web search and software coding agents using remote LLM APIs plus local tool containers. It reports task-dependent CPU, disk, memory and concurrency bottlenecks; faster remote responses or more CPU do not always reduce end-to-end latency. Prototype CPU-aware tool admission improves a CPU-sensitive task by about 5.4×, and task-aware allocation reduces average latency across multiple tasks by about 32% versus native agents.

Why Watchlist. The paper usefully treats agent execution as alternating remote waits and bursty local tools. Yet three task families, one experimental stack and prototype schedulers do not establish broad production economics; no public artifact, long-running availability test, security isolation analysis or cost-to-SLO comparison is linked. Watch trigger: promote after reproduction on internal traces across at least three agent classes shows tail-latency and cost gains under realistic concurrency without cross-tenant interference or safety regression. Research Utility Score: 84/100.

Cross-paper synthesis

Independent Analysis — qualification must be conditional and longitudinal

The papers converge on a single operating principle: an AI component does not have a context-free value. Its effect is a function of the state around it.

  1. Harness state: planning, tools and compaction interact with model capability, task family and context budget.
  2. Knowledge state: retrieval must match the active investigation stage, not merely the initial ticket or the whole historical case.
  3. Human state: personalization changes as familiarity, disclosure and advice-seeking accumulate over repeated sessions.
  4. Document state: selective automation depends on template familiarity, field type, calibration population and risk budget.
  5. Infrastructure state: an agent alternates between remote waiting and bursty local execution; static CPU allocation ignores that phase structure.

The enterprise implication is not “make everything adaptive.” Unbounded adaptation is itself a risk. The safer pattern is measured conditional selection: define a small set of approved policies; observe the variables that make them applicable; log why one policy was chosen; preserve a stable fallback; and requalify on model, data, prompt, tool, workflow or user-population change.

  • Enterprise problem or opportunity: One-shot UX evaluation misses cumulative disclosure, familiarity, reliance, regret and displacement of human review or advice channels.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: Add consented longitudinal evaluation cohorts, explicit data-source labels, non-personalized controls, disclosure/reliance measures, memory inspection/redaction and human-escalation tracking before enabling persistent personalization.
  • Likely architectural placement: applications, context/memory service, evaluation, security/governance and human-review layer.
  • Expected business value: customer experience, trust, adoption quality, risk reduction and strategic differentiation.
  • Technical value: Separates exposure effects from personalization mechanisms and creates versioned behavior evidence by data source.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: Large preregistered trial but one advice domain, model and five-day horizon. Ready for consented internal study, not broad deployment policy. Confidence: Moderate. Horizon: Now. Effort: M.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: Consent UX, data minimization, privacy review, memory provenance, delete/export controls, cohort analytics, protected demographic slices and human-support metrics.
  • Key risks: Collection itself increases privacy exposure; self-report bias; emotional influence; worker monitoring concerns; disparate effects; inferred sensitive traits; low opt-out salience.
  • Build / Buy / Partner: Build governance and measurement; partner with privacy, legal, human-factors and domain experts. Vendor memory features cannot supply internal consent policy.
  • Minimum reversible validation experiment: Two-week synthetic-profile study with volunteers; compare no memory, conversation-summary memory and declared profile context; prohibit consequential actions.
  • Success metrics and stop conditions: Success: user-rated benefit without material increase in sensitive disclosure, regret, mistaken reliance or avoidance of human channels; ≥95% success inspecting/deleting memory. Stop on adverse-event signal, unclear consent, sensitive-trait inference or subgroup harm.
  • Roadmap trigger: Two independent cohorts and two low-risk domains show stable benefit, negligible harm deltas and effective recourse under privacy review.

Prepare Foundations

4. Context-compaction telemetry and overflow control — 88.5

  • Category: Memory & Context / MLOps & Platform / Evaluation & Observability.
  • Research signal and supporting papers: Managed tiers eliminate observed window-overflow terminations; staged elision plus summarization controls peak context, while recall adds little. Evidence label: Paper Fact and Enterprise Inference.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity: Context failure is often invisible until a run truncates, loops or silently loses evidence.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: Emit per-turn budget, elision, summary, evidence-loss and overflow events; preserve recent turns and immutable provenance pointers; qualify compaction profiles by task/model.
  • Placement: orchestration, context/memory, observability and model gateway. Business value: reliability, cost, speed and quality. Technical value: makes context a controlled resource with auditable loss.
  • Maturity/readiness: Strong benchmark signal; transfer uncertain. Confidence: High. Horizon: 3–6 months. Effort: M.
  • Dependencies: Token accounting, message lineage, summary versioning, retrieval pointers, replay corpus and failure taxonomy.
  • Risks: Summaries distort instructions/evidence; telemetry contains sensitive data; thresholds overfit; recall remains unused.
  • Build/Buy/Partner: Build thin telemetry and policy; buy tracing storage if controls and retention are adequate.
  • Minimum experiment: Replay long trajectories with deterministic elision, summary-only and staged policies; manually audit lost decision-critical facts.
  • Success/stop: Zero overflow, <1% critical-fact loss and ≥15% token reduction. Stop if fact loss exceeds 2% or privacy review rejects trace retention.
  • Roadmap trigger: Stable results across two models and three long-horizon workflows.

5. Retrieval evidence-coverage evaluation — 87.6

  • Category: Evaluation & Observability / RAG & Knowledge.
  • Research signal: RAFT separates Case Hit from root-cause and resolution-step coverage; large hit gains coexist with smaller coverage gains. Evidence label: Paper Fact.
  • Problem/opportunity: Top-k retrieval success can mask incomplete actionable evidence.
  • Capability: Atomize gold explanations/procedures, score whether retrieved context supports each atom, calibrate model judges against humans, and report coverage by case stage and access slice.
  • Placement: evaluation, retrieval/knowledge and observability. Business value: quality, reliability and risk reduction. Technical value: measures evidence sufficiency rather than mere similarity.
  • Maturity/readiness: Metrics are clear; judge validity is unproven. Confidence: Moderate. Horizon: 3–6 months. Effort: M.
  • Dependencies: Curated gold cases, atomization guidelines, human adjudication, judge registry, ACL-aware evaluation.
  • Risks: Expensive labels, judge circularity, ambiguous procedures, stale gold, gaming coverage.
  • Build/Buy/Partner: Build domain-specific gold/evaluation; buy generic annotation workflow only.
  • Minimum experiment: 100 held-out cases, two retrieval systems, double human annotation, compare judge precision/recall and stage-wise coverage.
  • Success/stop: Judge F1 ≥0.85 versus adjudication; coverage predicts expert preference/resolution. Stop if inter-annotator agreement is too low or coverage fails to correlate with outcomes.
  • Roadmap trigger: Metric remains stable across two domains and a time-based split.

6. Inspectable and redactable personalization memory — 86.2

  • Category: Memory & Context / Safety & Governance / Human-AI Workflow.
  • Research signal: Memory personalization changes observable disclosure while users may not report deeper disclosure; authors call for paths to surface and redact data. Evidence label: Paper Fact and Enterprise Inference.
  • Problem/opportunity: Users cannot give durable consent when they cannot see what the system remembers, why, or where it is used.
  • Capability: Store memory atoms with source, purpose, sensitivity, retention, owner and last-use metadata; provide inspect, correct, redact, export and disable controls; require policy checks before use.
  • Placement: context/memory, data platform, applications and security/governance. Business value: trust, compliance, risk reduction and customer experience. Technical value: creates a governed memory contract and deletion proof.
  • Maturity/readiness: Governance need is strong; causal product evidence is limited. Confidence: Moderate. Horizon: 3–6 months. Effort: L.
  • Dependencies: Identity, consent ledger, data classification, deletion propagation, audit log and UI research.
  • Risks: Incomplete deletion, inferred traits, dark-pattern consent, cross-tenant leaks, user confusion, degraded personalization after edits.
  • Build/Buy/Partner: Build the policy/data model; partner with identity/privacy teams; buy storage only with verifiable deletion.
  • Minimum experiment: Synthetic accounts exercise create/view/edit/delete/export across downstream caches; measure propagation and comprehension.
  • Success/stop: 100% deletion propagation in test graph, ≥90% user comprehension, no undisclosed source. Stop on orphaned replicas or unclear ownership.
  • Roadmap trigger: Independent privacy test verifies lifecycle controls and a consented pilot shows useful personalization after edits.

Monitor for Maturity

7. Multi-signal selective document gate — 84.1

  • Category: Multimodal / Evaluation & Observability / Human-AI Workflow.
  • Research signal: P/L/V fusion raises AUROC to 0.90–0.99 and accepted coverage to 49–72% at a reported ≤10% error target. Evidence label: Paper Fact.
  • Problem/opportunity: Native VLM confidence is poorly calibrated for straight-through processing.
  • Capability: Fuse image quality, OCR agreement, layout familiarity, type/checksum rules and cross-model agreement; apply field-specific selective-risk gates and route uncertain fields to review.
  • Placement: applications, evaluation, human-review and model gateway. Value: productivity, quality and cost. Technical value: decomposed reason codes plus bounded selective acceptance.
  • Maturity/readiness: Strong public-dataset results; weak drift and critical-field evidence. Confidence: Moderate. Horizon: 6–12 months. Effort: L.
  • Dependencies: Historical labeled documents, template index, field loss taxonomy, calibration/drift service and review queue.
  • Risks: Exchangeability failure, template drift, sensitive-document leakage, 10% target misuse, correlated model errors.
  • Build/Buy/Partner: Partner/buy extraction components; build internal risk gate because thresholds and losses are domain-specific.
  • Minimum experiment: Time- and counterparty-disjoint shadow evaluation with zero automation; stratify by field criticality.
  • Success/stop: No critical-field false accept, useful coverage at internal loss budget, stable calibration. Stop if drift breaks bounds or review load rises.
  • Roadmap trigger: Independent audit and two time windows sustain calibrated coverage under production-like mix.

8. Phase-aware agent-container scheduler — 79.0

  • Category: MLOps & Platform / Infrastructure.
  • Research signal: Task-aware prototypes report ~5.4× improvement on a CPU-sensitive task and 32% average latency reduction. Evidence label: Paper Fact; production transfer is Uncertainty.
  • Problem/opportunity: Static quotas waste CPU during remote waits yet under-provision bursty tool phases.
  • Capability: Classify tool phases, predict CPU sensitivity, admit bursts within tenant budgets, and preserve hard isolation/fairness.
  • Placement: infrastructure, orchestration and observability. Value: cost, speed and reliability. Technical value: aligns resources with agent phase behavior.
  • Maturity/readiness: Narrow research prototype; insufficient availability and security evidence. Confidence: Low. Horizon: 12+ months. Effort: XL.
  • Dependencies: Container telemetry, workload classifier, scheduler hooks, tenancy model, quotas and tail-SLO simulator.
  • Risks: Starvation, noisy neighbors, timing side channels, prediction error, scheduler instability and operational complexity.
  • Build/Buy/Partner: Monitor/partner with infrastructure vendors; do not build a bespoke scheduler before trace evidence.
  • Minimum experiment: Offline trace replay plus a non-production cluster with synthetic contention and fixed safety quotas.
  • Success/stop: ≥15% p95 latency or cost improvement without fairness/isolation regression. Stop on starvation, unstable oscillation or weak savings.
  • Roadmap trigger: Gains reproduce across three internal agent classes at realistic concurrency for four weeks.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

Independent Analysis

Over 12–24 months, the recommended options imply a shift from a monolithic agent stack to a qualified-policy architecture:

  • A model and harness registry stores approved combinations, evaluation evidence, budgets, risk classes and expiry dates.
  • An orchestration policy selector chooses among a small approved set using task stage, model version, context pressure and risk—not free-form self-modification.
  • A context service exposes token budgets, provenance-preserving compaction and memory lifecycle controls.
  • A stateful knowledge layer represents cases as timelines with ACLs, source identity and stage-aware retrieval.
  • An evaluation plane measures paired success, overflow, cost, evidence coverage, selective risk and longitudinal human outcomes.
  • An observability plane links policy choice to model/tool/data versions, cost, tail latency and fallback behavior.
  • A human-review layer receives uncertain document fields, unresolved support cases and personalization concerns with reason codes and reversible controls.

Shared enabling foundations unlock multiple backlog items: identity and authorization, data provenance, a model gateway, experiment infrastructure, cost telemetry, policy enforcement, evaluation registries, privacy-preserving trace stores and human escalation. Before roadmap commitment, internal context is required: representative workflow volumes, failure severity, support-case sensitivity, retention rules, model procurement, existing observability, user roles, regional privacy obligations, latency targets and change-management capacity.

Implementation priorities

  1. Now — instrument and replay. Build the harness qualification matrix, context-overflow telemetry and a 500-case RAFT-like offline corpus. No live automation.
  2. Next — validate people and evidence. Run a consented longitudinal personalization study and calibrate retrieval coverage against humans.
  3. Then — introduce bounded pilots. Expose stateful retrieval to support engineers in shadow/read-only mode; keep personalization synthetic or low risk; retain stable fallbacks.
  4. Later — selective automation. Consider document auto-approval or adaptive infrastructure only after time-split drift evidence, field-specific loss budgets, isolation tests and operational ownership exist.

Knowledge reinforcement

Five things to remember

  1. Context management mostly prevents resource exhaustion; that is different from improving reasoning.
  2. Planning can be an accuracy scaffold for weak models and an efficiency aid for strong ones.
  3. A retrieved matching case is not the same as complete root-cause and resolution evidence.
  4. Repeated exposure can change user perceptions even when personalization has no incremental effect.
  5. Personalization implementation matters: conversational memory and pre-collected profiles should not share one risk label.

Decision checks

  • What model, task, budget, user population and time horizon qualified this policy?
  • Which evidence metric measures the actual objective rather than a convenient proxy?
  • What is the stable fallback when state classification is uncertain?
  • Can users and operators inspect, correct and delete the state that conditions behavior?
  • Which change—model, prompt, data, tool, schema, population or regulation—forces requalification?
