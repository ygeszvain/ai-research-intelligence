Scope, window, and evidence policy

Research window. The exact window was Friday, September 25, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT through Saturday, September 26, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT (America/Chicago; 09:00 UTC to 09:00 UTC). It contained no eligible arXiv announcement batch. The window therefore expanded to the most recent complete 72 hours, beginning Wednesday, September 23 at 4:00 AM CDT, and screened 646 records across cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.CR, cs.IR, cs.SE, cs.MA, cs.HC, cs.CY, cs.DC and stat.ML, plus linked first-party pages and artifacts. Three previously unreviewed papers cleared deep analysis; two remain Watchlist-only.

Prestigious-source discovery. Nature, Science, ACL Anthology and OpenReview were also checked for newly indexed work inside the exact window. No independently verified item with a stronger decision-usefulness profile displaced the selected primary papers. This is not a venue ranking: the selected papers are recent preprints, and their claims are bounded accordingly.

Anti-duplication. Titles and identifiers were checked against the 47-report archive through September 25 and the local source corpus. None of today's five papers appeared previously. Yesterday established that evidence must remain outside an agent's rewrite boundary. Today's incremental result concerns the decision state itself: an evaluation needs dated, replayable provenance; an enterprise answer needs the hidden rule that selects authoritative evidence; and a RAG controller needs the effect of an action, not confidence in the current answer.

Evidence policy. Paper Fact is directly reported or verified in a first-party artifact. Independent Analysis is this briefing's calculation, comparison or critique. Enterprise Inference translates evidence into a bounded future option. Uncertainty marks missing causal, external-validity, artifact, licensing, economic, privacy, security, governance, operational or adoption evidence. Provider-scale experiments were not rerun. No benchmark score authorizes production. All proposed experiments are isolated, reversible, measurable and non-destructive.

Executive signal

Reliable enterprise AI must preserve the state that makes a decision valid: what was measured and when, which source or rule was authoritative, and what each available action would actually change.

Three deep papers establish the signal from complementary directions:

  1. How Reproducible Are Evaluation Conclusions? audits an LLM evaluation rather than only reporting one. Across eight included model variants, mean node-set Jaccard spans .39–.96 and 72% of prompt–model cells are not node-set-perfect. Joint prompt bootstrapping preserves the bottom two ranks in 99% and 86% of replicates, but middle ranks only 27–48%; an alternative campaign-merge rule changes four of eight rows and the headline by 7.1 points. Four of eight evaluated endpoints were retired within ten weeks.
  2. Era by Eon: Benchmarking Enterprise Agents on Hidden Knowledge separates computation from organizational semantics. With code, four strong agents answer 22–25 of 27 computable questions; on eight hidden-knowledge questions the best model–program pair answers 18 of 24 runs, while all twelve agents solve the two hardest record-selection questions only once in 72 attempts. Code helps aggregation but does not identify which record or convention the company treats as authoritative.
  3. Return or Revise? grades both an existing draft and its evidence-conditioned revision before training a controller. Across 25,870 held-out questions, revision repairs 9.32–13.42% of drafts and harms 3.05–3.33%. A paired-outcome policy improves over always revising by 1.10–1.33 points and closes 35.9–41.4% of the oracle gap, yet still applies 38.0–46.4% of harmful revisions. When a standard-RAG answer is also available, revision adds no significant third-action gain.

The Watchlist adds two narrower signals. Risk-Controlled KV-Cache Eviction turns memory compression into a predeclared request-level degradation contract and falls back to full KV when no policy is certified; its guarantee is mixture-specific and does not imply per-task or shifted-population safety. Trusted Model Environment combines confidential-computing hardware, adversarial training, output control and attestations for semantic computation; the proof-of-concept is promising, but code is only promised and the threat model excludes important side channels and compromised TEE/model supply chains.

Decision posture. Prototype evaluation-evidence contracts, hidden-knowledge lineage tests and paired action-effect routing in shadow mode. Prepare raw-run escrow, model/end-point identity and operational-semantics foundations. Monitor certified inference compression and confidential semantic execution. Deprioritize average-only leaderboards and confidence-only RAG control as acceptance evidence.

Top 3 Future Bets

  1. Versioned evaluation evidence contract — 92.9/100. Why it matters: a ranking is unsafe when prompt support, merge rules, run artifacts, endpoint identity and measurement date are not preserved. Evidence that would change the recommendation: internal evaluations remain rank-stable under prompt resampling, analysis-choice sensitivity and endpoint replay without additional provenance. Next reversible action: rerun one release comparison with per-cell raw outputs, joint cluster bootstrap, a second defensible merge rule and dated endpoint/model identifiers.
  2. Hidden-knowledge lineage qualification — 91.5/100. Why it matters: enterprise agents can calculate correctly over the wrong record when the authoritative source or business rule is implicit. Evidence that would change the recommendation: representative internal workflows contain no material answer differences after source-of-record, temporal-validity and rule-lineage tests. Next reversible action: build 30 synthetic cross-system questions with plausible decoys and exact keys; compare the current agent with a read-only authoritative-source map.
  3. Paired action-effect RAG gate — 90.1/100. Why it matters: confidence estimates whether a draft is right, not whether a specific revision will repair or corrupt it. Evidence that would change the recommendation: a confidence-only or return-versus-standard-RAG controller matches paired-effect performance under domain-specific harm costs and distribution shifts. Next reversible action: offline-grade draft, revision and standard-RAG alternatives for 1,000 historical non-sensitive questions, freeze a harm-weighted policy, then run it in shadow mode.

Deep paper 1 — Evaluation conclusions need a replayable evidence contract

Identity and status. How Reproducible Are Evaluation Conclusions? A Self-Audit of LLM-Inferred Prompt Structure, Dipankar Sarkar; submitted September 24, 2026; arXiv:2609.30074v1; cs.CL/cs.AI/cs.LG; previously submitted to a NeurIPS 2026 workshop; CC BY 4.0. The arXiv record includes ancillary corpus files, raw intermediate representations and scripts; the full rerun is impossible because four of eight included endpoints were retired. (paper)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

The case study asks hosted models to infer typed structure from prompts. It persists 293 raw intermediate representations, uses annotated synthetic prompts plus permissively licensed real SKILL.md files, and then audits its own measurement, ranking, analysis code and endpoint availability.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Prior work supplies error bars, rank-confidence methods, perturbation sensitivity and reproducibility guidance. This paper's advance is to apply those safeguards together to one evaluation and publish the failures, including two analysis-specification mistakes and a withdrawn claim. Joint prompt resampling preserves model pairing; per-cell provenance exposes incomplete support; explicit precision separates repeatability from correctness; and dated endpoint rechecks expose temporal non-reproducibility.

External validity is narrow. The task is prompt-structure inference, 64–100% of nodes in the synthetic regime are explicitly marked, the panel is incomplete and unbalanced, two rows have only three and six prompts, and all measurements use one commercial endpoint. The 19-prompt recovery campaign changes multiple factors at once. The strong conclusion is procedural—preserve raw cells, dates, identities and analysis choices—not a prevalence estimate for evaluation failure across RAG, coding or agent systems.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper releases the corpus, 293 raw IRs, per-cell provenance and scripts as arXiv ancillary files, enabling offline regeneration of much of the audit. Exact endpoint reruns are impossible. Enterprise readiness: high for adopting the evidence-contract pattern and adding rank/sensitivity checks; medium for reuse of the specific task; low for treating its model ordering as current. Research Utility Score: 94/100 (methodological rigor 25/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 19/20; enterprise relevance 13/15; prior-work advance 13/15).

Deep paper 2 — Computation cannot recover an unstated source-of-truth rule

Identity and status. Era by Eon: Benchmarking Enterprise Agents on Hidden Knowledge, Benjamin Gruenbaum, Doron Porat, Assaf Natanzon, Roy Zavida, Chen Dinachi and Or Itzahary; submitted September 24, 2026; arXiv:2609.30055v1; cs.SE/cs.AI; arXiv non-exclusive distribution license. The first-party Era console provides generated companies and system simulators; the site explicitly warns that synthetic environments do not replace staging with real data. (paper)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

Era generates one cross-system fictional company with exact keys. The evaluated company contains 250 accounts, 4,190 contacts, 198 contracts, 2,500 Salesforce deals, 3,000 tickets, 3,506 calls, 9,880 Jira issues, 1,918 Slack messages and 2,367 stored documents. Twelve agents pair six models with a no-code or sandboxed-code program.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

BIRD supplies evidence sentences; Spider 2.0 moves structured reasoning into enterprise warehouses; WorkArena, CRMArena-Pro, TheAgentCompany and τ-bench test tools, conversations or state. Era's contribution is an exact, cross-system synthetic estate where the obvious record can be wrong and another artifact implies the answer. This distinguishes data computation from knowledge of which system, date, promise or convention governs.

The study is intentionally small: one company, one seed, eight questions, three repeats and six provider models. The two program variants differ by code execution but also implementation; runs are not paired statistically. Human performance is not measured. Four call remarks repeat the same sentence across companies, some rules select records by metadata rather than content, and the retained results omit full answers and tool traces. The benchmark demonstrates a failure mode, not its enterprise frequency. In real firms, authority may require policy owners and dispute resolution rather than one exact key.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Question templates, deterministic generation, limits, exact grading and first-party simulators are documented; public console and container interfaces support inspection, but the reported proprietary model runs were not reproduced. Enterprise readiness: high for synthetic read-only qualification; medium for a source-of-record lineage pilot; low for inferring live accuracy or organizational agreement. Research Utility Score: 91/100 (methodological rigor 22/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 17/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 13/15).

Deep paper 3 — Route on action effect, not answer confidence

Identity and status. Return or Revise? Learning When Revision Helps Retrieval-Augmented QA, Nicholas Kashani Motlagh, Tim Anderson, Jeremy Gwinnup and Grant Erdmann; submitted September 24, 2026; arXiv:2609.30087v1; cs.CL/cs.IR/cs.LG; CC0 1.0. Upstream datasets and pinned model/retriever revisions are public, but the experimental code, generated answers, trained policies and row-level outputs are not released. The authors estimate one reproduction at about 900 H200 GPU-hours. (paper)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

For every question, the study grades the draft and a candidate evidence-conditioned revision under the same judge. It defines recoverability as revision correctness minus draft correctness, trains before-action policies, and evaluates DPR, BM25 and BM25+MonoT5 setups over NQ-Open, TriviaQA and evaluation-only PopQA.

Claim Directly reported evidence Decision meaning Boundary
Revision has both benefit and harm Repair 9.32–13.42%; harm 3.05–3.33%; always-revise gain 6.27–10.37 points Aggregate gain hides corrupted correct answers Short-form open-domain QA; same checkpoint drafts and revises
Paired policy improves on fixed revision +1.10–1.33 points; closes 35.9–41.4% of oracle gap A specific action effect is learnable Final accuracy objective values repair and harm symmetrically
Harm remains substantial Policy applies 38.0–46.4% of harmful revisions while capturing 94.0–96.0% of repairs Shadow telemetry and stop rules remain necessary Judge errors and domain costs can change the operating point
Paired target beats matched draft correctness Larger accuracy–revision-rate area in all nine setup×seed fits; selected-threshold gains .23–.68 points Confidence is useful but incomplete Run-level significance only for DPR; three seeds give limited power
Action rate is unstable Development-selected revision rate spans 29.9–74.4% while accuracy stays relatively flat Thresholds and costs require explicit governance Many revisions leave normalized answer unchanged
Better retrieval does not eliminate routing MonoT5 raises repairs but not policy gain; harmful revisions persist Retrieval quality and action safety are separate Five passages from one Wikipedia collection
Full action set can dominate revision Return-or-standard-RAG beats return-or-revise by ~2 points for Llama and 4.0–4.6 for OLMo; third revision action adds no significant gain Evaluate all feasible alternatives before adding a router No long-form, domain-specific, high-stakes or deployed pipeline test

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Selective prediction asks whether to trust the current answer; adaptive RAG asks whether or how to retrieve; retrieval-utility prediction asks whether context helps a fresh answer. This work moves the decision after retrieval and estimates what a fixed refiner will do to an observed draft. Offline paired grading observes both actions for every example, avoiding logged-treatment ambiguity, and matched target/backbone/seed comparisons isolate more of the supervision effect than an unmatched baseline would.

The paired label still depends on one judge, one refiner and one evidence set. PopQA supplies 55% of test and no training cases. The same modest-size checkpoint drafts and revises; all retrieval comes from one Wikipedia passage collection; three training seeds do not support broad multiplicity-corrected inference. Code and row outputs are unavailable. Most importantly, accuracy treats one repair and one harm symmetrically, whereas enterprise systems may price corruption, latency, abstention and human review differently.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Prompts, splits, pinned checkpoints, software versions, deterministic settings and compute estimates are detailed; the missing experiment artifacts and ~900 H200-hour cost materially limit replication. Enterprise readiness: high for an offline paired-action audit; medium for a shadow router with domain utilities; low for autonomous high-stakes revision. Research Utility Score: 92/100 (methodological rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 14/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 15/15).

Watchlist

Risk-Controlled KV-Cache Eviction — credible contract, narrow guarantee

Risk-Controlled KV-Cache Eviction: From Memory Budgets to Risk Targets, Beomgu Kang, SoJin Yun, Hojoon Kim and Hyunseok Seo; submitted September 23, 2026; arXiv:2609.27981v1; cs.CL/cs.LG; CC BY 4.0. It predeclares material degradation tolerance τ=.10, allowed frequency ε=.05 and confidence error δ=.05, uses fixed-sequence learn-then-test certification, and falls back to full KV when no candidate qualifies. On Llama LongBench it certifies SnapKV at 75% retention, but on RULER-32K no tested compressed policy qualifies. Aggregate held-out degradation rates of 2.50–4.08% conceal task rates up to 10%; ReFreeKV averages 3.50% but reaches 20% on TriviaQA. The certificate applies to the task-balanced mixture, assumes independent calibration requests and does not cover per-task risk, distribution shift, larger models or end-to-end latency/throughput. (paper)

Trusted Model Environment — promising confidential compute, incomplete threat closure

Trusted Model Environment for Private Semantic Computations, Vasisht Duddu and Xi He; submitted September 24, 2026; arXiv:2609.30032v1; cs.CR; arXiv non-exclusive distribution license. The proof-of-concept runs three 7–9B open models on an Intel TDX/NVIDIA H100 confidential-computing stack, combines latent adversarial training with an LLM output monitor/paraphraser, and attests code/model/operation state. Across three applications it reports unrelated-task utility drops below six points, semantic-leakage rates reduced near zero in tested conditions, and small attestation overhead; the much larger IFC and full-scan costs dominate. Code is promised “upon publication” but was not verified as public. Sensitive entities are predefined, runtime extraction is future work, adaptive attacks still leak, and TEE side channels, denial of service, compromised models, vendor trust and supply-chain attacks remain outside or only discussed. (paper)

Cross-paper synthesis

The five papers expose the same structural error at different layers: compressing a decision into a single scalar removes the state needed to judge it.

Layer Unsafe compression Missing state Safer control
Evaluation One ordered leaderboard prompt support, raw cells, merge rule, endpoint identity, date, rank uncertainty versioned evidence contract + sensitivity and replay
Enterprise knowledge One plausible record source-of-record rule, temporal validity, promise, derivation, owner authoritative lineage map + decoy qualification
RAG action Draft confidence or average gain repair/harm for each available action and enterprise utility paired outcome grading + harm-weighted action gate
Inference efficiency Average quality at a memory budget request-level degradation event, population and confidence predeclared risk contract + full-KV fallback
Private computation “runs in a TEE” output leakage policy, attested model/code, access pattern and side channels layered confidential execution + explicit residual threat model

Independent Analysis. The common enterprise primitive is a decision evidence envelope: immutable identity and time; source and derivation; candidate actions; observed or estimated outcomes; population and scope; uncertainty; policy owner; and reversible fallback. This does not eliminate judgment. It makes the judgment inspectable and prevents a model score, plausible artifact or confidence estimate from silently standing in for a different claim.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

Independent Analysis. Over the next 12–24 months, the recommended options shift enterprise AI from score-centric orchestration toward evidence-bearing decisions. This does not assume a current vendor, model gateway, data platform or maturity level.

Applications and human review
        ↑ answer + decision envelope + appeal
Orchestration / agent layer
        ↔ candidate actions, paired outcomes, abstention
Model gateway
        ↔ model/endpoint identity, configuration, cost, lifecycle
Knowledge and data plane
        ↔ source authority, temporal lineage, conflicts, provenance
Evaluation and observability
        ↔ raw-run escrow, rank stability, harm/repair, drift
Security and infrastructure
        ↔ policy enforcement, identity/authorization, attestation, fallback

Shared foundations unlock multiple enhancements:

  1. Identity and version lineage: model route, endpoint, harness, prompt, tool, judge, dataset and policy identities must be immutable enough for replay.
  2. Raw evidence escrow: retain the minimum privacy-safe per-run artifacts needed to recompute claims and diagnose endpoint or analysis drift.
  3. Operational semantics: version who owns a rule, which source is authoritative, when it applies and how conflicts escalate.
  4. Action and effect registry: name every available action, its expected costs/risks, observed outcome and reversible fallback.
  5. Evaluation infrastructure: joint resampling, sensitivity runs, paired grading, subgroup/tail slices and explicit stop conditions become routine release work.
  6. Observability and cost telemetry: measure harm transitions, route changes, latency, compute and human-review load rather than only final accuracy.
  7. Policy enforcement and human review: high-consequence ambiguity should surface with provenance and abstention, not be silently resolved by the model.
  8. Experiment infrastructure: isolated synthetic estates, shadow routing, immutable experiment manifests and rollback allow safe reversible learning.

Before implementation, internal context is required on workflow value, authoritative data ownership, error costs, regulated obligations, retention/privacy limits, model contracts, current evaluation maturity, acceptable latency/cost and human-review capacity.

Implementation priorities

  1. Now: choose one existing evaluation and add raw-cell provenance, joint prompt bootstrap, an alternate aggregation rule and dated endpoint identity.
  2. Next 30 days: create 30 synthetic cross-system hidden-knowledge cases with exact/adjudicated keys and plausible decoys.
  3. Next 45 days: offline-grade all feasible RAG actions for 1,000 historical non-sensitive questions using a harm-weighted utility.
  4. Next 60 days: establish raw-run escrow and automatic claim invalidation for retired or materially changed endpoints.
  5. Next 90 days: pilot an operational-semantics registry for five rules and shadow the paired-action router with explicit stop conditions.
  6. Hold: production authority from a static leaderboard, unverified source-of-record inference, confidence-only revision, KV compression without a declared population/risk contract, or sensitive semantic computation based only on TEE marketing.

Knowledge reinforcement

  1. Why is a reproducible output not necessarily correct? Repeatability measures agreement across runs; validity requires agreement with the intended construct or ground truth.
  2. Why can a leaderboard change without new model calls? Prompt resampling, missing cells and equally defensible merge or aggregation rules can change support and ordering.
  3. Why did code help computable Era questions but not hidden knowledge? Code can aggregate records once the rule is known; it does not reveal which source, date or convention is authoritative.
  4. What does recoverability add to confidence? Confidence estimates whether the draft is correct; recoverability estimates whether a specified revision will repair or harm that draft.
  5. Why must a router evaluate the full action set? A revision can be useful against “return” yet add no value once standard RAG or escalation is available.
  6. Why is mixture-level certification insufficient for every task? Aggregate risk can be below target while individual tasks or subgroups exceed it.
  7. What does an attestation prove? Only that identified code/model state ran in an approved enclave under the attested mechanism—not that outputs are safe, semantics are correct or every side channel is closed.
  8. What shared foundation supports all top bets? Versioned identities, source/action lineage, raw evidence, explicit uncertainty and reversible fallbacks.
