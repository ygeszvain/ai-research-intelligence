Scope, window, and evidence policy

Research window. Thursday, September 17, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT through Friday, September 18, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT (24 hours; America/Chicago). Discovery covered the September 18 arXiv announcement cycle—329 cs.AI records, including 89 new submissions—plus current cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.SE, cs.CR, cs.IR, cs.DB, cs.DC and stat.ML listings; Nature, Science, ACM Digital Library, ACL Anthology and OpenReview; and first-party paper pages, full texts, repositories, datasets and cited prior work. The normal window was sufficient; no expansion was used.

Anti-duplication. Exact titles, identifiers, central claims and proposed controls were checked against the 39-report archive through September 17. None of today's five selected papers appeared previously. Recent archive work on transactional tool contracts, task-scoped authorization, stateful trajectory qualification, artifact acceptance, evidence identity, benchmark lineage, reusable capability admission and persistent-state validation was treated as prior intelligence. Today's non-duplicate increment is the ordering and coverage problem: resolve an agent's reference before authorizing it, verify the completeness of the specification before trusting its proof, and preserve the retrieved evidence through the response before accepting its claims.

Evidence policy. Paper Fact means directly reported by a paper or verified first-party artifact. Independent Analysis is this briefing's calculation, comparison or methodological critique. Enterprise Inference is a bounded translation into a future enterprise option. Uncertainty marks missing causal, generalization, artifact, economic, security, privacy, governance, operational or adoption evidence. All five selected works are preprints. MAGS and AgenticSearchLens use CC BY 4.0; the GUI-nudge study also uses CC BY 4.0 and links an anonymous public artifact. Closed-World Resolution and EconSkills use arXiv's non-exclusive distribution license. AgenticSearchLens links a public repository but cannot release donated conversations; it supplies mock data. MAGS does not link code from the paper page. Closed-World Resolution describes an installable benchmark but does not link a public source repository from the paper page. EconSkills does not link its skill library or experiment code from the paper page. Benchmark performance is not production readiness. All proposed experiments are isolated, reversible, measurable and non-destructive.

Executive signal

A control is only as strong as the boundary it can name, specify and trace. Resolve the reference, challenge the specification and preserve the evidence path.

Three papers expose different ways an apparently rigorous control can miss its object:

  1. Closed-World Resolution shows that an authorization gate cannot reliably govern a fabricated or ambiguously merged tool reference. Namespace membership, provider identity, current schema and arguments must resolve before causal authorization.
  2. MAGS shows that machine-checked proof remains conditional on semantic coverage and fidelity. All 220 outputs received Dafny certificates; only 82 of 100 terminal programs passed the independent security oracle, and robotic functional preservation was initially 0 of 20 before task-success constraints raised it to 11 of 20.
  3. AgenticSearchLens shows that retrieval is an evidence supply chain, not a binary feature. Four platforms searched at materially different rates, returned different source mixes and frequently generated claims supported by uncited results or by no retrieved evidence at all.

The two Watchlist papers reinforce the same boundary discipline. GUI agents were highly steerable by defaults and social cues; more reasoning changed the attack surface rather than eliminating it. EconSkills improved controlled transfer when the correct procedure was known, but approximate matches erased the gain at library scale and the full 50-skill context reduced success to 11.4%.

Decision posture. Prototype a closed-world tool-call admission resolver, a specification-coverage and independent-oracle gate for high-consequence generated code, and an evidence-flow audit for search-grounded outputs. Prepare qualified MCP namespaces, frozen domain semantics with ownership, and coverage-aware procedural memory. Monitor GUI choice-architecture robustness and agentic formal verification until independent reproduction, artifact maturity and operating economics improve. Deprioritize any policy that treats a formal certificate, aggregate search score, larger model or higher reasoning setting as stand-alone production authority.

Top 3 Future Bets

  1. Closed-world tool-call admission resolver — 93.6/100. Why it matters: ten live models emitted 322 single-registry hallucinations and 154 multi-server MCP hallucinations; the paper's central ordering result is that reference resolution must precede a downstream gate. Evidence that would change the recommendation: an internal runtime where every call is already generated from a live, decoder-enforced, provider-qualified schema and independent testing shows negligible false acceptance and false rejection under registry drift. Next reversible action: shadow-validate 10,000 historical tool calls against immutable registry snapshots, qualified provider IDs, schema digests and strict argument types; block nothing until disagreement is adjudicated.
  2. Specification coverage and independent-oracle gate — 90.7/100. Why it matters: MAGS verified all 220 outputs, yet 18 terminal programs failed the external security oracle and 9 of 20 robotic policies still failed functional preservation after mitigation. Evidence that would change the recommendation: internal domains with complete, independently audited semantics and proof-to-executable mappings whose oracle disagreement remains zero across adversarial change. Next reversible action: choose one narrow safety property in a sandboxed code family, create the formal semantics and a separately owned adversarial oracle, and reject roadmap promotion if either misses injected defects.
  3. Search evidence-flow and citation-lineage audit — 90.2/100. Why it matters: AgenticSearchLens finds that 14–53% of claims across three platforms can be supported by retrieved-but-uncited URLs, while 15–20% remain ungrounded and score lower on factuality. Evidence that would change the recommendation: internal evaluation showing complete claim-to-source lineage and no material quality or attribution gap across representative search tasks. Next reversible action: instrument 200 non-sensitive search traces from query through result rank, claim generation and citation; measure support, attribution, source authority and omission without changing user-visible answers.

Deep paper 1 — Resolve the tool before asking whether it is allowed

Identity and status. Closed-World Resolution Against Tool Hallucination in LLM Agents, Laxmipriya Ganesh Iyer; independent researcher; submitted September 16, 2026; arXiv:2609.19425v1; cs.AI; preprint; arXiv non-exclusive distribution license. The paper describes the versioned Hallucinated-Tools Benchmark (HTB 1.0) and an installable toolguard-bench package, but the paper page does not link a source repository or independent benchmark submission. (paper, full text)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

The paper separates selection—which real tool best fits an intent—from resolution—whether an emitted name and arguments refer to any declared tool at all—and gating—whether the resolved real action is authorized in the current state. Its five single-registry classes cover fabricated names, hallucinated fields, type violations, unauthorized real tools and schema-valid arguments borrowed from another tool. A closed-world Resolution Rung checks registry membership and signature before any causal gate. H5 is deliberately residual when the borrowed arguments also satisfy the named tool's schema; only semantic selection can distinguish it.

The primary live study exposes a fixed 10-tool subset of a 100-tool registry to ten Amazon Bedrock models and runs 60 adversarial probes per model under a schema-enforced surface and an unconstrained raw-JSON surface. Models include Claude Opus 4.8, Sonnet 4.6, Haiku 4.5, two Amazon Nova models, GPT-OSS-120B, Llama-3.1 at 8B and 70B, Ministral-3-8B and Mistral-Large-3 at 675B.

For MCP, the resolver qualifies names by server, rejects absent providers, rejects same-name ambiguity, rejects lower-trust shadowing, verifies the current schema digest against the listing digest and then checks arguments. The design is deterministic, training-free and model-agnostic. It does not decide whether the resolved tool is the right tool for the user's intent, nor whether the action is authorized.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Function-calling leaderboards measure model propensity; tool-selection systems improve retrieval among declared tools; grammar-constrained decoding restricts syntax; least-privilege and prompt-injection defenses gate real actions; MCP scanners audit servers offline. The useful increment is the ordering claim: the emitted reference must resolve to a live, qualified contract before selection and authorization can make a complete decision. Multi-server identity also makes collision, shadowing and staleness first-class runtime failures rather than vague security findings.

The live transcript exhibit, two invocation surfaces, size comparisons and explicit by-construction labeling are strengths. The paper does not pretend that deterministic benchmark cells are empirical production leaks. It also states the trusted-registry assumption and the H5 limit rather than calling the resolver complete.

The principal evidence weakness is circularity in honest over-rejection: a valid call is generated using the same registry the resolver treats as authoritative. The reported 0% is construction consistency, not independent false-reject evidence. Per-model estimates use 60 probes and lack intervals. The registry, attack prompts and benchmark are author-designed. No deployed latency, concurrency, availability or registry-governance study is provided. Schema digests do not prove that a tool implementation matches its declaration. The described package is not linked to an inspectable repository from the paper page, and no third-party HTB result exists. The paper's “gating-only executes all hallucinations” result depends on an explicitly fail-open abstract gate; real systems may fail earlier, but then that earlier check is functionally a resolver.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The taxonomy, algorithms, trial counts, model list, registry size, benchmark scoring and transcripts are described. Deterministic synthetic components should be reproducible if the released package is obtainable. Live reproduction depends on hosted model versions and Bedrock behavior. Artifact provenance, package license, signatures and maintenance ownership were not verified from a linked source repository.

Enterprise readiness: high for a shadow resolver over existing tool-call telemetry; medium for blocking admission after independent honest-call tests, registry ownership and availability qualification; low for relying on the paper's resolver as a complete authorization system. Research Utility Score: 91/100 (methodological rigor 22/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 15/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 15/15).

Deep paper 2 — A proof certificate is bounded by the specification it proves

Identity and status. MAGS: Multi-agent Auto-formalization Guarantees Safety for Agentic Outputs, Albert Wu, Nicholas Roberts, Tzu-Heng Huang, Haoran Lin, Gil Friedman, Sungjun Cho, Gabriel Orlanski and Frederic Sala; University of Wisconsin–Madison and Princeton University; submitted September 16, 2026; arXiv:2609.19391v1; cs.AI; preprint; CC BY 4.0. No code or immutable experiment artifact is linked from the paper page. (paper, full text)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

MAGS separates execution language from proof language. Agents formalize domain APIs and safety requirements under human review, freeze that semantic core, translate generated programs into Dafny, plan proofs, use Dafny/Boogie/Z3 feedback to repair annotations or code, reject proof-bypass constructs, and deterministically compile the verified program back to CUDA, terminal or robot-control code. Program-level agents cannot see benchmark tests or independent oracles. Environmental assumptions become runtime expect checks.

The evaluation covers 100 KernelBench Level-1 CUDA kernels, 100 SecCodePLT terminal programs across 27 CWEs and 20 VLABench robotic-arm policies. Each task has a 25-agent-call retry budget. Safety is checked both by Dafny and by a domain oracle: CUDA compute-sanitizer, SecCodePLT cases, or MuJoCo collision/out-of-bounds metrics. Ten held-out functional tests per task compare original and compiled-back behavior.

The paper is most valuable when read against its own headline. “100% verified” is true relative to the Dafny specifications. It is not equivalent to 100% external security or functional preservation. The 18 terminal misses and robotic vacuity are not incidental defects; they identify the two canonical specification failures: under-specification admits unsafe behavior, and over-restriction admits safe but useless behavior.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

GPUVerify, AutoVerus, DafnyBench and related systems automate proof within a language or domain. VeriGuard verifies a monitoring policy rather than every generated program. ProofWright and verifier-guided repair automate parts of proof construction. MAGS's distinctive contribution is a reusable, frozen semantic layer plus a common verification-aware intermediate representation that returns executable code across three substantially different domains.

Independent safety oracles and functional suites are strong design choices because they challenge the specification from outside the proof loop. Agents are blinded to those checks, reducing direct benchmark gaming. Reporting 0/20 robotics preservation before mitigation is also unusually candid and decision-useful.

However, no repository, semantic library, prompts, traces or compiled outputs are linked. The paper uses future-generation hosted models named Opus 5, GPT 5.6 Sol, Gemini 3.0 Thinking and Claude Fable 5.1; reproducibility depends on access and version stability. Only 20% of autoformalized semantics are manually audited. Test-generation independence is organizationally weak if another related model creates all held-out tests. Ten cases per program are shallow evidence of functional equivalence. The deterministic compile-back mapping and runtime assumptions are trusted but not formally verified end-to-end. Safety checks cover selected properties, not supply-chain vulnerabilities, information flows, resource exhaustion or business authorization. The robotics success mitigation remains informal steering. Costs exclude the one-time semantic construction and review effort.

MAGS therefore strengthens, rather than replaces, yesterday's artifact-acceptance insight: proofs should be one evidence channel in a multi-oracle acceptance contract. A certificate without a coverage ledger should never be presented as global safety.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper specifies benchmark sizes, core formalisms, agents, verification loop, safety oracles, functional protocol, time/token/cost ranges and failure modes. CC BY 4.0 permits adaptation. Without linked code and semantic artifacts, exact independent reproduction is not presently supported.

Enterprise readiness: medium for studying a narrow, high-consequence code family with mature formal semantics; low for a general autonomous verification service; very low for safety claims beyond explicitly modeled properties. Research Utility Score: 89/100 (methodological rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 11/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 15/15).

Deep paper 3 — Search quality is an end-to-end evidence-flow property

Identity and status. Characterizing Web Search by Conversational LLM Agents: From Search Decisions and Strategies to Results and Responses, Mahsa Amani, Seungeon Lee, Abhisek Dash and nine co-authors; Max Planck Institute for Software Systems, Saarland University, École Polytechnique, Seoul National University, Ruhr University Bochum and Microsoft; submitted September 16, 2026; arXiv:2609.19244v1; cs.AI; preprint; CC BY 4.0. The public AgenticSearchLens repository includes analysis code and mock data; donated conversation traces are not released for privacy. (paper, full text, artifact)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

AgenticSearchLens studies the full search lifecycle: whether to search, how to query and refine, what domains the platform's engine returns, what the model cites, and which evidence actually supports each claim. It combines invivo donated multi-turn traces from ChatGPT, Claude, Grok and DeepSeek with invitro controlled API experiments using the same 1,000 sanitized first-turn prompts across provider models.

The invivo corpus contains 310 ChatGPT users and 143,730 conversations/690,754 turns; 102 Claude users and 9,267 conversations/64,354 turns; 100 Grok users and 9,005 conversations/53,840 turns; and 101 DeepSeek users and 9,262 conversations/36,020 turns. Search appears in 42,273, 1,696, 3,004 and 1,730 turns respectively.

The study also finds that nearly 80% of user prompts exceed 20 terms while almost all generated queries use fewer than 10–15; terms increasingly incorporate prior results and queries become more specific over iterations. Those are descriptive behaviors, not proof that a particular query plan is optimal.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Search benchmarks usually isolate retrieval, tool calling or answer grounding. Conversation datasets often cover one platform or self-selected shared links. This work's advance is to join observable search traces from four deployed products with same-prompt API experiments and claim-level evidence analysis. Its lifecycle view makes clear that “web enabled” collapses several separately fallible decisions.

The scale of the invivo corpus and controlled 1,000-prompt replay are strengths. The code release, prompt appendices, platform/model table, bootstrap intervals and evaluator-validation appendix improve auditability. GDPR-compliant donation and mock-data substitution are appropriate privacy choices even though they limit full replication.

Important limitations remain. Donors are self-selected, platform samples differ greatly in size and prompt distribution, and only first turns are used invitro. Hidden chain-of-thought, ranking algorithms and product harnesses are unavailable; model and platform effects cannot be cleanly separated. Product behavior changes quickly. The 30-item human validation samples are small and use authors as annotators. Factuality, entailment and citation mapping rely heavily on model judges, with only moderate agreement for replay scores. A URL returned by search is not necessarily read, and textual entailment does not establish source authority, freshness or independence. Citation presence does not prove causal use; citation absence does not prove fabrication. Donated data cannot be publicly released, so the most important invivo claims are only partially reproducible.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The repository exposes analysis code and mock schemas, and the paper includes prompts, model versions, confidence intervals and many appendix tables. Exact reproduction of invivo findings is impossible without the protected donations and proprietary platform traces; invitro behavior will drift with models, engines and harnesses.

Enterprise readiness: high for an internal trace-based evidence audit; medium for claim-level blocking after evaluator calibration and privacy review; low for adopting the reported platform rates as general thresholds. Research Utility Score: 92/100 (methodological rigor 23/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 16/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 14/15).

Watchlist

GUI choice architecture can steer agents; reasoning redirects rather than removes susceptibility

A Dual-Process Perspective on Nudge Susceptibility in LLM-Based GUI Agents, Haya Halimeh, Sascha Kaltenpoth, Kevin Bösch and Oliver Müller; Paderborn University; submitted September 17, 2026; manuscript completed November 2025; arXiv:2609.19843v1; cs.AI/econ.GN; preprint; CC BY 4.0. Public anonymous code and analysis are linked. (paper, full text, artifact)

Paper Facts. The authors run 3,600 single-shopping-episode agents across six models from OpenAI, Google and Anthropic, with two reasoning settings and three conditions: no nudge, a default nudge and a social-influence nudge. Six decisions per agent yield 21,600 observations. Bayesian multilevel logistic models use four chains of 4,000 iterations, 1,000 warmup and weakly informative priors; reported R-hat values are approximately 1.00. Pooled marginal odds of selecting the nudged product are 8.70 for defaults and 37.24 for social influence versus control. High reasoning halves default-condition odds (OR 0.51, 95% HPD 0.41–0.62; predicted 92% to 86%) but raises social-influence odds 1.71× (1.26–2.20; predicted 96% to 98%). Exploratory size splits and some model cells show heterogeneity and ceiling separation.

Why Watchlist. The randomized experiment, multi-provider sample, preregistered-style hypotheses, Bayesian uncertainty and public artifact make the behavioral signal credible for the specific simulated store. But all episodes use one shopping task, one interface, one choice structure, two nudge types and agent instances with no enduring identity. “Reasoning” is provider-specific, not a common treatment. The very high baseline susceptibility and ceiling cells make odds ratios visually dramatic while absolute changes can be small. The scale analysis is exploratory, some point estimates are prior-sensitive, and the study does not test deception, adversarial websites, brand familiarity, price tradeoffs or enterprise approval workflows. Watch trigger: promote after replication on at least three enterprise-like GUI workflows with realistic constraints and heterogeneous interfaces demonstrates stable choice-architecture effects, and a shadow control reduces unwanted influence without suppressing legitimate evidence. Research Utility Score: 87/100.

Reusable procedures help when coverage is real and harm when approximate context is forced

EconSkills: Studying Skill Transfer and Retrieval for Web Agents on Live Economic Data, Yinzhu Quan and Zefang Liu; Georgia Institute of Technology and Capital One; submitted September 17, 2026; arXiv:2609.19523v1; cs.AI/cs.CL; preprint; arXiv non-exclusive distribution license. The paper links the EconWebArena dataset and the upstream BrowserGym/AgentLab frameworks but not the 50-skill library or its experiment code. (paper, full text, dataset)

Paper Facts. Fifty successful seed trajectories across 37 authoritative websites and nine economic categories become seven-part parameterized procedures. Controlled transfer uses 100 held-out variants, three conditions and three seeds—900 episodes—on gpt-5-mini with a 30-step budget. A known matched skill raises success from 41.0% to 49.3% (+8.3 points); 46 baseline failures become successes versus 21 regressions (McNemar p=0.0031). Among 102 cells where both succeed, matched skills use 2.37 fewer actions on average (sign test p=0.0012). At library scale, five retrieved skills reach 28.3% versus 28.1% without skills overall, while leading 48.0% versus 44.0% on 150 directly covered tasks and trailing 14.3% versus 16.7% on 210 uncovered tasks. The full 50-skill prompt collapses to 11.4%. A one-hint condition is neutral on 100 held-out covered variants but reduces uncovered success from 16.7% to 11.4% (p=0.043).

Why Watchlist. The controlled experiment separates the value of a known procedure from retrieval and context selection, and paired outcome tests reveal regressions hidden by averages. Yet the end-to-end deployment is statistically tied with no skills, each library-scale task runs once, one model and one benchmark are used, websites drift, and the artifact needed to reproduce the library is not linked. The result argues for a coverage classifier and abstention policy, not immediate deployment of a procedural memory library. Watch trigger: promote after a released artifact or independent reproduction across two agent backbones and multiple runs shows that a calibrated coverage gate preserves matched gains while eliminating uncovered-task regressions under site drift. Research Utility Score: 85/100.

Cross-paper synthesis

Independent Analysis — three boundaries that enterprise controls must make explicit

The selected work converges on one architecture rule: governed AI requires named boundaries with independent evidence at every handoff.

  1. Reference boundary: Closed-World Resolution asks whether the call names a real, current, uniquely qualified contract. It must precede semantic selection and authorization.
  2. Specification boundary: MAGS asks whether code satisfies a frozen specification. An external oracle and functional suite must challenge whether the specification covers the real hazard and permits useful behavior.
  3. Evidence boundary: AgenticSearchLens asks whether retrieved evidence survives from result set to claim and citation. The final answer needs lineage, source authority and unsupported-claim handling.
  4. Influence boundary: the GUI-nudge study shows that interface cues become inputs to agent decisions even when no malicious instruction exists; increased reasoning shifts susceptibility.
  5. Coverage boundary: EconSkills shows that a stored procedure helps after a true match is known, while approximate or excessive context can reduce performance.

This is a deeper correction than “add another guardrail.” Each failure appears when a downstream layer silently assumes that an upstream object is valid: the gate assumes a real tool; the proof assumes faithful semantics; the citation assumes a preserved evidence path; the agent assumes interface cues are relevant; the retriever assumes a procedure applies. The future architecture should turn those assumptions into testable admission contracts.

1. Closed-world tool-call admission resolver — 93.6

  • Category: AI Security / Agentic AI / Safety & Governance.
  • Research signal and supporting papers: Closed-World Resolution measures 322 single-registry and 154 live MCP hallucinations across ten models and formalizes why reference resolution precedes gating. Evidence label: Paper Fact.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity: Tool gates, approvals and least-privilege policies can fail ambiguously when a call names no tool, invents arguments, uses a stale schema or collides across providers.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: Insert a deterministic admission step before selection and authorization. Require exact membership in a versioned registry, provider-qualified identity, current schema digest, strict types and explicit rejection codes. Schema-valid intent confusion proceeds to selection; resolved high-risk calls proceed to policy/approval.
  • Likely architectural placement: agent layer, orchestration, model gateway, security/governance and developer platform.
  • Expected business value: reliability, risk reduction, quality, incident prevention, auditability and faster root-cause analysis.
  • Technical value: Establishes a typed, closed-world trust boundary independent of model family; separates parse, resolution, selection and authorization failures.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: Strong structural argument and live-model emission evidence; incomplete independent false-reject and field evidence. Ready for shadow evaluation. Confidence: High. Horizon: Now. Effort: S.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: Authoritative tool inventory, owners, immutable versions, qualified provider IDs, canonical schema hashing, strict parser, telemetry, fail behavior and escalation paths.
  • Key risks: Registry errors become shared-oracle failures; stale inventory causes false blocks; resolver availability becomes critical; schema-valid H5 confusion persists; ambiguous names create adoption friction.
  • Build / Buy / Partner: Build the thin resolver around internal contracts; buy mature schema, service-catalog and policy components where useful; partner with tool owners. Identity and fail semantics are enterprise-specific.
  • Minimum reversible validation experiment: Replay 10,000 historical calls plus seeded H1–H5 and M1–M5 mutations in shadow mode. Compare resolver decisions with tool logs and owner adjudication; do not block production.
  • Success metrics and stop conditions: Success: ≥99.9% seeded H1–H3/M1–M4 rejection, <0.5% independently adjudicated false rejection, <5 ms p95 overhead and complete reason codes. Stop if the registry cannot be made authoritative, false rejection exceeds 1%, or resolver outage can bypass admission.
  • Roadmap trigger: Two representative agent stacks reproduce results under registry drift and multi-provider collision tests; security and tool owners accept ownership/SLOs; the H5 handoff to semantic selection is measured.

2. Specification coverage and independent-oracle gate — 90.7

  • Category: AI Software Engineering / Evaluation & Observability / Safety & Governance.
  • Research signal and supporting papers: MAGS obtains 220/220 Dafny certificates but only 82/100 terminal external security passes and 11/20 robotic functional-preservation passes after mitigation. Evidence label: Paper Fact.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity: Formal or policy proofs can create false confidence when specifications omit hazards or overconstrain useful behavior.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: Every certificate-producing pipeline must publish a property manifest, trusted computing base, semantic coverage ledger and executable mapping. A separately owned oracle team creates mutation tests, adversarial cases and functional-preservation checks; release requires both proof and oracle agreement.
  • Likely architectural placement: developer platform, evaluation, security/governance, infrastructure and human-review layer.
  • Expected business value: quality, reliability, risk reduction, fewer escaped defects and credible assurance for high-consequence code.
  • Technical value: Distinguishes proof soundness from specification adequacy and detects both unsafe under-specification and vacuous over-restriction.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: Strong within-paper counterevidence; no released MAGS artifact or independent reproduction. Ready for a narrow sandbox. Confidence: High. Horizon: Now. Effort: M.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: Narrow domain, mature formalism, semantic owners, independent oracle owners, mutation corpus, deterministic build, runtime assumptions, certificate storage and review criteria.
  • Key risks: Shared model/test blind spots, unmodeled environment effects, compiler/solver trust, high cost, slow tails, property drift, review fatigue and certificates miscommunicated as global safety.
  • Build / Buy / Partner: Partner with formal-methods experts; build domain semantics and oracles; buy supported solvers/toolchains. Internal properties and business invariants require ownership.
  • Minimum reversible validation experiment: Formalize one bounded memory-safety or transaction invariant for 30 sandboxed generated programs; inject covered, uncovered and vacuity defects; measure proof/oracle disagreement and function preservation.
  • Success metrics and stop conditions: Success: all covered mutations rejected, all uncovered mutations surfaced by the external oracle, ≥95% useful-function preservation, reproducible certificates and bounded p95 cost. Stop on any silent external-oracle miss, unreviewed semantic extension or irreversible effect.
  • Roadmap trigger: Independent reviewers reproduce the certificate and oracle results; property coverage is approved; build-to-runtime mapping and rollback pass; economics are acceptable for the selected risk class.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

Independent Analysis — 12–24 month architecture shift

Today's evidence favors an enterprise AI architecture built around admission contracts and independent acceptance, rather than one monolithic “agent safety” layer.

  1. Model gateway: preserve model/version, reasoning configuration, tool-invocation surface, prompt and cost telemetry. A model upgrade cannot silently change whether calls are schema constrained or how evidence is sought.
  2. Context and evidence service: attach source identity, timestamp, authority, access decision, content digest and provenance to every context item. Mark interface cues, retrieved facts and model-generated content as different trust classes.
  3. Tool and capability registry: use provider-qualified identities, signed/versioned contracts, schema digests, owners, risk class, preconditions, effects, permissions and rollback semantics. Flat names become display labels, not security identities.
  4. Resolution before orchestration: deterministically resolve membership, provider, freshness and arguments. Only then perform semantic selection, planning and authorization.
  5. Policy and identity plane: evaluate task-scoped authorization against the resolved action, user/service identity, purpose, resource, amount and approval state. Resolution cannot replace authorization; authorization cannot repair resolution.
  6. Evaluation infrastructure: keep specification authors, proof agents and independent oracle owners separable. Store property manifests, covered/uncovered hazards, functional tests, mutation results, evaluator versions and confidence.
  7. Observability: trace query → result → claim → citation and plan → call → resolution → authorization → effect. Log structured rejection reasons, not just model prose.
  8. Human-review layer: surface ambiguity, ungrounded consequential claims, uncovered specification properties, high-impact GUI sensitivity and negative-transfer risk with the exact evidence needed for a decision.
  9. Experiment infrastructure: support shadow resolution, sandbox execution, paired UI variants, mutation testing, replay, abstention calibration and non-destructive rollback.

The shared foundations unlock multiple enhancements: authoritative inventories, immutable version lineage, content/schema digests, data provenance, identity and authorization, evaluator registries, policy-as-code, cost/latency telemetry, sandboxing, reversible experiments and explicit human ownership. Additional internal context is required before implementation: the existing tool/API inventory, MCP exposure, model invocation surfaces, critical workflows, application owners, evidence sources, data-retention policy, formal-methods capacity, acceptable latency, incident history and review budget. None is assumed here.

Knowledge reinforcement

  1. Why must resolution precede authorization? Because a policy gate can only reason about an action with a real, current identity and declared arguments; fabricated or ambiguous references are not complete gate inputs.
  2. What does a formal certificate actually establish? That a program satisfies specified properties under modeled assumptions and trusted tooling—not that the specification covers every real hazard or that the program remains useful.
  3. Why is search invocation rate a poor quality metric? Search includes separate decisions about when to search, how to query, which results appear, which evidence is used and what is cited; more calls can add noise.
  4. What is the H5 residue? A call that names one real tool but uses another tool's argument pattern in a way that still satisfies the named tool's schema; only intent/state reasoning can distinguish it.
  5. Why does more reasoning not guarantee robustness? In the GUI study it reduced automatic default effects but increased reflective social-influence effects; reasoning changed which cues mattered.
  6. What is the operational lesson from EconSkills? Reusable procedures need typed scope and abstention. Correct known matches helped, approximate matches erased gains and full-library context harmed performance.
