Discovery. Today's incremental question is narrower: when a plan, tool return, benchmark label, or redundant checker says that something happened, what evidence proves the corresponding state transition actually occurred?

Evidence labels. Paper Fact is directly reported in a paper or verified first-party artifact. Independent Analysis is this briefing's comparison, calculation, or methodological judgment. No benchmark result authorizes production deployment.

Executive signal

Reliable enterprise AI must distinguish declared control from executed state: a planning label does not guarantee faithful execution, a successful tool call does not guarantee truthful evidence, and a benchmark score does not guarantee the advertised state mutation occurred.

The three deep papers expose different breaks in the same evidence chain:

  1. Executable-contract benchmark audit finds seven confirmed tool defects and one evaluator property across 34 mutating tools in four agent benchmarks. In the clearest case, a clinical tool reports a successful write while its documented implementation intentionally performs no write, and the grader accepts the returned message rather than changed state.
  2. ToxicBench keeps source tables fixed while corrupting plausible tool observations. Across 118 GPT tasks and three adapters, poisoning lowers task success by 26–39 percentage points. A second check helps when it reaches clean evidence; when matching checks are repeatedly poisoned, agents can verify and still adopt the wrong answer.
  3. Planning-as-Routing separates plan-mode declaration from execution. Generic Plan+ReAct preserves declared structure in only 22–45% of trajectories across three benchmarks; pattern-specific executors increase one reported ALFWorld result from .48 to .92 and a SWE-bench Verified result from .36 to .44. Yet current models do not reliably choose the best pattern, and retry-matched controls erase most of the apparent per-task oracle advantage.

The Watchlist reinforces the boundary. UserProxyBench holds the agent fixed and changes only the simulated user; mean task reward moves 15.2 points across 375 tasks, and 24.4% of successful episodes violate the private user specification. Independent Verification Paths Are Not Independent shows two technologically different data routes agreeing on seven counts while three were wrong because both imported the same semantic error; even a corrected pipeline remained wrong on 42 of 261 disagreements.

Decision posture. Prototype executable tool contracts, evidence-diverse verification, and plan-to-executor binding in isolated shadow environments. Prepare state/effect lineage, dependency-aware verification provenance, and user-proxy qualification. Monitor adaptive plan routing until selection evidence and artifacts mature. Deprioritize success-by-message, repeated same-source checking, and nominally independent LLM-generated verification paths without measured input independence.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Prior benchmark audits mostly inspect tasks, graders, agent claims, or benchmark practice. Contract testing is mature in software engineering, but usually assumes a specification and tests an application boundary. This paper joins those literatures at the tool interface: it infers a contract from what the agent sees, checks implementation behavior, and follows defective state into the score.

Strengths include pinned upstream commits, source-level witnesses, a frozen checker, negative controls, injected mutations, complete enumeration for one benchmark surface, provenance documents, coordinated disclosure, and an offline artifact. The authors also report adverse evidence: most findings were manual, real-benchmark recall has no useful denominator in one arm, three AgentDojo tools are untestable because the probe system crashes, and ten replayed gold trajectories yield no verdict flips.

The selection is not representative: two benchmarks entered because defects were already known. Contracts are interpretations of advertised surfaces, not maintainer-approved specifications. The probe corpus is the main bottleneck. Mutation operators cover six classes, and open-world toy mutations mostly escape. The repository history began after much design work, so pre-registration claims are limited. The clinical no-write case is compelling but one case cannot estimate benchmark-wide prevalence.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The artifact is unusually strong: MIT license, contract schema, 25 test modules, frozen checker, reports, audit plans, pinned commits, and deterministic offline reproduction. Full adapter checks require separately cloning upstream benchmarks at the documented revisions. Enterprise readiness: high for a sandbox contract registry and acceptance harness; medium for CI enforcement after organization-specific false-positive and state-observability testing; low for claims that the checker discovers most real defects. Research Utility Score: 94/100 (methodological rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 20/20; enterprise relevance 14/15; prior-work advance 12/15).

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Tool-use benchmarks often score invocation or final task success. Robustness work may corrupt APIs or retrieval content without distinguishing whether the agent checked, what evidence it obtained, and which answer it finally adopted. ToxicBench makes that sequence observable: delivery, anomaly detection, new evidence, adoption, and recovery.

Paired clean/poison tasks, explicit exposure denominators, reference auditing, a frozen scorer, public-table controls, repeated-poison matrices, two-route comparisons, and post-freeze human labels are substantial strengths. The matched-evidence result is especially decision-useful: retry and review both recover with clean evidence and both fail when every target alias remains corrupt, directly limiting claims about superior reasoning.

The benchmark is coverage-oriented, not an estimate of naturally occurring failures. Main tasks use small synthetic tables and structured answers. Some historical injector exceptions required separate audits. Scorer recall varies by metric and answer style. The 200-trajectory human set is task-ID-disjoint from development but not template-disjoint. Repeated-poison results establish adoption after checking, not that the agent ignored sufficient correct evidence. No production, privacy, adversarial, or multi-source incident rate is measured.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The repository provides code, data, selected traces, manifests, tests, and an offline heuristic demo; some model runs require endpoints and historical logs are explicitly not complete. Enterprise readiness: high for shadow injection and answer-adoption scoring; medium for an evidence-diversity gate after domain-specific sources, costs, and privacy rules are defined; low for production auto-blocking or claims that one retry policy is universally best. Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Planner–executor systems commonly compare final success or ask a model to critique plan quality. This work's advance is to separate mode declaration, structural execution, mode-conditioned performance, and routing. Forced dispatch across all four patterns gives unusually clear counterfactual coverage, and repeated seeds, process metrics, fixed budgets, human verifier comparison, cost appendices, and retry-matched oracle controls improve interpretability.

However, “best fixed” is known only after benchmark-wide evaluation and is not a deployable online selector. Search receives multiple candidate executions, so raw comparisons confound structure with compute; the authors' retry-matched analysis appropriately shrinks the oracle story. Structural agreement is only fair, reflecting ambiguous plan/action matching. Plan quality uses an LLM judge. Benchmarks are controlled and do not include governance, privilege, long-lived state, or human escalation. The exact executors may be benchmark-tuned, and code is promised rather than released.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The 51-page paper provides prompts, architecture descriptions, model/benchmark configurations, seeds, budgets, and extensive appendices, but runnable artifacts remain unavailable. Enterprise readiness: high for the principle that declared plans and executed control flow require separate telemetry; medium for a single bounded executor shadow test; low for adaptive multi-pattern routing or production autonomy. Research Utility Score: 90/100 (methodological rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 13/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 14/15).

Watchlist

UserProxyBench — the simulated user is part of the benchmark

UserProxyBench: Evaluating LLM User Simulators for Agent Benchmarks and Training (arXiv:2609.38043v1; NeurIPS 2026 AABA4ET workshop) adds a User Fidelity Score based on private instructions. Holding GPT-5.5 fixed while varying seven user proxies across 375 tasks changes mean task reward by 15.2 points; 24.4% of successful episodes violate the user specification, and premature disclosure reduces tool calls by 1.06 in successful episodes. This is highly relevant to benchmark provenance and simulated-user training. It remains Watchlist because each task has one rollout per proxy, rubric generation and grading both use Claude Opus 4.8, manual audit is small and non-blinded, UFS measures contract adherence rather than human realism, and the next necessary evidence is a blinded multi-rater human study plus repeated runs. (paper)

Independent Verification Paths Are Not Independent — different code can share the same meaning error

Independent Verification Paths Are Not Independent: A Case Study of Common-Mode Failure in a Satellite Catalogue Pipeline (arXiv:2609.37603v1; NeurIPS 2026 AI for Science workshop) reports a Python-set route and SPARQL/RDF route agreeing on seven counts while three were wrong; one was 932 instead of 220 because both imported the same mistaken status-code interpretation. The corrected pipeline still produced 261 disagreements, 42 of them artifacts. With tools off, 72/75 LLM-generated “independent” paths reproduced the defective count, including 29/30 when source definitions were in the prompt. The MIT/CC BY repository and Zenodo record are strong. It remains Watchlist because evidence is one pipeline, one semantic defect family, and a small three-model, single-family generation experiment; the general architectural signal is to track source and semantic dependence, not to extrapolate the rates. (paper · artifact)

Independent Analysis. These papers argue against treating AI reliability as a property of one model response. It is a chain of typed claims: intent declared, control dispatched, observation returned, evidence compared, state changed, score derived. Each arrow needs a durable witness and a distinct failure metric. A single “success” boolean compresses exactly the distinctions the papers show can fail.

The most consequential methodological convergence is that independence is an empirical property. A retry is useful when poison is one-shot and a later route reaches clean evidence. Two code paths are not independent if they import the same mistaken vocabulary. A simulated user and judge are not independent merely because they are different calls. An enterprise control should record shared sources, prompts, models, constants, services, owners, and graders before crediting redundancy.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

Independent Analysis. Over the next 12–24 months, these signals favor an architecture that treats intent, execution, observation, effect, and evaluation as separately governed objects rather than one agent trace.

Task intent and policy
        ↓
Plan registry → typed executor → tool gateway → effect witness
        │              │              │              │
        └──── declared/dispatched lineage ───────────┘
                               ↓
Evidence router → dependency registry → answer-adoption gate
                               ↓
Evaluation and score-provenance ledger → human review / rollback

Shared enabling foundations unlock multiple backlog items:

  • Model/tool gateway: stable identities, version pinning, budgets, source labels, and risk-tier routing.
  • Agent orchestration: deterministic executors, explicit replan events, typed step predicates, and reversible sandboxes.
  • Effect evidence: pre/post predicates, persistent-state witnesses, transactional IDs, idempotency, and rollback records.
  • Retrieval/knowledge: observation provenance, freshness, corruption domain, and evidence-dependency distance.
  • Evaluation infrastructure: clean/poison pairs, mutation testing, retry-matched baselines, human adjudication, and model/proxy sensitivity.
  • Observability: immutable plan, dispatch, tool, evidence, adoption, effect, and score lineage with cost and latency telemetry.
  • Security/governance: least privilege, policy-as-code, separation of optimized system and auditor, retention, privacy minimization, and exception ownership.
  • Human-review layer: conflict resolution, abstention, appeal, accessibility, and approved override with recorded rationale.
  • Developer platform: contract SDKs, fixtures, state adapters, dependency/SBOM integration, safe replay, and experiment registry.

Additional internal context is required before implementation: which actions are durable, what post-state is legally and technically observable, which evidence sources share infrastructure or ownership, what user data can be replicated for checking, current false-success and rollback rates, latency/cost budgets, model/provider constraints, and who owns final acceptance authority.

Implementation priorities and operating safeguards

  1. Instrument before optimizing. Add plan, dispatch, observation, effect, and score identities to one sandbox workflow; do not change production decisions.
  2. Choose one reversible write path. Encode contracts and effect predicates for ten tools with disposable state, explicit cleanup, and no production credentials.
  3. Construct matched failure sets. Seed numerical, entity-binding, schema, retrieval, partial-effect, and evaluator-field defects; include clean and successful controls.
  4. Test genuine independence. Compare same-call retry, same-backend review, distinct-source verification, and human adjudication under equal budgets.
  5. Separate metrics. Report structural fidelity, task success, effect acceptance, checking, evidence diversity, final adoption, latency, and cost independently.
  6. Gate expansion on evidence. Production consideration requires stable shadow gains, privacy/security review, reversible rollout, human escalation, and explicit stop conditions.

Knowledge reinforcement

  1. Why can a successful tool call still be unsafe? Because transport success and a plausible return do not prove the advertised state change or truth of the observation.
  2. What did ToxicBench show about retries? Ordinary retry can be a strong baseline when it reaches fresh clean evidence; verification prompts alone did not clearly outperform it.
  3. Why is checking frequency insufficient? A check may return correlated or poisoned evidence, and the final answer may still adopt the poisoned conclusion.
  4. What is the plan declaration–execution gap? The difference between the planning structure a model names and the structure its executor actually follows.
  5. Why is the planning oracle not direct evidence of smart routing? It receives multiple execution attempts; retry-matched controls reduce most of the apparent advantage.
  6. What makes two verification paths independent? Measured divergence in sources, definitions, transformations, models, infrastructure, and ownership—not different code syntax.
  7. What should an effect ledger preserve? Intent, versions, arguments, safe pre/post predicates, return, evaluator fields, verdict, and rollback evidence.
  8. What does UserProxyBench add? It evaluates whether the simulated user follows its private role separately from whether the agent gets reward.
