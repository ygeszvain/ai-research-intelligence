Scope, window, and evidence policy

Research window. The exact window was Wednesday, October 7, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT through Thursday, October 8, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT (America/Chicago; 09:00 UTC to 09:00 UTC). Discovery covered the complete Thursday arXiv artificial-intelligence announcement batch and relevant cross-lists, then expanded to full papers, repositories, data releases, and license pages. All five selected papers were submitted October 7 and were absent from the 59-report archive through October 7.

Selection. The screen emphasized controlled contrasts, executable tasks, intermediate-state measurement, uncertainty intervals, explicit negative results, artifact availability, and enterprise decision value. Three papers cleared deep review: Secure-CUA, AgentTime, and SkillSandbox. RECAST and LiveMACE enter the Watchlist because their signals are useful but present deployment-blocking gaps: unreleased artifacts and same-model evaluation in RECAST; one externally evolving market trajectory, composite LLM-judge metrics, and an unavailable stated repository in LiveMACE.

Anti-duplication. Yesterday's four selections—arXiv:2610.08662, 2610.07707, 2610.08168, and 2610.08364—were excluded, as were all prior archive identifiers. Earlier briefs already covered prompt injection, action ledgers, harness qualification, memory admission, and stateful evaluation. Today's incremental question is narrower: which independent contract must exist before untrusted information influences an action, a time instruction counts as productive work, or a learned skill becomes reusable capability?

Evidence labels. Paper Fact means directly reported in a reviewed paper or verified first-party artifact. Independent Analysis means this briefing's comparison, calculation, or critique. Enterprise Inference translates research into a bounded future option. Uncertainty marks absent adversarial, causal, external-validity, artifact, security, privacy, governance, economic, or production evidence. Benchmark success never authorizes production deployment.

Executive signal

Reliable enterprise agents need independent admission contracts: commit the permitted influence before reading untrusted content, prove productive use of a time budget rather than elapsed compliance, and admit reusable skills only after paired execution evidence.

The three deep papers support complementary parts of that rule:

  1. Secure-CUA formalizes decision and grounding integrity for GUI agents. Its action transaction commits queries and permitted uses before untrusted content is exposed. Across 400 WebArena tasks, three frontier planners, and five seeds—6,000 traces—mean benign task success is 53.55% versus 55.12% for Vanilla-CUA and 13.17% for CaMeL-CUA. The security claim remains conditional on correct trust labels, protected components, and an ideal execution model; comprehensive adversarial evaluation is absent.
  2. AgentTime evaluates 222 tasks from 18 sources. GPT-6 Astra deviates from requested runtime by a typical factor of 1.18×, compared with 1.77× for GPT-5.6 Sol and 2.86× for Fable 5.1. But among 158 classifiable Astra traces, 14 sleep after apparent completion and only 11 of 49 on-time agentic runs are classified as continuing work. Elapsed-time compliance is therefore not resource-use compliance.
  3. SkillSandbox synthesizes a task and executable environment per learned skill, then compares the same executor with and without the skill. Across ALFWorld and WebShop, three models, 100 source tasks, and 640 downstream tasks, it reports the strongest downstream performance among seven baselines. Yet its own verdict precision falls to 39.1% on ALFWorld, scenario construction and adjudication share a model, and no public artifact was verified.

Decision posture. Prototype wall-clock and productive-work qualification first because the evidence, code, run data, and reversible experiment are strongest. Build action-transaction prerequisites and skill-library provenance as shared foundations. Keep automatic untrusted-influence enforcement and synthesized skill admission in shadow mode until trust-label error, adversarial robustness, independent adjudication, cost, and false-rejection evidence are measured. Do not count deliberate sleeping as compliant budget use.

Top 3 Future Bets

  1. Productive time-budget qualification harness — 90.9/100. Why it matters: an agent can end on time by finishing early, repeatedly checking, or sleeping, while long requests may still terminate prematurely. Evidence that would change the recommendation: internal tasks show elapsed time strongly predicts useful progress, instrumentation materially distorts work, or human operators do not need runtime commitments. Next reversible action: replay 30 sandbox tasks at three requested durations, label every minute as productive work, verification, waiting, repetition, sleep, or blocked time, and compare outcome gain per incremental compute-minute.
  2. Precommitted untrusted-influence transaction layer — 90.8/100. Why it matters: content needed to complete a task should not gain authority to redefine the action or redirect its GUI target. Evidence that would change the recommendation: trust regions cannot be labeled with acceptable error, action transactions cannot express real workflows, or red-team tests find bypasses despite protected execution. Next reversible action: run 40 read-only browser tasks in shadow mode; generate a transaction before exposing marked untrusted regions and measure decision divergence, grounding failures, latency, and false blocks without executing mutations.
  3. Paired executable skill-admission sandbox — 87.0/100. Why it matters: source-task success does not prove that a distilled skill is reusable or efficient in a novel situation. Evidence that would change the recommendation: independently authored scenarios show low relevance, paired effects fail to predict held-out utility, or synthesis cost exceeds the value of reusable skills. Next reversible action: sample 20 candidate skills, build one independently reviewed novel scenario per skill, and compare identical executors with and without the skill under fixed seeds and budgets.

Deep paper 1 — Security depends on precommitted influence, not post-hoc instruction filtering

Identity and status. Secure-CUA: Controlling Untrusted Influence in Computer-Use Agents; Sarthak Choudhary, Mihai Christodorescu, Ashish Hooda, Somesh Jha, Tongxin Li, and Damien Octeau; arXiv:2610.09469v1; submitted October 7, 2026; preprint; CC BY 4.0. No public implementation or evaluation artifact is linked by the paper; the authors say code will be made available. (paper)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

Secure-CUA separates two integrity properties. Generation integrity restricts how untrusted observations may affect the next action. Grounding integrity prevents untrusted pixels from redirecting a permitted command to the wrong interface target. Before untrusted content is exposed, a planner emits an action transaction: a program containing the queries that may be answered from that content and the ways responses may be used. Untrusted regions are masked from the transaction generator; an isolated query model answers bounded questions; grounding occurs against the masked interface.

Conditional security can be enforced step by step The paper proves trace security when both integrity conditions hold at every step in its ideal execution model Make permitted information flow and grounding targets explicit before exposure The theorem assumes correct trust partitioning, protected models/runtime, and faithful enforcement
Benign utility nearly matches Vanilla-CUA on average 400 WebArena tasks × 3 planners × 5 seeds = 6,000 traces; Secure-CUA 53.55%, Vanilla-CUA 55.12%, CaMeL-CUA 13.17% A per-action contract may preserve more utility than static plan isolation Only benign tasks are systematically evaluated; no attack-success distribution

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Prior CUA defenses include instruction detectors, prompt segmentation, static information-flow restrictions, plan-then-execute isolation, and provenance-aware policies. Secure-CUA's distinctive move is not another detector. It asks the system to declare the specific questions that untrusted content may answer and the action fields those answers may affect before the content is read, then separately protects spatial grounding. This converts a broad “ignore instructions” rule into a per-action capability contract.

The evaluation's strongest features are matched WebArena tasks, three planning models, five seeds, paired systems, full trace count, separate transaction/query/grounding components, and a formal model connected to an implemented design. The authors also report token and runtime costs rather than presenting security as free.

The empirical security evidence is the central gap. The 6,000-trace evaluation is explicitly benign; it measures utility, not a comprehensive adversarial attack-success rate. Trust masks are manually authored and assumed correct. The transaction model can still receive an incorrect answer from the isolated query model; “bounded influence” is not factual correctness. Free-form query responses may carry semantic content that is hard to type. Protected model separation, region labeling, and masked grounding may be fragile in dynamic desktop applications, overlays, accessibility trees, cross-origin frames, or OCR-heavy screens. One demonstrated grounding manipulation does not establish broad robustness.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The CC BY paper exposes formal definitions, pseudocode, prompts, evaluation setup, tables, and ablations, but no usable code, task annotations, masks, traces, or environment lockfile was verified. Exact replication is therefore blocked. Enterprise readiness: high for using pre-exposure influence contracts as a security design target; medium for a shadow prototype on read-only tasks; low for autonomous mutation or a production security claim. Research Utility Score: 88/100 (methodological rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 10/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 15/15).

Deep paper 2 — Ending on time is not the same as using time well

Identity and status. AgentTime: Can Agents Estimate and Control Their Own Runtime?; Michael Ofengenden and Maksym Andriushchenko; arXiv:2610.09944v1; submitted October 7, 2026; preprint; CC BY 4.0. A verified MIT repository contains runners, analyses, tests, documentation, and a public per-run data release; transcripts are partially available through a gated dataset. The repository notes that the current forecasting code reflects an earlier 235-task version and does not reproduce the new 222-task Figure 4. (paper · code · data and dashboard)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

AgentTime measures three different capabilities: follow a requested wall-clock duration, forecast natural runtime before work, and estimate elapsed time afterward. It uses native Claude Code and Codex harnesses rather than a bespoke loop, 222 tasks from 18 sources, requested durations from roughly one minute to 60 hours, 3,442 attempts, about 1,700 agent-hours, and about $93,000 in model and hardware cost.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Prior work such as BATS, BAGEN, and time-awareness tasks evaluates deadlines, token budgets, or textual temporal reasoning. AgentTime advances the field by measuring real wall-clock behavior in native coding and computer-use harnesses and by separating duration following from forecasting and retrospection. The supervisor process is outside the agent's control; tasks use native graders; requests are crossed within tasks; confidence intervals are reported over tasks; and the authors manually classify behaviors that aggregate runtime would otherwise hide.

The largest limitation is repeated-measure uncertainty. Each task-duration cell is run once, so run-to-run stochasticity is mostly unmeasured; only 55 question tasks receive repeated runs. Three primary agents span two labs and two harness families. Very long requests are sparse: only four tasks exceed 8.3 hours. Hardware availability, model serving latency, rate limits, and native harness changes can alter wall-clock results. A single prompt formulation may cue compliance differently. Productive-work labels cover only classifiable Astra traces, and “rechecking” can be appropriate verification in high-risk workflows.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The public repository has ten commits, an MIT code license, tests, prompts, model/harness notes, and scripts for the main duration analysis. The website releases per-run data; 8,911 transcripts are gated, with only 777 of 1,991 main duration runs represented. The repository explicitly discloses which newer result is not yet reproducible. Enterprise readiness: high for internal runtime telemetry and evaluation; medium for scheduling and budget policies; low for hard SLA guarantees without workload-specific replication. Research Utility Score: 95/100 (methodological rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 25/25; reproducibility/artifacts 19/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 12/15).

Deep paper 3 — Skills need counterfactual execution before they become capability

Identity and status. SkillSandbox: Skill Verification via Dynamic Scenario Synthesis; Serin Kim, Kwangwook Seo, Dokyung Song, Jinyoung Yeo, and Dongha Lee; arXiv:2610.10088v1; submitted October 7, 2026; preprint; CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. No first-party repository or data artifact is linked in the paper, and no matching verified repository was found. (paper)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

SkillSandbox addresses a selection problem in self-evolving agents: a skill distilled from one task may encode accidental details, an incorrect procedure, or advice that is useful only on the source. A Proposer specifies preserved conditions and varied details, a Builder generates an executable task/environment, and a Verifier runs the same executor with and without the skill. A skill is kept only when the paired execution shows positive utility.

Dynamic verification improves held-out task success On Qwen3.5-9B, ALFWorld success rises 32.1%→66.4%; WebShop success 18.8%→22.2. Gemini rises 56.4%→60.0 and 25.6%→36.2; Qwen3.5-27B rises 75.7%→80.0 and 32.2%→45.0 Gate reusable skills on paired execution, not self-description Different base competence changes headroom; no confidence intervals

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Prior skill-memory systems such as ExpeL, ACE, ReasoningBank, MemP, and SkillOS extract reusable guidance, rank it, or validate it on the source experience. SkillSandbox's methodological contribution is a per-skill executable counterfactual: construct a novel situation in which the skill should matter, then compare otherwise matched trajectories with and without it. This is closer to a causal admission test than asking a model whether a skill sounds plausible.

The downstream study uses official ALFWorld and WebShop test tasks, three models, 100 source tasks, 140 ALFWorld and 500 WebShop evaluation tasks, fixed scenario counts, and matched rollout budgets. It measures both success and efficiency and analyzes whether verification verdicts agree with held-out utility. These choices are substantially stronger than reporting library size or source-task reuse alone.

The validity loop is not independent enough for enterprise admission. The same model synthesizes scenarios, executes them, and helps verify outcomes, so correlated blind spots can create apparently consistent evidence. Only five scenarios are generated per skill. The ALFWorld precision result means most “keep” decisions can be false positives in that domain. Main tables do not report repeated-seed uncertainty or statistical tests. Generated scenarios may be executable but distributionally irrelevant. WebShop and ALFWorld are simplified environments with constrained action spaces, not production tools, permissions, side effects, secrets, or human approvals. Artifact absence prevents checking prompts, seeds, failed scenarios, and evaluator leakage.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper reports algorithms, prompts, task counts, tables, and ablations, but no public code, generated environments, trajectories, verdict labels, or software license was verified. The paper license does not authorize deriving a software package. Enterprise readiness: medium for the paired-execution concept; low for automatic admission into a production skill catalog. Research Utility Score: 83/100 (methodological rigor 22/25; quantitative traceability 23/25; reproducibility/artifacts 8/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 15/15).

Watchlist

RECAST — retrieval becomes learned evidence computation

Paper Fact. RECAST: Learning to Compute the Right Context through Adaptive Evidence Routing; arXiv:2610.10507v1; submitted October 7, 2026; preprint; CC BY 4.0. A Qwen3.5-9B RouterLM selects retrieval/computation primitives or describes custom operations compiled into executable code. SFT plus GRPO reaches 75.6% mean success on six in-domain families, 15.9 points above the strongest large-model baseline; on three held-out benchmarks it reaches 79.3%, 15.0 points above the strongest baseline. Full RECAST averages 19,400 inference tokens—27.9% fewer than Gemini Interact-RAG but about 12.1× fixed retrieval's 1,600 tokens. (paper)

Why Watchlist. The system has unusually clear ablations: primitive operations alone 56.8%, adding synthesis 59.9%, training-free full routing 64.1%, SFT 67.3%, GRPO 72.6%, and both 75.6%. It also transfers across three CompilerLMs. But the paper evaluates answers with Gemini 3.5 Flash while the AnswerLM is also Gemini 3.5 Flash under an identical answer prompt, raising correlated-evaluation risk. Code synthesis expands the attack surface and requires sandbox, data-boundary, determinism, cost, and leakage controls. The training set is 4,660 questions, but final GRPO uses only 608 rows (567 unique). Code, data, and checkpoint are promised “upon acceptance” and were unavailable. Promote when artifacts, independent adjudication, generated-code security evaluation, workload-specific economics, and repeated-run uncertainty are available.

LiveMACE — process diagnostics expose capability/outcome gaps

Paper Fact. LiveMACE: Process-Aware Evaluation of LLM Agent Capabilities in Evolving Markets; arXiv:2610.09872v1; submitted October 7, 2026; preprint; CC BY 4.0. Five frontier models run persistent paper-trading trajectories for about 30 days across matched Base, Tool Use, Persistent Memory, Rule Following, and Multi-Agent Collaboration configurations. Outcome ranks reverse 21, 19, 19, and 29 times, while corresponding capability-score ranks reverse 0, 5, 8, and 2 times. Tool information coverage is above .8 for all four applicable models, but invocation efficiency is only .581–.731. Gemini stores 93 memories with memory-usage quality .497; Grok stores 12 with .855. Multi-agent return changes range from +3.72 to −4.39 points and increase drawdown/tail-loss magnitude for all four models. (paper)

Why Watchlist. The benchmark makes a crucial distinction among mechanism access, effective use, and realized effect. It preserves complete traces and combines objective checks with judge diagnostics. But each agent/configuration appears to experience one non-repeatable market path; market returns are not controlled causal outcomes. Composite scores contain arbitrary equal weights and multiple LLM-judge dimensions. Model/configuration cells are missing. Live data, stochastic trading, evolving APIs, and paper-account state complicate replication. The stated RaymondVoldemortFu/LiveMACE repository returned 404, so code and license were not verified. Promote after a stable artifact, replayable market snapshots, repeated windows, independent expert validity, sensitivity to score weights, and non-financial replication.

Independent Analysis. The shared pattern is an admission-before-authority architecture. Information should not gain the authority to change an action merely because it is in context. Elapsed time should not count as useful resource consumption merely because a process remained alive. A textual skill should not enter a reusable catalog merely because it helped once or sounds correct. Each transition needs an explicit contract, a protected measurement path, and a counterfactual test.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

Independent Analysis. Over the next 12–24 months, the recommended options would separate proposal from authority and create reusable admission services:

  • Trust and provenance plane. Label source, principal, application region, tenant, version, and trust zone before material enters model context. Preserve the mask and source anchors used for each decision.
  • Action-transaction service. Require agents to declare the intended operation, allowed queries to untrusted data, permitted response-to-action mappings, target selectors, and mutation ceiling. Policy evaluates the transaction; the model never self-authorizes it.
  • Runtime and progress control. Place trusted wall clocks, budget leases, progress checkpoints, activity classification, cost telemetry, cancellation, and human escalation outside the agent. Measure useful state change, not process liveness.
  • Skill registry and admission pipeline. Store origin task, creator/model/harness versions, dependencies, permissions, scenario evidence, paired effect, efficiency, known failures, owner, expiry, and rollback. Retrieval should respect compatibility and policy, not similarity alone.
  • Evaluation and observability layer. Support with/without counterfactuals, repeated seeds, stage-level metrics, mechanism/outcome separation, judge validation, source-linked replay, model/version capture, and cost/latency distributions.
  • Secure computation and human-review layer. Run synthesized evidence programs and skill tests in isolated environments with typed inputs, no default network or secrets, and immutable effect logs. Keep reviewers in the loop for trust-zone disputes, high-impact mutations, and low-confidence skill admission.

Shared foundations are model gateways; identity/authorization; policy enforcement; immutable data/effect provenance; context and knowledge systems; agent orchestration; sandboxed execution; evaluation infrastructure; observability; cost telemetry; experiment registries; and reversible feature flags. No particular enterprise vendor, model, data platform, security posture, or maturity level is assumed. Before implementation, internal task value, trust boundaries, GUI/app inventory, data classes, identity hierarchy, skill ownership, runtime economics, acceptable latency, retention, review capacity, and failure thresholds are required.
