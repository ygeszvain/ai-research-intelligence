SA-Bench: Evaluating Semantic Alignment in LLM-Based Paper Reproduction

Paper · verified public dataset · Accepted to Findings of EMNLP 2026 · CC BY 4.0 paper and annotation license

30 papersICLR, ICML, NeurIPS 2025
1,491 SAUsHuman-reviewed atomic claims
360 repos4 models × 3 scaffolds × 30

What changed

Paper Fact The authors define semantic drift as executable code that silently departs from a paper’s actual numerical settings, method/formula, experimental protocol, or step ordering. They convert paper specifications into Semantic Alignment Units (SAUs), then statically score generated repositories claim by claim on a five-level rubric.

Failure decomposition

Of 7,034 zero-scored claims, 40.8% were implementation mismatches, 16.2% stubs/placeholders, and 8.0% external-knowledge gaps. D3 experimental protocol was the weakest dimension in the published analysis.

Methodological critique

  • Strength: claims are atomic, source-localized, and human reviewed; the public artifact contains the SAUs and underlying paper materials.
  • Strength: factorial comparison separates model and scaffold effects and provides per-paper, per-dimension outputs.
  • Risk: the primary evaluation is static; it does not execute repositories or test scientific results.
  • Risk: GPT-5.5 performs judging. Human review applies to SAU construction, not necessarily every one of the 17,892 final judgments.
  • Risk: only 30 2025 ML papers and five domains; generalization to enterprise application code, regulated software, and other languages is unknown.
  • Interpretation: a score of .301 does not mean “30.1% of a system is correct”; it is an average over ordinal claim scores.

Prior-work comparison

Paper Fact SWE-bench-like tests validate functional outcomes; PaperBench and paper-to-code scaffolds emphasize runnable reproduction. SA-Bench’s contribution is a diagnostic semantic layer that asks whether the implementation matches the source specification, not merely whether code runs. Independent Analysis The enterprise analogue is requirements-to-code traceability, but that transfer must be tested against internal specifications and production test suites.

Enterprise readiness

Readiness: early prototype. The annotation artifact is usable now for a contained evaluation lab. A production gate would require deterministic tests, organization-specific requirement schemas, reviewer calibration, secrets-safe repository access, false-positive measurement, and proof that the judge is not merely matching terminology.

Deep paper 2 · Evaluation & multi-candidate inference

Evaluating Multiple LLM Generations with Validated Task Coverage

Paper · arXiv preprint · CC BY 4.0. The paper cites a project repository, but the URL returned 404 during this review.

5 domainsMolecules, repair, bugs, diagnosis, evidence
24 configsSame configurations per task
1,623 instances164 + 230 + 188 + 302 + 739 stated task counts
No model judgeDeterministic task validators

What changed

Paper Fact VTC evaluates a finite set of generations by counting the union of distinct, validated, task-specific outcomes. It generalizes pass@k: when all successes map to one outcome, VTC collapses to the familiar success formulation.

Selection proxyMacro regret vs VTC Top-5Top-5 agreementLargest mismatch
One-draw quality12.1%10/25Repository repair: 25.0%, 0/5
Surface diversity11.0%11/25Repository repair: 25.0%, 0/5

Quantitative claims traced

Paper Fact Raising temperature from 0.6 to 1.2 improved point-estimate VTC in 38 of 40 matched comparisons, while one-draw quality declined in 25 of those; paired instance-resampling intervals were above zero in 36/40. “Thinking” improved one-draw quality in 44/60 comparisons but VTC in only 36/60, and the two moved in opposite directions in 23 comparisons.

Methodological critique

  • Strength: automatic, task-grounded validators avoid circular LLM-as-judge scoring.
  • Strength: the five tasks span very different outcome spaces; robustness intervals trace sensitivity to instance reweighting.
  • Risk: outcome equivalence is designed by the researchers. Modified-function sets, UMLS concepts, and evidence aspects may merge or omit distinctions that matter operationally.
  • Risk: the authors explicitly state their resampling intervals do not quantify new-generation or population uncertainty.
  • Reproducibility gap: redistributable inputs and prompts are claimed, but the cited GitHub URL was unavailable; restricted AMIE inputs and coding records are not redistributed.
  • Safety: the clinical task is an evaluation substrate, not evidence for autonomous diagnosis.

Prior-work comparison and enterprise implication

Paper Fact pass@k, best-of-N, and self-consistency reduce multiple generations to a success event or selected answer; generic diversity metrics measure difference in form. VTC instead credits additional validated outcomes. Enterprise Inference For threat hunting, design exploration, evidence retrieval, root-cause hypotheses, and portfolio ideation, selection should consider validated coverage per cost—not only best answer quality.

Enterprise readiness

Readiness: prototype-ready metric pattern, not a universal benchmark. The hard work is defining defensible validators and equivalence classes for each internal workflow. Without those, “coverage” becomes arbitrary and can be gamed.

PeakBench: Benchmarking Resource-Aware Tool Invocation in LLM Agents

Paper · arXiv preprint. The paper cites code, but the repository returned 404 during verification; license is therefore unverified.

300 workflows150 easy, 100 medium, 50 hard
8 modelsSame prompt and parsing protocol
3 profilesSmall, medium, large simulated capacities
2 dimensionsDependency planning + scheduling

What changed

Paper Fact PeakBench explicitly separates logical workflow planning from physical scheduling. It measures Graph Edit Distance and Edge F1 for dependencies, then scheduling latency, Capacity Violation Area (CVA), and safe utilization under resource limits.

SchedulerLatency ↓CVA ↓Strict MRU ↑Interpretation
ASAP8.62s5.865.080Fast but overflow-prone

Quantitative claims traced

Paper Fact Case-level correlations between logical planning metrics and scheduling metrics were weak (reported Pearson r values roughly −0.115 to +0.102). In one DeepSeek-V4-Flash ablation, duration alone did not improve feasibility (74.9%); CPU+memory context raised it to 87.6%, and the full profile to 87.8%. Noise tests retained gains under 10–50% profile perturbation, but benefit declined with noise.

Methodological critique

  • Strength: factorial separation makes planning failures distinguishable from scheduling failures.
  • Strength: measured resource profiles, replayed schedules, classical baselines, input ablations, and perturbation tests improve causal interpretability.
  • Risk: workflows are synthesized near existing API/MCP tasks; they are not demonstrated to match enterprise production distributions.
  • Risk: “machine profiles” are percentile-derived simulation capacities, not specified hardware or production clusters.
  • Risk: no evidence of queue contention, tenancy, rate limits, retries, data locality, security domains, billing limits, or stochastic tool latency.
  • Reproducibility gap: cited code was unavailable during verification.

Prior-work comparison and enterprise implication

Paper Fact Most tool-use benchmarks emphasize tool choice, arguments, and end-to-end success. PeakBench adds resource feasibility and separates it from dependency planning. Independent Analysis The highest-value transfer is not to let an LLM schedule infrastructure directly; it is to make resource envelopes machine-readable and route proposed schedules through deterministic admission control.

Enterprise readiness

Readiness: shadow-test only. A production scheduler must remain authoritative. LLM output can propose ordering or concurrency, while policy, identity, budget, rate-limit, and capacity constraints enforce hard boundaries.

Adaptive Influence Graphs for Failure Attribution in Multi-Agent Systems

Paper

Paper Fact On the algorithm-generated Who&When partition, Opus-5 step accuracy rose from 46.4% on raw logs to 55.2% with adaptive graph construction and agent-directed traversal; the prior reported best was 51.6%. Watch · RAG & Knowledge

Constrained Entity Selection under Partial Knowledge for LLM-Based KGQA

Paper · Related peer-reviewed KI 2026 version

Paper Fact CES-PK filters LLM-generated candidate entities using satisfied/violated/unknown symbolic constraints. The three-valued semantics is intended to preserve recall under incomplete knowledge graphs while rejecting explicitly invalid candidates and ranking supported ones.

Why Watchlist: attractive governance pattern—generate broadly, verify narrowly—but the eight-page study uses one biomedical graph (Hetionet), one author, two tables, and no verified code artifact. Cross-domain precision/recall, constraint-authoring burden, latency, and schema-drift behavior remain unknown.

Cross-paper synthesis

One pattern across all five papers

Generation should be probabilistic; acceptance should be typed, grounded, and replayable.

Independent Analysis SA-Bench types requirements into four semantic-drift dimensions. VTC maps outputs into validated outcome classes. PeakBench separates dependency validity from resource feasibility. AIG grounds graph nodes back to raw trace steps. CES-PK distinguishes satisfied, violated, and unknown constraints. Each paper independently replaces an undifferentiated “success” score with structured evidence about what was correct, why, and under which constraint.

1. Correctness is multidimensional

Runnable code can be semantically wrong; a correct dependency graph can yield an unsafe schedule; a high-quality candidate can add no new useful outcome.

2. Representation changes diagnosis

Atomic claims, influence graphs, resource profiles, equivalence classes, and symbolic constraints materially change what a model can inspect and what an evaluator can measure.

3. Deterministic controls still matter

Automatic validators, classical schedulers, execution tests, policy constraints, and human escalation remain the final authority. Research systems provide proposals and diagnostics.

Implementation priorities for an enterprise evaluation lab

  1. Define typed outcome contracts: requirement claims, acceptable equivalence classes, dependency constraints, resource envelopes, and explicit unknown states.
  2. Capture provenance: bind each agent decision, retrieved item, generated patch, evaluator result, and human override to immutable run metadata.
  3. Evaluate in two stages: semantic/logical checks first; dynamic execution, capacity, safety, and policy checks second.
  4. Replay chronologically: compare proposed controls against frozen traces and shadow workloads before any live intervention.
  5. Promote only on internal evidence: require improvement on organization-specific tasks without unacceptable latency, cost, false blocks, privacy exposure, or operator burden.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

A 12–24 month control-plane architecture

Independent Analysis This is a stack-agnostic target pattern. It does not assume any current enterprise vendor, cloud, model, retrieval stack, security posture, or maturity.

Applications & human review

Decision UI, reviewer queues, escalation, override, outcome labeling.

Agent orchestration

Dependency DAGs, tool identity, concurrency proposals, retry and compensation.

Model & policy gateway

Model routing, sampling budgets, authorization, hard policy enforcement, cost caps.

Knowledge & context

Versioned requirements, provenance, constraints, retrieval evidence, memory boundaries.

Evaluation & observability

Semantic units, coverage, resource replay, trace graphs, calibration and drift.

Shared enabling foundations

Identity & authorizationPolicy enforcementImmutable run IDsData provenanceVersioned requirementsEvaluation registryOpen telemetryCost & resource telemetryReplay environmentsHuman overrideExperiment controls

The same foundations unlock several enhancements. Provenance enables semantic gates, influence graphs, and post-incident replay. Tool/resource telemetry enables safe scheduling and cost-aware inference. Typed validators enable both candidate coverage and knowledge-answer constraints. Identity and policy must sit below all model decisions as non-bypassable controls.

Architecture boundaries

  • No paper establishes readiness for autonomous production control.
  • Models may propose; deterministic systems authorize, schedule, execute, and roll back.
  • “Unknown” must be a first-class result, not silently mapped to success or failure.
  • Internal data classification, regulatory obligations, tenancy, latency SLOs, and cost economics are required before implementation decisions.
  • Experiments should be isolated, reversible, measurable, non-destructive, and incapable of changing customer-facing behavior.
Knowledge reinforcement

Five-minute retention loop

Recall prompts

  1. Why can a repository be executable and still receive a low semantic-alignment score?
  2. What does VTC measure that pass@k and generic diversity miss?
  3. Why did PeakBench separate dependency recovery from resource scheduling?
  4. What evidence prevented AIG and CES-PK from becoming implementation recommendations?
  5. Which three control planes form today’s executive takeaway?

Answer key

  1. It may substitute, omit, stub, or reorder source requirements while still running.
  2. The accumulation of distinct, validated, task-specific useful outcomes at a fixed attempt budget.
  3. Because a logically valid plan can still violate finite CPU, memory, rate, or cost capacity; empirical correlations were weak.
  4. Narrow benchmarks, incomplete artifacts, unverified transfer, and no production evidence.
  5. Semantic fidelity, resource-feasible execution, and validated outcome coverage.

Executive one-sentence memory

Reliable enterprise AI is not “generate and hope”; it is generate, ground, validate, constrain, replay, and only then decide.
