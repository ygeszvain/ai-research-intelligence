Executive decision

October 17, 2026 · Three deep papers · One Watchlist paper · Seven enterprise options

Central signal — qualify the meaning of a signal before letting it control a system. A generated SQL statement does not establish that the question is valid. A graph of previous tool calls does not establish that its next-tool advice helps. A long context window does not establish that the model can combine dispersed evidence. And a weak model's predicted failure does not establish that escalation will fix it.

Today's strongest opportunities are evaluation capabilities, not new autonomy permissions. PremiseSQL supports testing both appropriate critique and inappropriate rejection; the tool-memory study supports separating memory's contribution from extra inference; LURCH supports stratifying long-context tests by evidence structure. LEASH is a promising escalation design, but its economic and causal evidence remains too narrow for an implementation commitment.

Decision boundary: all proposals below are future options. “Now” means an isolated prototype or prerequisite assessment, never permission to deploy. No current enterprise architecture, vendor contract, data entitlement, security posture, staffing, or model access is assumed. Internal business loss functions, representative workloads, access boundaries, and operational costs remain necessary before roadmap decisions.

Top 3 Future Bets

  1. Bidirectional premise-critique qualification — 88.0/100. Why it matters: a defensive prompt can improve flaw detection while suppressing legitimate work. Evidence that would change the recommendation: representative internal pairs show no material false-acceptance or false-rejection problem, or human reviewers cannot agree which premises are flawed. Next reversible action: create an adjudicated, read-only set of 100 valid/flawed query pairs and compare three prompt policies without executing generated queries against live databases.
  2. Compute-controlled memory contribution test — 86.6/100. Why it matters: orchestration improvements can actually be extra-attempt improvements; a near-flat call count can still hide substantial token growth. Evidence that would change the recommendation: graph guidance wins consistently at matched tokens, calls, and task budgets on held-out workflows, or situation-level insights lose their advantage under the same controls. Next reversible action: replay 100 synthetic tool tasks in a sandbox with baseline, graph, insight, and budget-matched no-memory arms.
  3. Evidence-structure long-context qualification — 86.3/100. Why it matters: nominal context length misses how much evidence is needed, how dispersed it is, and how many derivation steps connect it. Evidence that would change the recommendation: those annotations add no stable out-of-sample diagnostic value beyond task family and length in the target corpus. Next reversible action: double-annotate 60 approved document questions, then test a preregistered length-only versus evidence-structure analysis.

Deep paper 1 — PremiseSQL

P1 · PremiseSQL · Shuo Lin, Jinzhe Li, Chenlu Guo, Yanhan Li, Yuan Wu, and Yi Chang · arXiv:2610.22158v1 · submitted October 15, 2026, 16:23:17 UTC. Primary record · Full paper · Verified code repository. Venue acceptance was not verified.

What changed and why it matters

Paper Fact. PremiseSQL pairs 1,800 flawed requests with their 1,800 original requests. Nine flaw subtypes span expression problems, contradictions, missing premises, and requests beyond the available system's ability. Sixteen models are evaluated under Base, Implicit, and Explicit prompting, producing 172,800 responses. The central measurement is bidirectional: identify supported flaws, but do not reject a valid original merely because the instruction says a flaw exists.

Independent Analysis. This is more decision-useful than measuring only refusal or only SQL execution accuracy. It exposes a two-sided operating problem: overconfident generation and instruction-induced rejection. The strongest enterprise signal is a paired qualification protocol, not a new mandatory warning prompt.

Method and measurement contract

Base asks for SQL. Implicit prompting allows that the question may contain errors. Explicit prompting asserts that a flaw exists, including when the input is an original negative-control query. Explicit is therefore a susceptibility stress test, not a neutral deployment instruction. Temperature is zero under the paper's default API settings; that does not remove backend nondeterminism or model-version uncertainty.

The reported SQL behavior score is an ordinal 0–2 score normalized into 0–1. It is not the fraction of cases with complete abstention. DET measures flaw detection, TYPE measures type identification, GEN measures SQL-generation coverage on originals, and VAL measures explicit recognition that originals are valid. GEN is not execution correctness. The paper separately assesses execution on an eligible original-query subset.

Two model judges and targeted human checks support scoring. The paper reports 2,160 human-reviewed outputs from two target models, high agreement on the checked dimensions, and family-disjoint judge robustness with aggregate changes no greater than 0.0016 and rank correlations 0.944–1.0. This is useful evidence against a simple judge-family artifact, but it is not human adjudication of the entire 172,800-output set.

Methodological critique and prior work

Independent Analysis — strengths. The paired negative controls make instruction-following separable from evidence-grounded critique. The execution subset prevents coverage from being silently relabeled accuracy. Subtype analysis makes the benchmark actionable, while judge robustness checks reduce one obvious evaluation concern.

Independent Analysis — limits. Synthetic, balanced flaw categories are not an estimate of workplace prevalence. Candidate filtering and human approval may favor recognizable perturbations. The benchmark's original requests inherit source-dataset conventions; a request deemed valid under one schema or naming convention may still be underspecified in a real business setting. An enterprise must adjudicate its own negative controls rather than treating every benchmark original as a universal semantic truth.

The failure-mechanism analysis is also conditional. Its eligible sample of 8,000 failures is filtered from a larger failure pool and excludes cases whose historical exposure cannot be reconstructed. The reported 72.6% unsupported-assumption share describes that selected analysis set, not the prevalence of unsupported assumptions across all SQL interactions. The mechanism human check is AI-assisted, including preselected agreements, rather than a wholly blind independent audit. Do not promote mechanism percentages into operational risk forecasts.

Prior-work comparison, as positioned in the paper. TriageSQL focuses on intention triage; TrustSQL incorporates unanswerability and penalties; KWIDK addresses ambiguity; NL2SQLBUGs studies translation errors. PremiseSQL's incremental contribution is a broader premise-error taxonomy with paired valid originals and prompt-conditioned bidirectional evaluation. This briefing did not independently re-review every cited predecessor, so comparative priority is not a claim of exhaustive novelty.

Reproducibility and readiness

Verified artifact. The repository contains construction, evaluation, failure-analysis, and schema-linking code and reports an MIT license for its code/documentation. Its README says benchmark data, databases, model responses, and annotations are distributed separately where upstream terms permit. It also distinguishes mock testing from a live end-to-end release-preparation run. Do not read MIT as a license to redistribute Spider/BIRD data or as evidence that the paper's runs are reproducible from the repository alone.

Enterprise Inference. Ready for a small, internally adjudicated evaluation prototype. Not ready to become a blanket production refusal policy. High confidence in the need for two-sided measurement; moderate confidence in transfer of the paper's subtype ordering; low confidence in any unmeasured business savings.

Falsification experiment. Hold out databases and business intents, preserve valid/flawed pair identity, and compare neutral, conditional-critique, and forced-flaw prompts. Measure false acceptance, false rejection, correct clarification, executable correctness, and reviewer time separately. A prompt that detects more flaws but blocks materially more correct work fails qualification unless the business loss function explicitly justifies that trade-off.

Deep paper 2 — Rethinking the Role of Tool Graph Memory in Multi-Turn Tool Use

P2 · Uiji Hwang, Richeng Xuan, and Taeuk Kim · arXiv:2610.21571v1 · submitted October 15, 2026, 09:24:30 UTC. Primary record · Full paper. No accepted venue, public executable repository, or code license was verified.

What changed and why it matters

Paper Fact. The study compares graph-based tool memory with a simple alternative that retrieves situation-level insights. ToolNet restricts visible tools using graph successors; the evaluated H-EPM inference variant keeps the full tool set but adds suggestions and summarization calls. Nine open-weight models are tested on ToolSandbox and BFCL-v4, with three runs per condition; additional proprietary-model experiments are narrower.

Independent Analysis. A memory module can change the agent's effort, not just its information. Its value therefore cannot be inferred from a better task score without counting extra calls and tokens. Conversely, a transition graph may encode the wrong abstraction: identical next tools can require different arguments, authorization, or decisions about whether a call is warranted at all.

Method and controls

ToolSandbox uses 825 training scenarios and 207 test scenarios with milestone-based soft scores. The paper's pass-style analyses apply a success threshold above .55; they should not be equated with exact workflow success. BFCL-v4 uses 640 training and 160 test tasks, with four variants per underlying task and a split by underlying task. Its checks require the relevant multi-turn state conditions. Cross-benchmark score magnitudes are therefore not directly comparable.

The main user simulator is GPT-4o, with a smaller GPT-4.1 robustness check for two models. Greedy decoding and repeated runs support within-harness comparisons, but simulator behavior and model snapshots remain part of the experimental treatment.

The Insight method embeds trajectory summaries with MiniLM, clusters them using a silhouette-based K choice, and retrieves two clusters using the last five messages. The resulting advice is a soft situation-level prompt rather than an enforced next-tool transition. It uses both successful and failed trajectories; successful-only and ExpeL-style ablations are included. This is a research implementation, not evidence that raw production traces should be retained or injected without permission and provenance checks.

Why the controls matter—and what they do not prove

Independent Analysis. Removing or randomizing suggestions while retaining parts of the harness is useful evidence against attributing all gains to graph content. It is not a perfectly cost-matched randomized mediation study. Some arms differ in calls, context, or instructions. The next enterprise experiment should explicitly match budgets and report both fixed-budget quality and fixed-quality cost.

An oracle next-tool analysis still finds failed scenarios attributed to call necessity (81), tool selection (6), dependency (7), and arguments (44) among 138 examined failures. Those are author annotations on a selected failure set; ten final-answer-only failures are excluded. They suggest that knowing the next tool can be insufficient, but are not causal prevalence estimates for all enterprise agents.

The manual mechanism analysis focuses on selected models and changed outcomes. Repeated scenarios and many per-model comparisons reduce confidence in isolated significance findings; the main effect table's significance labels are not corrected for all comparisons. Separate corrected analyses elsewhere in the paper do not retroactively resolve that issue.

Safety boundary. An instruction to continue making tool calls can improve benchmark completion while becoming unacceptable in a real workflow. No memory experiment may override authorization, action limits, termination criteria, or a correct decision to abstain. Repeated attempts that trigger external effects are not a harmless source of extra compute.

Prior work, artifacts, and readiness

Prior-work comparison. The paper directly tests ToolNet-style constrained transitions and an inference-only H-EPM variant. It does not reproduce the full trained H-EPM system or invalidate richer graphs encoding arguments, dependencies, world state, and permissions. Its Insight alternative belongs closer to experience-based soft guidance such as ExpeL than to a prescription that the next graph edge must be followed.

Uncertainty. No public executable artifact or license was verified from the paper and targeted discovery. Therefore replication depends on reconstructing enough of the harness, or obtaining the authors' release. Do not turn a conceptual implementation into a claim of author-code reproduction.

Enterprise Inference. Prototype the evaluation method now, and test situation-level insights only inside the sandbox. Confidence is moderate that naive next-tool graphs deserve skepticism; moderate that insight memory will transfer; low that either provides cost savings before complete token, latency, and operational accounting.

Falsification experiment. Split tasks by intent and environment, not by paraphrase. Compare no memory, graph suggestions, situation insights, and a matched-budget no-memory control. Hold tools, authorization, termination, and maximum effects constant. Repeat the best result across at least two model families and two workflow classes. An insight gain that disappears when its 43%-type token overhead is matched is not a memory-specific win.

Deep paper 3 — Beyond Context Length: Characterising the Difficulty of Long-Context Reasoning

P3 · LURCH · Shiwen Qin, Cameron Barker, Henry Gouk, Elliot J. Crowley, Shay B. Cohen, Mirella Lapata, and Miao Li · arXiv:2610.22067v1 · submitted October 15, 2026, 15:28:33 UTC. Primary record · Full paper · Author publication page. The author page is dated October 11 and says under review; its older “arXiv coming soon” wording is not evidence of a later release or acceptance.

What changed and why it matters

Paper Fact. LURCH characterizes 6,550 questions from 17 sources using three evidence-structure properties: scope, dispersion, and derivation depth. It studies English contexts of 32k–256k tokens, with a median of 103k under the specified Qwen tokenizer. Four models and three samples per question yield 78,600 model outputs by arithmetic, before distinguishing the additional annotation and filtering calls.

Independent Analysis. Context length is a capacity measurement. It is not a sufficient account of task difficulty. The useful near-term enhancement is a richer evaluation matrix: hold length approximately stable while comparing narrow/local/shallow evidence needs with broad/dispersed/deep ones. The paper does not validate an automatic production router that can observe all these properties before answering.

Definitions and selection effects

Scope is evidence size S divided by context length L. Dispersion uses (H−S)/(L−S), where H covers the evidence span and gaps under the paper's definition. Depth is the longest path through an annotated derivation DAG, including lookup nodes. Evidence is expanded to paragraph or table units; it is not necessarily the minimal token set logically sufficient for an answer.

The pipeline starts from 323,040 questions across 26 sources and applies language, length, evidence, answerability, and quality filtering. A verifier must answer correctly in all three evidence-only trials; zero-context screening checks whether answers can be obtained without the supplied context. These choices reduce some leakage and evidence-quality problems but select for cases that the chosen verifier can already solve from extracted evidence. Results do not describe the entire unfiltered long-document population.

Derivation annotation uses the answer. It is therefore a retrospective task characterization, not the evaluated model's actual chain of thought and not a free ex-ante routing feature. Two annotators agree exactly on depth in 65.6% of cases, with correlation .48. Human review covers 40 examples enriched for both agreement and disagreement. One reviewer accepts both graphs in 38 cases; another in 23, preferring Gemini in 14 and GPT in three. This supports multiple defensible derivations and consequential annotation uncertainty, rather than a single indisputable depth label.

The main analysis is fractional-logit regression with transformed and standardized predictors, task/model effects, question-clustered standard errors, and Holm adjustment for the specified confirmatory tests. That is incremental predictive value, not a 0.58-point accuracy gain, and task identity accounts for much of the predictable variation.

An exploratory high-on-all-three subset of 164 questions has a −5.4-point baseline gap, but its residual contrast is −1.5 points with an interval [−5.7, +2.6]. A high-dispersion/depth cell is heavily composed of SciTrek (62%); removing that source reduces a depth gap from −6.7 to −.8 points. These analyses do not establish a general multiplicative “triple difficulty” failure law.

Methodological critique and prior work

Independent Analysis — strengths. Explicit constructs, source-level composition checks, regression controls, clustered uncertainty, and held-out predictive comparisons are substantially better than attributing every long-context failure to token count. The paper exposes uncertainty in the annotations rather than hiding it.

Independent Analysis — limits. The relationships are observational. Task composition, filtering, evidence expansion, tokenizer choice, and answer-conditioned derivations can change measured properties. Five-example manual checks per source and the 40-example graph audit are not a complete validation of thousands of annotations. A model may solve a task through a different legitimate derivation. The modest incremental predictive improvement argues for a low-cost annotation pilot, not an expensive platform-wide ontology project before testing utility.

Prior-work comparison, as reviewed in P3. RULER and Lost in the Middle emphasize context length, retrieval, and positional effects; earlier scope/dispersion framing and coverage-oriented work motivate richer properties. LURCH's contribution is a joint quantitative characterization of evidence footprint, separation, and derivation structure across heterogeneous long-context tasks. This is a measurement advance, not evidence that a particular retrieval, compression, or context-reordering intervention will fix the observed problems.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Uncertainty. The primary paper and author publication page were verified, but a released executable benchmark, annotation set, or license was not. Public access to a paper does not imply rights to redistribute every underlying source corpus. A faithful reproduction requires the exact filters, evidence annotations, source versions, evaluator, and model snapshots.

Enterprise Inference. Moderate confidence in testing evidence-structure strata on approved internal documents. Lower confidence in paying for exhaustive annotation or using predicted difficulty to route production traffic. In a 60-question pilot, compare inter-annotator agreement, annotation minutes, held-out error localization, and whether the resulting strata actually change a model or retrieval decision. If a length-only plus task-family baseline is equally useful, stop the additional annotation work.

Watchlist — LEASH

W1 · LEASH · Qingni Wang, Changdae Oh, Yuzhe Yang, Sharon Li, Jiachen Yang, Ang Li, and Xin Eric Wang · arXiv:2610.22280v1 · submitted October 15, 2026, 17:40:12 UTC. Primary record · Full paper. This is the sole Watchlist selection; the second slot is intentionally unused.

Paper Fact — mechanism. LEASH combines an initial uncertainty check, runtime escalation using latent representations, and a weak-model shadow observer after handoff to a stronger agent. Its runtime classifier uses linear discriminant analysis of successful and failed trajectories. The main setting has a 99-task reference pool, 262 OSWorld test tasks, a 50-action limit, four weaker backbones, and a stronger Opus model that is also used in the Manager role.

Paper Fact — promising numbers. Table 1 reports Qwen3.5-35B-A3B with LEASH at 73.66% success and estimated $0.4117 per task, compared with always-strong at 72.90% and $0.5094. The reported estimated cost reduction is 19.2%. The 0.76-point success difference does not establish equivalence, superiority, or noninferiority without an appropriate uncertainty analysis and margin. Prices are the study's assumed token costs, not a verified current procurement quote or measured local-inference bill.

Independent Analysis — why monitor rather than implement. The always-strong comparison does not include the same Manager arrangement. Random routing is matched on escalation frequency, which is not identical to matching total cost. Runtime AUROC is modest for some backbones (.609 and .575), and for one model initial-token probability outperforms the LDA initial signal (.671 versus .600). A predictor of weak-model failure is not necessarily a predictor of the benefit from switching models.

Appendix A.2 reports task-grouped cross-validation and illustrates why leakage matters: ignoring grouping can produce AUROC .997. Realized escalation rates span roughly 43–64% despite a nominal 50% target. This makes task-disjoint calibration and drift monitoring essential before any routing recommendation.

The ablations report Manager-only 41.15%/$0.1372, escalation 67.94%/$0.4050, and the complete system 73.66%/$0.4117. Yet runtime-only reaches 71.65%/$0.4169, so an added initial gate does not have established incremental benefit in that comparison. The limitations acknowledge that post-handoff shadow effects are not fully causally isolated. “Zero extra forward passes” applies to the runtime signal, not to every initial probe and shadow operation.

Safety distinction. Discarding a pending stronger-model action before execution and asking for reflection is not rolling back an already durable external effect. The theoretical analysis covers an initial single decision under distributional and monotonic-gain assumptions; it is not a sequential safety guarantee. No public executable release or license was verified. Keep LEASH in an offline shadow test until task-disjoint replication, genuinely matched-harness economics, measured latency, authorization preservation, and escalation-benefit calibration are demonstrated.

Independent Analysis. The shared lesson is not “add another guardrail.” It is to preserve the distinction between a proxy, the construct it measures, and the decision it authorizes. A critique score, memory label, difficulty estimate, or latent classifier should enter a decision only with a documented measurement contract and a separately validated operating policy.

The papers differ in their evidentiary strength. P1 directly perturbs prompting and observes paired behavior; P2 includes ablations but does not perfectly isolate all compute differences; P3 estimates conditional associations; W1 evaluates a composite system with incomplete component isolation. Their numbers must not be pooled into a common effect size. Cross-paper agreement on evaluation discipline is a synthesis, not an independent replication of one mechanism.

What is incremental to prior briefings

Recent reports emphasized harness contracts, human-grounded judges, authority gates, and memory provenance. Today's additions are narrower: bidirectional premise validity, memory-content versus compute attribution, and evidence-structure difficulty beyond length. Existing internal evaluation infrastructure, if present, should be extended with these tests rather than duplicated. The current enterprise stack has not been verified, so “extend” is conditional.

Priority method and decision bands

Independent Analysis. Future Enterprise AI Priority Score = .25×strategic/business value + .20×technical leverage + .20×evidence strength + .15×enterprise readiness + .10×feasibility + .10×risk-adjusted differentiation. Every component is scored 0–100. Weighted totals are shown to one decimal solely for arithmetic transparency; differences of a few points are not meaningful. A reasonable change in internal workload, access, or loss assumptions could move most totals by 5–10 points. These are judgment ranges, not statistical confidence intervals.

Readiness means readiness for the proposed bounded next step, not production approval. Bands also apply safety and prerequisite gates; they are not simple score cutoffs. A high evidence-strength score can support deprioritizing an option when that evidence is negative. No build/buy decision presupposes a vendor is already present.

Prototype Soon

1. Bidirectional premise-critique qualification

  • Category: Evaluation & Observability / Safety & Governance.
  • Research signal and supporting paper(s): P1, Table 3 and Appendix B.7: stronger flaw assertions raise detection while sharply reducing valid-query coverage and overall execution success.
  • Evidence label: Enterprise Inference; supporting results are Paper Fact; thresholds below are Independent Analysis.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity addressed: SQL assistants may either invent missing premises or reject correct requests, creating bad analysis and avoidable human rework.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: A paired test harness binds each valid request to a minimally altered flawed request, expert rationale, schema snapshot, and expected clarification/answer behavior. It evaluates both error directions before a prompt/model change is admitted.
  • Likely architectural placement: Applications, evaluation, retrieval/knowledge, and human-review layer; enforcement decisions remain outside the test generator.
  • Expected business value: Quality, productivity, and risk reduction through fewer unsupported analyses and fewer unnecessary escalations.
  • Technical value: Separates detection, explanation, abstention, generation coverage, and execution correctness; catches defense-induced regressions.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: Controlled multi-model preprint with a verified code release, but incomplete data packaging and no enterprise transfer study. Ready for an isolated internal test, not automated blocking.
  • Confidence / horizon / effort: High / Now / S. Confidence concerns the value of testing both directions, not any promised model improvement.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: Approved schemas or synthetic databases, adjudicated pairs, query read-only controls, model/prompt versioning, reviewer rubric, and a baseline measured before tuning.
  • Key risks: Technical—ambiguous gold labels; security—generated SQL or malicious query text; governance—misstated business definitions; privacy—schema and query disclosure; operations—review load; adoption—users bypassing overzealous refusal. Use synthetic data and deny all database writes in the pilot.
  • Build / Buy / Partner recommendation: Build a thin paired-case extension on whichever evaluation system is approved; partner with domain owners for semantic labels. No evidence supports buying a new platform solely for this benchmark.
  • Minimum reversible validation experiment: 100 paired requests across at least four business-intent families, split by schema/intent into development and locked holdout. Compare neutral, conditional, and asserted-flaw prompts offline; retain all candidate outputs for blinded adjudication.
  • Success metrics and stop conditions: Track false acceptance and false rejection separately, correct clarification, EX and EX|GEN, latency, and reviewer minutes. A candidate should reduce flaw acceptance without more than a pre-agreed two-point valid-query correctness loss; require paired uncertainty estimates. Stop on any write attempt, data-boundary violation, or irreconcilable labels above 10% of cases. The small pilot may identify failure patterns without sufficient power to approve a policy.
  • Roadmap trigger: Held-out replication on two schemas plus business-owner acceptance of the two-sided loss trade-off, security review, and a documented human escalation path.
  • Internal context required: Actual SQL use cases, allowable ambiguity, cost of wrong answers versus delays, sensitive fields, database permissions, and ownership of business definitions.

2. Compute-controlled memory contribution test

  • Category: Memory & Context / Evaluation & Observability.
  • Research signal and supporting paper(s): P2, Tables 2, 6, and 8: suggestion controls can match graph memory; Insight uses 1.03× calls but 1.43× ToolSandbox tokens; H-EPM uses 2.72× calls.
  • Evidence label: Enterprise Inference, grounded in Paper Fact; experimental controls are Independent Analysis.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity addressed: A team may pay to maintain complex agent memory when gains arise from more inference rather than useful stored experience.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: A factorial evaluation runner varies memory content and inference budget independently. It records all summary, retrieval, reasoning, and tool costs and compares fixed-budget quality as well as fixed-quality cost.
  • Likely architectural placement: Agent layer, orchestration, evaluation, observability, model gateway, and experiment infrastructure.
  • Expected business value: Cost, reliability, productivity, and speed through evidence-based simplification of orchestration.
  • Technical value: Identifies whether retrieved information contributes beyond retries and longer context; exposes model/workflow interaction rather than declaring one universal memory winner.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: Rich controlled preprint; artifact release not verified and exact cost matching remains an open experiment. Safe to prototype on synthetic workflows.
  • Confidence / horizon / effort: Moderate / Now / M.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: Replayable synthetic tools, task-disjoint splits, complete token/call telemetry, deterministic effect limits, representative workflow classes, and equal permissions in every arm.
  • Key risks: Technical—unequal budgets or task leakage; security—extra calls changing effects; governance—optimizing a proxy score; privacy—sensitive trajectories retained as memory; operations—inference spend; adoption—attachment to existing orchestration. Bound calls and disable real-world side effects in every arm.
  • Build / Buy / Partner recommendation: Build the experimental wrapper; reuse approved tracing and model access if available. Partner with agent owners to reconstruct their real cost boundary. No purchase justified before incremental value is measured.
  • Minimum reversible validation experiment: 100 tasks, at least two workflow classes, two model families, and baseline/graph/insight/no-memory-extra-budget arms. Three repeats per cell, randomized run order, locked task holdout, fixed maximum effects and explicit stop rules.
  • Success metrics and stop conditions: Report task success, invalid calls, total tokens, p95 latency, and cost per correct completion with paired intervals. Advance a memory arm only if it shows a useful fixed-budget improvement or at least 10% total-cost reduction at a pre-agreed quality margin. Stop if gains disappear under budget matching, any permission differs between arms, or the spend cap is reached.
  • Roadmap trigger: A memory-specific improvement replicated across the intended workload strata, with auditable accounting and no safety regression. A negative result can justify simplification instead of a new capability.
  • Internal context required: Existing orchestration topology, model pricing or hardware costs, current retry policy, offline memory-building expense, tool side effects, service-level objectives, and maintenance burden.

3. Evidence-structure long-context qualification

  • Category: RAG & Knowledge / Evaluation & Observability.
  • Research signal and supporting paper(s): P3, Tables 1–2 and Appendix G.1: scope, dispersion, and depth add information beyond length, but held-out predictive improvement is modest and task composition matters.
  • Evidence label: Enterprise Inference; observational associations are Paper Fact, not causal deployment guarantees.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity addressed: Model selection based on maximum context size or an aggregate document-QA score can miss failures on dispersed, multi-step evidence.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: A qualification matrix groups document questions by length, required evidence footprint, dispersion, and derivation depth, then compares answer correctness, support completeness, and cost across models or retrieval settings.
  • Likely architectural placement: Retrieval/knowledge, evaluation, data platform, and human-review layer.
  • Expected business value: Quality, reliability, customer experience, and cost through better-targeted model and retrieval choices.
  • Technical value: Distinguishes capacity limitations from evidence integration failures and reveals weaknesses hidden by task-family averages.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: Detailed observational preprint; no released annotation artifact verified. Ready for a diagnostic pilot, not automatic routing from answer-conditioned labels.
  • Confidence / horizon / effort: Moderate / Now / M.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: Approved source documents, versioned evidence spans, answer keys, double annotation, stable task families, and held-out model outputs.
  • Key risks: Technical—label instability and overfitting; security—document prompt injection; governance—answer leakage into a purported online feature; privacy—document copying; operations—annotation costs; adoption—overcomplex scorecards. Keep annotation offline and data access inherited from the source corpus.
  • Build / Buy / Partner recommendation: Build a lightweight annotation and analysis extension; partner with subject-matter experts. Do not buy a larger-context model based solely on advertised window size or this paper's pooled associations.
  • Minimum reversible validation experiment: Double-annotate 60 approved questions balanced over three document families; reserve a holdout before inspecting model errors. Compare length+task-family baselines with added structure labels, then assess whether the extra diagnoses alter a concrete model/retrieval decision.
  • Success metrics and stop conditions: Measure agreement, annotation minutes, held-out diagnostic stability, error concentration by strata, and decision changes. Continue only if added labels reveal a repeatable, actionable weakness worth the annotation cost. Stop if annotators cannot achieve a practical agreed rubric, structure effects vanish under source-held-out checks, or no decision changes.
  • Roadmap trigger: Stable annotation and out-of-source replication that improves a specified evaluation decision; any online routing proposal requires a separate ex-ante estimator and causal policy test.
  • Internal context required: Document types, access controls, acceptable evidence granularity, answer adjudication, typical context length, reviewer capacity, and the actual model/retrieval choice under consideration.

Prepare Foundations

4. Evidence coverage and derivation annotation service

  • Category: Data & Knowledge Infrastructure / Reasoning & Planning.
  • Research signal and supporting paper(s): P3's answer-conditioned evidence and DAG annotations; P1's distinction between valid premises, missing premises, and unsupported assumptions.
  • Evidence label: Enterprise Inference. The shared service is a design synthesis, not a system validated by either paper.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity addressed: Teams cannot compare evidence-dependent failures if each evaluation stores only a question, answer, and aggregate score.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: A versioned evidence record links question, source snapshot, authorized spans, premise status, alternative legitimate derivations, annotator disagreement, and adjudication history. It separates offline gold annotations from online-accessible information.
  • Likely architectural placement: Data platform, retrieval/knowledge, evaluation, security/governance, and human-review layer.
  • Expected business value: Quality, reliability, productivity, and risk reduction through reuse of defensible evaluation evidence.
  • Technical value: Enables P1 paired semantic tests and P3 structural strata without conflating gold answers, generated explanations, and actual reasoning traces.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: Prerequisite concept grounded in two verified studies; enterprise schema, lifecycle, and economic value unvalidated. Build only the smallest record format needed by the pilots.
  • Confidence / horizon / effort: Moderate / 3–6 months / M.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: Source version IDs, entitlement checks, retention policy, annotation ownership, separation of gold/test/runtime stores, and a documented disagreement workflow.
  • Key risks: Technical—one derivation falsely treated as canonical; security—gold leakage into prompts; governance—unreviewed labels gaining authority; privacy—duplicated source data; operations—stale snapshots; adoption—annotation burden. Prefer references to controlled sources over uncontrolled document copies.
  • Build / Buy / Partner recommendation: Build the minimal schema and adapters; evaluate approved annotation tooling only after the required workflow is known. Partner with knowledge owners on rights and deletion semantics.
  • Minimum reversible validation experiment: Represent the cases from prototypes 1 and 3 in a local schema and round-trip them through an evaluator without a production integration. Record both competing annotations rather than forcing silent consensus.
  • Success metrics and stop conditions: All cases must resolve their source/version and entitlement; no gold content may enter the runtime test context accidentally. Track reuse rate and maintenance minutes. Stop expansion if reconciliation costs exceed reuse benefit or any access mismatch occurs.
  • Roadmap trigger: At least two independent evaluations reuse the same records successfully, with ownership, source-rights approval, deletion tests, and measurable avoided rework.
  • Internal context required: Knowledge ownership, retention obligations, tenant boundaries, source licensing, evaluation tooling, and whether stable source snapshots already exist.

5. Provenance-bound situation-insight registry

  • Category: Memory & Context / Safety & Governance.
  • Research signal and supporting paper(s): P2's situation-level Insight alternative and its heterogeneous gains; P1's evidence that unsupported premises can drive seemingly competent output.
  • Evidence label: Enterprise Inference. Admission and provenance controls are Independent Analysis, not measured benefits of P2.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity addressed: Reusable agent advice can outlive the conditions that made it valid, turn an assumption into a rule, or cross principal boundaries.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: Store a candidate insight with originating trajectories, environment/tool versions, applicability conditions, known failures, owner, expiry, and test results. Retrieval supplies advisory context only; an insight cannot grant tool permissions or suppress termination.
  • Likely architectural placement: Context/memory services, agent layer, retrieval/knowledge, evaluation, identity/authorization, and security/governance.
  • Expected business value: Reliability, productivity, quality, and risk reduction, conditional on the memory-contribution experiment demonstrating useful gains.
  • Technical value: Makes guidance contestable, retractable, and scoped; permits negative evidence and counterexamples rather than encoding every past transition as a future prescription.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: Research concept with no verified production artifact. Foundation only; do not populate a durable enterprise memory from unrestricted live traces.
  • Confidence / horizon / effort: Moderate / 3–6 months / M.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: Prototype 2's incremental-value result, redacted trajectories, access inheritance, source provenance, expiry/revocation, held-out tests, and an owner for each insight family.
  • Key risks: Technical—overgeneralization and stale hints; security—poisoned memory or instruction injection; governance—advice mistaken for policy; privacy—cross-user leakage; operations—retrieval/context cost; adoption—opaque advice undermining trust. Keep policy and authority outside memory.
  • Build / Buy / Partner recommendation: Build a thin admission schema around an approved store if one exists; partner with workflow/security owners. A new memory vendor is not justified without measured retrieval value and an enforceable deletion model.
  • Minimum reversible validation experiment: Create at most 30 synthetic situation insights with source links, counterexamples, and expiry. Run retrieval in shadow and test revocation, tool-version changes, wrong-tenant queries, and malicious source text.
  • Success metrics and stop conditions: Require complete provenance, zero cross-scope retrieval in the test suite, successful expiry/revocation, and demonstrable utility above no-memory controls. Stop if unqualified insights are used as authority, source deletion leaves active advice, or benefits fail budget matching.
  • Roadmap trigger: Measured memory-specific gains plus access, deletion, drift, and poisoning tests pass under an accountable operating owner.
  • Internal context required: Whether memory is shared or personal, data sensitivity, retention rules, available identity model, tool-version cadence, and maintenance budget.

Monitor for Maturity

6. Latent uncertainty handoff and shadow qualification

  • Category: Model & Inference / Agentic AI.
  • Research signal and supporting paper(s): W1 LEASH's composite escalation/shadow system, 19.2% estimated cost reduction in one highlighted comparison, task-grouping leakage warning, and incomplete component isolation.
  • Evidence label: Enterprise Inference with substantial Uncertainty. Reported benchmark outcomes are Paper Fact; enterprise economics are not established.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity addressed: Always using the strongest model can be expensive, while blind escalation can add cost or transfer a task without improving its chance of success.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: An offline-calibrated signal estimates escalation benefit; a gateway shadow policy recommends handoffs while independent policy checks preserve permissions. Post-handoff observation can intercept pending actions, never claim to undo durable effects.
  • Likely architectural placement: Model gateway, orchestration, observability, evaluation, security/governance, and human-review layer.
  • Expected business value: Potential cost, speed, and reliability gains, subject to measured net benefit after all probes and shadow work.
  • Technical value: Adds a candidate early-warning signal and a testable escalation policy; separates failure probability from treatment benefit.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: Single benchmark, estimated pricing, modest classifier discrimination in some settings, no verified executable release. Monitor rather than commit to implementation.
  • Confidence / horizon / effort: Low / 6–12 months / L.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: Authorized hidden-state access, task-disjoint training/calibration/test pools, same-harness always-strong baseline, full latency/hardware accounting, permission-preserving handoff, and rollback-free sandbox actions.
  • Key risks: Technical—distribution shift and weak benefit prediction; security—escalation bypassing capability checks; governance—unvalidated threshold authority; privacy—latent traces leaking task data; operations—extra model hosting and latency; adoption—unexplained handoffs. A human override must remain available for high-impact workflows.
  • Build / Buy / Partner recommendation: Partner with researchers or an approved model/platform provider to establish reproducibility and hidden-state access. Defer procurement and production build; conceptual similarity to a vendor router is not evidence of equivalent behavior.
  • Minimum reversible validation experiment: When artifacts and authorized model access exist, run an offline shadow classifier on task-disjoint synthetic trajectories. Compare random, token-probability, latent, and always-strong policies under the same Manager and action budget; no real escalation executes.
  • Success metrics and stop conditions: Measure calibration, AUROC, escalation treatment benefit, task success, p95 latency, all-in cost, and permission invariance. Require a preregistered quality noninferiority margin and material net cost reduction with uncertainty. Stop on task leakage, no benefit over a simple baseline, cost savings disappearing under full accounting, or any privilege expansion.
  • Roadmap trigger: Independent task-disjoint replication, public/reviewable implementation, same-harness cost-quality evidence, reliable benefit calibration, and security/operations approval.
  • Internal context required: White-box model access, provider restrictions, actual inference economics, acceptable delays, task criticality, handoff semantics, and current gateway capabilities.

Deprioritize for Now

7. Prescriptive next-tool transition router as default

  • Category: Agentic AI / Reasoning & Planning.
  • Research signal and supporting paper(s): P2's negative ToolNet results, suggestion ablations, and oracle failures involving necessity and arguments rather than only tool identity.
  • Evidence label: Independent Analysis supporting a defer decision; the broader enterprise generalization remains an Enterprise Inference.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity addressed: Transition graphs promise simpler planning and smaller tool menus, but can eliminate the correct action or encourage a call when no call is warranted.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: A graph would restrict or strongly prescribe successor tools from historical transitions. This entry deliberately records a capability not recommended as a default; explicit workflow constraints are a different, potentially valid use case.
  • Likely architectural placement: Agent layer and orchestration, with security/governance retaining independent authority.
  • Expected business value: Hypothetical speed and cost benefits; current evidence does not establish positive net value for a general default.
  • Technical value: Potentially smaller action spaces for genuinely deterministic workflows, offset by brittle state and argument handling.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: Substantial negative evidence for the tested variants; not a verdict on all graphs or trained variants. Insufficient basis for a general rollout.
  • Confidence / horizon / effort: Moderate / 12+ months / M. Horizon means reconsider only if evidence changes, not a promised implementation date.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: A narrowly specified workflow with complete state, argument and authorization semantics, measurable baseline, exception handling, and proof that the graph does not exclude valid actions.
  • Key risks: Technical—wrong action exclusion; security—history mistaken for permission; governance—silent process changes; privacy—sensitive transition traces; operations—maintenance under tool drift; adoption—agent loops or unnecessary manual recovery.
  • Build / Buy / Partner recommendation: Defer buy/build for a universal router. If a specific deterministic workflow warrants it, build a narrow formal workflow controller and test it separately rather than claiming a memory graph solves planning.
  • Minimum reversible validation experiment: Only after a concrete use case emerges, compare 50 synthetic exception-rich tasks against an unrestricted but policy-constrained baseline, with matched calls/tokens and a fallback path. No production tools.
  • Success metrics and stop conditions: Measure valid-action exclusion, wrong/no-op calls, exact state success, recovery cost, and total latency. Stop on any authorization regression or systematic exclusion of correct actions. Reconsider only if it outperforms the baseline at fixed cost without those regressions.
  • Roadmap trigger: New replicated evidence for richer state/argument-aware graph guidance or a bounded workflow-specific result that survives negative controls and change tests.
  • Internal context required: Which workflows are truly deterministic, exception frequency, tool drift, current process constraints, and the business cost of forced versus omitted actions.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

Independent Analysis — a 12–24-month option map, not a description of an existing enterprise stack. The highest-leverage change would be to make evidence contracts reusable across applications. The papers do not justify replacing every application with an agent platform or deploying all seven options. They support a common foundation that lets teams test particular enhancements without giving a proxy measurement authority it has not earned.

First 0–6 months: inventory existing capabilities and run the three bounded pilots. Use shared record formats and telemetry only where they solve immediate duplication. Keep production prompts, routing, permissions, and databases unchanged. Establish who adjudicates business validity, owns memory advice, and pays for annotation.

Months 6–12, conditional on evidence: connect accepted paired tests and evidence strata to model/prompt qualification. Add a small provenance-aware insight registry only if memory-specific gains survive cost controls. Adopt source-linked revocation and cost attribution before broadening memory use. Human review should be a measured control with a queue, owner, and service target, not an unbounded fallback.

Months 12–24, conditional on replicated benefit: evaluate policy-controlled routing and workflow-specific guidance under the same evidence contracts. A gateway might use validated online features to choose models or handoffs, while knowledge services expose authorized evidence and the orchestration layer retains effect/termination control. P3's gold-conditioned depth and W1's unvalidated latent score must not become production authority by convenience.

The strategic leverage is composability of evaluation evidence: the same task identities, source versions, effect traces, and cost records can support semantic validity tests, memory ablations, long-context qualification, and routing experiments. The major unresolved internal choices are workload importance, data/tenant boundaries, actual inference economics, infrastructure ownership, reviewer capacity, and acceptable error trade-offs. These choices could materially reorder the backlog.

Implementation priorities and knowledge reinforcement

Immediate sequencing

  1. Assign an evaluation owner, not a deployment owner. Choose one SQL/document workload and one synthetic tool workflow; establish business loss criteria and access boundaries before collecting cases.
  2. Freeze baseline and accounting. Capture model/prompt/tool versions, task splits, all calls/tokens, and effect limits. Without this, an improvement may be a hidden budget or data change.
  3. Run the three pilots independently. Do not combine critique, memory, and routing changes into one treatment. A failed pilot should leave production behavior and permissions untouched.
  4. Review evidence before investing in foundations. Expand the annotation service and insight registry only when more than one validated use needs them. Keep LEASH and general transition prescriptions outside implementation commitments.

Knowledge reinforcement

Recall: Why can a higher DET score be bad news? Because a prompt may teach a model to agree that every request is flawed, increasing rejection of valid requests. Pair positives and negatives.

Recall: Why is 1.03× model calls not evidence of 3% overhead? Longer contexts and responses can raise tokens substantially; P2 reports 1.43× tokens for Insight on ToolSandbox, before full operational accounting.

Recall: Why is a derivation DAG not the model's reasoning trace? LURCH's graph is an answer-conditioned annotation of a defensible derivation, not an observation of the evaluated model's internal computation.

Recall: Why is failure prediction not enough for escalation? The stronger agent may fail too, may cost more without benefit, or may need a different permission boundary. Estimate the treatment benefit, not just the weak agent's risk.

Transfer exercise: Before accepting any new AI component, write three separate sentences: “The measured signal is …”; “The decision it would influence is …”; “The evidence that connects them is …”. If the third sentence is missing, the component belongs in evaluation or watchlist, not in an approval path.
