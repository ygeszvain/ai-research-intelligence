AI Research Intelligence · Evidence before enthusiasm

Evaluation is an interface, not a score.

Fresh evidence shows that safety weakens when cues arrive across time, AI annotators frequently fail task-specific replacement tests, numeric scales can change judge conclusions, and executable agents remain far below reliable autonomy. The enterprise response is a measurement fabric that tests time, disagreement, interface sensitivity, artifacts and behavior distributions before authority.

Executive brief

Three signals that should change evaluation practice

Paper Fact

Safety decays across turns

96→31% Grok

The same five safeguarding cues triggered at least gentle signposting in 96% of concentrated conversations but only 31% when dispersed; every tested model declined significantly.

Paper Fact

Most AI judges failed admission

35.3% pass

Only 71 of 201 judge–dataset pairs passed AltRA-Test with default chain-of-thought. The best prompt per judge raised this to 47.3%, still below half.

Paper Fact

Grounding is necessary, not sufficient

32.8% best selected

Spreadsheet grounding and K=4 selection more than doubled a compact model’s accuracy, yet roughly two-thirds of tasks remained wrong after native recalculation.

Independent Analysis. These papers converge on one design principle: treat model evaluation as a composed decision system. Temporal ordering, annotator disagreement, numerical scale, artifact execution, context and selection policy can each change the result without changing the apparent task.

  1. Primary timestamp, identifier, venue status and license verified.
  2. Full paper reviewed beyond title and abstract.
  3. Headline claims traced to denominators, tables, figures or appendices.
  4. Methods, ablations, uncertainty and failed conditions retained.
  5. Prior work and claimed contribution compared.
  6. Code/data labeled only when a link was verified.
  7. Exact-title anti-duplication checked against the published archive.

  9. Enterprise readiness does not follow from benchmark novelty.
  10. Experiments remain isolated, reversible, measurable and non-destructive.

Deep-paper analysis

Three papers that cleared the full-review gate

Paper Fact

Evaluating safeguarding in multi-turn conversations with AI assistants

Lucy Lu Wang, Anna B. Antoniades, Reuben Binns et al.
Oxford Internet Institute · arXiv:2611.26009v1 · submitted November 19, 2026 12:28 UTC · paper · arXiv non-exclusive distribution license

9 practitioner-designed scenarios21-turn conversations6 models / 5 developers7,400+ conversationskappa 0.93 audit

Contribution and prior-work comparison

Single-turn safety benchmarks place all evidence near the decision. This paper keeps five cues constant but distributes them across 21 turns, testing whether assistants integrate risk over time. Three community mental-health support workers designed nine scenarios spanning depression, psychosis and domestic abuse; two workers independently set expected safeguards. The contribution is the controlled temporal manipulation, not a new safety classifier.

Methodological critique

  • Strength: matched cues, concentrated/dispersed variants, multiple models, repeated runs and a verbatim-cue sensitivity analysis isolate temporal integration.
  • Human grounding: practitioners designed cases and expectations, but all were from one London service; the setting and English phrasing constrain external validity.
  • Judge validation: Claude Sonnet 4.6 graded each reply; the first author audited a stratified n=80 with quadratic-weighted κ=0.93. This supports consistency, not full independence.
  • Production gap: API-only models lacked product system prompts, classifiers, memory, user history and escalation integrations.
  • Interaction gap: the scripted user could not answer follow-up questions, so adaptive clarification and user disengagement were not tested.
  • Safety tradeoff: stronger escalation is not automatically better; false positives, cultural variation and user agency require separate evaluation.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Reproducibility: Moderate. Scenarios and procedures are described, but no verified code repository was established in the reviewed record and proprietary model versions can change. Enterprise readiness: High for evaluation, low for autonomous safeguarding. Use the result to expand test design, not to delegate crisis judgment or escalation.

Paper Fact

AltRA-Test: An Alternative Annotator Test for LLM Judges Without Shared Annotators

Ofir Maron, Rotem Dror, Roi Reichart et al.
Technion, University of Haifa & Google Research · arXiv:2611.26073v1 · November 19, 2026 · paper · CC BY 4.0

20 real datasets18 LLMs5 prompting methods201 judge–dataset pairsB=300 bootstrap

Contribution and prior-work comparison

Prior replacement tests often require the same people to label every item or compare an LLM directly with a resolved gold label. AltRA instead asks whether replacing one human in a randomly resampled panel changes inter-annotator agreement beyond a non-inferiority margin. It is designed for datasets with multiple labels per item but no globally shared annotator.

Methodological critique

  • Statistical contribution: the authors prove asymptotic level control and use paired item resampling, directly addressing unknown annotator identity.
  • Agreement floor: the method is meaningful only when humans share signal; the paper recommends inter-annotator agreement around 0.2 or higher.
  • Margin sensitivity: the 10% increase-in-disagreement allowance is a policy choice. Different harms require predeclared margins and power.
  • Construct boundary: matching human disagreement can preserve human bias, systematic error or underrepresented perspectives.
  • Benchmark breadth: text, image, audio and video improve generality, but 201 pairs are not independent because models and datasets repeat.
  • Artifact status: CC BY licensing is clear; no public implementation link was verified in the reviewed primary record.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Reproducibility: Moderate-high methodologically, moderate operationally. Equations, defaults, simulations and datasets are documented; the absence of a verified code artifact increases reimplementation risk. Enterprise readiness: Moderate-high for internal labeling governance. The gate is useful only with lawful, representative human labels and subgroup analysis.

University of Melbourne research team
arXiv:2611.26069v1 · November 19, 2026 · paper · CC BY 4.0

912 SpreadsheetBench tasks1,660 domain tasks5,925 training tasks3 random seedsQwen3-4B-Thinking

Contribution and prior-work comparison

Spreadsheet agent work commonly optimizes code generation while leaving workbook inspection, API grounding and executable verification implicit. SheetAlign structures the plan (where/from/what/write mode/risks/API needs), focuses workbook observations, retrieves API documentation, isolates generator and reviewer learning, repairs from the original workbook and selects across multiple executions.

Methodological critique

  • Strength: the study evaluates end workbooks after native recalculation rather than accepting agent self-reports or code execution.
  • Ablations: task inspection, API grounding and repair all matter; however, interdependent stages make causal attribution to any single module incomplete.
  • Model/tool scope: one compact open model and Python/openpyxl limit transfer to formulas, macros, Office Scripts, external data and specialized Excel features.
  • Selection weakness: the gap to the K=4 oracle shows reviewer/selector reliability is a first-class unsolved problem.
  • Benchmark risk: benchmark task distributions and exact-match comparators may not capture operational materiality or reviewer time.
  • Artifact status: the paper says code will be released upon publication; it was not available to verify at this review cutoff.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Reproducibility: Moderate-low today. Training/evaluation recipes are detailed and license is open, but the implementation is not yet available. Enterprise readiness: Moderate for read-only shadow trials, low for autonomous writes. Restrict to disposable copies, independent recalculation, hidden checks and human release.

Watchlist

Promising evidence that should not yet drive deployment

Uncertainty

Same Judgement/Different Scores: Numerical Scoring Topology in LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluation

Accepted Findings of EACL 2027 · arXiv:2611.26077 · November 19, 2026 · paper · CC BY 4.0

Signal. Across 17 judge models, 13 numerical topologies, six primary and two additional benchmarks, changing only the score range changed outcomes. A shifted 0.74–1.48 scale reduced Arena-Hard from 97.4 to 89.9, AIME from 70.7 to 65.7 and LiveCodeBench from 85.1 to 79.4. Average disagreement above 10 points was 19.9% on judgment-intensive tasks versus 11.8% on answer-verifiable tasks; above 20 points it was 7.9% versus 3.6%.

Critique. The study holds prompt, candidate output, criteria and judge fixed, which isolates topology. Yet the scales are constructed; zero-shot judges may infer different semantics from endpoints; results pool repeated models/benchmarks; and humans did not regrade every topology. Explicit maximum values reduced disagreement but did not eliminate it. Treat numeric interface design as a sensitivity variable, not proof that one scale is universally correct.

Watch trigger. Replication on pairwise judging, calibrated ordinal rubrics and real enterprise release gates, with human assessment of which topology best preserves the intended construct.

Uncertainty

When Agents Play Differently: Localizing Human–Agent Behavioral Divergence with Conditional Distributional Testing

Microsoft Gaming & KTH · arXiv:2611.26012 · November 19, 2026 · paper · CC BY-NC-SA 4.0

Signal. In a commercial match-3 game, the authors compare about 110,000 human sessions with about 5,000 sessions per agent across 24 context cells. Two process metrics—score per move and cascades per move—had low win-outcome dominance (R² 0.036 and 0.121), supporting analysis beyond wins. The full synthetic pipeline achieved FDR 0.004, precision 0.94 and recall 1.00; a mean-only tree had FDR 0.216, precision 0.31 and recall 0.13.

Critique. Euclidean MMD, 200 permutations, 100 bootstraps, random effects and depth-wise Benjamini–Hochberg correction are thoughtful. But the reference population is observational, context cells are researcher-defined, sparse cells retain uncertainty, and behavior difference is not necessarily harm or cause. Directly optimizing the discovered metrics invites Goodhart effects. The abstract says an implementation is open-source, but a repository URL was not verified in this review.

Watch trigger. Cross-domain replication where localized process divergence predicts independently assessed quality, safety or user-experience outcomes.

Cross-paper synthesis

Five ways an evaluation can change while the model appears unchanged

Time

Safeguarding cues spaced across 21 turns produced materially weaker responses than the same cues concentrated at the end.

Human reference

AltRA shows judge admission depends on the task’s human agreement and can fail a subgroup even when pooled panels pass.

Numeric interface

Equivalent-looking score ranges changed judge outputs and benchmark conclusions, especially for judgment-intensive tasks.

Executable artifact

Spreadsheet code may run while the workbook is wrong; native recalculation and hidden comparators expose that gap.

Behavior distribution

Average outcomes can hide context-specific process differences; conditional distributions show where divergence occurs.

Selection

SheetAlign’s K=4 oracle exceeded its selected result by 6.9–10.6 points, making verifier quality part of agent quality.

Independent Analysis. The operational unit is not “the model.” It is the model plus conversation history, human reference population, scoring interface, tool runtime, selector, verifier and release policy. Model governance that versions only weights or API names is incomplete.

Decision gates

Evidence gate

Locked holdouts, paired conditions, justified margins, sufficient human agreement, subgroup coverage and independent executable checks.

Risk gate

Least privilege, no source overwrite, no silent sensitive memory, lawful labels, human release, rollback and named incident owner.

Economics gate

Cost per verified outcome includes model calls, K candidates, failures, native runtimes, human review, latency and operational support.

Knowledge reinforcement

What to remember

Five durable rules

  1. Safety evidence must be tested across time, not only inside one prompt.
  2. Human-like agreement is task- and subgroup-specific; it is not factual correctness.
  3. A numeric scale is part of an LLM judge’s prompt and can change its conclusion.
  4. Code execution is not artifact correctness; recalculate and compare the native output.
  5. Average success can hide where process behavior diverges across contexts.

Retrieval practice

What was the strongest temporal safety result?

Every model weakened significantly when identical cues were dispersed; Grok’s at-least-gentle signposting fell from 96% to 31%.

What does an AltRA pass mean?

Replacing one human with the LLM did not reduce the selected agreement statistic beyond the predeclared margin. It does not prove truth or fairness.

Why is 98.6% spreadsheet execution not enough?

The selected task accuracy was only 32.8%; runnable code often produced a workbook that failed the official comparator.

Why test score topology?

The same judge, candidate outputs, criteria and prompt can change when the numeric range or endpoints change.

One-sentence synthesis. Before increasing an AI system’s authority, prove that its decision survives changes in time, human reference, numeric interface and context—and that its native artifact passes an independent verifier.
