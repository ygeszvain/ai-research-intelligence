Executive decision

October 18, 2026 · Two deep papers · Two Watchlist papers · Eight enterprise options

Central signal — test what a control actually depends on, not only whether its output looks stable. A guard can appear language-robust because the user-request field contributes nothing to its decision. A dialogue metric can rank synthetic procedural deviations well while being insensitive to an explicit permutation of turns. A successful agent run can conceal a costly recovery tail. These are distinct measurement problems, not one universal failure mechanism.

The best immediate options are paired multilingual guard qualification, request-dependence counterfactual tests, and workflow-fork regression tests. They can be explored without granting an agent more authority. This edition does not recommend a new guard vendor, automatic translation of authorization requests, production interruption by a learned flow detector, or refactoring justified by an assumed token saving.

Important artifact finding: DialFork's linked repository contains only a “Coming Soon” README, despite the paper's release language. AgentPerturb's linked repository is empty. LinguaGuardBench's release was not located. CodeHealth's versioned replication-package metadata and file listing were verified, but the package was not executed. These differences materially affect readiness and scoring.

All proposed capabilities are future options, not approved roadmap commitments. No enterprise stack, vendor access, data entitlement, language mix, risk appetite, or operational maturity is assumed. “Now” means a bounded offline experiment. Adoption requires internal context and separate authorization.

Top 3 Future Bets

  1. Paired multilingual guard qualification — 85.1/100. Why it matters: an English-only test can miss changes in both unsafe-action recall and benign-action blocking. What would change the recommendation: native-speaker-adjudicated internal tests find no material language-conditioned degradation after instruction-level uncertainty and repeated-run noise are accounted for. Next reversible action: create 100 synthetic action/request pairs in the enterprise's actual supported languages, freeze action/history bytes, and run guards in an execution-disabled harness. See enhancement 1 for gates.
  2. Request-dependence counterfactual audit — 83.2/100. Why it matters: robustness to a rewritten request is weak evidence if the request was not needed to classify the action. What would change the recommendation: guards reliably distinguish permission-changing request pairs while remaining invariant to meaning-preserving variants, including with non-redundant histories. Next reversible action: author 40 mock action cases whose correct verdict changes with the principal's authorization; compare full request, omitted request, and equivalent translations. No real action is permitted. See enhancement 2.
  3. Workflow-fork regression suite — 82.1/100. Why it matters: fluent and factually plausible conversations can still omit a required step, pursue the wrong process, or substitute a new goal. What would change the recommendation: process owners cannot define stable valid transitions, or the extra tests fail to find actionable errors beyond existing outcome checks on a held-out sample. Next reversible action: select two low-risk synthetic workflows and manually validate forks at four stages, retaining legitimate exceptions. See enhancement 3.

Deep paper 1 — Language Is a Security Surface for Agent Guards

P1 · Hasan Demirkiran and Jens Ernstberger, Kontext · arXiv:2610.22244v1 · submitted October 15, 2026, 17:19:40 UTC. Primary record · Full paper · HTML full text.

Research question and controlled intervention

Paper Fact. LinguaGuardBench starts with 1,220 labeled tool-invocation steps from 589 TS-Bench AgentDojo trajectories: 868 safe and 352 unsafe, representing 58 unique user instructions. Only the user-request field is translated. The candidate action, history, observations, metadata, and labels remain byte-identical. The five target languages are German, French, Turkish, Swahili, and Amharic. Six English rewriting conditions and a whitespace placebo distinguish language changes from ordinary wording sensitivity and serving noise.

The reported roster comprises 19 guard configurations, including specialized guards, a request-withheld scanner control, and a separately served copy of one model. It is not 19 independent model families. Managed configurations use three repetitions; self-deployed configurations use greedy decoding once. The complete language-by-guard matrix is not rectangular: several partner-hosted models were not run on Swahili or Amharic, and gpt-oss-120b did not complete the translation conditions.

The design is a good diagnostic of a guard's reaction to a particular input-channel intervention. It does not reproduce a naturally multilingual end-to-end agent conversation: the history remains English. Translation equivalence is also an assumption requiring validation, not guaranteed by holding other bytes fixed.

Methodological strengths

Independent Analysis. The single-field intervention, preserved labels, English paraphrase controls, whitespace control, repeated managed calls, and direction-specific FPR/TPR reporting are substantially stronger than a headline multilingual accuracy comparison. Exact slot preservation matters: a translated amount, filename, or authorization scope can alter the correct verdict rather than reveal guard instability. The paper describes two cross-family judges, human review of the initial conditions, back-translation for the low-resource extension, and frozen hashes before inference.

The request-withheld control is particularly valuable. It probes whether a high benchmark score requires the channel that the benchmark purports to test. The appropriate follow-up is an authorization-sensitive counterfactual suite, not simply selecting the most invariant model.

Methodological limits and unresolved claims

  1. The effective intervention unit is closer to 58 instructions than 1,220 independent steps. The paper bootstraps 589 trajectories, while many trajectories share the same rewritten instruction. The authors acknowledge that confidence intervals are likely too narrow. Reanalysis should cluster at the instruction level or use a justified hierarchical resampling scheme. Many guard-language comparisons also warrant a prespecified multiplicity policy; isolated nominal significance is weaker than the table's visual emphasis suggests.
  2. The strongest causal wording exceeds the intervention's reach. The experiment shows that changing the request field changes some verdicts under the supplied histories and translation pipeline. It does not isolate linguistic competence from script, tokenization, translation nuance, or mismatch with English restatements. Amharic is both low-resource and the only non-Latin script tested. Size trends are observational across heterogeneous model families, prompts, and serving stacks—not controlled scaling laws.
  3. Translation validation is incomplete for the most sensitive languages. No author reads Swahili or Amharic. Back-translation and two model judges are helpful but do not replace native-speaker adjudication. Four Amharic rows required repairs, including distinctions involving superlatives and conjunctions. These are consequential meaning changes.
  4. The mechanism is post-hoc and mixed. The paper's abstract emphasizes flips where English reasoning restates the request. Section 4.7 shows a split: some recall-losing guards have larger adjusted effects on restatement-rich steps, while several over-blockers have larger effects on non-restatement steps. “Channel inconsistency” is a plausible explanation for part of the data, not a causal mechanism established for every guard. This briefing does not infer hidden reasoning from a verbal trace.
  5. An invariant guard can fail the intended task. The request-removal result is consistent with redundant history or decisions driven by trajectory cues. It is not direct evidence of comprehension or request-aware authorization. A guard that blocks all actions would also be invariant; absolute error rates and permission-changing contrast pairs are essential.
  6. Adapters change the construct. ShieldAgent, Safiron, and AgentDoG judge trajectory prefixes while the labels are step-level. Safiron's format lacks a native observation channel. Their within-guard flips remain informative, but their absolute FPR/TPR values should not be treated as a clean vendor leaderboard against native step guards.
  7. Readiness remains unproven. No mitigation was evaluated. Translate-then-guard, per-language thresholds, and request canonicalization are proposals, not demonstrated fixes. The release could not be located from the paper and targeted searches, so raw verdict reanalysis, frozen prompts, hashes, and licensing remain unverified.

Internal reporting nuance: the abstract's nine-guard aggregate and §4.3's six-guard/five-general-model aggregates use different populations; specialized guards are then discussed separately. This brief uses exact named-model cells instead of blending those aggregate percentages into a single “small-model effect.”

Prior-work comparison and reproducibility

Paper Fact / source-bounded comparison. P1 places itself after AgentDojo's untrusted-input prompt-injection evaluation, ToolSafe's step-level guarding, Task Shield's task alignment, and multilingual content-safety work. Its incremental contribution is to vary the trusted request's language while holding the action and history fixed, rather than translating the unsafe content itself. The comparison is based on the paper's related-work treatment, not new full reviews of every cited predecessor.

Independent Analysis. The useful transferable method is an invariance test with semantic controls and a separate sensitivity test for meaningful authorization changes. It should preserve request provenance and independent policy enforcement. A model-based guard remains advisory until qualified against the local action classes, languages, losses, and latency budget.

Reproduction gate: obtain a versioned release with materializer, 696 rewrites, verdict rows, prompts, adapters, licenses, and exact model IDs; verify hashes; reproduce selected raw and placebo-adjusted cells; rerun instruction-clustered intervals; then add native-adjudicated, non-redundant, authorization-changing cases. Missing files are not evidence of fraud or permanent unavailability, but they prevent verified reproduction today.

Deep paper 2 — DialFork

P2 · Sergio Burdisso, Esaú Villatoro-Tello, Dairazalia Sánchez-Cortés, Yanis Labrak, Jordi Luque, Milos Cernak, and Petr Motlicek · arXiv:2610.21561v1 · submitted October 15, 2026, 09:18:32 UTC. Primary record · Full paper · Linked repository.

What is actually being measured?

Paper Fact. DialFork measures procedural deviation in raw conversations. It creates 2,134 conformant synthetic dialogues from STAR's workflow and role-play metadata, then 16 variants per dialogue: four corruption families at four fork positions. The total is 2,134 × 17 = 36,278 dialogues, including 34,144 forks. Both simulated participants use Gemma4-31B. The paper reports about 750k turns, 10.4 million words, and 33 GPU-days of generation.

The corruption families are workflow identity replacement, step-selection-policy corruption, goal replacement, and conflicting auxiliary constraints. A fork is created by replaying the scenario with a fixed seed and injecting an instruction before a chosen system turn. Fixed seeding can still permit minor prefix rewordings. The generator is deliberately instructed to enact the corruption: this is a benchmark of detectors and distance metrics, not an estimate of how often deployed agents spontaneously fail or resist an attacker.

Evaluation level Input and target Proposed method What it cannot establish
Set-to-set Two dialogue sets; recover the ordering of four fork-depth groups FlowKL on flow-likelihood distributions No individual action authorization or causal attribution of live drift
Dialogue-to-dialogue A conformant dialogue and its fork variants; rank four distances FlowScore turn-level matching; FlowDTW alignment comparison No production guarantee that a suitable conformant twin exists
Turn-level streaming Only the prefix observed so far plus conformant references N-gram transition/surprisal alarms over clustered turn embeddings No access to future context; no demonstrated safe interruption policy
Turn-level retrospective Full transcript, optionally the known workflow Zero-shot LLM localization Cannot be compared as if it were a causal, real-time detector

FlowKL induces an embedding-cluster transition graph, computes a geometric mean of smoothed transition probabilities, and compares the resulting reference/target likelihood distributions by KL divergence. FlowScore uses a BERTScore-like best-match precision/recall construction at the turn level. With the independently embedded turns used in this paper, FlowScore does not explicitly encode turn order. The authors say contextualized turn encoders are future work. A benchmark about procedure does not automatically make every successful metric an order-sensitive verifier.

Human evidence and methodological critique

Independent Analysis — strengths. The benchmark separates content from process, provides controlled perturbation types and locations, compares several encoder families, includes trivial controls, supplies prompts and generation details, and uses human judgments rather than relying only on a model judge. Blind conformance ratings precede the corruption cue. Foils test acquiescence. Within-family significance tests are Holm-corrected, and Appendix J describes paired bootstraps.

Independent Analysis — limitations that change the decision:

  • Synthetic fork depth is a proxy for severity. Earlier intervention usually means a longer corrupted suffix, but the clinical or operational importance of a skipped step need not scale with suffix length. Conflicting constraints are a notable exception: human conformance varies little with fork location. Across the three workflow-breaking types, the within-quartet human τb is .64 over 23 quartets; including all four types gives .45 over 33 quartets. Neither number is a universal human agreement rate.
  • The human audit does not make every label exact. True injected behaviors are confirmed fully or partly in 91% of evaluated forks; foil behaviors are confirmed in 8% of conformant dialogues and 45% of already-corrupted dialogues. The categories can overlap in observable behavior. Prefix conformance is 86% “yes” plus 9% “mostly,” not 95% perfectly clean prefixes. Onset labels and real visible deviations can differ.
  • Metric success is not procedural understanding. FlowScore's independent-turn representation lacks explicit order. Permuting the same turn embeddings preserves its best-match construction. An enterprise should add same-content/different-order negatives and legitimate reordered exceptions before using it for procedure-sensitive retrieval or alarms.
  • Matched-source evaluation is easier to structure than real incident triage. Each fork has a known conformant source. Production logs may contain multiple valid paths, different customer goals, and no unique reference transcript. The shared-prefix control is useful, but does not demonstrate generalization to unrelated natural conversations or new generators.
  • Uncertainty units deserve care. The 8,536 Task-2 units reuse 2,134 source dialogues across corruption families. A source- and task-clustered resampling analysis would better test generalization than treating every pair as independent. Table 7's per-unit mean differences do not exactly equal every subtraction of Table 3's displayed aggregates; raw data and aggregation code are needed to reconcile weighting rather than silently mixing them.
  • Streaming calibration is not an independent deployment validation. The paper calibrates thresholds from conformant dialogue extrema at nominal fifth/95th percentiles. A clear held-out enterprise calibration/test split and future-model drift test are still required. The construction contains single corruptions, one English source corpus, and one generator; compound failures and real customer exceptions remain outside the demonstrated result.
  • Detection is not safe control. A 4.61-turn mean localization error can arrive after an irreversible action. Even an early text alarm cannot replace effect-level authorization, idempotency, or an independent stop mechanism. This brief does not recommend blocking live customer workflows with these thresholds.

Prior work and artifact status

Source-bounded prior-work comparison. The paper distinguishes itself from factuality-oriented dialogue benchmarks such as DiaHalu and FaithDial, and from process-following work that needs action/intent/slot annotations. It builds on STAR's explicit workflows, Dialog2Flow embeddings, BERTScore-like matching, DTW, and existing distribution distances FBD/PRD. The novel package is a controlled procedural-deviation benchmark across set, dialogue, and onset granularity—not a claim that workflow conformance itself is a new problem.

Verified artifact state, October 18: GitHub's contents API lists only a 23-byte README in idiap/dialfork; the public page says “Coming Soon.” No executable pipeline, dataset, annotation tool, judgments, or license file was verified at that repository. The paper states MIT licensing, but that statement must not be promoted to a verified license grant for files that have not been retrieved. The source corpus and model checkpoints may carry separate terms.

Enterprise Inference. The construction principle is ready for a small, manually adjudicated internal test. Reusing the authors' benchmark or detector is not currently verified as reproducible. The near-term recommendation is to measure procedural failures offline, not to install a new autonomous process enforcer.

W2 — CodeHealth predicts agentic review token cost

Lo Gullstrand Heander, Markus Borg, Emma Söderberg, and Christofer Rydénfält · arXiv:2610.21914v1 · October 15, 2026, 14:00:33 UTC. Primary record · Paper · Versioned replication record · Record API. Review scope: abstract, study framing, principal results, selected sensitivity/size analyses, and disclosures; not the full 32-page methodological audit.

Paper Fact. A preregistered observational study samples 465 agent-authored pull requests from 246 repositories. A frozen pipeline has four review agents and one fix agent, evaluated with three LLMs. Separate mixed models of log token cost adjust for PR footprint, language, and repository. The completed samples differ: 453, 461, and 453 PRs by model. Table 8 / §4.2 reports estimated token reductions per CodeHealth point of 6.1% [3.4, 8.6], 4.8% [1.4, 8.1], and 3.5% [0.1, 6.8], respectively. These are conditional associations, not the observed effect of raising a code-quality score.

Crucial counterevidence: adjusting for the size of the scored files absorbs the CodeHealth association for every model; the adjusted intervals include zero. CodeHealth and log file size correlate approximately −.71 to −.73. The authors interpret a file-size pathway, but an observational refit alone does not establish causal mediation. Removing Qwen's 103 PRs with non-complete sessions attenuates its coefficient and makes its interval include zero; this is a selected sensitivity sample, not proof of no association. Review correctness and fix quality were not evaluated. Token counts and comment counts are not adequate measures of useful engineering output.

Verified artifact metadata: the concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22127920 resolves through the API to version 10.5281/zenodo.23392202, published October 15. The record lists an open CC-BY-4.0 replication package, agentic-review-codehealth-20261015T100248Z.tar.gz, size 100,459,710 bytes, with an MD5 checksum. This verifies the record and listed package, not its contents, runnability, underlying repository permissions, model terms, or the CodeHealth service's commercial entitlement. The archive was not downloaded or executed. The paper discloses a coauthor's employment by CodeScene, the metric vendor; that is relevant context, not disqualifying evidence.

Enterprise Inference: instrument reviewed-file size, agent turns, cached/uncached tokens, and validated defects before purchasing a quality metric or funding refactoring for token savings. Monitor trigger: a controlled, behavior-preserving refactoring experiment that improves total useful-review cost without degrading defect recall or increasing engineer effort. Cross-paper synthesis

Four distinctions that should remain separate

Apparent success What the evidence actually tests Missing enterprise proof Consequence
Guard verdict unchanged across languages P1's fixed-history, translated-request classification Correct response when authorization meaning genuinely changes Test invariance and required sensitivity
Dialogue metric ranks forks correctly P2's controlled synthetic source/fork ordering Natural procedural violations, valid exceptions, explicit turn-order sensitivity Validate against process-owner labels and order controls

Independent Analysis. The common architecture opportunity is not a universal guard. It is a stronger evaluation contract: identify the input that should matter, the changes that should not matter, the outcome that should change, and the unit at which uncertainty is calculated. Preserve those distinctions in versioned evidence, rather than averaging them into a single benchmark score.

P1 and P2 are complementary, not replications. One varies a trusted request while holding a tool step fixed; the other deliberately changes the procedure followed by a dialogue. W1 changes input integrity during execution; W2 observes naturally different code structures. Combining their effect sizes would be invalid. The recommendation to share replay, provenance, adjudication, and telemetry infrastructure is Enterprise Inference, not an architecture evaluated by any paper.

1. Paired multilingual guard qualification

  • Category: AI Security.
  • Research signal and supporting paper(s): P1, Tables 1–3 and §§3–4: meaning-preserving translations can change guard operating points beyond a whitespace noise floor; English paraphrases are an informative control.
  • Evidence label: Enterprise Inference, grounded in Paper Fact. Priority: 85.1; components B93/T90/E78/R78/F85/D80.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity addressed: a guard qualified only on English can block valid customer requests or miss unsafe actions in supported languages.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: a release-qualification job replays fixed action/history cases under approved equivalent requests, English rewrites, whitespace controls, and repeated calls; it reports language-specific recall, false-positive rates, and adjusted flips rather than one average.
  • Likely architectural placement: evaluation, model gateway, security/governance; initially outside the runtime decision path.
  • Expected business value: risk reduction, reliability, customer experience, quality.
  • Technical value: detects operating-point drift attributable to an input transformation and distinguishes it from nondeterminism.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: one controlled study with meaningful controls, but instruction clustering and native-language validation gaps; locally authored offline testing is feasible without the unretrieved release. Production thresholds remain unqualified.
  • Confidence / horizon / effort: Moderate / Now / M.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: supported-language inventory, synthetic or approved step cases, native-language reviewers, explicit action labels, stable guard interfaces, budget owner, repeatable serving configuration.
  • Key risks: translation changes scope; benchmark labels are wrong; repeated steps inflate precision; sensitive requests leave the boundary; small language groups are misrepresented; excessive blocking causes users to bypass controls; tests become a model-selection shortcut without an actual risk model.
  • Build / Buy / Partner recommendation: Build the internal test contract; Partner with language specialists for adjudication. No evidence supports buying a particular guard based on this study's ranking.
  • Minimum reversible validation experiment: 100 distinct synthetic instructions, paired with safe/unsafe mock actions and actual supported-language variants; three repeated calls per condition where feasible; no tool execution. Freeze labels and retain original requests. Treat this as a diagnostic pilot, not proof of rare-event safety.
  • Success metrics and stop conditions: every admitted variant passes scope/slot adjudication; compare unsafe-action recall and benign-block rate by language with instruction-clustered intervals. An illustrative review trigger is a >2-pp adverse shift, but size the later confirmatory test to the internal loss threshold. Stop on any meaning-changing variant, privacy leakage, unbounded retries, or a critical unsafe allow attributable to the changed request.
  • Roadmap trigger: repeatable, actionable language-conditioned errors on a representative held-out corpus and a named owner willing to maintain the suite; if no material problem appears, retain a small regression set rather than building a large new service.
  • Internal context required: real language mix, high-consequence action classes, tolerated false blocks, model/guard contracts, and whether requests may be processed by external providers.

2. Request-dependence counterfactual audit

  • Category: Evaluation & Observability.
  • Research signal and supporting paper(s): P1 §4.5: the full-request guard and request-withheld scanner make identical original-condition decisions on 3,660 paired verdicts. High invariance alone does not establish request-dependent authorization.
  • Evidence label: Enterprise Inference, with Independent Analysis of the control's implication. Priority: 83.2; B90/T91/E74/R73/F82/D85.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity addressed: a guard may score well from attack-looking trajectory cues while failing to distinguish an authorized operation from the same operation under a different principal request.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: a counterfactual test library holds the mock action fixed, varies authorization-relevant request facts, and separately tests equivalent wording. Request omission and history-restatement removal are diagnostic ablations, never production input-removal policies.
  • Likely architectural placement: evaluation, security/governance, human-review layer.
  • Expected business value: risk reduction, reliability, quality.
  • Technical value: separates desirable invariance from necessary sensitivity; exposes benchmarks that do not exercise the intended security input.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: direct ablation evidence from one benchmark, but the proposed permission-changing suite was not evaluated in P1. Feasible as a small expert-authored experiment; no validated automated authorization replacement.
  • Confidence / horizon / effort: Moderate / Now / M.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: policy-owner labels, principal/resource/action definitions, synthetic histories without accidental intent duplication, access to guard outputs, and a separate deterministic authorization baseline.
  • Key risks: counterfactuals become internally inconsistent; removing history changes more than one construct; a model guesses desired labels from wording artifacts; logs expose sensitive permissions; a convincing test result is mistaken for comprehensive authorization coverage; reviewers disagree about delegation scope.
  • Build / Buy / Partner recommendation: Build a domain-specific test set with internal security owners. A generic purchased benchmark cannot define the organization's real permission semantics.
  • Minimum reversible validation experiment: 40 independently adjudicated mock cases with at least two authorization states each. Compare full request, omitted request, equivalent translation, and a separately controlled non-restating history. Keep all effects simulated.
  • Success metrics and stop conditions: require correct distinction between opposite-permission pairs, stability on equivalent variants, and an audit trail for disagreements. Zero critical unsafe allows is a pilot gate, not a confidence guarantee; compute sample-size needs for a larger test. Stop if experts cannot agree labels or if benchmark cues, not permission semantics, explain the apparent gain.
  • Roadmap trigger: the suite reveals failures missed by ordinary malicious-content tests and a tested change improves those failures without excess benign blocking on held-out cases.
  • Internal context required: the boundary between user intent, identity-derived entitlement, business policy, and delegated authority; these may not be represented by the same text field.

3. Workflow-fork regression suite

  • Category: Human-AI Workflow.
  • Research signal and supporting paper(s): P2 §§3–6 and Appendix N: controlled workflow, step-policy, goal, and conflicting-constraint forks expose process deviation that can coexist with plausible content.
  • Evidence label: Enterprise Inference. Priority: 82.1; B90/T88/E76/R72/F80/D80.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity addressed: conversation quality tests can miss skipped verification, repeated completed steps, wrong-workflow selection, and apparently helpful goal substitution.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: process owners define required transitions and legitimate branches; a test generator creates matched clean/forked dialogues, with human-confirmed visible onset and valid exceptions. Models and metrics are assessed on both process and outcome, including turn-permutation controls.
  • Likely architectural placement: applications, developer platform, evaluation, human-review layer.
  • Expected business value: quality, reliability, productivity, customer experience.
  • Technical value: localizes process failures and enables regression testing at several workflow stages without changing live agents.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: controlled synthetic evidence and partial human validation; author code/data unavailable at the linked repository. A small manually constructed suite is ready to attempt; benchmark transfer is not established.
  • Confidence / horizon / effort: Moderate / Now / M.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: two bounded low-risk workflows, owner-approved process definitions, exceptions and completion criteria, synthetic transcripts, independent adjudicators, and held-out source scenarios.

Manually approve all usable forks, include same-content/reordered-step negatives, and reserve source scenarios for testing rather than splitting sibling forks across train/test.
  • Success metrics and stop conditions: measure violation recall, valid-exception false alarms, onset error relative to the first visible deviation, reviewer agreement, and incremental defects found beyond outcome-only tests. Stop if >10% of labels remain disputed after adjudication or if a metric's advantage disappears on source-disjoint/order controls. Thresholds are proposed pilot gates, not paper results.
  • Roadmap trigger: process owners confirm that newly detected failures are material and fixable, and results replicate on a second generator or approved natural-log sample without unacceptable false alarms.
  • Internal context required: which steps are mandatory, which can reorder, how exceptions are authorized, and what customer or employee costs a false process violation would create.

5. Input-channel stress and recovery-cost harness

  • Category: Evaluation & Observability.
  • Research signal and supporting paper(s): W1's 4×3 perturbation taxonomy and single-model tail-cost finding; P1's paired controls provide a complementary model for isolating transformations.
  • Evidence label: Enterprise Inference. Priority: 77.1; B84/T88/E70/R65/F72/D75.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity addressed: an agent can pass clean tests yet loop, guess interfaces, or consume excessive context when a procedure, memory, user request, or tool description is incomplete or wrong.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: a sandbox injects one validated input-channel defect at a time into mock workflows; a replay runner measures task outcome, clarification, safe abandonment, token distributions, retries, timeouts, and total cost per successful task including failed runs.
  • Likely architectural placement: orchestration, evaluation, observability, infrastructure.
  • Expected business value: cost, reliability, speed, risk reduction.
  • Technical value: reveals recovery tails and channel-specific brittleness while separating quality from extra inference effort.

  • Confidence / horizon / effort: Moderate / 3–6 months / M.

  • Internal context required: normal failure modes, valid clarification channels, deadlines, token prices, caching, retry semantics, and the business cost of safe abandonment versus delayed completion.

6. Shadow procedural-deviation monitor

  • Category: Agentic AI.
  • Research signal and supporting paper(s): P2's streaming transition detectors reach 93.82% hallucinated-dialogue coverage with 4.61-turn MAE and 5.58% conformant coverage in the cited configuration; retrospective workflow access can raise false alarms sharply.
  • Evidence label: Enterprise Inference. Priority: 70.3; B85/T85/E65/R40/F55/D75.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity addressed: process regressions may be noticed only after a customer or operator complains.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: a shadow-only detector scores permitted conversation prefixes against versioned workflow references and sends sampled cases to reviewers. It has no ability to block, authorize, edit memory, or terminate a production task during validation.
  • Likely architectural placement: observability, human-review layer; potentially orchestration only after separate qualification.
  • Expected business value: quality, reliability, customer experience, risk reduction.
  • Technical value: supplies process-oriented drift signals distinct from factuality and final-answer metrics.
  • Evidence maturity and enterprise readiness: synthetic English evidence, unretrieved implementation, uncertain held-out calibration and real-world false-alarm cost. Monitor, not deploy.
  • Confidence / horizon / effort: Low / 6–12 months / L.
  • Dependencies and prerequisites: enhancements 3–4, released or independently validated detector, process-owner labels, natural-log permissions, alert ownership, source-disjoint calibration, and a known pre-effect deadline.
  • Key risks: detecting after the action; excessive false alarms; language and domain drift; embedding clusters encode sensitive attributes; raw conversations expose private data; legitimate exception paths are misclassified; operators become over-reliant or ignore alerts.
  • Build / Buy / Partner recommendation: Partner with research/process experts for validation; Build only a small shadow adapter. Do not Buy a live control on the strength of these benchmark numbers.
  • Minimum reversible validation experiment: replay 200 approved synthetic or de-identified transcripts offline, with visible-onset labels and no future turns exposed to the streaming detector. Compare with simple rule-based process checks at the same false-alarm budget.

Knowledge reinforcement

Invariance versus sensitivity. A guard should ignore harmless surface changes but respond when authorization meaning changes. Test both; either alone can reward a trivial or irrelevant decision rule.

Flip rate versus harm. A changed verdict may correct an earlier error. Always pair instability with unsafe-action recall, benign-block rate, absolute operating point, and the business loss of each error.

Ranking versus verification. A high Kendall correlation on synthetic forks says a metric recovers an ordering. It does not prove exact process compliance, explicit turn-order sensitivity, or prevention of consequential actions.

Unit of evidence. Hundreds of rewrites or thousands of steps can share a few dozen instructions. Confidence calculations should respect the shared source, not only the number of rows.

Correlation versus intervention value. The CodeHealth association motivates a controlled experiment; it does not establish that raising the score lowers cost, nor that lower token use preserves review quality.

Retrieval practice: Why is 100% agreement after removing a request a warning as well as a robustness result? Because the benchmark may not require request-dependent decisions. Why does a 5.58% benign-dialogue alarm rate matter? Because operational review cost and user disruption can dominate a favorable composite. Why retain failed and timed-out runs in cost reporting? Because otherwise the most expensive or least reliable behavior can disappear from the denominator.

Connection to prior coverage: yesterday's distinction between a useful signal and a justified decision now becomes a concrete test design: preserve inputs that should not matter, change the meaning that must matter, and inspect the full cost and error tradeoff before giving the signal control authority. This is a new evidence contribution, not a restatement of yesterday's paper selections.
