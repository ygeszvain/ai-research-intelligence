Executive intelligence

Measurement suitability is the first control

Routing

Oracle headroom does not prove routeability. Ties, score granularity, portfolio identity and weak predictors can erase apparent opportunity.

Monitoring

A target monitor’s measured false-negative rate moved from at most 0.5% to 23.0% when only the attack-generation policy changed.

Verification

Evidence certificates outperform scalar judging in one benchmark, but they cost far more time and still inherit model-dependent decomposition.

Decision implication · Independent Analysis. Before improving a router, monitor or verifier, establish that its input signal, adversarial policy and evidence substrate support the decision being claimed.

Deep-paper analysis

Three controls, three distinct evidence traps

Paper Fact

Attack Policy Selection can Mislead Monitor Evaluators

Charlie Griffin et al. · arXiv:2611.34934v1 · submitted Nov 25, 2026 18:59 UTC · CC BY 4.0

0.5→20.8%RL attack-policy FNR0.3→23.0%transcript-edit FNR30control tasks2,000bootstrap replicates

Question and design. How much does attack-policy choice alter a monitor evaluation? The authors hold the target monitor and infrastructure-as-code scenario fixed: an ex-production OpenAI GPT-5.4 monitor judges full Qwen 3.8 27B agent transcripts. Attacks are developed against proxy monitors using a basic prompt, optimized prompt, reinforcement learning or transcript editing.

Quantitative claim trace. Basic prompting yields ≤0.5% target false negatives; optimized prompting reaches 6.4%. RL rises from 0.5% to 20.8%, while transcript editing rises from 0.3% to 18.7% after 50 edits and 23.0% after 100. The paper uses 95% hierarchical bootstrap intervals with 2,000 replicates.

Methodological critique. The result is compelling as an identification warning, but not a deployment FNR. Attacks are off-policy and built only against proxies; the setting is safe and controlled; attack prompts are removed; “side task” artifacts may influence the monitor. Legitimacy—whether an attack resembles plausible deployment behavior—cannot be inferred from transfer alone.

Prior work comparison. Unlike papers that compare monitors on a fixed attack set, this work treats the attack generator itself as a confounder and shows that stronger proxy optimization can transfer sharply to the target.

Artifacts. No public code artifact was verified. The paper is CC BY 4.0.

Reproducibility. Moderate: attack procedures, checkpoints and bootstrap method are described, but the ex-production monitor is not reproducible.

Enterprise readiness. High as a red-team design principle; low as assurance evidence. Internal threat model, attacker capability, task legitimacy and target isolation are mandatory.

Paper Fact

Routeability Before Routing: Routeability Audit Protocol (RAP) and RouteabilityBench for Audited LLM Model Selection

Hannah Kirk et al. · arXiv:2611.34825v1 · NeurIPS 2026 · CC BY 4.0

66LLMs2,987score rows−0.324IFEval gap closure1/4benchmarks with positive full-router gap closure

Question and design. RAP asks whether a benchmark is routeable before optimizing a router. Across Omni-MATH, GPQA, MMLU-Pro and IFEval, the authors audit single-best versus virtual-best headroom, ties, score granularity, portfolios, predictors, clusters and statistical corrections.

Quantitative claim trace. Headroom ranges from .045 to .200, yet median tied-best models range from 27.3 to 49.3. Full-portfolio baseline gap closure is negative on GPQA (−.135±.126), MMLU-Pro (−.147±.191) and IFEval (−.324±.477), positive only on Omni-MATH (.081±.089). Under a common 66-model oracle, routed regret worsens on three benchmarks and improves only on Omni-MATH (−.0164).

Inference audit. A forced-versus-single-best Omni claim has CI [−.0251,−.0020] but Holm p=.0912 and is downgraded. IFEval ExtraTrees versus Ridge survives Holm at .0126; Omni prompt clusters versus global survives at .014. An LLM-medoid advantage of −.0224 reverses to +.0800 under the common oracle.

Methodological critique. Frozen 30-seed splits, train-only transformations, paired Wilcoxon, Holm correction and prompt-cluster bootstrap/sign-flip tests are strong. Yet four single-turn public benchmarks cannot establish enterprise routeability; parameter count is only a cost proxy, contamination remains possible and no headline survives the most conservative family correction.

Prior work comparison. RAP reframes routing from “how much oracle headroom exists?” to “is winner identity learnable from pre-inference features under a fair portfolio and comparator?”

Artifacts. Reviewer-runnable frozen evidence is described, but no verified public production router or feature pipeline was identified.

Reproducibility. High for reported audits; moderate for router refitting.

Enterprise readiness. High as an audit protocol; unproven for deployment routing. Requires internal tasks, costs, model identities and safety constraints.

Score. Novelty 4.5 · Methods 4.8 · Evidence 4.7 · Reproducibility 4.0 · Enterprise 4.8.

Paper Fact

Agent-as-a-Verifier: From Scores to Evidence for General Verification

Yuxuan Sun et al. · arXiv:2611.34132v1 · NeurIPS 2026 Workshop “Who Verifies the Agents?” · arXiv license

80/89offline selection30/36mixed-pool selection11/12FrontierMath online≈10×in-loop wall time

Question and design. Can a verifier emit evidence rather than a score? A deterministic orchestrator coordinates six stateless LLM roles to extract a claim DAG, compile obligations, propose/run/review evidence and fold a verdict. The final certificate records provenance, artifacts and unresolved residue; no role directly awards the reward.

Quantitative claim trace. On 445 Terminal-Bench 2.1 traces (89 tasks, five per task), same-model text judging selects 74/89 overall and 24/36 mixed pass/fail pools; the agent verifier selects 80/89 and 30/36. Online, reported success moves 8→11/12 FrontierMath, 63→73/87 Terminal-Bench and 37→45/50 CyberGym. One reward-hack case is demonstrated with an executable counterexample after 4,083 arrays.

Methodological critique. Role separation and deterministic folding improve auditability, while executable evidence is a meaningful advance over prose rationales. Evidence is still model-proposed and model-reviewed. Offline results use one terminal suite, candidate pool and configuration; online tasks run once per arm, with FrontierMath n=12. Certification takes tens of minutes and in-loop execution is roughly an order of magnitude slower.

Prior work comparison. The work moves beyond LLM-as-judge and reported log-probability rewards by making verdicts inspectable and replayable, but it is not an end-to-end proof system.

Artifacts. Public General-Verifier repository verified; Apache 2.0 license verified.

Reproducibility. Moderate-high: open implementation and explicit orchestration; benchmark environments and model access still matter.

Enterprise readiness. Moderate in offline/shadow review; low for automatic authorization. Secure tools, evidence provenance and cost limits are prerequisites.

How Much Do Coding Agents Over-Edit, and Why? An Empirical Study of Unneeded Edits in Repository-Level Agent Patches

arXiv:2611.34875v1 · 7,660 resolved Copilot CLI trajectories · 18 models

Dynamic replay attribution removes each patch unit and reruns tests. On SWE-bench, extra edits are mainly tests (61%) and docs (13%); on DeepSWE they are mainly code (58%). Eleven percent of agent code units are unneeded, but 89% change behavior. Test-based pruning reaches 67% recall and only 46% precision; self-audit reaches 36% recall and 66% precision.

Why Watchlist. Successful-only trajectories, oracle dependence, single-unit reverts, one harness, Python benchmarks and major DeepSWE replay gaps weaken causal and enterprise claims. No public artifact was verified.

Trigger. Open artifact, interaction-aware replay, temporal repositories, failures included and blinded maintainer validation.

Uncertainty

When to Trust a PFN: Detecting Harmful Shift in Tabular Foundation Models

arXiv:2611.34767v1 · NeurIPS 2026 · 14 datasets · two PFNs

CALD briefly adapts a decoder to maximize disagreement on a new unlabeled batch while preserving anchor accuracy, then applies a one-sided conformal test to anchor loss. Harmful-shift TPR is often .99–1.00 and improves harm specificity over 15 generic post-hoc baselines, but benign false alarms remain dataset- and model-dependent.

Why Watchlist. The scope is tabular PFNs; label, concept, open-set, adversarial and engineering shifts are excluded. Conformal calibration relies on exchangeable in-distribution anchors.

Artifacts. Public CALD repository and MIT license verified.

Trigger. Prospective shadow results on a second model family with stable benign FPR and an explicit response policy.

Knowledge reinforcement

Five rules to carry forward

  1. Oracle headroom is necessary but does not prove task-level routeability.
  2. A monitor score is inseparable from the policy that generated its attacks.
  3. Evidence certificates improve auditability but do not eliminate model-dependent judgment.
  4. Common oracles and frozen comparators prevent portfolio cherry-picking.
  5. Production authority requires prospective evidence, named humans and rollback.

Retrieval practice

Why can a higher monitor FNR be a better evaluation?

Because a stronger, legitimate attack policy may expose failures hidden by a weak generator; the number is meaningful only with attack provenance.

Why can virtual-best headroom fail to produce a useful router?

Winner identity may be tied, coarsely scored or unpredictable from features available before inference.

What makes a certificate more useful than a score?

It exposes claims, obligations, evidence, provenance, artifacts and unresolved residue for replay and human review.

Why is CALD Watchlist?

It is promising for two tabular PFNs but excludes important shift types and still raises benign false alarms.

One-sentence synthesis. Audit whether the decision is learnable, attackable and evidentially reviewable before optimizing the system that acts on it.
