Evidence policy. Paper Fact means directly reported by a paper or a verified first-party artifact. Independent Analysis is this briefing's synthesis, calculation, or critique. Reported model names, dates, metrics, licenses, and configurations are preserved as stated. Benchmark performance is not production readiness. Every proposed experiment is isolated, reversible, measurable, and non-destructive.

Executive signal

A score is interpretable only when the evaluation observes the whole deployed surface, counts the opportunities the system consumed, and verifies the ground truth it uses.

The three deep papers expose different breaks in the same measurement chain. K-Bench finds secrets outside the final-answer channel and distinguishes suppression from agent collapse. VRL-Bench shows that failure-derived memory can underperform a memory-free retry unless complete-trial opportunities are matched. Expert re-grading of physics benchmarks shows that apparent model failures can instead be benchmark or grader failures. (K-Bench, VRL-Bench, physics audit)

Decision posture. Prototype a whole-trajectory evaluation gate, a finite-opportunity retry harness, and a benchmark-ground-truth audit workflow. Build the shared substrate inventory, immutable opportunity ledger, evaluator challenge set, and human-adjudication path before allowing any evaluation to authorize deletion, autonomous retries, or production promotion. Do not treat refusal as verified unlearning, extra attempts as learning, corrected benchmark scores as vendor rankings, or a benchmark score as a production-readiness certificate.

Top 3 Future Bets

  1. Whole-system observability and utility gate — 92.5/100. Why it matters: a model can hide a target in tool output, reasoning text, retrieval, or summary even when the answer looks clean; a low leak rate can also be caused by a broken agent. Evidence that would change the recommendation: representative internal tests showing answer-only evaluation catches every material disclosure and agent collapse without additional telemetry. Next reversible action: replay 100 synthetic secrets through one non-production agent and compare answer-only scoring with a union over all authorized observable channels plus retain and stability checks.
  2. Finite-opportunity retry qualification harness — 90.8/100. Why it matters: a memory update must beat a matched memory-free retry under the same complete-trial, interaction, and reset budget before it earns credit as learning. Evidence that would change the recommendation: internal tasks where retry is infeasible or where a simpler deterministic controller consistently dominates under equal cost and risk. Next reversible action: evaluate archived tasks under six matched attempts with shared first trials, memory-free retry, the current feedback method, and separate call/token/action ledgers.
  3. Benchmark ground-truth and evaluator audit gate — 90.2/100. Why it matters: in the physics audit, 238 of 250 rejected cases were attributed to benchmark or grader error after expert review; release decisions cannot assume the benchmark is right. Evidence that would change the recommendation: independent internal audits showing negligible label, reference, ambiguity, and evaluator-equivalence error across decision-critical tests. Next reversible action: stratify 100 recent evaluation disagreements, blind-review them as model, grader, or benchmark errors, and quantify disagreement before changing any threshold.

Deep paper 1 — Final-answer cleanliness does not certify deployed-system forgetting

Identity and status. K-Bench: A Benchmark for LLM Unlearning in Agentic Deployments, Guangsheng Yu, Yanna Jiang, Qin Wang, Baihe Ma, and Xu Wang; submitted September 11, 2026; arXiv:2609.12808; cs.AI; preprint; CC BY 4.0. The paper says the benchmark ships a harness, adapters, configurations, data splits, and replication scripts, but no independently resolvable first-party repository or tagged release was found during review. (paper, full text)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

K-Bench wraps a ReAct agent and observes six channels: chain-of-thought, tool call, wide tool observation, retrieved text, answer, and elicited summary. It places the same synthetic PII in one of four isolated substrates—parametric weights, prompt context, free-text retrieval, or structured retrieval—and counts a query as leaked when any channel exposes the target. Its K-Score multiplies leakage suppression, retain-set preservation, and agent stability so collapse cannot masquerade as forgetting.

The paper's strongest result is not that unlearning is impossible. It is that the verdict changes when the observer expands and usability is enforced. ECO, an input-corruption reference with oracle target membership, reaches the reference verdict across eligible cross-model cells by corrupting the queried entity before substrate-specific encoding. That is an evaluated suppression mechanism, not proof that underlying knowledge was removed. A refusal-tuned method can similarly resist the tested extraction without verified deletion.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

TOFU, MUSE, WMDP, and LUME read a single answer surface; agentic privacy work such as AgentLeak broadens channels but does not evaluate forgetting, retain preservation, and collapse together. K-Bench's contribution is the combination of a six-channel union observer, four causally isolated substrate lanes, pre-registered paired inference, failure localization, a collapse-aware score, and a large method audit.

The main corpus contains 5,000 Faker-generated biographies with four fields, split into 1,000 forget and 4,000 retain entities. Parametric memory is created by LoRA continuation, not naturally occurring pretraining memorization. Each experiment puts a secret in exactly one substrate, while a production secret may exist simultaneously in logs, caches, prompts, retrieval replicas, weights, and downstream systems. The fixed query set is not an adaptive attacker. The OR-of-channels detector cannot reconstruct a secret split across channels and depends on known canonical target values. Chain-of-thought availability is provider-dependent and should never be assumed in an enterprise architecture.

Method ports and base models materially affect the outcome. Several methods use OpenUnlearning; others use official modules or disclosed bounded adaptations. The authors found that the top method and eligible set change by base model. Therefore the roster supports a benchmark-level warning, not a universal ranking of unlearning algorithms. Compliance claims would also need a legal definition of erasure, data lineage, downstream deletion, access controls, repeated adaptive extraction, and an independent audit.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The full paper discloses model families, corpus construction, LoRA settings, retrieval index configuration, query counts, seeds, gates, statistics, method provenance, and many port differences. Exact replication is blocked today by the lack of an independently verified public repository, immutable release, checksums, environment image, and complete cost ledger. Enterprise readiness: high for adapting the evaluation pattern in a synthetic red-team harness; medium for adding multi-channel deletion qualification to an existing data-governance process; low for using this benchmark as legal proof of erasure. Research Utility Score: 95/100 (rigor 25/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 17/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 14/15).

Deep paper 2 — Failure memory must beat a matched retry under the same opportunity budget

Identity and status. VRL-Bench: Benchmarking agents on computer control tasks under finite trial budgets, Yu Bai et al.; submitted September 11, 2026; arXiv:2609.12404; cs.AI; preprint; CC BY 4.0. The paper and appendices specify prompts, model settings, environment versions, and the evaluation protocol, but no independently verified first-party code repository or tagged release was found. (paper, full text)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

VRL-Bench asks whether a failure-derived textual update improves over simply trying again. It fixes a six-complete-trial budget, shares the realized first trial across methods, resets the environment between trials, and separately records success within budget (SR@6), recovery after the first failure (RR@6), censored first-success trial (AvgT@6, unsolved = 7), calls, tokens, and environment transitions. The model parameters stay fixed.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Reflexion carries language feedback across attempts. Dynamic Cheatsheet and ACE typically learn across query streams; LATS spends a search budget on branch expansion and state restoration; Agent-Pro learns policies on a separate train phase. VRL-Bench's advance is measurement discipline: define a complete attempt, align the first realization and external-information boundary, cap interactions, preserve each update's internal representation, and compare against a strong memory-free retry.

The paper explicitly does not match model calls or token use, only complete trials and interaction opportunities; the extra writer, curator, or scheduler computation therefore requires a separate economic comparison. MiniWoB uses fresh instances from the same family while WebShop resets the same goal, so the estimands differ. Irreversible actions, hidden feedback, asynchronous execution, human approvals, side effects, and security boundaries are untested. The scheduler's semantic policy tree is advice, not an executable state-search guarantee. Its positive point estimates should not become an autonomous retry policy without idempotency controls, permission scopes, action budgets, rollback or compensating transactions, and human review for consequential actions.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper specifies runtime versions, model identifiers, prompts, token settings, transition limits, seed pairing, bootstrap units, exposure schedules, and resource ledgers. It discloses that transports retries were disabled and separates public environment evidence from model-generated text. Missing public code, frozen trajectories, checksums, container images, and a total cost table limit exact reproduction. Enterprise readiness: high for an offline matched-retry benchmark and opportunity ledger; medium for a read-only or reversible shadow retry controller; low for autonomous production retries with side effects. Research Utility Score: 93/100 (rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 17/20; enterprise relevance 14/15; prior-work advance 14/15).

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Prior work has documented benchmark contamination, faulty references, brittle exact-match or rule-based graders, and LLM-judge variance. This paper's distinctive contribution is a large domain-expert audit that jointly inspects problem statements, reference solutions, graders, and model responses, then reports corrected scores. Reviewers outline an approach before reading the reference for CMT and CritPt, reducing anchoring to the supplied solution.

The audit's strongest headline is also easy to misuse. The four pooled audit sets were deliberately enriched for rejected answers; 95.2% non-model error among rejections does not mean 95.2% of all benchmark items are broken. Corrected scores condition on repaired or retained subsets, and CritPt even changes attempt count and item count. The evaluation routes are heterogeneous. The 28.57% initial disagreement among double-reviewed items demonstrates that taxonomy boundaries—especially benchmark versus grader error—need adjudication and uncertainty reporting.

Near-saturation applies only to well-posed, text-only, closed-ended physics problems under these specific routes. The authors explicitly state this does not imply end-to-end physics research capability and report no complete solution from their separate attempts on open theoretical problems. Enterprise teams should translate the paper into benchmark QA and evaluator challenge sets, not into a general claim about scientific autonomy or vendor superiority.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper documents benchmark selection, item counts, model configurations, tool access, audit categories, reviewer assignment, disagreement resolution, corrections, and corrected scoring. Reproduction is limited because the audit forms, repaired dataset, frozen outputs, prompts/evaluator code, and checksums are not released in a verified artifact. Enterprise readiness: high for adopting stratified human audit and disagreement adjudication; medium for repairing internal benchmarks under version control; low for treating corrected public scores as production-readiness or procurement evidence. Skill Issue: Lessons from Optimizing Repository SKILLs for Coding Agents, Mykhailo Kozyrev, Andrei Kozyrev, and Anton Podkopaev; arXiv:2609.12742; submitted September 11; preprint. The authors mine merged pull requests from three Kotlin repositories, revert them at a frozen base commit, and compare an agent with and without optimized repository documents. GEPA documents improve the score by 4.9 percentage points on average; SkillOpt improves 0.1 point over the seed. The paper explicitly says the GEPA gain cannot be separated from run-to-run variance at the number of tasks available from one repository. A maintainer qualitatively found valuable project knowledge. Watch trigger: promote when results replicate across more languages and repositories with paired uncertainty, fresh held-out PRs, security/secret-retention tests, and a verified reusable artifact. Embodied-BenchForge — promising closed-loop benchmark construction, but artifact and independence evidence are incomplete

Embodied-BenchForge: A Closed-Loop Agentic Workflow for Embodied Benchmark Construction, Baoyang Jiang et al.; arXiv:2609.13082; submitted September 11; preprint; CC BY 4.0. Cross-paper synthesis

Independent Analysis — the evaluation contract has three orthogonal boundaries

  1. Observation boundary. K-Bench asks what the deployed system can expose across every channel and substrate. An answer-only scorer is incomplete.
  2. Opportunity boundary. VRL-Bench asks what additional attempts, environment interactions, and feedback a method consumed. A retry advantage is not automatically a learning advantage.
  3. Ground-truth boundary. The physics audit asks whether the item, reference, and grader deserve authority. An apparent failure is not automatically a model failure.

These boundaries should be recorded independently because fixing one does not fix the others. A six-channel observer can still use a bad label. A perfect reference can still compare unequal opportunity budgets. A matched trial budget can still miss a disclosure in a tool trace. The enterprise control plane should therefore treat evaluation as a versioned evidence object rather than a scalar score.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

Independent Analysis — 12–24 month direction

The likely change is not another model service. It is an assurance plane that sits across applications, agents, model gateways, retrieval, data systems, evaluators, observability, governance, and human review. A request or evaluation receives a stable identity. Every substrate that can hold relevant data is declared. Every attempt, tool transition, reset, model call, feedback item, and evaluator configuration enters an immutable ledger. Observable outputs are unioned under an explicit privacy policy. Ground truth is versioned and periodically audited by humans. Scores are released only with uncertainty, eligibility gates, and links to the underlying evidence.

Shared enabling foundations unlock multiple enhancements:

  • Model gateway and identity/authorization: bind model route, version, tools, permissions, and tenant context to each run.
  • Context, memory, and knowledge services: expose substrate identity, retention, lineage, deletion mechanisms, retrieval provenance, and replica state.
  • Agent orchestration: enforce attempt/action budgets, reset semantics, idempotency, side-effect classes, and human approvals.
  • Evaluation and experiment infrastructure: pair runs, freeze fixtures, create strong retry baselines, maintain holdouts, and preserve benchmark versions.
  • Observability and cost telemetry: record calls, tokens, tool events, latency, errors, and channel evidence without indiscriminate sensitive-reasoning capture.
  • Policy enforcement and security: control who may observe, retain, export, challenge, delete, or adjudicate traces and benchmarks.
  • Human-review controls: support blind expert review, disagreement resolution, benchmark repair, and version-impact analysis.
  • Data provenance: connect benchmark items, references, model outputs, graders, repairs, and deployment decisions.

Additional internal context is required before implementation decisions: current agent and model topology, data classifications, applicable deletion duties, model-provider contracts, retrievable telemetry, benchmark ownership, side-effect severity, reset/idempotency capabilities, risk appetite, reviewer capacity, budget, latency targets, and adoption constraints. Today's backlog is a set of evidence-grounded future options, not an approved roadmap.

  4. Add access-controlled evidence storage, cost telemetry, purge tests, and experiment rollback.

  2. Re-evaluate Watchlist items after public artifacts and independent replications appear.
  3. Measure whether the assurance plane predicts production-relevant error severity better than scalar benchmark scores.

Knowledge reinforcement

Five checks to remember

  1. Where else could the behavior appear? Inspect the deployed system's authorized observable channels, not only the answer.
  2. What opportunity did the method consume? Match complete trials, interactions, resets, tools, calls, and feedback before attributing learning.
  3. Who verified the ground truth? Audit the task, reference, and grader; report reviewer disagreement.
  4. Did the system stay useful? Pair safety or suppression metrics with retain behavior, task viability, and stability.
  5. Can the decision be reconstructed? Preserve immutable identities, versions, artifacts, costs, uncertainty, and human adjudication.

Compact test: if a cleaner answer can hide a leaking tool trace, an extra attempt can look like learning, and a broken reference can look like model failure, then the scalar score is not yet decision-grade.
