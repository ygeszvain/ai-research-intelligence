Scope, window, and evidence policy

Research window. Tuesday, September 15, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT through Wednesday, September 16, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT (24 hours; America/Chicago). Discovery covered the September 16 arXiv announcement cycle—297 cs.AI records, including 72 new submissions—plus current cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.SE, cs.CR, cs.IR, cs.DB, cs.DC, stat.ML, first-party project pages and code repositories, and the prior work cited by the selected papers. All three deep papers and both Watchlist papers first appeared in this cycle. The normal window was sufficient; no expansion was used.

Anti-duplication. Exact titles, arXiv identifiers, central claims, and proposed enterprise controls were checked against the 37-report archive through September 15. None of today's five papers had appeared previously. The archive's recent work on plan injection, task-scoped permissioning, transactional tool effects, benchmark contracts, RAG safety, memory promotion, and persistent evidence identity was treated as prior intelligence. Today's differentiating signal is narrower: enterprise evidence must follow an agent across time, into the native artifact it changes, and through the domain semantics of the data it touches.

Evidence policy. Paper Fact means directly reported by a paper or verified first-party artifact. Independent Analysis is this briefing's calculation, synthesis, or critique. Uncertainty marks missing causal, generalization, artifact, economic, security, governance, privacy, operational, or adoption evidence. All five selected works are preprints. BLINDSPOT and CADWorld have public project artifacts; Symbolic Separation links a public EXASAGE implementation and query set. Executive signal

Enterprise agents must be qualified as stateful systems, accepted through the artifacts they leave behind, and constrained by the semantics of the domains they act upon.

BLINDSPOT shows that unsafe behavior can emerge after initially safe steps, that repeated executions expose additional vulnerable configurations, and that final-response-only evaluation misses both unsafe completion and over-refusal. CADWorld shows why a visually plausible or verbally completed task is not enough: the saved professional artifact can still be structurally wrong, geometrically invalid, uneditable, or unusable downstream. Symbolic Separation shows a complementary control: when an agent must access heterogeneous operational data, a domain-semantic contract can prevent invented relations that syntactic tool schemas do not catch.

The combined lesson is not “add a stronger model.” It is to build an operational evidence plane that retains trajectory state, policy and authorization transitions, native-artifact checks, domain-semantic validation, repeated-run evidence, evaluator provenance, cost, and human review. Model output is one observation inside that plane—not the final authority.

Decision posture. Prototype a stateful trajectory qualification harness, native-artifact acceptance gates for one bounded professional workflow, and a read-only semantic access layer over one governed data domain. Prepare common trace identity, evaluator versioning, policy composition, and human-release controls first. Keep GUI replay memories and broad neurosymbolic analytics on a controlled watchlist until drift, poisoning, privacy, and lifecycle evidence improve. Do not permit autonomous engineering release, consequential cross-domain action, or production data write access based on today's papers alone.

Top 3 Future Bets

  1. Stateful trajectory safety qualification — 93.3/100. Why it matters: one-turn evaluation misses delayed failures, refusal instability, stochastic failure paths, state mutation, and cross-domain policy composition. Evidence that would change the recommendation: representative internal traces showing no material difference between stateful multi-run qualification and existing tests, or unacceptable evaluation cost without added incident coverage. Next reversible action: replay 50 high-risk and 50 benign workflows in an isolated simulator across ten stochastic runs, with authorization expiry, environment mutation, and response-conditioned adversaries; compare trajectory labels with final-response-only labels.
  2. Native-artifact validation and structural acceptance gate — 91.1/100. Why it matters: professional work is often a persistent structured object, not a screenshot or prose response. CADWorld's strongest agent achieved only 17.5% on the full 200-task benchmark while an expert reference reached 87%, and all evaluated agents scored 0% on CAM, FEM, and TechDraw. Evidence that would change the recommendation: an internal workflow where output is fully disposable, has no structure or downstream dependencies, and human review already catches every material failure at acceptable cost. Next reversible action: define executable, task-specific checks for 25 historical artifacts in one sandboxed workflow and measure defects found beyond text/visual review.
  3. Domain-semantic access plane — 87.9/100. Why it matters: schema-valid calls can still invent relationships across sources. In one operational-telemetry study, ontology-constrained access improved correct end-to-end results from 29% for the strongest non-symbolic comparison to 86% while using 2.4× fewer median tokens. Evidence that would change the recommendation: target data domains with stable, simple, single-source schemas where a typed API already fully encodes admissible relationships, or internal reproduction showing ontology maintenance cost exceeds error reduction. Next reversible action: expose one read-only data domain through a small typed ontology, run 30 paired questions against semantic and direct-query paths, and stop if correctness, silent-error detection, or cost does not materially improve.

Deep paper 1 — Safety is a trajectory property, not a first-response property

Identity and status. BLINDSPOT: A Benchmark for Safety and Refusal Calibration in Long-Horizon Tool-Using Agents, Sadia Asif, Mohammad Mohammadi Amiri, Momin Abbas, Tejaswini Pedapati, and Prasanna Sattigeri; Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute and IBM Research; submitted September 14, 2026; arXiv:2609.16305v1; cs.AI with cs.CE, cs.CL, cs.LG, and cs.MA cross-listing; preprint; CC BY 4.0. A public code-and-dataset repository is linked from the paper. (paper, full text, artifact)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

BLINDSPOT treats safety as a joint property of a user, target agent, tools, policies, authorization state, and mutable environment over a complete episode. Its five terminal classes distinguish Safe Completion, Correct Refusal, Unsafe Completion, Over-Refusal, and Indeterminate. That is a material advance over attack-success-only scoring: a safe system must resist prohibited actions and complete allowed work without unnecessary refusal.

The benchmark's strongest design choice is execution-grounded adjudication. An action may be allowed early and prohibited later after authorization expiry, recipient change, or earlier tool actions. BLINDSPOT therefore records messages, tool calls, observations, state deltas, policy state, and authorization state, then uses deterministic checks first, narrow semantic judges where interpretation is needed, and human review for low-confidence or materially inconsistent cases. Indeterminate explicitly represents infrastructure incompleteness rather than silently reclassifying it as safe or unsafe.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Prior agent-safety benchmarks cover one-shot harmful requests, indirect prompt injection, tool attacks, or long-horizon attack success. AgentLAB and related work already establish that multi-turn attacks matter. BLINDSPOT's useful increment is calibration across both adversarial and benign trajectories, response-conditioned adversaries, executable state, repeated runs, post-refusal persistence, scenario–attack applicability, and a five-way outcome that separates refusal correctness from over-refusal.

The benchmark is stronger than static prompt collections because the adversary sees the trajectory and can reframe after refusal or authorization checks. Scenario–attack pairs are instantiated only when required tools, state, policy, and attacker capabilities exist, avoiding meaningless Cartesian-product tests. It also preserves failure-to-execute as evidence instead of discarding it. The deterministic/semantic ablation makes the measurement architecture inspectable.

Material limits remain. The same full pipeline defines the labels and serves as the reference for ablations, so the reported 96% automated agreement is not independent validation. Scenario and attack taxonomies are authored and unevenly sampled; aggregate rates are not prevalence estimates. Model settings, provider updates, sampling controls, and judge behavior can affect results. The paper reports many point estimates but limited uncertainty intervals and no cost accounting for the full multi-run harness. Human review is described but inter-rater reliability and reviewer burden are not the central result. The five outcome classes still compress severity, reversibility, affected assets, and duration of harm.

Most importantly, the benchmark evaluates synthetic, sandboxed policies. An enterprise must rebuild its own state objects, approval semantics, identity boundaries, tool effects, data classifications, and escalation rules. The correct enterprise inference is “adopt the measurement pattern,” not “reuse these rates as vendor rankings.”

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper exposes algorithms, prompt templates, scenario and attack schemas, tool specifications, evaluator logic, the eight metric definitions, outcome rules, and a linked public repository. The CC BY 4.0 license supports inspection and adaptation. Reproducibility is still moderated by hosted-model drift, stochastic long-horizon generation, external service versions, and incomplete evidence about judge calibration and human review.

Enterprise readiness: high for creating an isolated trajectory-level release harness and logging state/policy evidence; medium for model-gateway admission decisions after internal scenario construction and evaluator calibration; low for directly transferring model rankings or UCR thresholds into production. Research Utility Score: 95/100 (methodological rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 19/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 13/15).

Deep paper 2 — The artifact is the product

Identity and status. CADWorld: Computer-Use Benchmark for Long-Horizon Computer-Aided Design, Zihan Dong, Yuanzhe Liu, Zhiyuan Ma, Qishi Zhan, Dehan Kong, Guohao Li, and Kaixin Li; submitted September 14, 2026; arXiv:2609.16251v1; cs.AI; preprint; CC BY 4.0. The project page provides code and a VM image. The manuscript evaluates 200 tasks across 11 mechanical-CAD categories; the public v0 project page describes a 60-task release and explicitly holds back the remainder for rolling evaluation. (paper, full text, project and artifacts)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

CADWorld makes a simple but consequential measurement choice: it evaluates the native .FCStd project and auxiliary outputs, not merely the final screenshot, the agent's statement of completion, or a judge's impression. Task-specific checks inspect geometry, dimensions, constraints, document structure, feature history, joints, stock removal, toolpaths, FEM objects and outputs, materials, measurements, meshes, and technical drawings. Success requires every required check to pass.

The central result is the gap between apparent completion and artifact validity. GPT5.4's 62.5% finish rate versus 17.5% success means that most “finished” runs do not survive the task-specific structural checks. CADWorld further reports death loops, missed compound mouse/keyboard interactions, incorrect workbenches, underconstrained sketches, wrong fields, omitted toolpaths, and wrong save paths. These are not cosmetic errors: a professional must reopen, inspect, edit, simulate, manufacture, or certify the resulting object.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Earlier computer-use benchmarks test web navigation, desktop tasks, and professional workflows, while CAD benchmarks often score generated code, point clouds, meshes, rendered images, or short edits. CADWorld's advance is the combination of long-horizon screenshot interaction, a restricted action interface, native editable artifacts, human-authored task requirements, task-specific executable checks, and deterministic failure diagnostics.

The design is unusually aligned with enterprise assurance: the VM is reset, the agent sees the same GUI a human uses, direct scripting and filesystem access are excluded, artifacts are extracted after termination, and evaluator outputs remain rerunnable. Keeping continuous geometry diagnostics beside a binary score supports both release decisions and debugging. A held-back task remainder is also a useful anti-overfitting mechanism.

Several limits materially constrain inference. FreeCAD is open and reproducible but differs from commercial CAD kernels, UI conventions, cloud integrations, licensing, and downstream toolchains. The benchmark is coverage-oriented rather than sampled from enterprise incident frequency or business value. Models use different native computer-use interfaces, context policies, and provider infrastructure. Some category cells are tiny. The expert row is a single demonstration path. All-checks-pass scoring is appropriate for safety-critical engineering but may understate partial productivity in assistant workflows. No causal experiment separates model reasoning, GUI grounding, training exposure, and action-schema limitations.

The artifact evaluator itself is part of the system under test. If it omits a material requirement, an agent can “pass” an invalid design. Enterprise adoption therefore needs evaluator validation, independent domain review, versioning, and challenge cases—not simply more agent testing.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper reports task packaging, action schemas, VM configuration, token and step accounting, deterministic evaluator families, failure rules, model adapters, and public project links to code and a VM image. CC BY 4.0 enables adaptation. Reproduction still depends on sizeable VM artifacts, hosted-model versions, provider interfaces, and the difference between the public 60-task release and the full 200-task manuscript evaluation.

Enterprise readiness: high for the measurement pattern—native artifact, task-specific checks, retained trace, diagnostic failure class, and held-out tasks; medium for adapting the harness to one low-risk internal professional workflow; low for autonomous engineering release or substituting these results for qualified human approval. Research Utility Score: 94/100 (methodological rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 23/25; reproducibility/artifacts 20/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 12/15).

Deep paper 3 — A tool schema is not a domain-semantic contract

Identity and status. Symbolic Separation: Grounding Deep Agents in Knowledge Graphs for Trustworthy Operational Data Analytics, Baibek Davletiyarov, Junaid Ahmed Khan, and Andrea Bartolini; submitted September 15, 2026; arXiv:2609.17107v1; cs.AI; preprint; CC BY 4.0. The authors link a public GitLab implementation of the EXASAGE Reflect Agent and a 75-question query set. Experiments use the public 49.9 TB M100 ExaData supercomputer telemetry corpus. (paper, full text, artifact)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

The paper's “symbolic separation” principle lets the LLM reason freely but constrains data access to an ontology-conforming Virtual Knowledge Graph (VKG). The agent can propose entity extraction and SPARQL; deterministic components validate inputs, materialize the required graph, check ontology conformance, and execute the query. Complex cross-source relationships are defined before the agent acts rather than inferred across multiple tool calls.

The most valuable conceptual distinction is between an interface contract and a domain-semantic contract. A typed tool can prevent an invalid field or malformed call while still allowing the agent to compose a false relationship across valid calls. The VKG puts admissible entities, containment, equipment, software, metrics, time, and external signals into a queryable graph and rejects nonconforming constructs before execution.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Knowledge-graph RAG, semantic layers, text-to-SQL, SPARQL agents, ontology-based data access, and constrained decoding all precede this paper. The new contribution is the combination of a deep-agent coordinator, specialist retrieval and code agents, ontology-constrained VKG access, deterministic pre-execution validation, clarification and state checkpoints, bounded reflection, and a direct non-symbolic ablation on a large operational corpus.

The paper responsibly separates nominal completion from correctness and reports a cross-container escape rather than hiding it. It also analyzes token distribution and shows that context processing, not generated output, dominates multi-agent cost. The 75-query retrieval study helps explain why the prior 93.6% templated result collapses outside its ten archetypes.

The evidence is still narrow. Fourteen end-to-end questions cannot support a general “trustworthy analytics” claim. Architecture and model choices are not fully crossed in the strongest headline comparison, and no repeated stochastic runs or uncertainty intervals are reported. The ontology, benchmark, system, and evaluation all come from the same research line, raising construct-validity and independence concerns. Correctness classification and “zero hallucinations” rely on manual inspection. The paper does not measure ontology authoring/maintenance cost, schema drift, poisoning, access-control enforcement, row/column security, purpose limitation, privacy, concurrency, failure recovery under live load, or human analyst time.

The public implementation improves inspectability, but a project page is not an immutable reproducibility package. Enterprise use would require dependency locks, signed releases, ontology ownership, provenance for mappings, separation of duties, adversarial queries, audit of the escape path, and explicit authorization below the semantic layer.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The manuscript reports the ontology classes, pipeline stages, acceptance/clarification thresholds (0.7/0.5), state tuple, MCP tools, two model configurations, H100/vLLM environment, GraphDB and Polars components, question counts, per-query token and retry data, and the public data corpus. A GitLab repository exposes the Reflect Agent. Exact reproduction is limited by mutable dependencies, the 256K inference context, absence of repeated seeds, and incomplete evidence about annotations and the full runtime image.

Enterprise readiness: high for a read-only proof of concept over one bounded data domain and for encoding semantic relationships as policy; medium for analyst-assistance with provenance, authentication, authorization, and human review; low for broad write-capable cross-domain analytics or treating “zero hallucinations” as a production guarantee. Research Utility Score: 91/100 (methodological rigor 22/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 18/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 12/15).

Watchlist

Ground-truth-as-code — promising for live-data evaluation, but the released evidence is not independently reproducible

Skill-based Agentic Evaluation for Real-time Data Science Tasks, Aniruddha Tamhane, Raghavendra Addanki, Ayushi Aggarwal, Aditya Bansal, Rui Wang, Charles Menguy, and Swati Jain; submitted September 15, 2026; arXiv:2609.16487; cs.AI/cs.LG/cs.MA; CC BY 4.0. (paper)

Paper Facts. The framework encodes each expected answer as an executable function that recomputes ground truth from current data, then decomposes both answer and reference into atomic factoids and scores precision, recall, and accuracy independent of prose, list, table, or HTML format. In a human–LLM agreement study on an internally developed production ML skill using a synthetic database that reproduces production schemas and relationships, the authors report a 29% relative improvement in Matthews Correlation Coefficient over natural-language ground truth and 16% fewer tokens per case. A self-directed baseline without explicit ground truth is reported as anti-correlated with human judgment.

Why Watchlist. This is a high-value measurement pattern for non-stationary dashboards, operations, finance, and data science, but the evidence is not yet portable. The production skill, schemas, reference functions, synthetic database, human labels, prompts, error breakdown, and code are not released from the paper page. The abstract reports relative MCC improvement without the absolute MCC values, sample size, confidence intervals, or task strata. Executable reference code can also be wrong, stale, overfit to schemas, or unsafe if it receives broader data access than the agent. Watch trigger: promote after an auditable public artifact or an internal reproduction shows absolute expert agreement, evaluator mutation-test coverage, temporal stability, access controls, and clear wins over deterministic comparison without an LLM judge. Research Utility Score: 82/100.

EchoPath — large replay-efficiency gains, but start-state rejection and interface drift remain deployment blockers

EchoPath: Execution-Level Replayable Memory for GUI Agents, Yao Zhao, Aditya Shanmugham, Swastik Roy, and Yanxun Xu; submitted September 15, 2026; arXiv:2609.16635; cs.AI; arXiv perpetual non-exclusive license. (paper, full text)

Paper Facts. EchoPath promotes only artifact-validated GUI traces into versioned memories with intent keys, application/state preconditions, fixed versus flexible parameters, screenshots, target crops, validation provenance, and lifecycle state. In a paired two-pass OSWorld-Verified design, it intentionally changes resolution from 1920×1080 to 1600×900. Across 159 active memories, Codex replay completes 145/159 (91.2%) with median 20,370 tokens and 127.5 seconds; a Synapse planning-memory baseline achieves 91.8% with median 586,386 tokens and 315.7 seconds. Retrieval remains 100% in an offline stress test as the active library grows from 159 to 659 memories. Random-scale target re-aiming accepts 95% of 200 pointer cases; 188 of 190 accepted cases are within two pixels. Flexible rebinding accepts all 50 declared substitutions and rejects all 50 forbidden mutations. However, the state gate rejects only 39/50 incompatible starts (78% accuracy).

Why Watchlist. The results support a bounded efficiency claim for repeated tasks under controlled compatibility, not general autonomous GUI automation. The same tasks are replayed on the second pass; semantic task variation, software upgrades, localization, theme changes, responsive layouts, near-duplicate controls, compromised memories, privacy, and enterprise repository governance remain untested. An incompatible-start false accept can replay a valid procedure in the wrong state. No public implementation link or immutable release is provided on the paper page. Watch trigger: promote after independent replication with unseen task variants, live UI/version drift, adversarial memory poisoning, privacy controls, signed provenance, and ≥99.9% rejection of materially incompatible or unauthorized states on the target application. Research Utility Score: 87/100.

Cross-paper synthesis

Independent Analysis — the operational evidence plane

The three deep papers identify different points where an output-centric architecture loses the evidence needed for enterprise control:

  1. Across time: BLINDSPOT shows that the same current request can be safe or unsafe depending on previous state, authorization, and actions. The unit of evidence is the trajectory.
  2. Across persistence: CADWorld shows that a correct-looking terminal screen or completion message can hide an invalid native object. The unit of acceptance is the saved artifact and its downstream invariants.
  3. Across semantics: Symbolic Separation shows that individually valid calls can compose an invalid relationship. The unit of policy is the domain graph, not the API syntax.

These are complementary controls. A trajectory log without artifact checks can faithfully record the creation of an unusable file. Artifact checks without trajectory policy can approve an object produced through unauthorized data or actions. A semantic data layer without longitudinal evaluation cannot detect delayed authorization drift or repeated-run fragility. The shared architecture should therefore retain evidence identity from context and policy, through action and tool execution, into native artifacts and domain consequences.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

Independent Analysis — 12–24 month evolution

Today's evidence favors an architecture in which the model is an interchangeable reasoning component inside a governed execution and evidence system:

  1. Model gateway: pin model/configuration identity, collect cost and latency, and route by qualified task class. No model-wide “safe” label should substitute for scenario evidence.
  2. Context and memory services: store provenance, lifecycle, purpose, and version for retrieved context and replayable procedures; treat promotion and replay as gated operations.
  3. Agent orchestration: make trajectory state, retries, refusal transitions, authorization changes, and environment deltas first-class. Preserve logical-operation identity across components.
  4. Identity and authorization: bind task, user, agent, domain, tool, and data scope; expire authority and make cross-domain composition visible.
  5. Knowledge systems: add semantic contracts for high-value heterogeneous domains while keeping direct data access read-only during qualification. Ontology and mapping changes need code-review-like controls.
  6. Evaluation infrastructure: support repeated stochastic runs, adaptive adversaries, dynamic ground truth, native artifact checks, deterministic+semantic adjudication, held-out scenarios, and evaluator mutation tests.
  7. Observability and provenance: join context, policy, authorization, action, effect, data mapping, artifact, evaluator, cost, and review evidence under stable IDs. Integrity proves record continuity, not semantic truth.
  8. Human-review controls: route ambiguous policy, low-confidence semantics, irreversible effects, and professional artifact release to qualified reviewers with diagnostic evidence—not raw transcripts alone.
  9. Experiment infrastructure: shadow, simulate, replay, inject state changes, and compare control surfaces without external writes. Every future enhancement should have a stop condition and rollback path.
  10. Cost telemetry: attribute tokens, retries, graph construction, evaluator execution, storage, and reviewer time to a logical task. Symbolic structure and replay may reduce model cost while increasing maintenance cost; both must be measured.

The shared enabling foundation is the operational evidence plane. It unlocks stateful safety qualification, artifact gates, semantic access, dynamic references, replayable procedures, incident reconstruction, and human review without assuming any current vendor, data platform, or maturity level. Before implementation, the enterprise must supply workflow criticality, supported artifact types, identity model, data classifications, tool inventory, policy ownership, retention/privacy constraints, SLOs, model mix, cost targets, and human-review capacity.

Implementation priorities

  1. 0–30 days: choose one bounded read-only or non-production workflow; inventory states, policies, artifacts, semantic relations, tools, and acceptance criteria. Establish stable task/trajectory/artifact/evaluator IDs.
  2. 30–60 days: build the trajectory simulator and native-artifact checks; replay historical failures; create benign twins and state/auth mutation cases; define human-review escalation.
  3. 60–90 days: add a small semantic access layer or dynamic reference functions where the workflow uses changing heterogeneous data; compare direct and mediated paths.
  4. 90–180 days: integrate evidence envelopes with existing observability and identity controls; run repeated qualification for two releases; measure cost, reviewer burden, and failure coverage.
  5. Do not advance write-capable semantic agents, autonomous professional release, or shared GUI procedure memories until internal context, security review, drift testing, privacy controls, and qualified human accountability are verified.

Knowledge reinforcement

  1. Why is a final refusal insufficient safety evidence? Because an agent may later comply under continued pressure, and the permission state can change across turns. BLINDSPOT measures post-refusal failure separately.
  2. What does a native artifact evaluator add beyond screenshots? It checks structure, constraints, geometry, feature history, and downstream state that can remain wrong even when the visible result looks plausible.
  3. Why is a typed tool schema not a semantic contract? It can validate each call while still allowing the agent to invent how valid entities relate across calls. A semantic graph constrains those relationships before execution.
  4. Why repeat the same safety configuration? Stochastic interactions explore different paths; Fail@k can reveal vulnerable configurations a single run misses.
  5. What is the safe near-term use of replayable GUI memory? Controlled, low-risk replay with strict state gating, declared flexible parameters, artifact/effect checks, privacy controls, and immediate fallback—not unattended consequential action.
