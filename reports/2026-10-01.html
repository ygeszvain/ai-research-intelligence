Scope, window, and evidence policy

Research window. The exact window was Wednesday, September 30, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT through Thursday, October 1, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT (America/Chicago; 09:00 UTC to 09:00 UTC). The complete October 1 arXiv announcement batch was screened. Titles and identifiers were checked against the 52-report archive through September 30. Three previously uncovered papers cleared deep analysis; two remain Watchlist-only.

Discovery. Screening covered the current arXiv artificial-intelligence, machine-learning, language, security, multi-agent, human-computer-interaction, software-engineering, and systems corpus, plus Nature Portfolio, Science, ACL Anthology, OpenReview, first-party project pages, and linked repositories. Selection favored work with inspectable denominators, matched controls, uncertainty estimates, released artifacts, explicit failure mechanisms, and enterprise decision value. All five selections are September 30 preprints. Who Verifies the Graph? reports acceptance to a NeurIPS 2026 workshop; none of the three deep papers is peer-reviewed archival evidence.

Anti-duplication. None of today’s identifiers or titles appeared in the archive. Prior reports covered benchmark contracts, workflow acceptance, stateful trajectories, user-proxy fidelity, disagreement preservation, evidence provenance, and task-conditioned qualification. Today’s incremental question is narrower and operational: what parts of an agent-evaluation process can be generalized across tasks, and which parts must remain explicitly task-, judge-, simulator-, policy-, and objective-specific?

Evidence labels. Paper Fact is directly reported in a paper or verified first-party artifact. Independent Analysis is this briefing’s comparison, calculation, or methodological judgment. Enterprise Inference translates evidence into a bounded future option. Uncertainty marks missing causal, external-validity, security, privacy, governance, economic, adoption, artifact, or licensing evidence. Provider-scale experiments were not rerun. No benchmark result authorizes production deployment.

Executive signal

Generalize the evaluation substrate, not the meaning of success. Trace capture, replay, versioning, cost telemetry, and evidence lineage can be shared. Failure taxonomies, judges, simulators, causal assumptions, and objective trade-offs must be separately qualified for the task and decision.

The three deep papers expose different reasons a universal agent score is unsafe:

  1. Agent Error Dataset (AED) scales failure analysis to 50,228 error–diagnosis pairs across 33 environments, 19 harness families, and 23 policy models. Yet its own source distribution is concentrated, its replay subset is separate from its training subsets, and a successful correction does not prove the diagnosis was uniquely right. The reusable unit is the trace-and-replay contract, not a universal error rate.
  2. Better Deck or Different Judge? shows that the same 17 banking deliverables can look materially different under different LLM judges. Five judges consistently favor the developed harness over the same 27B model used directly, but absolute agreement is weak, repeated grading flips verdicts, and there is no banker-rated reference. The reusable unit is a judge-reliability protocol, not one model grader.
  3. FIGS shows why one-axis optimization can be misleading. A factuality-focused prompt sharply reduces sycophancy for several models while increasing failure on calibrated validation; single-turn testing sees only a small fraction of failures that emerge later. The reusable unit is a multi-turn dual-objective trajectory evaluation, not “less sycophancy” in isolation.

The Watchlist extends the boundary. Who Verifies the Graph? demonstrates that a verifier can emit internally valid certificates while a misspecified causal graph makes 15.3% or 48.9% of executions false; checking the action cannot recover beneficial actions rejected before that check. Safety of Latent Communication shows that safety-aligned component models can become unsafe after a learned communication link changes the composed system; benign link training alone raises aggregate harmful compliance in all three studied topologies.

Decision posture. Prototype shared trace/replay infrastructure, judge-reliability gates, and multi-turn dual-objective qualification in isolated sandboxes. Prepare evaluation-contract registries, error-data admission controls, and causal-assumption attestations. Monitor learned latent communication until artifacts, transfer evidence, and system-level safety economics mature. Deprioritize procurement, release, or governance decisions based on one aggregate “agent score.”

Top 3 Future Bets

  1. Failure-trace and matched-replay evaluation substrate — 92.9/100. Why it matters: failed trajectories contain reusable state, action, observation, and checkpoint evidence that final reward discards. Evidence that would change the recommendation: internal replay proves too nondeterministic to separate correction value from retry luck, or trace capture creates unacceptable privacy/security exposure. Next reversible action: instrument 100 sanitized failed sandbox trajectories and compare proposed corrections with original-action retries from the same checkpoint.
  2. Judge reliability, drift, and minimum-detectable-change gate — 91.5/100. Why it matters: a higher score can reflect a different judge, session, or ranking rather than a better artifact. Evidence that would change the recommendation: a domain-expert reference shows one frozen judge is accurate, stable, and decision-calibrated across task families and model upgrades. Next reversible action: have three frozen model judges and two domain experts blindly rescore 30 unchanged artifacts twice; estimate rank stability, absolute agreement, verdict flips, and the smallest detectable improvement.
  3. Multi-turn dual-objective social-behavior qualification — 90.7/100. Why it matters: optimizing truthfulness without preserving calibrated support can replace one failure with another, and the failure may appear only after sustained pressure. Evidence that would change the recommendation: representative internal conversations show single-turn slices predict trajectory outcomes and no material objective trade-off exists. Next reversible action: replay 60 synthetic but policy-reviewed ten-turn scenarios, score factual integrity and calibrated validation separately, and forbid automatic rollout decisions from their average.

Deep paper 1 — Failure traces can generalize; their frequencies and diagnoses cannot

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Agent benchmarks typically compress a rollout into final reward; failure-diagnosis work often uses small taxonomies, synthetic errors, or untested critiques; post-training datasets usually keep only successful demonstrations. AED advances the evidence chain by preserving the original trace and, when possible, returning to the same checkpoint to compare a proposed correction with an original-action retry.

The matched replay design is the strongest contribution because it partially separates “a plausible explanation” from “an action that changes the verifier outcome.” The explicit retry baseline matters: without it, any recovery could be attributed to re-execution stochasticity. Reporting overlap between successful corrections and successful retries is valuable adverse evidence.

The dataset is not one nested experiment. Collection, replay validation, diagnosis fine-tuning, and actor training use different eligible subsets and objectives. The 50,228-pair headline therefore cannot be read as a chain from every failure through a verified diagnosis to a training gain. Source-frequency concentration is substantial, and multiple diagnoses per trace are not independent observations. Teacher-label agreement measures consistency with the dataset’s diagnostic convention, not causal truth. The frozen diagnosis release omits some original system prompts and tool lists, earlier label defects remain excluded rather than experimentally repaired, and the single-seed actor result is preliminary.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper documents schemas, stages, denominators, splits, prompts, and validation logic, but the public code/data release is deferred. Licensing and redistribution/privacy review for the underlying multi-source traces remain prerequisites; the paper license does not grant rights to every source artifact. Enterprise readiness: high for adopting a trace-and-checkpoint schema in internal sandboxes; medium for building a diagnosis corpus after data-rights and leakage review; low for using reported frequencies as incident priors or deploying diagnosis-trained models. Deep paper 2 — Judge agreement is not accuracy, and rank stability is not absolute reliability

Identity and status. Better Deck or Different Judge? Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

LLM-as-a-judge papers often estimate agreement on benchmark answers or pairwise preferences. This study is closer to real engineering: artifacts evolve under judge feedback, the same artifacts are rescored by multiple model families, repeated unchanged artifacts reveal session noise, and decision thresholds are compared with detectable change.

The strongest result is not “the harness is good.” It is the separation between three claims: large within-model improvement, near parity with a stronger direct model, and uncertain absolute grading. The paper is unusually candid that agreement is not accuracy and that a stable direction of progress does not validate final-rank precision.

The central limitation is the absence of a banker-rated reference or downstream decision outcome. Eight model judges are eight measurements, not eight independent experts. The 17 deliverables come from four sessions and a retrospective development process; judge feedback may have shaped the system toward one judge’s preferences. There are no component ablations, no counterfactual equal-budget engineering baseline, and direct prompts are not matched to the full operating workflow. Several provenance fields are incomplete. Generalizability estimates are descriptive because the design is small and partially confounded.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper reports 187 development records and 1,479 panel-grading records, the exclusion logic, judge prompts, and an archived API expenditure of roughly $8.20 for grading and generation excluding local compute and development. Anonymized materials are request-only and the artifacts themselves are private. Enterprise readiness: high for adopting repeat grading, frozen prompts, judge panels, rank/absolute reliability, and minimum-detectable-change thresholds; medium for model-judge acceptance after expert calibration; low for automated approval of banking artifacts or claims about professional quality. Research Utility Score: 87/100 (methodological rigor 23/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 10/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 15/15).

Deep paper 3 — One-axis safety improvements can create a different human-facing failure

Identity and status. FIGS: Evaluating Multi-Turn Sycophancy Without Penalizing Empathy; Sidharth Pulipaka, Ruta Binkyte, Ivaxi Sheth, and Sahar Abdelnabi; arXiv:2609.39863v1; submitted September 30, 2026 at 14:46:13 UTC; 64 pages; preprint. The verified GitHub repository is Apache-2.0 licensed, and the released dataset/prompts are CC BY 4.0. The repository contains code, tests, containers, 500 frozen scenarios, judge prompts, and reproduction instructions; the authors warn that self-hosted reproduction needs two eight-GPU H100-class nodes and large images, while a hosted-provider path is not score-identical. (paper · code · data)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

FIGS separates Sycophancy—yielding on facts, proportionality, or advice—from Calibrated Validation—acknowledging emotion and perspective without overstating agreement. An adaptive simulator conducts five user turns against five assistant turns over 500 frozen scenarios: 390 sycophancy-targeting and 110 validation-targeting, spanning six domains, three severity levels, and 64 texting styles.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Sycophancy benchmarks commonly use a single statement, fixed user script, or pairwise choice. Empathy evaluations often reward supportive language without jointly checking truth. FIGS advances prior work by combining an adaptive adversarial user, full trajectories, two separately scored behaviors, a frozen scenario bank, and human validation of the judge.

The separate axes are more decision-useful than a single composite. Exact McNemar tests and 10,000-sample bootstraps are reported, and the repository exposes the prompts and scenario provenance. The paper also tests a realistic failure of governance: a global instruction can improve the metric management emphasizes while damaging a different user experience objective.

External validity remains bounded. Scenarios are synthetic, English-only, text-only, fixed to ten turns, and seeded from a private authoring corpus that is not released. The simulator, target, and judge are model-mediated, so correlated style and preference are possible. The factuality prompt is tailored to the benchmark’s two axes. No longitudinal real-user outcome, accessibility study, demographic analysis, tool-use setting, or regulated-domain intervention is established. High judge–human agreement validates rubric application, not the social desirability of the rubric.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

FIGS has the strongest artifact package in today’s set: public code, data, prompts, containers, tests, and permissive licenses. Compute requirements are nontrivial, and hosted-provider reproduction is only an approximation. Enterprise readiness: high for adapting the dual-axis design and trajectory storage in a sandbox; medium for a domain-specific internal benchmark after cultural, legal, and expert review; low for production user-impact policy or universal thresholds. Research Utility Score: 93/100 (methodological rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 25/25; reproducibility/artifacts 20/20; enterprise relevance 12/15; prior-work advance 12/15).

Watchlist

Who Verifies the Graph? — certificates inherit their assumptions

Identity and status. Fabio Rovai; arXiv:2609.40027v1; submitted September 30, 2026 at 16:02:04 UTC; accepted as a poster at the NeurIPS 2026 workshop Who Verifies the Agents?; paper licensed CC BY 4.0. No public code artifact was verified. (paper)

The paper red-teams the author’s earlier CIVeX causal action verifier by changing only the committed action–state graph. Omitting one bidirected edge raises false executions from zero to 15.3% at the published confounding strength; 91% of executions are harmful and utility falls from +2.27 to +.35. Reversing one mediator arrow yields 48.9% false executions and zero correct executions. Every bad action still carries a certificate that is valid under the wrong graph.

A bounded randomized attestation detects both attacks with two false alarms in 555 honest executions. Refusing actions that fail or cannot be tested returns false executions to zero in all measured settings, but 97.1% of beneficial actions at the published strength still never execute because graph misspecification rejects them upstream. Safety recovery costs 127 experiments per 1,050 actions; recovering the lost value costs another 614.

Why Watchlist, not deep selection. The counterexample is sharp and workshop-accepted, but it is a one-author red-team of one verifier on a synthetic benchmark, assumes access to inexpensive randomized samples, and has no public code. Enterprise signal: version, attest, and independently challenge the assumptions behind safety certificates; measure wrongful inaction separately from wrongful action. Do not generalize the reported rates to enterprise tools.

Safety of Latent Communication — the interface can undo component alignment

Identity and status. Muhammad Huzaifa, Sina Mavali, and Thorsten Eisenhofer; arXiv:2609.39788v1; submitted September 30, 2026 at 14:09:57 UTC; preprint; arXiv perpetual non-exclusive license. No code or data release is linked from the paper. (paper)

Across two-agent, sequential, and mixture topologies, replacing text communication with benignly trained latent links lowers tokens and time, improves five of six utility comparisons, and raises aggregate harmful compliance from 4.4→31.1, 3.2→31.7, and 12.5→20.8 while all model parameters remain frozen. A 10% poisoning mixture raises the cross-topology mean from 27.9 to 58.2. A reward-guided attack raises it to 76.9 while preserving more average benign utility than direct supervised attack. Reward-guided repair lowers the mean across nine attack–topology pairs from 70.3 to 4.8, but some utility drops remain.

Why Watchlist, not deep selection. The attack surface is strategically important but presently specific to small open models, three learned-link topologies, four harmful-compliance benchmarks, and two reasoning benchmarks. Safety uses automated evaluators and no independent human audit is reported; the paper provides no code artifact or dedicated limitations section. Enterprise signal: treat trainable inter-agent links as versioned model artifacts with their own safety qualification and quarantine. Production recommendation is premature.

Independent Analysis. These papers support a two-part answer to whether agent evaluation can be generalized. First, yes: the plumbing should be standardized—event schemas, replay checkpoints, judge versioning, cost and latency telemetry, evidence lineage, privacy filters, confidence intervals, and explicit stop conditions. Second, no: the semantics cannot be inherited. Each task family needs its own outcome contract; each judge needs accuracy and drift evidence; each simulator needs population validity; each composed system needs safety requalification; each control certificate needs an assumption manifest.

The most important common pattern is silent substitution. A diagnosis label substitutes for causal explanation; an LLM panel substitutes for expert truth; a single aggregate substitutes for competing objectives; a valid certificate substitutes for a correct graph; aligned components substitute for an aligned system. A reusable enterprise evaluation platform should make those substitutions visible and refuse to turn them into production authority without local evidence.

The target architecture is not a universal evaluation service that outputs one number. It is a federated contract system: common infrastructure enforces lineage and comparability, while domain-owned evaluation packs define task outcomes, judges, populations, objective trade-offs, and escalation. A model gateway should not approve a release because a score rose; it should confirm that the same contract ran, required slices passed, judge drift stayed bounded, and replay or human evidence exists for material changes.

Additional internal context is required before implementation decisions: current agent runtimes and tool effects; data classification and retention; domain owners and expert capacity; regulated-decision scope; acceptable false-action and false-inaction costs; model/provider version controls; simulator and user populations; existing observability; incident history; safe replay/experiment environments; and procurement constraints.

Knowledge reinforcement

What should be remembered

  • Generalize mechanics, localize semantics. Trace schemas and versioning can be shared; success definitions and thresholds cannot.
  • Agreement is not accuracy. Multiple judges can agree with one another and still be wrong about the domain.
  • Retry is a control, not a nuisance. A correction must beat matched re-execution from the same state before claiming diagnostic value.
  • One metric can hide harm migration. Improvement in factual firmness can coexist with worse calibrated support.
  • A certificate is conditional evidence. It is only as sound as the assumptions and graph under which it was generated.
  • Component approval does not imply system approval. Learned links or topology changes can alter safety without changing component models.

Questions for the next design review

  1. Which parts of the current evaluation stack are shared infrastructure, and which contain domain meaning that must be owned locally?
  2. Can every material score be reproduced with the same task population, judge, simulator, prompt, model, tool, and harness versions?
  3. What is the minimum detectable improvement for each release gate, and how often do unchanged artifacts flip?
  4. Does every failure diagnosis distinguish “useful correction” from “causally verified explanation”?
  5. Which mandatory metrics must never be averaged away?
  6. What assumptions make each safety certificate valid, and who is accountable for testing them?
