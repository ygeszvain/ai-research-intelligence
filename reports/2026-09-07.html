Scope, window, and evidence policy

  • Research window: September 4, 4:00 AM–September 7, 4:00 AM America/Chicago. This is the established 72-hour Monday window. The September 7 arXiv release includes manuscripts submitted September 4; selection is based on public release availability inside the window, not the authors' upload timestamp. No seven-day expansion was required.
  • Discovery: current arXiv AI, machine learning, language, information-retrieval and multi-agent listings were screened alongside Nature Portfolio, Springer Nature, ACM, and conference-linked records. Prestige was used for discovery and venue verification, never as a substitute for accessible methods, traceable results, or limitations.
  • Anti-duplication: titles, identifiers, and central claims were checked against the archive through September 6. No selected paper repeats an earlier deep or Watchlist item.
  • Selection: three papers cleared full-paper review. Two remain on the Watchlist because artifact or external-validity evidence is weaker. A September 7 Scientific Reports cyberattack paper was screened but excluded because its calendar-date publication boundary relative to the 4:00 AM cutoff was not independently resolvable and the accessible record was an early unedited manuscript.
  • Research Utility Score (RUS): rigor 25%, quantitative traceability 25%, reproducibility/artifacts 20%, enterprise relevance 15%, and prior-work advance 15%. Scores are ordinal analyst judgments, not measurements.
  • Evidence labels: Paper Fact = directly reported by a paper or verified artifact; Independent Analysis = synthesis or critique; Enterprise Inference = a bounded future option; Uncertainty = evidence is missing, conditional, inaccessible, or not portable.
  • Safety boundary: none of these studies authorizes autonomous production learning, unrestricted agent action, or deployment of benchmark winners. All future options require isolated, reversible, non-destructive experiments and internal context before roadmap commitment.

Executive signal

Independent Analysis — the decisive control surface is the boundary between what a system optimizes, what it carries forward, and what the evaluator can still observe. HackProbe shows that repeated optimization against a visible score can separate that score from the underlying capability. The identity-handoff audit shows that an upstream-selected object can disappear before the reader even when conventional dataset recall looks acceptable. APT-RAG shows that fixed reasoning structures can under-retrieve when evidence requirements grow.

The shared design rule is protect → declare → trace → test → promote:

  1. Protect an independent capability target from the optimizer.
  2. Declare the identity, population, cutoff, and rule expected at every component boundary.
  3. Trace selected objects, retrieved evidence, reasoning nodes, evaluator versions, and costs.
  4. Test the mechanism—not only the final answer—under attacks, omissions, growing evidence load, and component substitution.
  5. Promote only after external replay and an accountable human gate.

This is not a claim that one detector, schema, or tree planner is production-ready. It is evidence that endpoint accuracy alone cannot distinguish model failure from measurement gaming, evidence loss, or planning under-expansion.

Top 3 Future Bets

  1. Identity-preserving retrieval contract — 87.6/100, Prototype Soon. Why it matters: exact key/title handoffs returned all 1,463 aligned objects in the reviewed audit, while body-only BM25 omitted 389 (26.6%) at top five and hybrid reranking omitted 14 (1.0%). Evidence that would change the recommendation: representative internal pipelines show no mismatch between selected and delivered identities, or explicit preservation reduces answer quality/cost beyond the value of fewer omissions. Next reversible action: replay 500 historical retrieval traces, record selected ID and delivered IDs, and compare explicit lookup with the current ranked handoff without altering live responses.
  2. Independent holdout control for adaptive AI — 87.1/100, Prototype Soon. Why it matters: HackProbe's secret, distribution-fixed comparison core outperformed a same-core level baseline by 0.100 AUROC, yet its 0.434 false-positive rate also demonstrates why this is a measurement experiment rather than a production gate. Evidence that would change the recommendation: the signal fails on a second evolution harness, cannot maintain secrecy, or causes more clean-run loss than recovered capability under representative workloads. Next reversible action: run a shadow monitor on an offline prompt-optimization loop with disjoint calibration, online probe, and final audit sets.
  3. Adaptive evidence-topology RAG harness — 84.7/100, Prototype Soon. Why it matters: APT-RAG improved answer F1 on two evidence-intensive benchmarks and cut latency relative to a fixed average-breadth variant, but absolute retrieval recall remained incomplete and planning errors can propagate. Evidence that would change the recommendation: matched internal replay shows no advantage after controlling for tokens, calls, model, corpus, and evaluator—or tail latency exceeds the service budget. Next reversible action: compare adaptive and fixed-depth retrieval on 100 non-sensitive, gold-evidence questions behind a no-user-impact shadow path.

Deep paper 1 — Detecting when optimization outruns capability

Citation: Rongxin Yang, Yang Liu, Shang Luo et al. Harness-agnostic detection and immunization of reward hacking in self-evolving language models. arXiv:2609.04665v1, publicly listed September 7, 2026; manuscript submitted September 4. Abstract · Full HTML · PDF. CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.

RUS: 82/100 — rigor 20/25; quantitative traceability 23/25; reproducibility/artifacts 10/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 14/15.

Contribution and quantitative trace

The paper introduces HackProbe, a black-box monitor for self-evolving language-model loops. It observes the visible optimization score and evaluates candidates on a secret comparison core whose distribution stays fixed. A separately rotated fresh layer is intended to reduce memorization without contaminating the longitudinal capability estimate.

  • Paper Fact: the host is a prompt-evolution loop over Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with a frozen proposer. Each run lasts 48 generations with eight candidates. Six seeds for each of four injected hacking channels produce 1,152 generation-level observations.
  • Paper Fact: four statistics address different failure modes: a score–capability level gap, change-point divergence between the visible score and the core estimate, capability stagnation, and conditional confidently-wrong behavior. Six one-sided p-values are fused, with empirical recalibration because the constituent tests are not independent.
  • Paper Fact: pooled hacking prevalence is 0.241. HackProbe reports AUROC 0.763 versus 0.663 for the strongest same-core baseline, precision 0.382, recall 0.845, false-positive rate 0.434, and mean detection delay 0.28 generations.
  • Paper Fact: at the tested core size of 240 probes, the paper says the monitor is on the steep portion of the detection curve; its own bound indicates roughly 600 probes would be needed for 0.90 detection in that setup.
  • Paper Fact: bandwidth-limited reselection (F2) is the only tested protection level with average recovered true capability under hacking greater than its clean-run cost: +0.052 versus -0.047, a ratio of 1.11. Most channel-specific effects are not individually significant.
  • Paper Fact: leave-one-hacking-type-out AUROC is 0.814 for judge collusion, 0.787 for self-consistency bias, 0.724 for contamination, and 0.600 for a gameable grader. Base rates differ, so precision and F1 are not comparable across those rows.
  • Paper Fact: the theoretical probe budget grows with the inverse square of the divergence gap. No matching lower bound is claimed. The return channel reveals at most log2 of the candidate-pool size per generation—three bits for eight candidates—but leakage accumulates over a run.
  • Artifact check: arXiv provides full HTML, PDF, and TeX source. No dedicated code, data, container, or experiment repository was verified from the paper record on September 7.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

The design has uncommon strengths: ground-truth attack labels, disjoint calibration and final-audit sets, paired longitudinal measurement, explicit clean-run cost, leave-one-channel-out transfer, a leakage accounting argument, and candid reporting of under-provisioning. The detector is evaluated as part of a recovery loop rather than only as a retrospective classifier.

The evidence remains narrow. One prompt-level host with injected attacks is the entire empirical basis for “harness-agnostic”; weight-level evolution, a second host, natural attacks, and adaptive adversaries are external-validity gaps. A 43.4% false-positive rate would interrupt too many honest generations for most production systems. The per-generation error target is not a run-level sequential guarantee. The fused tests share inputs, surface-format exploits can fall outside them, fresh-probe rotation protects against memorization rather than a learned low-complexity generator, and the secret core becomes a security asset with cumulative leakage risk. No cost, latency, model-provider, or artifact replication study is available.

Prior work on Goodhart effects and reward-model overoptimization uses held-out gold reward or post-hoc diagnosis; contamination and adversarial benchmark work tests fixed evaluation sets. HackProbe advances that literature by combining a longitudinal protected coordinate, change detection, and bandwidth-limited candidate reselection. It does not establish general immunity to reward hacking or safe autonomous self-improvement.

Enterprise readiness

Enterprise Inference — moderate for a shadow evaluation-control prototype; low for automatic promotion or rollback. The transferable elements are independent holdouts, fixed longitudinal comparators, rotating hardening probes, leakage budgets, and explicit clean-run cost. Before implementation, internal context is required on the optimized object, evaluator access, candidate-pool size, protected-data handling, incident tolerance, acceptable false-alarm budget, human promotion authority, and whether an independent ground truth exists.

Deep paper 2 — Adaptive topology for evidence-intensive RAG

Citation: Songeun Lee, Kyungjin Min, Injae Na et al. A Tree-based RAG Framework for Evidence-Intensive QA via Adaptive Planning and Topology-Aware Evidence Gathering. Findings of EMNLP 2026 (to appear); arXiv:2609.04981v1, publicly listed September 7, 2026. Abstract · Full HTML · Official code. CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 manuscript; repository license was not visible on the landing page checked.

RUS: 86/100 — rigor 21/25; quantitative traceability 23/25; reproducibility/artifacts 18/20; enterprise relevance 13/15; prior-work advance 11/15.

Contribution and quantitative trace

APT-RAG recursively builds a reasoning tree. For each node, a planner chooses to reuse sibling answers, retrieve externally, or decompose vertically. Evidence follows the reasoning topology, and sibling questions with overlapping evidence can be batched under a context-length constraint.

  • Paper Fact: MoNaCo contains 1,315 questions, averages 43.3 gold documents, and uses a 784,776-document/1,212,878-chunk corpus. QAMPARI contains 1,000 questions, averages 13.0 gold documents, and uses a 6,311,809-document/25,856,230-chunk corpus.
  • Paper Fact: all main methods use Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, top-20 retrieval, and Qwen3 4B or 30B instruction backbones. MoNaCo answer evaluation uses GPT-5.4; Gemini-3.5-Flash is reported as a corroborating judge. Experiments ran on an NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000.
  • Paper Fact: with the 4B backbone, APT-RAG reports MoNaCo F1 40.69 and retrieval recall 40.12; the strongest structured baseline F1 in that block is 36.32. QAMPARI F1 is 20.91 versus a best baseline of 18.76.
  • Paper Fact: with the 30B backbone, APT-RAG reports MoNaCo F1 50.84 and retrieval recall 50.79, versus Plan*RAG F1 47.03 and retrieval recall 39.28. QAMPARI F1 is 23.28 versus a best baseline of 21.37.
  • Paper Fact: the authors report every answer-F1 gain over baselines as statistically significant at p ≤ 0.001 and place 95% confidence intervals in the appendix.
  • Paper Fact: full-system latency in the MoNaCo 30B ablation is 104.59 seconds per question. A fixed average breadth variant reaches F1 49.79, retrieval recall 51.60, and latency 314.97 seconds. Removing vertical gathering reduces the system to a near-naive path with F1 46.64 and latency 1.90 seconds, exposing the accuracy–cost tradeoff.
  • Paper Fact: lateral gathering and evidence clustering reduce average latency by 41.4% and 8.2%, respectively. Tail savings are larger for evidence-heavy questions, but long-context processing can offset clustering gains.
  • Artifact check: the official repository contained five commits, source for APT-RAG and six baseline families, evaluation scripts, corpus/index builders, fixed 300-example ID lists, dependency metadata, and a Hugging Face artifact link. It intentionally omits benchmark questions, gold answers, generated outputs, full traces, and several large corpora; MoNaCo access is gated.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Strengths include two evidence scales, two model sizes, shared retrieval settings, multiple structured baselines, answer and retrieval metrics, cost instrumentation, component ablations, confidence intervals, judge comparison, and a substantial official implementation. The distinction between performance-oriented and efficiency-oriented modules makes the engineering tradeoffs inspectable.

Limitations are decision-critical. Both benchmarks are open-domain QA, not enterprise corpora with authorization, freshness, or confidential evidence. Absolute retrieval recall remains roughly 51% or lower in the reported blocks. The main MoNaCo metric depends on an LLM judge. The planner and answerability checker can create unnecessary branches or propagate errors. The best quality comes with roughly 105 seconds per question in the cited setting, while the fastest ablation is also materially less accurate. The paper does not report end-to-end cost in dollars, energy, concurrent load, adversarial retrieval, stale evidence, or production incident behavior. Gated or omitted artifacts reduce turnkey replication.

Linear iterative RAG can propagate early errors and under-explore; prior tree and graph systems impose fixed structures or premature sufficiency judgments. APT-RAG's advance is to couple adaptive tree expansion with lateral/vertical evidence movement and evidence-overlap batching. It does not show that a tree is always superior, that planner decisions are calibrated, or that benchmark gains survive enterprise access controls and changing corpora.

Enterprise readiness

Enterprise Inference — moderate for offline RAG replay; low for direct replacement of a production retrieval path. The reversible opportunity is a shadow comparison with matched model, corpus snapshot, token/call ceiling, evaluator, and gold evidence. Internal context required: evidence permissions, corpus scale and change rate, question mix, latency SLO, cost ceiling, acceptable retrieval omissions, citation requirements, and human-review obligations.

Deep paper 3 — Auditing whether the selected evidence object reaches the reader

Citation: Siddharth Vohra, Runmin Jiang, Xiaomo Li, and Min Xu. Does the Selected Object Reach the Reader? Auditing Identity Handoffs in Grounded Language-Model Pipelines. EMNLP 2026 GroundLM Workshop commitment record; arXiv:2609.04579v1, publicly listed September 7, 2026. Abstract · Full HTML · OpenReview record. CC BY 4.0.

RUS: 88/100 — rigor 22/25; quantitative traceability 23/25; reproducibility/artifacts 16/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 12/15.

Contribution and quantitative trace

The paper separates three stages often collapsed into one RAG score: selecting an object, returning passages tied to that object, and using those passages to answer. It defines the upstream selected object U separately from the dataset-traced object G and introduces a Returned-Object Profile (ROP) that declares identity resolution, expected population, returned-ID field, cutoff, membership rule, and run status.

  • Paper Fact: 600 HybridQA questions are processed by three selector families, producing 1,800 planned model–question records. Of 1,792 resolvable records, U and G differ on 329; top-five checks under original-question rankings disagree on 106 records (5.9%).
  • Paper Fact: among 1,463 aligned and resolvable records where U = G, exact key dereference and exact decoded-title equality return all 1,463 selected objects. Body-only BM25 omits 389 (26.6%) at cutoff five; ColBERTv2 omits 20; the final hybrid reranker omits 14 (1.0%).
  • Paper Fact: frozen reader comparisons associate presence of the aligned object with 28.6–31.0 points higher exact match across three arm pairs. The authors correctly label this association rather than causation because whole passage sets differ.
  • Paper Fact: a deletion experiment preselects 64 high-contrast items. Intact exact match is 92.7 versus 15.1 after deleting the traced passage, a paired difference of 77.6 points (95% interval 68.8–85.4). For 59 items with a length-matched sham deletion, sham exact match is 96.0 versus 14.7 for trace deletion, a difference of 81.4 (71.8–89.8); intact versus sham is -4.0 (-9.0–1.1).
  • Paper Fact: the corpus-wide decoded-title test covers 286,270 objects. Exact title equality omits zero at cutoffs 1, 5, and 20; title-only BM25 omits 15,379 at one, 7,667 at five, and 5,258 at 20. This is an identity-resolution test, not answer-quality evidence.
  • Paper Fact: ROP distinguishes PASS, FAIL, UNVERIFIABLE, and invalid-population states. A run expecting two records but observing only one is marked unverifiable even if the surviving record's pass fraction is 1.0.
  • Artifact check: the arXiv source describes hash-bound inputs, execution metadata, frozen rankings, reader records, reference evaluator, data, and offline replay. No standalone repository link was exposed in the arXiv abstract record; artifact provenance and packaging therefore have lower independent verifiability than APT-RAG's public repository.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

The study's strongest contribution is measurement decomposition. It fixes the selected identity while varying the handoff rule, separates benchmark coverage from selected-object delivery, declares the complete expected population, preserves terminal failures in planned cells, and uses a controlled passage deletion with a length-matched sham comparison. Its intervals resample question IDs rather than model rows independently.

Limits are explicit and important. The empirical setting is one English Wikipedia-derived table/text corpus. One recorded selector outcome per model–question pair does not estimate selector stability. The 329 U/G mismatches lack semantic annotation, so the study does not establish which object is correct. The deletion cohort is deliberately selected for high contrast; its 77.6-point effect cannot be generalized as average benefit. ROP v1 verifies hash and membership claims supplied to it but cannot prove when declarations were created or who produced the logs; those require external signing and governance. The hard-mode example uses a synthetic interface.

RAGChecker, MIRAGE, counterfactual RAG, and agentic tracing evaluate retrieval support, token attribution, or multi-step reasoning. This paper adds an interface-contract target: did the exact object chosen upstream survive the handoff to the reader, and was every expected record accounted for? It complements rather than replaces semantic relevance, answer correctness, and causal evidence-use tests.

Enterprise readiness

Enterprise Inference — high for a passive trace audit; low for forcing exact-object preservation in every workflow. A typed identity ledger can be added without changing retrieval outcomes. Whether a selected object must always be present is a domain contract, not a universal truth. Internal context required: canonical IDs, alias rules, chunk-to-object mapping, access-control behavior, acceptable alternatives, cutoffs, complete-run semantics, and evidence retention/privacy policy.

SciDocBench — valuable benchmark design, but advertised project artifact is not currently retrievable

Citation: Shenxi Wu, Yuhong Liu, Haosong Zhang et al. SciDocBench: A Workflow-Centered Benchmark and Data Pipeline for Scientific Document Understanding. arXiv:2609.05141v1, publicly listed September 7, 2026. Abstract · Full HTML. The advertised project URL, github.com/InternLM/SciDocBench, returned 404 during verification. CC BY 4.0.

Paper Fact: the benchmark has 124 expert-authored underlying questions, seven capability groups, 19 subtasks, five scientific domains, and four matched language/document-representation variants per question, creating 496 evaluation instances. The strongest evaluated system scores 62.6/100. SciDocIR represents text, equations, figures, tables, layout, cross-references, and provenance; SciDocDataset is reported as approximately 15,000 supervised and 8,000 reinforcement-learning samples over 14 verifiable subtasks.

Method signal: deterministic outputs use rules, executable artifacts use execution, and semantically judged outputs use an LLM judge. Generated records retain source page, block ID, bounding box, inserted content, text modification, and expected answer. The four variants support paired representation-sensitivity analysis.

Uncertainty: 496 instances are not 496 independent problems. Cross-document tasks span only two to four papers, synthetic evidence cannot reproduce every natural failure, and OCR/layout errors can enter both benchmark construction and training data. On September 7, the project URL advertised by the arXiv record returned HTTP 404, while a third-party evaluation toolkit showed partial SciDocBench support. Until the authors' artifact is accessible and its data/license can be verified, the benchmark remains Watchlist rather than an implementation dependency.

Agent-team interchangeability — coordination costs move before task scores, but evidence is dyadic and artifact-light

Citation: Jianxin Gao, Tianyi Yu, Linna Deng et al. Testing Interchangeability in LLM Agent Teams. arXiv:2609.05279v1, publicly listed September 7, 2026. Abstract · Full HTML. CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.

Paper Fact: eight teams per main setting train for ten episodes, keep private notebooks, and then swap role-matched agents across four independent pairs. Conditions replay identical held-out work and include a placebo roster disruption. A swap raises communication per unit progress by 16% in low-coupling Collab-Overcooked, 51% in high coupling, and 63% in Hanabi, while task scores move less.

Paper Fact: swapping the initiating role costs 7.5 progress points versus 2.5 for the responder in the high-coupling condition. Greedy decoding lowers the partner-specific ratio from 0.27 to 0.14 while progress rises from 63.7 to 66.2. Extending formation from 5 to 20 episodes raises the ratio from 0.17 to 0.41.

Uncertainty: the independent swap unit is four pairs per main setting; the systems are dyadic; ten formation episodes are short; the protocol-signature correlation is exploratory; deletion controls are not length matched; and the intervention suggested by the results—fixing communication protocols—is not tested. No code, traces, or data repository was verified. The result supports a swap-regression hypothesis, not a general claim about production multi-agent failover.

Cross-paper synthesis

  1. Independent Analysis — optimize and audit on different channels. A score visible to an adaptive system becomes part of its environment. Capability evidence needs isolation, distribution control, spend accounting, and a final disjoint audit.
  2. Independent Analysis — IDs are first-class evidence. “Relevant text was retrieved” is weaker than “the object selected upstream was present, authorized, and traceable in the reader context.” Semantic correctness remains a separate layer.
  3. Independent Analysis — planning depth should follow evidence demand. Fixed breadth is cheap but can under-cover; unbounded adaptive trees improve coverage but raise cost and error-propagation risk. The control variable is a typed evidence requirement plus budget, not “use agentic RAG.”
  4. Independent Analysis — complete populations prevent deceptive pass rates. A missing trace cannot silently disappear from the denominator. PASS, FAIL, UNVERIFIABLE, and INVALID must remain distinct operational states.
  5. Independent Analysis — benchmark gains and production readiness diverge. HackProbe has a 43.4% FPR; APT-RAG retrieves only about half the gold evidence in its strongest cited block; the identity deletion effect comes from a selected cohort. All three are useful because their limitations define safe experiments.
  6. Independent Analysis — observability should span coordination and cognition. Watchlist evidence suggests component swaps can preserve task success while communication cost rises. A robust control plane needs objective, identity, topology, team, latency, and cost telemetry together.

  • Architectural placement: retrieval/knowledge, orchestration, observability, security/governance.
  • Expected business value: quality, reliability, risk reduction, customer experience.
  • Technical value: localizes failure to selection, handoff, retrieval, or reading; stabilizes denominators; enables offline replay.

2. Independent holdout control for adaptive AI

  • Category: Evaluation & Observability / Safety & Governance.
  • Research signal and supporting papers: HackProbe separates a visible optimization metric from a secret, distribution-fixed capability proxy and reports 0.763 AUROC, but 0.434 FPR. SciDocBench's paired variants reinforce the need to separate system capability from representation artifacts.
  • Evidence label: Paper Fact for the monitored divergence; Enterprise Inference for enterprise integration.
  • Enterprise problem/opportunity: repeated tuning, prompt evolution, or feedback selection can overfit the evaluator while dashboards still improve.
  • Future capability: a protected evaluator lane with disjoint calibration, online comparison, rotating hardening probes, one-time final audit, evaluator versioning, and human promotion control.
  • Architectural placement: evaluation, model gateway, developer platform, security/governance.
  • Expected business value: reliability, risk reduction, quality, strategic differentiation.
  • Technical value: detects score–capability divergence before a candidate becomes the only retained state.
  • Evidence maturity/readiness: strong controlled design, one host and high FPR; suitable only for shadow use. Confidence: Moderate. Horizon: Now. Effort: M.
  • Dependencies/prerequisites: independent gold data, secrets management, replayable candidates, immutable evaluator registry, false-alarm budget, human owner.
  • Risks: probe leakage, stale holdouts, biased gold data, overfitting to the monitor, excessive interruption, hidden evaluator drift, cumulative information leakage.
  • Build/Buy/Partner: Build the isolation, registry, and promotion policy; partner/buy domain evaluation content where independent expertise is required.
  • Minimum reversible experiment: attach a read-only monitor to a sandbox prompt optimizer; reveal no item-level feedback to the optimizer.

3. Adaptive evidence-topology RAG shadow harness

Shared enabling foundations unlock several backlog items at once:

  • a versioned model and evaluator gateway;
  • a typed context/evidence service with canonical IDs, provenance, authorization, and deletion semantics;
  • orchestration that retains candidate histories and can replay or roll back;
  • an evaluation registry separating training, tuning, online comparison, and final audit sets;
  • trace telemetry covering selection, retrieval, planning, reading, tool use, cost, and complete expected populations;
  • policy enforcement and identity/authorization at every evidence and action boundary;
  • a human-review layer with named promotion authority and explicit unverifiable states;
  • cost and latency telemetry tied to valid outcomes, not raw requests;
  • isolated experiment infrastructure that cannot mutate production state.

No current enterprise stack, vendor, data platform, security posture, or maturity level is assumed. Architecture decisions require internal inventories of workflows, data classes, canonical identifiers, side effects, incident history, SLOs, model contracts, evaluator ownership, and regulatory obligations.

Implementation priorities

Now — measurement before behavior change

  1. Add selected-ID, returned-ID, cutoff, component version, and complete-population fields to one low-risk RAG trace.
  2. Inventory evaluation sets by purpose: training, tuning, online comparison, hardening, and final audit. Record every exposure path.
  3. Establish PASS, FAIL, UNVERIFIABLE, and INVALID as separate telemetry states.
  4. Build matched offline baselines for retrieval quality, evidence coverage, latency, tokens, calls, and authorization.

Next 3–6 months — isolated comparative pilots

  1. Run the identity-handoff replay and investigate every omission before enforcing preservation.
  2. Shadow an independent capability comparator on a non-production optimizer; keep item-level feedback air-gapped.
  3. Benchmark adaptive versus fixed-depth RAG under identical model, corpus, budget, and evaluator.
  4. Create a minimum typed-evidence graph spanning documents, objects, chunks, citations, and lineage.

6–12 months — foundations and governance evidence

  1. Threat-model test-bank access, leakage, rotation, and retirement.
  2. Add signed provenance for evaluator declarations and trace populations.
  3. Pilot agent-swap tests only where persistent multi-agent coordination already exists.
  4. Expand document evaluation only with licensed data, expert adjudication, and deterministic validators.

12+ months — conditional capabilities

Consider autonomous adaptation only if independent evidence demonstrates low run-level error, reliable rollback, external validity, secure holdouts, bounded side effects, and accountable human promotion. Until then, keep self-evolving candidates inside research sandboxes.

Knowledge reinforcement — five checks to remember

  1. What was optimized? If the evaluator was visible to the optimizer, treat improvement as provisional.
  2. Which exact object crossed the boundary? Record identity, aliases, authorization, cutoff, and delivered IDs.
  3. Is the denominator complete? Missing traces are not passes; they are unverifiable or invalid.
  4. Did complexity scale with evidence need? Compare evidence coverage and cost by required-document count, not only average F1.
  5. Can the result survive a component change? Replay evaluator, retriever, model, planner, and agent substitutions separately.

Reproducibility and evidence audit

  • HackProbe: full manuscript/source available; no public experimental package verified. Reproduction would require implementing the host, injected channels, probe-bank governance, baselines, and calibration protocol.
  • APT-RAG: strongest artifact package in the briefing. Code, baselines, evaluators, corpus builders, scripts, dependencies, and subset IDs are present. Several datasets are gated or externally hosted; full outputs and traces are excluded, so exact numerical reproduction is not turnkey.
  • Identity handoff: the paper describes an executable, hash-bound replay with frozen records. Because no standalone artifact URL appears in the arXiv metadata, independent retrieval and packaging verification are incomplete.
  • SciDocBench: paper and schema are accessible, but the advertised GitHub URL returned 404 on September 7. Treat dataset and training-resource availability as unverified.
  • Agent interchangeability: full paper is accessible; no code/data/trace artifact was verified.

Final decision summary

The evidence supports three reversible prototypes: an identity-handoff audit, an independent holdout comparator, and an adaptive evidence-topology replay. It supports three shared foundations: decomposition-aware traces, typed evidence/provenance, and evaluation-bank isolation. It does not support automatic production promotion, universal tree-RAG replacement, or the assumption that a benchmark winner preserves the right evidence.

The strongest near-term investment is not a new model. It is a verifiable contract connecting objective, object identity, evidence topology, evaluator, and complete execution record—so the organization can tell whether a system improved, gamed the test, lost the evidence, or simply spent more to look successful.
