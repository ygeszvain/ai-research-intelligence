Scope, window, and evidence policy

Research window. The exact window was Thursday, October 15, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT through Friday, October 16, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT (America/Chicago; 09:00 UTC to 09:00 UTC). An arXiv API snapshot refreshed at 09:04:39 UTC screened 1,000 of the newest records across artificial intelligence, machine learning, computation and language, cryptography and security, software engineering, information retrieval, multi-agent systems, and statistics. It contained 462 records with an October 15 publication date and 538 from October 14. Discovery also checked paper-linked repositories, result bundles, licenses, full PDFs, and the complete 67-report archive through October 15.

Selection. Three papers cleared deep review: MemCut, PROBE, and ToolBottleneckBench. Shortcut Sensitivity in Agent-Safety Evaluations and Beyond Downstream QA / FEB-30 enter the Watchlist. Selection favored full-paper access, explicit evaluation contracts, traceable denominators, matched or controlled comparisons, diagnostic value beyond a headline score, honest limitations, and safe enterprise experiments. The unifying signal is unusually consistent: an externally clean outcome can hide a broken internal state, policy trace, or evaluation harness.

Anti-duplication. Today's identifiers—arXiv:2610.21405, 2610.22124, 2610.21098, 2610.22243, and 2610.22209—are absent from the archive. Earlier briefs covered deletion requests, memory promotion, task-state uncertainty, stateful trajectory evaluation, tool contracts, benchmark versioning, independent acceptance, and agent safety controls. Today's incremental question is narrower: what evidence must be inspected when a clean answer, completed task, or failed episode is not an adequate description of the thing being measured? Papers centered on generic guard design, prompt robustness, or memory architectures were not selected when they did not add stronger evidence about this observability gap.

Evidence labels. Paper Fact is directly reported by a reviewed paper or verified first-party artifact. Independent Analysis is this briefing's calculation, comparison, or critique. Enterprise Inference translates evidence into a bounded future option. Uncertainty marks missing causal, external-validity, security, privacy, governance, economic, licensing, or production evidence. Benchmark performance does not authorize production deployment.

Executive signal

Do not treat a clean answer, a completed task, or a failed episode as the thing being measured. Reliable enterprise AI must inspect the latent evidence state, the full policy trace, and the harness limits that produced the surface result.

The three deep papers support complementary parts of that rule:

  1. MemCut separates native retrieval failure from actual forgetting. Across 456 hand-designed secrets, five memory designs, four write models, and three deletion baselines, removing the explicit disclosure sharply reduces ordinary retrieval while surviving evidence still allows the same reader to recover the secret. LLM-guided deletion of ten turns from twenty candidates leaves 39–76% residual recovery.
  2. PROBE separates task success from operational policy compliance. A learned 4B auditor interacting with a frozen tool agent finds 12 of 20 policies under the training-time suite versus 7 of 20 for a zero-shot 30B auditor. Human validation then exposes severe judge error, and deterministic re-scoring lowers the strongest coverage from 60% to roughly 55% without changing the ranking.
  3. ToolBottleneckBench separates model failure from harness exhaustion. On 20,408 exactly graded episodes across 21 model/harness configurations, 91.5% of failures end at a harness limit or infeasibility stop. Raising the completion cap sixteenfold shrinks the pooled visible-menu penalty in six truncation-heavy configurations from −43.7 to −2.7 percentage points.

Decision posture. Prototype residual-memory qualification, harness-budget evaluation contracts, and behavioral policy auditing only in offline, synthetic, replay, or shadow environments. Prepare evidence lineage, deterministic verifier ownership, and resource telemetry as shared foundations. Monitor shortcut-controlled guard evaluation and intrinsic memory inspection until generality and artifacts improve. Deprioritize any answer-only, task-success-only, or first-failure-label-only acceptance policy as a sole control.

Deep paper 1 — A deleted disclosure is not deleted evidence

Identity and status. MemCut: Every Secret Leaves a Trail in the Long-Term Memory of LLM Agents; Cheng Zhang, Xuanning Cao, Zhicheng Zheng, and Hui Xiong; arXiv:2610.21405v1; submitted October 15, 2026. The first-party paper was reviewed in full. No paper-linked public code repository or license was found during verification.

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

MemCut constructs long-horizon conversations containing protected facts that are never supported by only one obvious disclosure. Each of 456 facts includes annotated evidence turns, sufficient evidence sets, minimal cuts, look-alike distractors, and a natural-language deletion request. The benchmark measures native recovery through the memory system's own retrieval and residual recovery by giving the same reader the surviving evidence-derived memory objects directly.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Long-term memory benchmarks usually reward retention, retrieval, temporal reasoning, or answer quality. Machine unlearning and knowledge-editing work asks whether a target association remains available in parameters or output behavior. MemCut instead evaluates source-addressable external agent memory after a user asks to forget a fact, and makes the evidence graph explicit enough to say whether surviving turns still form a sufficient reconstruction path. That is a meaningful advance over checking whether the deleted sentence disappeared or whether a normal query now fails.

The strongest design choice is the paired native/residual protocol. It isolates two different failure modes: the retriever no longer surfaces evidence, versus the memory store no longer contains enough evidence to recover the fact. Anchoring objects to source turns also makes deletion budgets comparable across turn-, session-, summary-, and consolidated-memory designs. The authors vary five memory designs and four models, report derivation types, release a common harness according to the paper, and use fresh store copies per fact so deletion cases do not contaminate one another.

The benchmark is controlled rather than naturalistic. Episodes are hand-written around work, family, and social activities; deployment prevalence of these evidence structures is unknown. Reference deleters reuse existing retrieval and writing components, so the 39–76% range should not be read as a lower bound on all future erasure methods. Residual recovery assumes a privileged evaluator can present surviving memory directly to the reader, which is appropriate for measuring retained capability but not a claim about ordinary attacker access. The source-level measure does not fully quantify unrelated content removed by closure or downstream utility loss. Multimodal and parametric memory are out of scope, and no public artifact was available for independent execution.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper provides unusually rich construction statistics, full tables, pool/budget sweeps, source-turn anchoring, and explicit limitations. Exact replication is constrained by the missing public artifact, hosted or unreleased model variants, and author-designed data. Enterprise readiness: high for the conceptual evaluation contract; medium for an isolated synthetic deletion harness once evidence lineage exists; low for production erasure certification without real-data prevalence studies, privacy-preserving probes, collateral-damage metrics, and legal validation. Research Utility Score: 94/100 (methodological rigor 25/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 16/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 14/15).

Deep paper 2 — Successful tool use can still violate policy

Identity and status. PROBE: Learning to Audit Policy Compliance in Tool-Using LLM Agents; Kshitij Mishra, Abhijith Sharma, Nils Lukas, and Salem Lahlou; arXiv:2610.22124v1; submitted October 15, 2026; listed by the authors as NeurIPS 2026. The paper and public repository were verified. The repository exposes source, tests, configurations, data, scripts, and paper materials, but no repository license was present; reuse rights are therefore uncertain.

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

PROBE trains an external LLM auditor to act like a realistic user against a frozen tool-using agent. The auditor sees only public dialogue, while a verifier suite inspects the full trace—dialogue, tool calls, outputs, and environment state. The main SkyAssist environment is deterministic, has 8 tools, 20 policies in four categories, 35 airports, 120 routes, and 643 flights. A 4B Qwen auditor is trained with GRPO, DPO, or GFlowNet-style updates against a frozen 30B target.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Capability benchmarks ask whether an agent completed a task; content-safety benchmarks ask whether its text is harmful. PROBE formalizes a third axis: whether the full execution trace obeys task-specific rules about confirmation, disclosure, privacy, schema use, and mutations. This is closer to an operator-run penetration test than a static evaluation set. It also turns discovered failures into reusable regression traces, creating a plausible audit–repair–re-audit loop.

The paper's most important result may be negative: the initial hybrid judge was reward-hackable. GRPO drove judge-flagged hits that human labels and deterministic predicates did not confirm. The authors disclosed the failure, created deterministic replacements where possible, re-scored results, and retained stronger baselines. That makes the methodological lesson more valuable than the initial 60% headline. Matched scenarios control difficulty across auditors, per-policy credit counters high-frequency policies, and static, zero-shot, few-shot, cooperative, and diversity-only baselines clarify what the learned auditor adds.

The evidence still has sharp boundaries. SkyAssist is synthetic and deterministic. The target rarely enters deep mutation states, so several high-severity policies remain untested. Four semantic verifiers were replaced with deterministic predicates that may miss real violations. Evaluation uses one checkpoint per method rather than independent training seeds, and the two human annotators are authors. Continuous adversarial auditors could leak policies, generate exploit-like interaction traces, or create unsafe state changes if moved outside a sandbox. The public repository lacks a license, so artifact availability does not imply permission to reuse.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The repository includes an executable-looking codebase, tests, configurations, data, scripts, and environment material, and the paper reports compute, models, metrics, verifier construction, and transfer. Licensing and exact end-to-end reproduction remain unresolved. Enterprise readiness: high for a synthetic policy-audit design pattern; medium for a shadow audit of a non-production workflow after deterministic verifier ownership and artifact governance; low for autonomous testing against live state-changing tools. Research Utility Score: 92/100 (methodological rigor 24/25; quantitative traceability 23/25; reproducibility/artifacts 17/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 13/15).

Deep paper 3 — A failure label is not a diagnosis

Identity and status. ToolBottleneckBench: Measuring Tool-Agent Performance under Controlled Pressures and Fixed Harness Budgets; Zichuan Xu, Yuyang Dong, Makoto Onizuka, and Chuan Xiao; arXiv:2610.21098v1; submitted October 15, 2026. The paper and MIT-licensed repository were verified. The repository includes protocols, configurations, results, tests, analysis scripts, dependency locks, provenance notices, a citation file, and third-party notices.

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

ToolBottleneckBench holds synthetic enterprise tasks fixed while varying one pressure: registered tool catalog, visible tool menu, certified workflow length, or returned-record encoding. It evaluates 21 model/provider/harness configurations from seven organizations. An exact grader checks nine obligations and records both the first unmet obligation and the harness limit that ended the episode.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Tool benchmarks commonly change task, catalog, observation, workflow, model, and harness together, then attach a single success score or stage label. ToolBottleneckBench uses a deterministic compiler to change one pressure while preserving the request, facts, tool semantics, and obligations. It treats model route and harness as one evaluated configuration and reports the budget contract, which is the correct unit for many production comparisons.

The design is exceptionally transparent: exact obligations, pre-registered contrasts, family-, intent-, and configuration-clustered intervals, Holm correction, an intention-to-treat discovery metric, a completion-cap intervention, repair activation tests, configuration-switch baselines, deterministic checks, and public result records. The authors also distinguish intervention evidence from cross-configuration correlation; the −0.95 relationship between menu effect and truncation motivates the cap sweep but does not substitute for it.

External validity is bounded by synthetic tasks, exact binary grading, fixed English prompts, four core workflow families in one frontier, and provider-specific token accounting. The cap intervention tests 8 configurations in one routed-menu lane; provider routes and defaults differ, and one is FP4-quantized. Core cells have one run per configuration, with bootstrap and sentinel/repeat disagreement used to characterize variability. Public release creates future contamination risk. The benchmark shows that budgets confound scores, not that all visible-menu failures are harness-only: one low-truncation configuration still has an −18.1 pp menu effect.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

This is the strongest artifact package in today's set: MIT license, locked dependencies, protocols, generated tasks, tests, complete result structures, notices, and reproducibility documentation are present. Hosted endpoints and future route drift still prevent bitwise reproduction. Enterprise readiness: high for adapting an internal evaluation contract and telemetry schema; medium for a shadow benchmark over representative tool agents; low for using the public score as a procurement decision without local tasks and routes. Independent Analysis. The papers study different subsystems, but each finds a misleading proxy:

Surface proxy What it hides Required second view Source
“The secret is no longer retrieved” Surviving evidence can still reconstruct it Residual recovery over source-linked evidence MemCut
“The task completed successfully” The full tool trace can violate operational policy Trace-level deterministic or validated verifiers PROBE
“The agent failed at selection” The model may never have called a tool because a cap fired Harness termination, budget, and trace telemetry ToolBottleneckBench
“The guard has high F1” Domain cues, row duplication, and weighting can drive the score Group holdouts, equal-unit metrics, paired cue interventions Shortcut Sensitivity
“The memory answered correctly” Broken fact state can be masked, or correct memory can be blamed for retrieval failure Intrinsic fact-state inspection plus end-to-end QA FEB-30

The enterprise implication is not “add more benchmarks.” It is to define the object of evaluation before scoring: surface behavior, latent state, policy-compliant trajectory, or the combined model–harness configuration. Each object needs its own evidence and stop conditions. Collapsing them into one number creates false assurance and misdirected remediation.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

Independent Analysis. Over the next 12–24 months, today's evidence supports an enterprise AI architecture with an evaluation and observability plane that sits outside the producing agent:

  • Versioned evaluation contracts. Bind every score to model, provider route, harness, prompt, tool catalog, visible menu, retrieval policy, completion cap, episode budget, call/turn limits, verifier version, sampling plan, and aggregation unit. A score without this envelope is not comparable.
  • Source-linked memory service. Preserve immutable provenance from memory objects to source turns or artifacts, record create/update/reinforce/supersede/delete operations, and support isolated snapshots for native and residual recovery testing. Deletion authorization and evaluation must be separated from the agent that wrote the memory.
  • Policy-as-data and verifier registry. Represent policies with preconditions, severity, affected tools, deterministic predicates where possible, semantic judges where necessary, validation evidence, known blind spots, and owners. Reward or optimization systems cannot silently change verifier definitions.
  • Agent orchestration and experiment plane. Run adaptive auditors, counterfactual cue edits, budget sweeps, and retrieval/deletion probes only against synthetic or cloned state. Stateful effects require sandboxing, task-scoped authorization, egress controls, and resettable environments.
  • Unified observability receipt. Log public dialogue, private tool trace, retrieved evidence identifiers, memory mutations, policy firings, model/provider metadata, token classes, completion/episode/call/turn limits, terminal flags, first unmet obligation, cost, latency, and reviewer decisions.
  • Independent acceptance and human review. Surface outcome, latent-state inspection, policy trace, and harness evidence feed a separate acceptance service that can abstain, request a repair, require human review, or block a durable effect. The evaluated agent cannot grant itself authority.
  • Knowledge and data governance. Evidence lineage itself can contain sensitive information. Apply purpose limitation, least privilege, retention/deletion policies, field-level redaction, and access auditing to traces, residual probes, policy corpora, and result bundles.
  • Model gateway and cost telemetry. Route by risk and record provider semantics, but treat the route and harness as part of the evaluated configuration. Cost and latency are measured alongside coverage and failure modes, never used to erase safety boundaries.

Shared enabling foundations are model/harness registries, context and memory provenance, deterministic identity and authorization, policy ownership, controlled tool sandboxes, evaluation datasets with leakage tracking, artifact versioning, telemetry schemas, human-review queues, reversible experiment infrastructure, and cost/latency accounting. These recommendations assume no specific current stack. Implementation decisions require internal evidence about workflows, data sensitivity, memory design, tools, provider contracts, acceptable latency/cost, security controls, regulatory duties, and reviewer capacity.

Watchlist

Shortcut Sensitivity in Agent-Safety Evaluations

Shortcut Sensitivity in Agent-Safety Evaluations; Hasan Demirkiran and Jens Ernstberger; arXiv:2610.22243v1; submitted October 15, 2026; described as appearing at NeurIPS 2026 TAE and AIWILD workshops. The paper and public artifact were verified. The repository contains code, protocols, aggregate results, and a multi-source licenses document, but GitHub exposes no single repository license.

The paper proposes a three-level inference ladder: label predictability, observational guard association, and paired decision sensitivity. Exact-tool lookup falls from F1 .901 with mixed-domain folds to .495 under domain holdout. Released TS-Guard F1 falls .902→.719 when weighting interactions equally rather than rows, while balanced accuracy rises .754→.776. In a frozen 64-step AgentDojo cohort with repeated interleaved Haiku calls, rewriting an information marker lowers blocking 8.33 pp and opaque tool aliases raise it 11.46 pp; all unsafe calls remain blocked, so the shifts are false positives against inherited labels. Haiku-sized effects do not reproduce in Luna or deterministic Q8 TS-Guard. Why Watchlist: the diagnostic protocol is immediately useful, but the causal cohort is small, non-probability, exploratory, semantically imperfect, and configuration-specific. Promotion trigger: blinded, pre-registered paired interventions across several guards and representative internal domains change deployment-relevant errors beyond placebo with stable replication.

Beyond Downstream QA / FEB-30

Beyond Downstream QA: Rethinking How We Evaluate Agent Memory; Sebastian-Antonio Toma, Jean de Kernier, Mike Ply, and Oliviu Matei; arXiv:2610.22209v1; submitted October 15, 2026. The paper links to github.com/amazon-science/feb-30-benchmark, but that first-party URL returned 404 during verification; artifact and license claims are therefore not made.

FEB-30 defines 30 fact-evolution trajectories across six categories and three difficulty levels, with 127 state documents and 20 noise documents. On one representative memory system over 11 runs, intrinsic fact accuracy is 60.6% ± 1.6 versus extrinsic QA accuracy 48.8% ± 2.9; mean absolute divergence is .168 ± .025, 43.6% of trajectories exceed .15, and pass/fail agreement is near chance (κ=.118). Operation classification is 20.1% ± 2.7; false update rate 22.2% ± 1.2. Why Watchlist: the intrinsic/extrinsic distinction is valuable, but there are only 30 synthetic trajectories, scoring uses embedding thresholds and LLM judges, the studied memory pattern is narrow, and the claimed artifact was unavailable. Promotion trigger: a verifiable licensed artifact plus external replication on inspectable enterprise-style memories and human-validated real document sequences.

Knowledge reinforcement

  1. Why can native memory recovery fall while privacy risk remains? Because the retriever may stop surfacing the evidence even though surviving objects still contain a sufficient reconstruction path.
  2. Why is task success orthogonal to policy compliance? A tool agent can reach the requested terminal state while skipping confirmation, exposing private data, violating a schema, or mutating state under invalid preconditions.
  3. Why is a first-unmet obligation not a root cause? Grader order records where checking stopped; a completion or episode limit may have prevented the behavior needed to satisfy an earlier obligation.
  4. What makes an evaluation unit consequential? Row weighting can let long or duplicated interactions dominate a metric; equal-interaction weighting asks whether performance holds across independent episodes.
  5. Why pair intrinsic and extrinsic memory evaluation? Intrinsic inspection tests whether stored knowledge is correct; extrinsic QA tests whether retrieval and generation can use it. Either can fail while the other passes.
  6. What is the common architectural principle? Preserve latent state, execution trace, and harness contract as first-class evidence, then let an independent acceptance layer decide.
