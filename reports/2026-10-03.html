Scope, window, and evidence policy

Research window. The exact window was Friday, October 2, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT through Saturday, October 3, 2026 at 4:00 AM CDT (America/Chicago; 09:00 UTC to 09:00 UTC). Because arXiv publishes no new Saturday announcement batch, the search expanded to 72 hours, back to September 30 at 4:00 AM CDT. All five selections were submitted October 1 and announced October 2. Titles and identifiers were checked against the 54-report archive through October 2; none was previously covered.

Discovery. Screening covered current arXiv artificial-intelligence, machine-learning, language, information-retrieval, security, multi-agent, human-computer-interaction, software-engineering, and systems listings, plus Nature Portfolio, Science, ACL Anthology, OpenReview, first-party project pages, repositories, and dataset hosts. Selection favored causal identification, matched or repeated controls, inspectable denominators, explicit failure mechanisms, usable artifacts, and enterprise decision value. All five selected works are preprints; none supplies peer-reviewed production evidence.

Anti-duplication. Yesterday’s five papers—arXiv:2610.01618, 2610.02202, 2610.01833, 2610.02200, and 2610.01834—were excluded, as were all earlier archive identifiers. Prior briefings already addressed memory curation, agent-harness qualification, provenance, process conformance, task-state uncertainty, and external control planes. Today’s incremental question is narrower: can the system identify which experience caused utility, place each experience in a reversible component, and qualify the harness or proof authority that mediates it?

Evidence labels. Paper Fact is directly reported in a paper or verified first-party artifact. Independent Analysis is this briefing’s comparison, calculation, or methodological judgment. Enterprise Inference translates evidence into a bounded future option. Uncertainty marks missing causal, external-validity, security, privacy, governance, economic, adoption, artifact, licensing, or replication evidence. Provider-scale experiments were not rerun. No benchmark result authorizes production deployment.

Executive signal

Reliable enterprise AI must distinguish what can be measured, where experience should live, and who is trusted to certify the evidence. Intervene at the true causal boundary, qualify the harness as part of the system, and keep context, weights, and proofs separable and reversible.

The three deep papers triangulate this claim:

  1. Causal Memory Policy shows that storage-level perturbations often do not change retrieved context and therefore cannot identify a memory’s utility. Randomizing the actual retrieval exposure raises prediction of useful memories, but also exposes a fundamental support problem: memories never surfaced by the candidate selector cannot be evaluated by the policy.
  2. Mingbird evaluates four agent harnesses with four small open models on 18 artifact-producing tasks. The harness with explicit planning, verification, rollback, and local execution substantially outperforms alternatives—especially at 2B parameters—while the paper’s own external comparison shows that a sound harness still does not erase model capability differences.
  3. Component Routing finds that stable, recurrent locator and lesson knowledge benefits from weight updates, while procedures and transient state facts benefit from contextual reuse. A simple routing rule predicts the preferred destination across held-out model-family cells, but the absence of released artifacts and the use of shared authored component extractors sharply limit readiness.

The Watchlist extends the control boundary. Can AI Oversight Be Zero Knowledge? proves an impossibility for general oracle-aided computation in the random-oracle model and a positive construction only when the oracle signs its answers—moving trust to the signer and key custody. AI Assessment Sandbox Configurator translates EU regulatory-sandbox requirements into a plugin architecture with evidence and role controls, but several requirements remain only partially exercised.

Decision posture. Prototype retrieval-support audits, randomized memory exposure in shadow mode, harness-qualified local execution, and component-routing experiments with immutable lineage and rollback. Prepare shared exposure/effect ledgers, componentized experience registries, evidence schemas, and signer/key-governance models. Monitor zero-knowledge oversight until there is an implementation and multi-party threat-model evidence. Deprioritize utility-only autonomous eviction and indiscriminate whole-trajectory fine-tuning.

Top 3 Future Bets

  1. Retrieval-support audit and reversible memory exposure gate — 92.7/100. Why it matters: an eviction score is not causal evidence if the candidate selector never exposes the memory or the intervention leaves the prompt unchanged. Evidence that would change the recommendation: representative internal stores show near-complete support, storage perturbations reliably change retrieved context, and simpler recency/frequency policies match causal estimates. Next reversible action: shadow-log 500 queries, candidate pools, propensities, exposed memories, answers, and delayed outcomes; randomize only two non-critical context slots under a hard privacy and latency budget.
  2. Harness-qualified local execution lane — 92.0/100. Why it matters: a small local model can become operationally useful when planning, tool contracts, verification, and rollback are qualified as one system, but a defective harness can also hide or fabricate capability. Evidence that would change the recommendation: representative internal tasks show no meaningful quality, privacy, latency, or cost advantage over an approved remote lane, or independent security review finds uncontrolled effects. Next reversible action: replay 30 sanitized, artifact-scored tasks across two local models, the candidate harness, and one approved baseline with network disabled and all writes redirected to disposable workspaces.
  3. Experience-component routing with versioned rollback — 88.7/100. Why it matters: putting every successful trajectory into weights or every lesson into context creates avoidable staleness, cost, and interference. Evidence that would change the recommendation: internal task families show no repeatable destination effect, or component extraction errors erase the routed advantage. Next reversible action: classify 100 historical lessons into locator, procedure, state-fact, and lesson candidates; replay them in context-only, adapter-only, and routed shadow arms without updating a production model.

Deep paper 1 — Memory utility is a retrieval-mediated causal effect

Identity and status. Causal Memory Policy: Making Memory Utility Identifiable by Intervening on Retrieval; Arman Behnam and Binghui Wang; arXiv:2610.02070v1; submitted October 1, 2026 at 17:11:25 UTC; preprint; CC BY 4.0. The paper links implementation, protocols, raw outputs, seeds, and cost accounting. (paper · code)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

The paper defines the utility of a stored memory as the causal effect of including that memory in the model-visible retrieved context for a query. A policy fixes k randomized exposure slots, records each memory’s known inclusion propensity, and estimates per-query effects with inverse-propensity weighting. Balanced assignment controls exposure counts more tightly than independent randomization.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Most memory systems infer utility from retrieval frequency, similarity, recency, downstream reward, leave-one-out deletion, or heuristic importance. Those signals confound a memory’s intrinsic usefulness with whether the retriever chose it and whether the prompt actually changed. This paper’s contribution is to state the estimand at the model-input boundary and design a randomized exposure mechanism with known propensities.

That identification gain has a cost. Exposure requirements scale with pool size divided by randomized slots; uniform support is unrealistic for large enterprise stores. Forced exposure can displace useful context, raise latency, leak sensitive information, or degrade answers. Self-normalized inverse-propensity estimates reduce variance but are biased in finite samples. Benchmark-required memories are imperfect proxies for business utility. The experiment studies API models and labeled conversational-memory benchmarks, not regulated records, long-lived teams, or adversarial data. Query-distribution shift can invalidate aggregated policy decisions, and the paper itself shows that per-query effects do not produce a reliable global retention ranking.

The most important negative result is therefore architectural: no estimator can recover effects for memories that never enter the candidate pool. A causal memory policy needs a candidate-support contract, not only a better scorer.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The authors report estimation code, assignment logic, harnesses, protocols, raw outputs, seeds, model-call counts, and a cost ledger: 80,187 calls costing $193.06 on a CPU workstation with no GPU. Known discrepancies are documented rather than silently normalized. Exact results still depend on external model APIs and benchmark labels. Enterprise readiness: high for logging candidate support, visible exposures, propensities, and intervention identity; medium for a tightly bounded shadow randomization; low for production eviction or uniform exploration of sensitive stores. Research Utility Score: 97/100 (methodological rigor 25/25; quantitative traceability 25/25; reproducibility/artifacts 20/20; enterprise relevance 14/15; prior-work advance 13/15).

Deep paper 2 — The harness is part of the model’s effective capability

Identity and status. Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness Enabling Small Open Models to Complete Real Tasks; Hao Wang and Ting Huang; arXiv:2610.02001v1; submitted October 1, 2026 at 16:32:07 UTC; 44 pages; preprint. The paper is under arXiv’s perpetual non-exclusive license; the verified repository is Apache-2.0 and the released data is CC BY 4.0. (paper · code)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

The Local Real-Artifact Benchmark (LRAB) crosses four harnesses, four local models, and 18 tasks, producing 288 harness-model-task cells on one Windows machine. Tasks are scored from the created artifacts with deterministic checks. Mingbird emphasizes planning, a local tool server, explicit success criteria, artifact validation, rollback, default-deny delegation, destructive-operation refusals, and offline operation.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Agent research often compares models inside one scaffold or compares scaffolds on text-only benchmark completion. LRAB’s artifact-centered tasks, local execution, deterministic scoring, small-model emphasis, and cross-harness matrix are useful. The external tau² comparison partially addresses benchmark authorship.

But the authors built both the harness and LRAB; mechanism development and benchmark construction overlap, and no held-out task-construction split is described. The 288 main cells appear to be single trials, so stochastic uncertainty is inferred mainly through task resampling rather than repeated identical runs. All main runs occur on one Windows workstation. Campaign windows are blocked, not randomized, and model/server drift can confound harness comparisons. Deterministic artifact checks improve auditability but may miss semantic defects. The security posture is described and partially tested, not independently audited.

The correct prior-work comparison is not “local beats cloud.” The frontier comparison shows that competent harnesses converge while model capability still matters. The paper instead demonstrates that a weak harness can waste model capacity and that small local models require especially explicit planning, validation, and effect boundaries.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The repository exposes the harness, pinned dependencies, raw results, benchmark tasks, and a substantial test suite (the paper reports 482 tests; the repository showed 58 commits at verification). Apache-2.0 code and CC BY data reduce adoption friction. Provider/backend versioning, Windows dependencies, and local model availability remain reproduction variables. Enterprise readiness: high for an isolated local-task replay, artifact-based acceptance, offline execution, and rollback design; medium for low-risk internal developer workflows after independent security review; low for sensitive or durable production actions. Research Utility Score: 94/100 (methodological rigor 22/25; quantitative traceability 24/25; reproducibility/artifacts 20/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 13/15).

Deep paper 3 — Route experience by recurrence and state dependence

Identity and status. Not All Experience Belongs in the Weights: Component Routing for Self-Improving GUI Agents; Beining Wu, Zihao Ding, and Jun Huang; arXiv:2610.01787v1; submitted October 1, 2026 at 14:34:32 UTC; preprint under arXiv’s perpetual non-exclusive license. The paper says code and data will be released; no public artifact was available at verification. (paper)

Contribution and quantitative claim trace

The authors decompose experience into locators, procedures, state facts, and lessons, then compare reuse through model weights with reuse through context. Experiments span three backbone families, two GUI environments, and three seeds. Their routing rule uses two attributes: recurrence and state conditionality.

The main MobileGym setup covers 20 applications and 416 templates: 160 train and 256 test. A 122-template experience pool supports 165 practiced-app and 59 unseen-app test templates, with up to eight instances and 1,106 paired instances per seed.

Methodological critique and prior-work comparison

Continual-learning work often treats replay, fine-tuning, retrieval, or memory writing as system-wide choices. This paper makes the destination decision component-specific and tests plausible mechanisms. Cross-family producer/consumer experiments add useful evidence that contextual procedures transfer broadly while weight reuse can become family-specific.

However, the four component types and their extractor/state signatures are authored by the same team. The routing rule is derived from four aggregate component points, so perfect prediction across model-family cells is less independent than “24/24” suggests. Both environments are GUI benchmarks with related task families; only three seeds are reported. “Weights” use the paper’s adaptation setup, not every possible parameter-efficient or modular memory method. Privacy, memorization, poisoning, deletion, consent, and cross-user isolation are not central evaluations. Missing code and data prevent verification of the paired-instance construction, extraction pipeline, or confidence intervals.

Staleness is especially decision-relevant but not sufficient for deployment. Version rollback for contextual notes is straightforward; removal of knowledge embedded in weights is harder to prove. That asymmetry should be treated as a governance property, not only a quality metric.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

The paper documents datasets, component definitions, routing rule, seeds, compute, ablations, and producer/consumer tests. The promised artifacts were not yet public. Enterprise readiness: medium for offline component labeling and replay; low-to-medium for adapter-based shadow experiments; low for self-modifying production agents or cross-user experience sharing. Research Utility Score: 90/100 (methodological rigor 22/25; quantitative traceability 23/25; reproducibility/artifacts 15/20; enterprise relevance 15/15; prior-work advance 15/15).

Watchlist

Can AI oversight be zero knowledge?

Identity. Can AI Oversight Be Zero Knowledge?; Alessandro Chiesa, Ziyi Guan, and Burcu Yıldız; arXiv:2610.01995v1; submitted October 1, 2026 at 16:30 UTC; 38 pages; CC BY. (paper)

The paper proves that, in the random-oracle model, there is no zero-knowledge proof system for all oracle-aided computations—even without a succinctness requirement. The negative result extends to a formalization of debate. It then gives a positive construction when the oracle digitally signs each query-answer pair: under collision-resistant hashing, any oracle-aided computation can be verified in zero knowledge with an efficient prover and succinct verifier.

Why Watchlist, not deep selection. This is a crisp theoretical boundary, not an implemented enterprise oversight system. The signed-oracle construction shifts trust rather than eliminating it: the signer can observe queries, key custody becomes critical, availability and revocation matter, and an incorrect or compromised oracle can authenticate bad evidence. Promote after an open implementation, performance measurements on representative inference/tool traces, explicit key-rotation/revocation design, signer-compromise analysis, and multi-party privacy review.

AI Assessment Sandbox Configurator

Identity. The AI Assessment Sandbox Configurator: A Modular Framework for Regulatory Experimentation; Francesco Buscemi and coauthors; arXiv:2610.01539v1; submitted October 1, 2026 at 12:10 UTC; CC BY 4.0. The verified implementation is Apache-2.0. (paper · code)

The authors derive 11 requirements for EU AI regulatory sandboxes and implement a plugin API, shared data model, dashboards, report generation, role-based access control, portable deployment, and immutable audit logging. A live AIRS pilot contributed to an official Exit Report. The current framework contains ten plugins spanning safety/robustness and classical machine learning.

Why Watchlist, not deep selection. The framework is unusually concrete, but evidence is implementation- and pilot-centered rather than a comparative evaluation. The paper rates requirements R2, R3, and R8 strongly while R9, R10, and R11 remain partial; several controls are architecturally committed but not broadly exercised. LLM-assisted system-card creation lacks a quantified reliability analysis, and Verified versus Community plugin tiers remain roadmap items. Promote after independent regulator/assessor adoption, adversarial plugin-isolation tests, evidence-portability tests, and quantified report consistency.

Independent Analysis. These papers separate three layers that enterprise AI programs often collapse. First is the estimand layer: what effect is being claimed, on which population, under which support. Second is the placement layer: where experience, tools, and controls live, and how they are versioned or revoked. Third is the authority layer: which component is allowed to attest that an effect, artifact, or rule is valid. Reliability improves when each layer has a different identifier, log, owner, and rollback path.

The synthesis also cautions against a false universal: neither context nor weights, local nor remote, privacy proof nor audit log is always superior. The right choice depends on recurrence, state conditionality, candidate support, reversibility, threat model, data sensitivity, model capability, and cost. Those variables must enter qualification explicitly.

7. Signed evidence-oracle and key-custody model — 81.8

  • Category: AI Security / Safety & Governance / Evaluation & Observability.
  • Research signal and supporting paper(s) · Evidence label: Signed oracle answers enable a general zero-knowledge construction where the unsigned random-oracle model has an impossibility — Can AI Oversight Be Zero Knowledge?. Paper Fact; enterprise trust model is Enterprise Inference.
  • Enterprise problem or opportunity addressed: Privacy-preserving verification proposals often omit who attests to model/tool outputs and who can revoke that authority.
  • Proposed future capability and how it works: Define signer identities, attested query/answer envelopes, key hardware, rotation, revocation, timestamping, transparency logs, and separation between inference provider, prover, verifier, and auditor.
  • Likely architectural placement: model gateway, identity/authorization, security/governance, evaluation.
  • Expected business value: confidentiality, auditability, strategic differentiation, regulated collaboration.
  • Technical value: Makes the trust root explicit and creates a prerequisite for later proof systems.

  • Confidence / horizon / effort: Moderate / 6–12 months / L.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

Independent Analysis — 12–24 month synthesis. The backlog points toward an architecture where “memory,” “agent,” and “evaluation” are no longer opaque subsystems. The shared foundation is an evidence control plane that links identity, version, exposure, effect, and authority across layers.

Shared foundation Capabilities unlocked Architectural implication Internal context required before implementation
Model/harness gateway Local execution lane, repeated qualification, signed oracle Route by full configuration, not model name; attach immutable version and cost/effect telemetry Current model vendors, local hardware, latency/SLA, data residency, procurement
Context and memory service Support audit, exposure gate, component routing Separate candidate selection, final context assembly, policy, and retention; log known propensities Store scale, sensitivity, tenant model, retrieval stack, query volume, outcome labels
Componentized experience registry Routing, rollback, deletion, transfer tests Make experience atomic, typed, scoped, expiring, and source-linked Data ownership, consent, retention, adaptation methods, team boundaries
Evaluation and experiment infrastructure Randomized exposure, replay, artifact acceptance, plugin assessments Treat experiments as first-class, isolated deployments with frozen oracles and stop conditions Risk tolerance, representative tasks, test-data rights, compute budget, release process
Observability and provenance Effect ledger, causal support, process/artifact tracing Preserve candidate→context→action→artifact→outcome lineage with access-controlled raw evidence Logging policy, PII/regulated-data constraints, incident history, retention cost
Policy enforcement and identity Default-deny tools, signer trust, approval, revocation Bind human, model, harness, tool, signer, and tenant identities to every effect IAM maturity, authorization model, key custody, separation of duties
Human-review layer Ambiguous component labels, sensitive exposure, evidence approval Review only uncertainty, exceptions, and irreversible effects; record reviewer authority Available expertise, review latency, materiality thresholds, labor model
Cost telemetry Local/remote routing, exposure budget, proof feasibility Report quality, privacy, energy, latency, and review cost per accepted outcome Chargeback model, energy goals, provider contracts, operational baselines

Over 12–24 months, the most leveraged sequence is: instrument identities and exposures; build replay and reversible experiments; separate component registry from model weights; qualify local and remote harnesses as complete systems; then add advanced adaptation or proof mechanisms. This sequence does not assume any current vendor, vector database, model gateway, data platform, or maturity level. Before any roadmap commitment, internal owners must supply representative workflows, materiality, data classification, model/tool inventory, risk tolerance, economics, and change-control constraints.

Questions leaders should now ask

  • Did the intervention change what the model saw, or only what the store contained?
  • Which memories were outside candidate support, and are scores abstaining there?
  • Is a reported capability a property of the model, the harness, or their versioned combination?
  • Which experience is recurrent, state-conditional, sensitive, stale, or hard to remove?
  • Can every adaptation be traced to source evidence and rolled back?
  • Who signs the evidence, who sees the query, and who can revoke that authority?
  • Are assessment plugins isolated and are their caveats preserved in reports?
