Strongest research signal

The control plane around an agent is becoming a learnable, testable and partially verifiable software artifact.

Recommended executive action

Build a failure-evidence layer before adding autonomy. Version harness rules, require proof-bearing plans for critical code, and record per-request state-retention diagnostics.

Executive Summary

Independent Analysis Three new papers point toward a more disciplined agent stack. SHE treats the safety harness—not only model weights—as an object that can be revised from failed trajectories. P³ requires an agent to co-plan a program and its machine-checkable proof before either is elaborated. KVDiagnosis replaces aggregate long-context scores with matched failure records that explain whether evidence was evicted, poorly accessed, representationally damaged or lost later in decoding.

The work is methodologically meaningful, but its deployment horizons differ. KVDiagnosis provides the strongest reproducibility package and a statistically tested intervention; its protocol can be adopted immediately for infrastructure evaluation. SHE has an executable repository and held-out transfer evidence, yet it relies on small evolution data, model-based judges and text artifacts that could encode unsafe or evaluator-specific rules. P³ shows consistent gains across three benchmarks and four frontier backends, but only single runs, Lean-only tasks and closed-model APIs.

Enterprise Inference The practical direction is a three-plane architecture: a versioned policy/harness plane, a proof or invariant gate for high-impact actions, and a diagnostic state plane that traces which memory or context influenced the result. These papers do not justify unattended production decisions; they justify controlled experiments that make failure causes auditable.

1 · SHE: Trajectory-driven Safety Harness Evolution for LLM Agents

AuthorsWanying Qu, Qinghua Mao, Yu Li, Jiyao Liu, Xin Zhang, Dadi Guo, Yanxu Zhu, Qingyu Liu, Leitao Yuan, Xi Lin, Shanfeng Zhu, Yanwei Fu, Jing Shao, Xia Hu, Dongrui Liu
IdentifierarXiv:2608.09885 · cs.AI / cs.CV
SubmittedAug 10, 2026 · 17:35 UTC
StatusUnreviewed preprint · arXiv license

Abstract · PDF · Full HTML · Verified repository

Artifact audit: repository attribution is verified through the paper; Apache-2.0 code, fixed splits, configuration, tests and harness artifacts are visible. Generated harnesses, rollout outputs, external benchmark packages and credentials are intentionally excluded.

Thesis: Safety failures can be converted into localized, versionable edits to a four-part agent harness, improving attack resistance without treating the model itself as the only alignment surface.
Architecture · Paper Fact
Workflow · Paper Fact
Problem, contribution and technical approach

Static guardrails struggle when failures originate across context assembly, memory retrieval, tool authority and final-response handling. SHE decomposes those responsibilities into four editable artifacts, then uses evaluated trajectories to diagnose a harm domain, attack surface and failure mode. A model routes the case to one or a small set of artifacts, proposes bounded edits, validates them against schema and reward-hacking checks, and promotes a candidate only if safety improves while utility does not decline.

Independent Analysis The novelty is architectural and systems-oriented: failure attribution is made explicit before the system edits its own non-parametric policy. This resembles configuration-as-code with automated regression testing, not weight-level continual learning.

Assumptions: the evaluator can identify safety-relevant failures; text artifacts can express the necessary boundary; the benchmark environment represents future risks; and the selection metric does not reward superficial refusal or benchmark-specific phrasing.

Results, ablations and evidence strength
Agent-SafetyBench · percentage points

Paper Fact Evolution used a 15-task stratified subset, or 90 task-condition instances, while 185 tasks were reserved for final evaluation; two rollout replications were run per condition. On held-out AgentHarm, Harm Score fell from 19.8% for the seed harness to 9.8%, Harm Refusal rose from 78.4% to 86.4%, and benign non-refusal was essentially unchanged at 77.9% versus 77.8%. Experimental evidence.

Replacing individual evolved artifacts with seed versions generally weakened the safety–utility trade-off, supporting—but not uniquely proving—the value of localized edits. Alternate evolution models found different operating points: average ASR 5.5% with GPT-5.5, 4.3% with DeepSeek-V3.2 and 5.9% with GLM-5.2, alongside different utility.

Uncertainty No formal significance tests or confidence intervals are reported for the main outcomes. Temperature zero does not remove model/API drift, and judge/model coupling remains.

Risks & limitations
  • No dedicated limitations section; critical constraints must be inferred.
  • Evolution is based on only 15 tasks and 20 rounds; small data can produce benchmark-specific policy.
  • GPT-5.5 is both evolution model and Agent-SafetyBench trajectory judge, creating evaluator coupling.
  • Generated harnesses and rollout logs are excluded, limiting exact outcome replication.
  • Self-edited rules and memory can become a new prompt-injection, policy-conflict and rollback risk.
  • Held-out benchmark transfer is not evidence of production incident reduction.
Minimum viable experiment · Enterprise Inference

Hypothesis: artifact-routed harness edits reduce repeated safety failures more reliably than editing one monolithic policy.

Scope: disposable tool agent, 30 synthetic incident types, read-only fake systems, four versioned artifacts, independent human-reviewed evaluator. Compare static harness, monolithic evolution and routed evolution.

Success: ≥30% relative repeat-failure reduction, ≤2 pp clean-task regression, 100% edit provenance, deterministic rollback, no unauthorized tool action. Stop: any unsafe write, judge disagreement >10%, or cross-tenant memory. Effort: Medium.

Enterprise placement, academic opportunity and scorecard
Enterprise placement · Enterprise Inference

Readiness: controlled enterprise red-team experimentation now; not autonomous online self-modification. Build internally around existing policy registries and eval platforms; a vendor must expose artifact provenance, approval, rollback and influence traces.

Academic opportunity: repeat across seeds and independent judges; randomize evolution curricula; adversarially test the learned artifacts; quantify rule conflict and compression; compare textual edits with typed policies. Follow-on question: Can harness evolution be constrained by machine-checkable safety invariants so that every promoted edit is provably non-expansive in authority?

2 · P³: Joint Program-and-Proof Planning for Verified Code Generation

AuthorsZenan Li, Ziran Yang, Peiyang Song, Zhaoyu Li, Kaiyu Yang
IdentifierarXiv:2608.09277 · cs.AI / cs.PL
SubmittedAug 10, 2026 · 08:33 UTC
StatusUnreviewed preprint · CC BY 4.0

Abstract · PDF · Full HTML · Author-linked Figshare snapshot

Artifact audit: the paper links an anonymized Figshare snapshot containing the harness, prompts, benchmark and evaluation scripts. A public author-attributed GitHub repository was not found. The benchmark’s Lean checker is a strong outcome verifier, but reproduction still depends on closed-model APIs and pricing.

Thesis: An agent is more likely to generate verifiable code when it chooses the algorithm and the inductive argument as one coupled plan, rather than coding first and repairing the proof afterward.
Architecture · Paper Fact
Workflow · Paper Fact
Problem, contribution and technical approach

Program-then-proof agents can commit to implementations whose verification burden is hidden until proof search fails. P³ asks the planner to state the algorithm, decomposition, bridging predicate and auxiliary lemmas up front. It rejects plans that violate instructions or fail to connect the program structure to the postcondition, then freezes the selected structural commitments and allows local elaboration repair.

The paper also introduces Lean4Commit0: 1,030 placeholders across 108 repository-derived libraries, including relational specifications across APIs. Tasks pass reference satisfaction, mutation rejection and LLM review; mean aggregate specification quality is 90.7% under the paper’s own 50/50 metric.

Independent Analysis The algorithmic idea is an LLM implementation of refinement-style programming: code structure and correctness argument co-determine each other. The benchmark contribution is almost as important as the workflow because it moves beyond isolated textbook functions.

Results, ablations and evidence strength
Claude Opus 4.7 solve rate (%) · higher is better

Paper Fact Across Verina (189 tasks), AlgoVeri (77) and Lean4Commit0 (108 library bundles), P³ had the highest solve rate in all 12 model–benchmark cells. Gains over the stronger Plain or sequential baseline ranged from 4.6 to 11.2 percentage points. A Claude Opus 4.7 ablation found joint planning beat implementation-only planning by 4.8 points on Verina, 3.3 on AlgoVeri and 8.3 on Lean4Commit0. Tables 2–3.

On the hardest quartile by mean cost, P³ reduced API cost by 3.0–39.6% and wall time by 3.1–37.2% versus the better baseline for each metric. On easier all-solved intersections, planning overhead sometimes remained.

Uncertainty Every task–model–method cell was run once; no stochastic variance or formal significance test was estimated. The four backends are proprietary, and costs reflect time-specific API prices.

Risks & limitations
  • Lean 4 only; transfer to Dafny, Verus, F⋆ or Rocq is plausible but untested.
  • Repository benchmark emphasizes core APIs and Python-origin projects, not complete builds or dynamic systems.
  • Automatically constructed specifications can remain under-constrained despite mutation and review gates.
  • Single runs prevent uncertainty estimates; closed backends constrain replication and privacy.
  • A machine-checked proof guarantees the supplied specification—not the correctness or completeness of business intent.
Minimum viable experiment · Enterprise Inference

Hypothesis: joint code-and-proof planning reduces repair cycles for one bounded rules engine.

Scope: 20–30 synthetic functions in Lean or an equivalent verifier; compare existing coding agent, program-then-proof, and joint plan under equal tokens/time.

Success: ≥5 pp checker-accepted solve gain, ≥20% fewer full restarts, no spec modification, reviewable plans, cost within 25%. Stop: under-constrained specs, private code sent to external APIs, or proof accepted only through prohibited axioms. Effort: Medium–Large.

Enterprise placement, academic opportunity and scorecard
Enterprise placement · Enterprise Inference

Readiness: prototype in narrow, high-value correctness domains—protocol parsers, authorization logic, numerical kernels or compliance rules—where formal specifications already exist. The likely near-term choice is build, not buy: verification substrate and domain invariants dominate the integration work.

Academic opportunity: multi-seed replication; open-weight backends; cross-verifier transfer; adversarial specification adequacy; whole-project builds; formal models of when planning overhead pays. Follow-on question: Can a planner estimate proof burden well enough to choose among algorithm families before expensive elaboration, under open-model and whole-system conditions?

3 · KVDiagnosis: A Diagnostic Benchmark for KV-Cache Compression in Long-Context Language Models

AuthorsChen Qiu, Ziwu Liu, Chao Fei, Guozhong Li, Panos Kalnis
IdentifierarXiv:2608.09412 · cs.AI
SubmittedAug 10, 2026 · 10:38 UTC
StatusUnreviewed preprint · CC BY 4.0

Abstract · PDF · Full HTML · Verified code and data

Artifact audit: verified MIT-licensed repository includes the full population ledger, 12,520-row failure corpus, eight-method adapters, tests, fixed model revision, requirements, SHA-256 manifests and regeneration scripts. The manuscript contains KDD 2027 template metadata, but no independent acceptance record was verified; it is treated here as an unreviewed preprint.

Thesis: KV-cache compression should be evaluated by matched, method-specific failures with mechanism-valid diagnostics—not by one aggregate accuracy number or a nominal compression ratio.
Architecture · Paper Fact
Workflow · Paper Fact
Problem, contribution and technical approach

KV caches grow with context length and concurrency, making compression attractive. But compressors transform different objects: token positions, head/layer budgets, channels, chunks, latent representations or tensor precision. KVDiagnosis defines a 25-method taxonomy, evaluates eight implementations, and records explicit applicability states so invalid measurements remain N/A rather than being imputed.

For every method–setting, all sources are run against a matched FullCache control before that cell selects its own FullCache-correct/compressed-wrong failures. Diagnostics include slot-averaged evidence retention and coverage, next-token likelihood drift, attention access and decoding behavior. This prevents one compressor’s failure population from defining another’s test set.

Independent Analysis The key methodological advance is failure-set integrity. It turns an inference optimization from a leaderboard problem into an observability problem: which requests break, why, and whether a targeted intervention repairs only the hypothesized cohort.

Results, intervention and evidence strength
Low-EAR intervention · repair rate (%)

Paper Fact On Qwen3-8B, the study logged 59,800 supported compressed runs over 2,600 sources and selected 12,520 C→W rows. Low or partial mapped evidence coverage accounted for 63.2%. All ten diagnostics separated C→W failures from C→C successes, with stratified AUROC 0.684–0.871. Results.

Among 96 reproducible low-evidence-attention-ratio failures, a 4× evidence-attention boost repaired 29.2% versus 6.3% for count-matched non-evidence positions. This is the day’s strongest causal-style evidence because the intervention, sham and paired test target a predeclared mechanism.

Across Qwen, Falcon and Mistral, coverage profiles correlated strongly (Spearman ρ=0.969 and 0.957 relative to Qwen across 18 cells), but likelihood drift under quantization differed. The procedure generalizes better than the metric magnitude.

Risks & limitations
  • Latency, throughput, actual bytes and energy are outside scope, so production value is not measured.
  • Primary causal attention intervention is Qwen-only and covers a small 96-failure cohort.
  • Repeated sources make AUROC intervals descriptive; not every method exposes every diagnostic.
  • Greedy decoding, fixed splits and three logged model revisions limit generalization.
  • The audit excluded PyramidKV after finding its adapter duplicated SnapKV behavior—excellent integrity work, but also evidence that benchmark adapters can silently invalidate claims.
  • Failure diagnosis does not itself choose the best compressor for a workload.
Minimum viable experiment · Enterprise Inference

Hypothesis: a compressor that looks acceptable on average produces unacceptable, diagnosable failures on enterprise long-context tasks.

Scope: 200 de-identified documents with exact evidence spans; FullCache plus two compressors at light/medium settings; paired logs for retained positions, logits, latency and bytes.

Success: ≤1% critical C→W rate, ≥95% failure-trace completeness, ≥30% memory saving, ≥10% concurrency gain, and documented rollback to FullCache. Stop: any silent C→W on regulated content or irreproducible adapter output. Effort: Medium.

Enterprise placement, academic opportunity and scorecard
Enterprise placement · Enterprise Inference

Readiness: ready for controlled enterprise evaluation now. Integrate it as an offline/CI diagnostic around inference-serving changes, not as a runtime autonomous repair system. Build-versus-buy depends on observability access: a serving vendor that hides cache mappings and paired logits cannot support this level of diagnosis.

Academic opportunity: add byte-equivalent budgets, throughput/energy, stochastic decoding, multimodal contexts, learned compressors and causal interventions across models. Follow-on question: Can mechanism-specific diagnostics predict the Pareto frontier of quality, bytes, latency and energy before full production replay?

AMIE (Video) is a Gemini-based Talker–Planner–Perception system evaluated in a randomized OSCE with 30 primary-care physicians, 15 patient actors and 100 scenarios. Patient actors rated video over text for communicating concerns (89% vs 79%), convenience (88% vs 71%) and feeling understood (90% vs 81%); automated ablations reduced mean turn latency from 21.4 s to 2.6 s. Primary paper.

Why monitor: it is unusually broad evidence for asynchronous multimodal clinical agents. Weakness: actors, not patients; discrete turn-taking; subtle affect/movement failures; no code, weights or actor recordings; Alphabet-funded and unreviewed. Enterprise relevance: telehealth research and human-supervised intake only. Trigger: prospective multi-site study with real patients, subgroup safety, workflow outcomes and external replication.

New first-party research paper · Anthropic

Learning more about Claude’s mathematical capabilities

Anthropic reports that an unreleased Claude research model, using roughly 31 million output tokens and about 60 subagents, produced a 35-page argument raising a lower bound related to zeros of the Riemann zeta function from 41.6% to 67.2%. Two Anthropic mathematicians studied the result; outside experts Brian Conrey and Dan Goldston examined it on short notice; a Lean formalization, concise note and process transcripts are public.

Why monitor: the process is a consequential example of agentic mathematical discovery with a formal artifact. Weakness: not peer reviewed, model unavailable, validation partly internal, enormous token budget and unclear reproduction probability. Enterprise relevance: scientific R&D workflow design more than immediate product deployment. Trigger: independent expert verification, accepted manuscript and replication on a held-out open problem with a public model/workflow.

Knowledge Reinforcement

Key Concepts

  • Harness evolution: revising non-weight agent controls—prompts, rules, memory and tool policy—from evaluated failures.
  • Bridging predicate: an invariant connecting a program’s decomposition to the property its proof must establish.
  • C→W pair: a source answered correctly with FullCache but incorrectly after compression.
  • Applicability state: an explicit declaration that a diagnostic is measured, projected, structural or not applicable.
  • Failure-evidence control plane: the enterprise layer that stores causes, proofs, policies, lineage and rollback—not only final outputs.

Research Connections

SHE connects agent safety to configuration management, fault attribution and online learning under regression constraints. P³ revives Dijkstra, Floyd–Hoare logic and refinement programming: implementation structure and correctness arguments should be co-designed. KVDiagnosis applies paired experimental design, measurement validity and causal intervention to distributed inference state. Organizationally, all three make decision rights explicit: who may change policy, who defines the specification, which evidence can influence execution, and which gate may promote a system change.

One idea to remember
The most valuable agent infrastructure is not more autonomy; it is a verifiable chain from failure evidence to controlled change.
