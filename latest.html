AI Research Intelligence · Evidence before enthusiasm

Trust improves when evidence is visible and doubt is actionable.

The only newly published study that cleared today’s freshness, primary-source, and methodological gates shows a practical interface result: answer-level sources and a concise accuracy warning reduced acceptance of incorrect AI-style answers from 45% to 21% and increased verification from 22% to 52%—in a controlled, low-risk experiment. The enterprise opportunity is measurable decision support, not a universal disclaimer.

Uncertainty

Not proof of better discrimination

57 point gap

The larger correct–incorrect acceptance gap may partly reflect a more conservative response criterion. The study did not measure criterion-independent metacognitive sensitivity.

Independent Analysis. Treat provenance, warnings, and verification as a coupled decision-support control. The study supports an isolated A/B test of interface behavior; it does not justify production rollout, high-stakes use, or a claim that warnings make users accurately calibrated.

Top decision

Prototype answer-level provenance plus a one-click evidence inspector in one low-risk workflow, instrument both correct and incorrect output cases, and predeclare a stop condition for false rejection of correct answers. Do not deploy a generic disclaimer as the only safeguard.

Deep paper 1 · Peer reviewed · Article in press
Paper Fact

Source transparency and accuracy warnings support more selective reliance on generative-AI answers in a randomized online experiment

Yanhua Xing, Zeyu Zhang, Yike Zhang, Min Zhang & Xiaomei Lu
Scientific Reports · published November 14, 2026 · DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-80733-5 · full PDF

800 participants19,200 trials2 × 2 × 2 mixed design24 fixed answers/personZenodo archive reported

Research question and intervention

The study asks whether two small interface cues—visible source or evidence information and a warning that AI answers may be inaccurate—change whether users accept correct versus incorrect AI-style answers and whether they open verification information. Source transparency and warning presence were randomized between participants; correctness varied within participant across 12 correct and 12 incorrect answers.

Participant flow and controls

Of 876 people who accessed the survey, 26 were excluded before randomization for consent or eligibility. The survey platform randomized 850 people using equal allocation. Fifty post-randomization exclusions were applied before outcome modeling for incomplete data, failed attention checks, unrealistically fast completion, or duplicate/unusable responses, leaving 200 participants in each of four conditions. Reported baseline standardized mean differences were below 0.10. Participants were English-fluent Prolific adults with at least a 95% approval rate; 78–80% reported prior generative-AI use.

Model results

  • All-trial acceptance: correctness was the strongest predictor (OR 5.58, 95% CI 4.78–6.51). Correctness × source (OR 1.36, 95% CI 1.08–1.72) and correctness × warning (OR 1.39, 95% CI 1.08–1.79) were positive.
  • No supported synergy: the correctness × source × warning interaction was not significant (OR 1.27, 95% CI 0.91–1.77, p=.158). For incorrect-answer acceptance, source × warning was OR 1.02 (95% CI 0.79–1.32, p=.879).
  • Incorrect-answer risk: source transparency reduced the odds of accepting an incorrect answer (OR 0.68, 95% CI 0.57–0.81); warning presence reduced them further (OR 0.47, 95% CI 0.39–0.57).
  • Verification: source transparency increased verification odds (OR 2.18, 95% CI 1.79–2.66); warnings increased them (OR 1.58, 95% CI 1.30–1.93). The interaction was not significant.

What the study does well

  • Randomized factorial design isolates two interface components while holding content and response options constant.
  • Within-participant correctness increases precision and permits direct examination of correct versus incorrect acceptance.
  • Mixed-effects logistic models include participant and item random intercepts; item-fixed and participant-aggregated sensitivity analyses reportedly preserve the main contrast.
  • Behavioral verification is measured as an actual click/open action rather than an intention or attitude.
  • Exact counts, odds ratios, intervals, exclusion flow, baseline balance, stimulus bank, crosswalk, codebook, analysis scripts, and sensitivity files are reported.

Methodological critique

Internal-validity boundary

Correctness is confounded with domain. Correct items were mainly general knowledge and everyday science; incorrect items were data interpretation, language, and reasoning. The acceptance gap cannot be assumed independent of domain difficulty or familiarity.

Construct boundary

Selective reliance is descriptive. Lower incorrect acceptance may reflect better discrimination, a more conservative criterion, or both. General 1–7 post-decision confidence was not confidence that the accept/reject decision itself was correct, so a Type-2 ROC interpretation would be unsupported.

Ecological boundary

Fixed answers are not live assistants. Participants could not ask follow-ups, compare models, challenge citations, or bring sustained workflow context. The evidence transfers most directly to single-turn evaluation interfaces.

Population boundary

Low-risk Prolific sample. Tasks excluded medical, legal, and financial advice. The sample is not representative of high-stakes professionals, organization-specific risk, or repeated use.

Mechanism boundary

Deliberation was inferred, not manipulated. Combined-cue response time rose from 14.2 to 20.4 seconds, but attention, deliberation, evidence quality, and answer fluency were not independently manipulated.

Durability boundary

No longitudinal evidence. Immediate cue effects do not establish resistance to warning fatigue, habituation, heterogeneous source quality, or strategic manipulation of provenance.

Prior-work comparison

Earlier automation-bias and misinformation research often studies trust, warning labels, or source credibility separately. This paper’s useful operational contribution is the paired manipulation of evidence visibility and accuracy risk, plus trial-level behavior. It does not supersede prior work showing that source cues can be weak, backfire, or operate differently under motivated reasoning; its own discussion explicitly limits generalization.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications · Independent Analysis

A 12–24 month evidence-to-decision control plane

This target pattern does not assume any current enterprise stack. Its feasibility depends on internal architecture, identity, data classification, workflow risk, regulatory duties, and operating maturity.

Shared enabling foundations

Identity, policy, and gateway

Authenticate user and application context; classify consequence; choose whether a warning, evidence view, or mandatory review is required.

Knowledge and provenance

Preserve claim-to-source links, retrieval and model versions, access scope, timestamps, transformations, and evidence integrity.

Evaluation and operations

Run paired experiments, record uncertainty and decision outcomes, monitor drift and cost, and retain non-destructive rollback controls.

Internal context required before implementation: current model gateway and application surface; RAG and source inventory; IAM and data entitlements; risk and regulatory classes; accessibility and localization requirements; existing experimentation/observability stack; acceptable false-rejection, latency, and review budgets; privacy and labor constraints on user telemetry.
Implementation priorities

A reversible 90-day validation sequence

Days 0–15Inventory one low-risk workflow, its claims, source systems, users, decisions, current warnings, verification path, and ground-truth process.
Days 16–35Implement the provenance manifest and read-only evidence inspector in an isolated test environment. Audit source permissions and excerpt faithfulness.
Days 36–60Build a balanced evaluation set and randomize control, source, warning, and combined variants with consent. Predeclare models and stop rules.
Days 61–90Estimate effects by item and user, inspect false rejection, privacy, latency, and comprehension; decide separately to advance, revise, or stop each control.

Minimum measurement set

  • Incorrect-output acceptance and correction
  • Correct-output rejection
  • Verification open, dwell, and decision change
  • Claim-to-source faithfulness
  • Source authorization and leakage
  • Response and workflow latency
  • Human review minutes and cost
  • Warning exposure and fatigue
  • User confidence and comprehension
  • Downstream task quality

Stop conditions

  • Any restricted source is exposed to an unauthorized user.
  • Provenance cannot reconstruct the displayed claim and evidence.
  • Correct-answer rejection or task degradation exceeds the predeclared tolerance.
  • The conclusion changes under reasonable item/domain specifications.
  • Warning fatigue, surveillance burden, or accessibility harm outweighs measured benefit.
  • Evidence inspection is opened but does not improve comprehension or outcomes.
Knowledge reinforcement

What to remember tomorrow

  1. A citation is not provenance. Useful provenance binds a claim to inspectable, authorized, current evidence.
  2. A warning is not a safety boundary. It can change user behavior but does not enforce truth or authorization.
  3. Selective reliance requires two error directions. Measure acceptance of incorrect output and rejection of correct output.
  4. Verification must be behavioral and consequential. Opening evidence is weaker than understanding it and correcting the decision.
  5. Short-term effects can decay. Warning fatigue and repeated use require longitudinal evidence.
  6. Production transfer must be earned. Low-risk fixed-answer results do not establish high-stakes agent reliability.
Self-check quiz · 5 questions
  1. Why is the 57-point acceptance gap not a sensitivity measure?
    Because correctness was domain-confounded and confidence was not elicited as confidence in accept/reject correctness; response criterion shifts remain plausible.
  2. What was the combined condition’s incorrect acceptance rate?
    21% (504 of 2,400).
  3. What did source transparency do to verification odds?
    It increased them (OR 2.18, 95% CI 1.79–2.66).
  4. What is the first enterprise experiment?
    A low-risk, reversible, balanced, consented A/B test—not a production rollout.
  5. What must accompany warnings?
    Provenance, usable verification, telemetry, human escalation, and predeclared stop conditions.
