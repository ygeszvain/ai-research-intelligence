AI Research Intelligence · Evidence before enthusiasm

Measure the decision system—not the demo.

The best new evidence is unusually consistent: multi-agent complexity can underperform a single agent; multimodal reviewers often notice the right figure without verifying the contradiction; small-model routing can cut compute while increasing latency; and local agents become economical only when failures are counted. The build order is therefore audit, verify, route, then automate.

Executive brief

Three signals that change enterprise AI priorities

Paper Fact

Complexity did not buy clinical discrimination

0.794 AUC

A locked redesign of a safety-constrained multi-agent triage system discriminated worse than a single-agent comparator at 0.821–0.823. The initial 88.2% accuracy was development-set apparent performance with documentation leakage.

Paper Fact

Attention is not verification

60.6% mention

Multimodal reviewers mentioned the flawed figure in 60.6% of 1,445 reviews, yet only 3.3% met a lenient diagnosis rubric. Naming the figure raised a controlled arm from 4.3% to 20.4%.

Paper Fact

Cheaper routing trades cost for time

−16.4% GPU-hour

A 120-minute small-model-first coding cascade preserved the large-model resolve rate in one trace simulation, but average sequential runtime rose from 41.6 to 84.5 minutes.

Independent Analysis. The shared opportunity is a measurement fabric that separates acquisition, reasoning, action, verification, latency and failed-attempt cost. The papers support isolated pilots and stricter evaluation—not production clinical autonomy, unbounded multimodal review, or automatic local-agent deployment.

Top 3 Future Bets

1

Leakage and holdout audit

Why it matters: a high score can be a documentation shortcut. What changes it: repeated locked tests on unseen, external data show stable benefit without forbidden fields. Next reversible action: remove outcome-bearing text and rerun a read-only shadow evaluation.

2

Independent executable verification

Why it matters: agent self-reports and plausibly worded reviews are not evidence. What changes it: internal verifiers add little beyond existing tests or require unsafe privileges. Next action: recompute 50 sanitized outputs against hidden checks.

3

Timeout cascade for async work

Why it matters: simple routing captured most of an oracle-style router’s measured savings. What changes it: internal tasks are hard-heavy, latency-sensitive, or savings disappear under replicated runs and actual billing. Next action: replay 100 background tasks with no write authority.

Evidence gates

  1. Primary source, timestamp, identifier and venue status verified.
  2. Full paper reviewed beyond title and abstract.
  3. Headline numbers traced to denominators, methods, tables or figures.
  4. Failed tests, leakage, latency, cost definitions and caveats retained.
  5. Prior work and claimed contribution compared.
  6. Code, data and licenses labeled only when verified.
  7. Artifacts not linked by the authors are marked unavailable—not assumed.
  8. Exact-title anti-duplication checked against the published archive.
  9. Paper facts separated from analysis and enterprise inference.
  10. Only isolated, reversible and measurable experiments recommended.
Evidence labels. Paper Fact means explicitly reported, not independently replicated. Independent Analysis is cross-paper synthesis. Enterprise Inference is a future option derived from evidence. Uncertainty marks unresolved validity, safety, privacy, economics or operational questions.
Deep-paper analysis

Three papers that cleared the full-review gate

Paper Fact

Safety-constrained multi-agent AI for emergency department disposition decisions

Lior Tsafrir, Liniv Arama, Gal Ben Haim & Mor Saban
Scientific Reports · published November 18, 2026 · accepted November 12 · DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-80974-4 · article · accepted early version, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0

469 valid-label encountersone 24-hour period~30 development cyclesno held-out setsingle-agent comparator

Contribution and prior-work delta

The paper is valuable because it does not protect a favorable prototype result. It separates an initial multi-agent system—role-specialized language-model agents plus deterministic safety rules—from a post-peer-review v2 evaluated under a protocol locked before runs, and tests whether physician documentation carries the outcome. Relative to clinical LLM papers that report only apparent accuracy, it exposes how development reuse and post-decision text can dominate the result.

Methodological critique

  • Strength: the authors explicitly identify optimistic bias, preserve a negative comparator result and require external/prospective validation.
  • Leakage: physician summary/recommendation text appears downstream of part of the clinical reasoning process; withholding collapses the initial result.
  • Development/evaluation coupling: roughly 30 prompt/rule cycles on the same cohort invalidate the initial confidence interval as a generalization interval.
  • External validity: a single center, adult internal medicine/geriatric population and one 24-hour window cannot represent temporal, site or specialty variation.
  • Comparator: multi-agent complexity did not beat a single agent. Deterministic gates may still matter for safety, but the study does not isolate their causal contribution.
  • Clinical utility: retrospective agreement is not a prospective outcome, and no deployment, randomized workflow or patient-safety benefit is established.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Reproducibility: Moderate-low. Supplementary files are provided, but patient-level clinical data are privacy constrained and the visible article record does not establish a public, licensed implementation. Enterprise readiness: Low for clinical decision support; high as a warning for evaluation governance. Do not deploy. Adopt the audit pattern: lock protocols, remove outcome-bearing fields, retain simple baselines and require unseen external data.

Paper Fact

When Figures Disagree with Text: Evaluating Autonomous Visual Evidence Verification in Scientific Papers

Guijia Zhang, Hongbo Qiu, Jiajun Zha et al.
arXiv:2611.24353v1 · submitted November 18, 2026 · paper · 28 pages · arXiv non-exclusive distribution license

31 full manuscripts6 multimodal models1,445 open reviews186 reviews/controlled arm100-review blind audit

Contribution and prior-work delta

Prior chart and scientific-verification benchmarks usually supply the figure, claim or knowledge that an error exists. This work hides a reviewer-documented PRISMM-Bench figure–text inconsistency inside a complete manuscript and asks for a neutral review. It measures the step that guided benchmarks omit: choosing what deserves verification before aligning pixels with a claim and deciding whether they conflict.

Grading audit and methodological critique

  • Grader agreement: two primary grader models reached κ=.62 on 1,221 decidable pairs; a replay pair accepted 2.74% versus 3.28%.
  • Human audit: two authors reached κ=.898; only 10 of 19 decidable rubric-accepted cases were confirmed by both. Absolute rates are likely optimistic.
  • Selection: 31 figure-centric manuscripts are a narrow, non-random slice; they are longer than the benchmark candidate pool.
  • Prompt validity: “review this paper” is realistic but does not ensure attention allocation matches professional reviewers with domain knowledge and time.
  • Condition comparability: cohort and controlled-arm mention rules differ, so the authors correctly avoid cross-run mention comparisons.
  • Mechanism: interventions identify where output changes, not whether the internal failure is search, visual parsing, alignment or contradiction reasoning.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Reproducibility: Moderate. Selection rules, prompts, model identifiers, request settings, bootstrap procedures and many appendices are disclosed; no public code/data repository or open-source license is linked in the paper record reviewed. Enterprise readiness: Moderate for evaluation design; low for autonomous document assurance. Use the result to add target-selection tests, human-confirmed grading samples and explicit “evidence checked” traces.

Paper Fact

Reducing Inference Cost of Coding Agents with Timeout Cascades

Boxi Zhou, German Sviridov, Dmitrii Ustiugov & Haris Javaid
arXiv:2611.24324v1 · submitted November 18, 2026 · paper · CC BY 4.0

36 open-weight models500 SWE-bench Verified tasksmini-swe-agentAMD MI300Xone run/model-task

Contribution and prior-work delta

Model routing work often trains a classifier before execution. This paper asks whether elapsed time from a small first-stage coding agent is already a useful difficulty signal. It standardizes 36 models under one scaffold and serving stack, then simulates a cold-restart cascade and an oracle-style difficulty router from recorded trajectories.

Methodological critique

  • Strengths: one scaffold, fixed step/time limits, explicit hardware/runtime, full benchmark, exact cost definition and latency trade-off.
  • Single-run variance: every model-task pair ran once. The reported 73.4% cascade versus 71.2% large-only may be complementarity or stochastic noise; authors treat only “no accuracy loss” as the main result.
  • Task subset: hybrid simulation uses 466/500 tasks with complete trajectories, potentially introducing selection bias.
  • Cost construct: GPU-hour = wall time × allocated GPUs under exclusive allocation. Up to 30 tasks ran concurrently; sums can exceed physical cluster GPU-hours and exclude price, energy, idle capacity and engineering overhead.
  • Timeout choice: the best point is the upper end of the 0–120 minute sweep, so the optimum may lie beyond the tested range while latency continues to deteriorate.
  • Generalization: SWE-bench is 91% easy/medium under the selected labels. A hard-heavy, interactive or proprietary workload may reverse the economics.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Reproducibility: Moderate. Models, hardware, vLLM/ROCm versions, scaffold, limits and formulas are specified; no paper-linked trajectory repository or implementation artifact is verified. Enterprise readiness: Moderate for a shadow test on asynchronous coding work. A rollout requires replicated seeds, real marginal-cost telemetry, security isolation, preserved test integrity and a latency SLO.

Uncertainty

Choosing What to Observe: Budget-Paired Counterfactual Audits for Credible Evaluation of Visual Agents

Ruiqi Liu, Jinming Li, Mo Wang, Yang Lu & Yanlan Kang · arXiv:2611.24867v1 · November 18 · paper

Across forty paired high-gap anchors, five open-weight VLMs made 0/40 optimal budget-dependent switches and lost 12.50–15.62 percentage points, while non-anchor averages of 0.59–0.85 points hid the failure. In three-read hierarchies, first-read loss was only 0.18–0.96 points, but later reads lost 22.20–26.76. On 30 FUNSD forms, Qwen3-VL-8B’s two-crop pipeline scored 70.0% versus 83.3% for native whole-image reading while using twice the input tokens.

Why watch: the counterfactual accounting is excellent for distinguishing acquisition from terminal decision loss. Generalization is limited by declared priors/read channels, short supplied-rule tasks, constrained decoding, closely related model families, AI-assisted FUNSD references without independent human adjudication and possible pretraining exposure. Research score: 84/100.

Uncertainty

Local AI Agents in Power Electronics Simulation: Deployment, Measurements and Costs

Yuzhuo Li, Pasan Gunawardena & Yunwei Li · arXiv:2611.24880v1 · November 18 · paper · CC BY 4.0

The paper reports 2,041 valid converter/data-centre runs plus 39 power-system attempts. Local agents passed 73% of defect repair and 75% of development-stage tasks, but only 2% of construction at a 115k-token budget. Doubling budget raised construction to 49% (90% on two nodes). In one low-success condition, a median 8.6 kJ run became 215 kJ per solved task after failures. The framework independently resimulates outputs and measures power, energy, tokens and time.

Why watch: cost per successful outcome and independent physical checks transfer well. Most local evidence comes from one DGX Spark and one main model; energy excludes the agent host and AC input; cloud comparisons change model as well as location; some cells have one or two passes, understating bootstrap uncertainty. Research score: 88/100.

Cross-paper synthesis

Four claims the papers support together

Independent Analysis

1. More components do not imply more reliable decisions

The clinical multi-agent system lost to a single-agent comparator; the visual-agent audit shows later acquisitions can destroy value even when the first choice is almost optimal. Architecture must earn its complexity on locked, end-to-end outcomes.

Independent Analysis

2. The evaluator must price search and failure

Figure verification fails primarily before a contradiction is diagnosed; local-agent energy is dominated by failed attempts when success is rare. Enterprise KPIs should include evidence acquisition, retries, abstentions and review effort—not only successful-call latency or token price.

Independent Analysis

3. Routing is a workload property

Timeout cascades work because the benchmark is easy/medium-heavy and asynchronous latency is tolerable. Budget-aware visual routing fails at matched boundary conditions. Every routing policy needs paired tests where the optimal action changes.

Independent Analysis

4. Independent execution beats agent testimony

The engineering-agent study ignores self-reported success and reruns deliverables at undisclosed operating points. The clinical study shows how narrative fields can leak the outcome. A trustworthy system needs a verifier with separate evidence, identity and authority.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications · Independent Analysis

A 12–24 month measurement-first architecture

The likely shift is from model-centric orchestration to evidence-centric orchestration: every task carries a data/holdout contract, every agent action is budgeted and identity-bound, every artifact can be independently verified, and the gateway records total cost per verified outcome. No current enterprise stack or vendor is assumed.

Shared enabling foundations

Model/tool gateway

Small/large routing, clean restart, tool side-effect control, identity, model/runtime manifests and price/energy normalization.

Data and holdout service

Temporal field availability, prohibited inputs, split lineage, external cohorts, sealed labels and contamination evidence.

Evaluation infrastructure

A reversible 90-day program

Days 0–15 · BaselineChoose one low-risk asynchronous workflow. Freeze task definitions, outcome oracles, forbidden fields, simple/single-agent baselines, latency and total-cost metrics.
Days 16–35 · InstrumentAdd trace IDs, acquisition decisions, model/tool identity, failure/retry cost, clean restart, sealed labels and independent verifier receipts.
Days 36–60 · CounterfactualsRun field-withholding, unseen holdout, paired-budget, seeded-failure and replicated-seed tests. Keep all outputs advisory and all tools non-destructive.
Days 61–90 · DecideReview quality, subgroup/calibration, security, privacy, total dollars/energy/time and reviewer burden. Promote only if predeclared gates pass; otherwise archive the evidence and stop.

Decision gates

Quality gate

Locked, unseen evaluation; simple baseline; replicated runs; non-inferiority margin; human-confirmed rubric precision; no outcome leakage.

Risk gate

Least privilege, disposable environments, no uncontrolled external writes, privacy/legal approval, rollback and named incident owner.

Economics gate

Total cost per verified outcome includes failures, retries, energy/compute, licenses, latency, human review and operational support.

Knowledge reinforcement

What to remember

Five durable rules

  1. A development-set confidence interval is not a generalization interval.
  2. Mentioning evidence is not verifying a claim against it.
  3. A routing policy must be tested where the optimal action changes.
  4. Cost per successful outcome includes failed attempts and human review.
  5. Agent-reported success is not evidence when an independent check exists.

Retrieval practice

Why did the clinical 88.2% not justify deployment?

Prompts and rules were tuned on the same cohort, physician documentation leaked disposition information, and there was no unseen, external or prospective validation.

What was the key gap in multimodal review?

60.6% mentioned the flawed figure, but only 3.3% met a lenient diagnosis rubric; selecting and aligning the evidence remained the bottleneck.

What did the timeout cascade save—and spend?

It cut effective GPU-hours 16.4% in the trace simulation while increasing average sequential runtime from 41.6 to 84.5 minutes at the 120-minute setting.

Why can per-run energy mislead?

When success probability is low, failed runs multiply cost per solved task; a low-energy run can still produce a very expensive verified success.

One-sentence synthesis. Before giving an enterprise agent more authority, prove on unseen evidence that it chooses the right information, survives matched boundary tests, produces independently verifiable artifacts and improves total risk-adjusted cost per successful outcome.
