Executive intelligence

What changed—and what it means

Paper facts, independent analysis, and enterprise inference are separated. Nothing below is a production-deployment approval.

Paper Fact

Three verified shifts

  • Formal verification: AlphaProof Code produced about 6.5 million lines of Lean proof over a 560 KB, roughly 140,000-instruction AArch64 build of libsignal in four weeks. The kernel checks proof correctness; humans still own the trusted specification and assumptions.
  • Monitorability: OpenAI reports that no-chain-of-thought capability rises smoothly as base-model perplexity improves, CoT controllability rises through reinforcement learning even without explicit CoT supervision, and controllability can transmit through model-generated SFT data.
  • Weight integrity: Microsoft’s Lazarus attack reprogrammed fewer than ten dormant neurons without retraining; seven neurons in Nemotron-Terminal-8B triggered the payload in at least 98.8% of held-out trigger trajectories while remaining inactive on benign trajectories.
Independent Analysis

Decision translation

Assurance must migrate from “did the model say the right thing?” to “did an independent mechanism validate the right artifact?” AlphaProof puts a small kernel and explicit specification above untrusted provers. Lazarus makes hashes, provenance, checkpoint diffs, and least privilege part of model safety. The monitorability study shows that visible CoT cannot be the only oversight channel.

Do not over-generalize. AlphaProof covers a constrained build and scenario, not Signal applications end to end. OpenAI’s primary measures are imperfect proxies and many models/checkpoints are internal. Lazarus is a preprint-scale demonstration on three open models, and its no-reference detector has a 9.7% false-positive rate.

Deep paper 1 · AI software engineering / AI security

AlphaProof Code: Verifying Signal’s Security at the Machine-Code Level

Swarat Chaudhuri et al. · Google DeepMind / Google · technical paper dated Nov 10, 2026 · 57 pages · code and Lean development released

Research postTechnical paperArtifacts
Paper Fact

Core contribution

AlphaProof Code is a human-guided, multi-agent formal-verification framework. Humans and reviewer agents define Lean specifications; unmodified Rust is compiled to AArch64; prover agents generate proofs; and a gatekeeper accepts only proofs that Lean’s kernel can replay against a held-out specification. The case study connects protocol-level security properties to a compiled build of libsignal rather than an abstract design or verification-friendly rewrite.

Method and scope

  • Two independent proof lines: a symbolic Dolev–Yao model and a computational game-based model.
  • Coverage includes PQXDH and the Triple Ratchet, with forward secrecy, authentication, post-compromise security, and handshake-secrecy properties.
  • About 6.5M proof lines rest on about 18,000 lines of trusted definitions plus roughly 8,000 lines of AArch64 semantics; the proof build checks compiled bytes.
  • Five researchers ran agent teams for four weeks. The verified build is about 560 KB / 140,000 AArch64 instructions.

Prior-work comparison

Earlier Signal proofs target abstract models, narrowed properties, modified implementations, or verification-oriented reimplementations. This work’s distinguishing claim is refinement from security models to the compiled machine code of an unmodified reference implementation build, with both symbolic and computational arguments.

What is strong

  • Proof agents are explicitly untrusted; a small kernel and a comparator check the artifact.
  • Held-out specifications and rejection of sorry/native_decide address goal weakening and vacuous success.
  • Public theorem-to-code mapping, build pins, source, proof scripts, and trusted-base disclosure materially support audit and reproduction.
  • The paper enumerates environment, cryptographic, scenario, scale, and quantum limitations rather than implying complete Signal verification.

Methodological critique

  • Specification correctness is not mechanically guaranteed; most human effort moved into defining what must be proven.
  • The symbolic proof idealizes cryptography; the computational proof has hardness, random-oracle, fixed-environment, and moderate-scale assumptions.
  • Coverage excludes Signal apps, multi-device state, broad trust-on-first-use behavior, some adversary actions, and parts of payload privacy/authentication.
  • The result is a single high-expertise cryptographic case study with a proprietary frontier model and undisclosed total inference cost.

Enterprise Inference The near-term opportunity is not autonomous proof of arbitrary systems. It is a narrow proof-gated assurance lane for stable, high-impact components where a small trusted specification can exclude costly defect classes. Internal code criticality, change rate, available formal-methods expertise, target ISA/runtime, and acceptable TCB size are required before prioritization.

Deep paper 2 · Safety & governance / evaluation

What affects chain-of-thought monitorability across the model lifecycle?

Jeffrey G. Wang et al. · OpenAI Alignment Research Blog · Nov 10, 2026 · internal and released frontier-model study

Full research postPrior monitorability frameworkCoT monitorability paper
Paper Fact

Core contribution

The study traces two proxies for worst-case CoT-monitor evasion—no-CoT capability and CoT controllability—across pretraining quality, architecture, reinforcement learning, tool supervision, and cross-generation SFT. It argues that stronger base models and longer RL can make visible reasoning less dependable as a sole oversight channel, even when no reward directly grades the CoT.

Method and scope

  • Four no-CoT benchmarks span public NCRI and Think Fast plus internal planning and math evaluations; historical and internal models range from GPT-5 to Astra-class checkpoints.
  • Three architecture scaling series are compared at shared base-model perplexity, with many multiple-choice no-CoT tasks.
  • RL compares a no-process-supervision run against a same-base, same-dataset tool-enabled run whose graders see terminal/browser traces.
  • SFT compares students trained on matched-capability teacher rollouts differing in controllability.

Prior-work comparison

Earlier monitorability research measured whether reasoning traces expose behavior and whether models can control those traces. This study moves upstream, asking which training-lifecycle choices change the proxies and showing transmission through model-generated training data.

What is strong

  • The study spans the full model lifecycle instead of attributing monitorability to one post-training recipe.
  • Iso-perplexity and same-base RL comparisons target major confounders.
  • The authors report a negative result: controllability did not predict evasion for a matched-capability pair and sometimes pointed the wrong way.
  • Explicitly scans for accidental CoT grading and distinguishes proxy capability from observed harmful behavior.

Methodological critique

  • CoT controllability is measured with five mostly stylistic/insertion instructions and is acknowledged as noisy.
  • No-CoT capability and controllability are imperfect proxies, not direct measures of real-world covert misbehavior.
  • Model capability confounds several historical correlations; internal model/data opacity limits independent reproduction.
  • No public checkpoint-by-checkpoint table supports independent effect-size reconstruction, and publication is not peer reviewed.

Enterprise Inference Do not make CoT visibility a single control. Track action outcomes, tool traces, policy decisions, provenance, deterministic validators, and independent model/activation monitors across model upgrades. Internal access to raw reasoning, model versions, safety architecture, regulated-data constraints, and incident taxonomy is required before designing such a program.

Deep paper 3 · AI security / MLOps

Lazare, veni foras: Reprogramming Dormant Neurons to Backdoor AI Agents

Yuchen Zeng, Giorgio Severi, Blake Bullwinkel, Eric Horvitz, Dimitris Papailiopoulos · Microsoft Research preprint · released Nov 10, 2026

PublicationResearch narrativeDefender guidance
Paper Fact

Core contribution

Lazarus is a no-training weight-edit attack. It repurposes dormant neurons in the final feed-forward block as trigger detectors, then sets their output weights to promote chosen payload tokens into the residual stream. Chaining roughly one neuron per payload token can emit an executable command when a trigger string appears anywhere in context.

Method and scope

  • Dormant neurons are selected and edited in closed form; optional isolated refinement uses a few hundred gradient steps.
  • Nemotron-Terminal-8B used seven of more than 12,000 neurons, fitted on 23 trigger/no-trigger trajectories.
  • The paper reports generalization to Qwen 3.6-27B and Gemma-4-31B-IT.
  • No-reference agent-built scanners analyze checkpoint structure; reliable confirmation still benefits from a known-good base-weight diff.

Prior-work comparison

Backdoors and sleeper agents are established threats, but typically rely on poisoned training or fine-tuning. Lazarus shows a targeted post-training checkpoint edit with inference-level compute, small structural footprint, ordinary-string triggers, and agentic tool execution as the consequence channel.

What is strong

  • Separates checkpoint integrity risk from data-poisoning and training-pipeline risk.
  • Connects model-layer compromise to the agent harness, where terminal/tool authority creates operational impact.
  • Includes a detector baseline and practical defender guidance: provenance, hashes, scanning, signed registries, and least privilege.
  • Microsoft states it has not observed threat-actor weaponization, preventing research from being misreported as an active incident.

Methodological critique

  • Primary detailed results are narrow relative to the diversity of open models, model formats, fine-tunes, merges, and quantizers.
  • No-reference detection’s 9.7% false-positive rate may be costly in a large checkpoint estate.
  • Ordinary behavioral evals are said to pass, but breadth, sensitivity, and confidence intervals are not disclosed in the public summary.
  • Attack success depends on trigger exposure, tokenization, generation settings, agent wrapper behavior, tool authority, and environment controls.

Enterprise Inference Treat model weights as executable supply-chain artifacts: verify publisher identity and hashes, sign internal derivatives, diff every update, restrict write paths, block runtime pulls, quarantine unexplained structural outliers, and sandbox agent actions. Internal model sourcing, registry, signing, fine-tuning, quantization, serving, identity, secret, egress, and incident-response context is required before implementation.

Paper Fact

EarthRecord: From Pixels to Land Change with Multimodal Agents

arXiv:2611.12934v1 · listed Nov 11, submitted Nov 6 · code and dataset declared; arXiv non-exclusive license.

Signal: 56 sites, four decades of raw data, 238 questions, 12 agents, and controls for prior knowledge and sensor manipulation. Leading agents score 81.0–85.4% pass³ overall, but six leaders drop from 92.0% on variable-computed questions to 61.1% when mapping imagery is required. After one sensor is altered, 53.5% of originally correct cases become wrong and 91% move with the alteration; placebo changes affect 12%.

Why Watchlist: unusually strong causal controls, but one domain, custom infrastructure, changing model versions, human-reviewed references, and no enterprise replication. The signal is about cross-source corroboration—not permission to automate geospatial decisions.

Paper Fact

SELD: Detecting Silent Evidence Loss in Context Compression

arXiv:2611.12903v1 · listed Nov 11, submitted Oct 14 · CC BY 4.0.

Signal: on 2,414 HotpotQA/2Wiki questions with length-matched support/control deletions, SELD ranks evidence deletion above control in 82.7% of unchanged-answer cases, versus 70.3% for JS divergence and 53.9% for answer-likelihood drop. On real compressor outputs, pooled gains remain; adding answer agreement improves AUROC by 2.8 points over SELD alone. Reported extra cost is one forward pass (29–30 ms with Qwen2.5-1.5B).

Why Watchlist: most evidence is QA-centric and reference-based, the score needs an original context, cross-question calibration is unresolved, and a passing check does not certify completeness or safety. Test on internal context types and compression mechanisms first.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications

From model-centric stacks to evidence-bearing systems

Independent Analysis A 12–24 month synthesis; no current enterprise stack is assumed.

1 · Artifact trustSigned model/code provenance, reproducible builds, lineage, checkpoint diffs, SBOMs.
2 · Governed executionIdentity, least privilege, sandboxing, policy enforcement, secret brokering, human approvals.

Shared enabling foundations

  • Model gateway: admit only signed, approved versions and attach lineage/risk metadata to every call.
  • Identity/authorization: task-scoped principals and tool-specific policies, independent of model intent.
  • Evaluation infrastructure: replayable trajectories, counterfactual edits, control/placebo cases, outcome validators, monitor disagreement.
  • Observability: prompt/context versions, model hash, tool calls, policy decisions, actions, evidence references, latency and cost.
  • Knowledge/context services: immutable originals, provenance, compression lineage, retention and privacy boundaries.
  • Human-review controls: explicit specification ownership, high-impact approvals, exception adjudication, stop/rollback authority.

Likely architecture changes

  • Models become untrusted executors behind gateways, not autonomous trust anchors.
  • Every deployable artifact carries a verifiable identity; every action carries a principal and policy decision.
  • Evaluation moves from output scoring to lifecycle regression: artifact → context → reasoning signal → action → outcome.
  • High-risk code gains a parallel proof/evidence lane where feasible, with trusted-base size as an explicit metric.
  • Context compaction and multimodal analysis become evidence transformations that must preserve or reconcile support.
  • Experiment infrastructure isolates new controls, measures false positives and economics, and supports one-click rollback.
Internal context needed before design: applications and risk tiers, model sourcing and derivatives, data sensitivity, tool/secret authority, identity and network controls, observability retention, regulated obligations, SLOs, cost envelope, and available formal/domain expertise.

Knowledge reinforcement

Recall the decision logic

Five questions for review; answers follow each prompt.

1. Why does AlphaProof not remove human review?

Answer: Lean checks whether a proof follows from the specification. Humans still decide whether the specification, scope, environment, cryptographic assumptions, and trusted computing base match the real security question.

2. What does Lazarus change about model governance?

Answer: behavioral evaluation is insufficient. Checkpoint identity, hashes, signatures, lineage, update diffs, write-path security, registry policy, and agent least privilege become first-class controls.

3. Why is CoT controllability not a deployment metric?

Answer: it is a noisy proxy. In a matched-capability comparison, greater controllability did not produce more monitor evasion. Use direct safety outcomes and multiple independent signals.

4. What do EarthRecord and SELD share?

Answer: an answer can look stable while its evidence is wrong or gone. Validate support and cross-source consistency, not just the final answer.

5. What is today’s governing principle?

Answer: trust independently checkable artifacts and controls—proof kernels, signatures, provenance, policies, source corroboration, and outcome validators—more than agent explanations.

6. What would justify roadmap promotion?

Answer: internal replication, measurable benefit, acceptable false positives/cost, security and privacy approval, documented ownership, reversible rollout, and evidence that the control beats a simpler baseline.
