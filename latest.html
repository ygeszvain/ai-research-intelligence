Executive brief

Three signals that change the enterprise question

Paper Fact

Scientific agents can be auditable and local

5 case studies

A peer-reviewed Nature Medicine article reports five expert-led biomedical case studies, open code, source data, and a fully air-gapped option. The authors still frame the system as an accelerator—not an independent scientist.

Paper Fact

Most cost–accuracy steps are unresolved

27 / 35

Across seven agent benchmarks, 27 of 35 Pareto-frontier steps were statistically unresolved. On SWE-bench Mini, a five-times-cheaper run trailed by 14.6 points but could not be distinguished with the available tasks.

Paper Fact

Experience can reduce coding-agent cost

18.4–30.9%

Across 277 tasks and five agent–model combinations, TokenEconomizer reduced reported mean cost in every repository–configuration cell, with broadly stable success. Its official open-source release is new; the paper dates to October.

Independent Analysis. The near-term portfolio should fund three reversible experiments: a budget-aware agent selection service, an experience-driven cost controller, and an auditable analysis sandbox with scientific/statistical guardrails. None of these findings supports autonomous clinical decisions, unsupervised scientific conclusions, or direct production changes.

Top 3 Future Bets

1

Decision-grade agent selection

Why: it prevents weak leaderboard evidence and incomparable cost accounting from driving procurement. What changes the recommendation: live-backlog tests show simple sampling is no better than full evaluation or selection is unstable. Next reversible action: replay two internal candidate systems on 50 sanitized tasks with paired outcomes and normalized costs.

2

Why: repeated repositories and workflows create reusable operational knowledge and predictable waste. What changes it: savings disappear on fresh tasks, quality falls beyond tolerance, or learned context creates unacceptable leakage. Next action: shadow-log one non-sensitive repository; enable only tool pruning after a blinded baseline.

Auditable scientific-analysis sandbox

Why: analysis, code, literature, provenance, and expert review can be joined in one contained workflow. What changes it: independent reruns fail, false discoveries persist after correction, or expert review cost exceeds benefit. Deep-paper analysis

Three items that cleared the full-review gate

Paper Fact

An open agentic AI co-scientist for clinical and biomedical discovery

Kaleigh F. Roberts, Zachary B. Abrams, Luca Cappelletti, et al.
Nature Medicine · published November 16, 2026 · accepted November 10 · DOI 10.1038/s41591-026-04808-6 · article · code (Apache-2.0) · run artifacts

5 main case studies93 development-period runs in source dataopen-sourceair-gapped inference optionexpert supervised

Contribution and prior-work delta

OpenScientist integrates literature retrieval, iterative hypothesis generation, Python analysis, an explicit knowledge state, tool/skill extensibility, and report generation. Its claimed delta from proprietary or demo-oriented AI-scientist systems is an open, auditable implementation evaluated on real biomedical datasets, with a local model path for sensitive data. The Nature article reports Alzheimer biomarker quantification, a hidden-evaluation plasma-proteomic survival model, a tau/lysosomal acidification mechanism, multiple-myeloma hypotheses checked on an external cohort and randomized controls, and a blinded prespecified proteomic comparison concordant with an independent human analysis.

Methodological critique

  • Strengths: real biomedical datasets; expert-defined questions; external cohort, hidden evaluation, blinded comparison and randomized controls across different cases; public source data, code and run archives.
  • Selection threat: featured cases emerged from a development workflow. Expert completion and engagement are not neutral sampling, so the success rate cannot be generalized to arbitrary biomedical questions.
  • Statistical threat: the reported workflow did not consistently apply cross-iteration multiple-testing correction or a uniform hypothesis-prioritization formula.
  • Domain threat: documented failures include treating a metabolite ratio as flux and initially analyzing an RNA-seq treatment incorrectly. Report generation from summaries can separate conclusions from raw evidence.
  • Control threat: randomized controls attenuated signal but did not fully eliminate every apparently supported hypothesis. A plausible narrative is not causal proof.
  • Privacy boundary: containment requires both the agent runtime and model inference to be local. A local agent calling a hosted model still transmits inputs.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Reproducibility: High for inspection, moderate for exact re-execution. Code, Apache-2.0 license, source data and detailed run archives were verified, but some clinical datasets, provider models and domain context may be access constrained. Enterprise readiness: Moderate for contained analysis assistance; low for autonomous scientific or clinical decisions. Human scientific review, statistical correction, provenance and data-governance controls are prerequisites.

Paper Fact

Beyond Leaderboard Ranks: Selecting Software Engineering Agents Under Budget Constraints

Srinath Srinivasan & Tim Menzies
arXiv:2611.21515 · submitted November 16, 2026 · paper · HAL traces

58,197 run–task records7 benchmarks5,371 pairwise comparisons27/35 unresolved frontier steps300 simulation replicates/condition

Method and prior-work delta

The paper reframes agent evaluation from ranking to a two-budget decision: how much to spend comparing systems and what deployment-cost cap the chosen system must meet. It reconstructs common task matrices from the Holistic Agent Leaderboard, uses paired exact McNemar tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction, robustness intervals and power analysis, normalizes token costs, then replays allocation strategies. Its novelty is the combination of statistical resolution, cost-accounting audit and budgeted selection—not a new coding agent.

Methodological critique

  • Strengths: paired task outcomes, exact tests, multiplicity correction, sensitivity analyses, explicit equivalence warning, cost reconstruction, multiple benchmarks and public traces.
  • Retrospective threat: allocation is replayed on historical matrices, not executed prospectively. System versions, prices and harnesses span time.
  • Primary-workflow threat: the software-maintenance comparison uses only 48 shared SWE-bench Mini tasks; patch maintainability and team review are not outcomes.
  • Idealized information: headline savings assume exact historical task cost is visible before purchase. Without advance cost checks, the advantage narrows and budgets can overrun.
  • Metric threat: Q rewards selecting an unresolved contender. High Q on a low-resolution benchmark does not prove future equivalence.
  • Economic boundary: reconstructed API cost excludes hosting, tools, retries outside traces, security review and human patch review.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Reproducibility: Moderate–High. Source HAL traces and methods are public; a distinct versioned analysis repository was not verified in this review. Enterprise readiness: High for an evaluation pilot, not for automatic vendor selection. Internal issue distributions, patch review, cost contracts and repeated live runs are required.

TokenEconomizer: Experience-Driven Black-Box Cost Control for Coding Agents

Aaron J. Li, Weijia Xu, Chandan Singh, Zixiao Chen, Jeevana Priya Inala, Charlotte Siska, Acar Tamersoy, Bin Yu, Ion Stoica, Michel Galley & Jianfeng Gao
arXiv:2610.22732 · original paper October 15, 2026 · official Microsoft Research release November 16 · paper · release · code (Apache-2.0)

277 SWE-bench tasks6 repositories5 agent–model bundles18.4–30.9% lower mean cost30/30 repo–bundle cells cheaper

Mechanism and prior-work delta

TokenEconomizer is an external controller: it does not modify the underlying model. It learns repository-specific micro-skills from completed sessions, prunes historically irrelevant tools while retaining core shell/read/edit capabilities, and uses a judge to redirect repeated failure loops. Existing cost methods mostly optimize an isolated request; this study treats a sequence of tasks in the same environment as the unit of learning.

For Claude Code with Sonnet 5, the official release reports 23.6% lower cost and Pass@1 of 76.0% versus 74.7%. Across repository sequences, cumulative cost fell below default by the sixth task. The open-source plugin currently enables tool pruning and failure-loop intervention; micro-skills are conservative/optional, and a persistent loop alerts a developer rather than forcibly stopping work.

Methodological critique

  • Strengths: same task sequences across five system bundles; controller cost included; repository-level breakdowns; mechanism ablations; open implementation and experiment harness.
  • External-validity threat: benchmark bug fixes in fresh checkouts are not long-running developer sessions with branches, team conventions and changing dependencies.
  • Quality threat: stable aggregate Pass@1 can hide task-level regressions. Confidence intervals, maintainability, security defects and reviewer time are needed.
  • State threat: learned micro-skills can become stale, poisoned or overfit. Tool pruning can remove a rare but critical capability.
  • Privacy/security boundary: the learned state remains local, but the underlying coding agent and loop judge may still transmit code/context depending on provider configuration.
  • Economics boundary: token/API cost is not total developer cost; latency, human interruption and remediation were not the primary outcomes.

Reproducibility and enterprise readiness

Reproducibility: High. Apache-2.0 code, experiments, task sets and integration guidance were verified. Enterprise readiness: Moderate for shadow-mode cost experiments. Production enablement requires repository isolation, provenance, expiry, poisoning tests, security review and quality gates.

Does the Voice Matter? Evaluating Frustration Detection on Deployed Clinical Voice-Agent Calls

Michael et al. · arXiv:2611.21001 · November 16 · paper

Signal. The study analyzes 150 turns from six real patient calls with three annotators and extends to 714 turns from 30 calls. Roughly 59% of deployed patient turns were one or two words versus 13% in the full acted corpus. Transcript-only models flagged only 3–4 of 39 frustrated one-word turns. Under delivery-specific prompts, two Gemini 2.5 variants gained 3.0 points with audio, but the interval (−1.0 to 7.5) included zero; confidently cleared false negatives improved from 13.0% to 6.9% (difference 6.1 points, interval 2.2–10.4). With identical prompts, no model’s audio–text interval excluded zero, and three models were worse with audio.

Why Watchlist. Real deployed data is unusually valuable, but call count is small, labels are subjective, model/prompt interactions are unstable, and the privacy stakes are clinical. The defensible enterprise inference is conservative multimodal escalation with abstention and human review—not automated clinical judgment.

Uncertainty

From Recursion to Convolution: Parallel Self-Improvement for Multi-Agent Systems

Wang et al. · arXiv:2611.20891 · November 16 · paper · code

Signal. ConvRSI replaces recursive self-improvement with intra-agent convolution and inter-agent graph convolution over latent representations. On MATH-500, the paper reports a 5.04% performance gain, 17.27% lower inference cost and 45.11% lower latency relative to RecursiveMAS.

Why Watchlist. One math benchmark does not establish utility on tool use, software work, enterprise data, long-horizon planning or adversarial conditions. Multiple-seed stability, significance, model dependence, safety and traceability remain unclear. Treat latent multi-agent compression as a research option, not an architecture commitment.

Cross-paper synthesis

Shared foundations matter more than the individual agents

1. The evidence unit is a workflow, not a model

OpenScientist’s result depends on question framing, tools, code, data and expert review. TokenEconomizer’s result depends on sequential repository experience. Beyond Leaderboard shows model-and-scaffold rankings lack decision resolution. Evaluate the whole bundle on the actual task distribution.

2. Memory must be governed as operational data

Repository experience can reduce waste, while scientific knowledge states can make iterative work inspectable. The same state can be stale, contaminated, sensitive or misleading. Provenance, scope, expiry, contradiction and deletion are first-class controls.

3. Statistical power is an architecture requirement

A benchmark too small to separate contenders cannot support confident routing or procurement. Scientific agents add another multiplicity problem: repeated hypotheses and iterations inflate false-discovery risk. Evaluation infrastructure needs power analysis and sequential-testing discipline.

4. Local execution is a boundary, not a checkbox

An air-gapped scientific agent requires local runtime, local models, local artifacts and controlled tool dependencies. Likewise, “local memory” does not prevent the underlying agent from transmitting code. System-level data-flow verification is necessary.

Enterprise AI Architecture Implications · Independent Analysis

A 12–24 month evidence-and-control fabric

The strongest common investment is a governed loop around agents: identity and policy define authority; model and tool gateways expose cost and data flows; context/memory services preserve scoped experience; scientific/evaluation services quantify uncertainty; isolated execution contains action; provenance and observability make every conclusion reviewable; humans retain release and escalation authority. No current enterprise stack is assumed.

Shared enabling foundations

Model/tool gateway + identity

Normalized invocation and cost records, model/tool versions, cache accounting, workload identity, least privilege, egress policy and auditable authorization.

Context/memory + knowledge

Tenant and repository scope, citations, source/data lineage, contradiction, expiry, deletion, poisoning defenses and permission-aware retrieval.

Evaluation + experiment infrastructure

Representative tasks, power analysis, paired outcomes, statistical correction, holdouts, resettable sandboxes, experiment registry and reversible releases.

Observability + cost telemetry

End-to-end traces, tool calls, token/cache rates, latency, human review, failed-loop cost, selection spend and total operating economics.

Policy + provenance

Claim-to-source/code/data links, model/package signatures, data snapshots, consent, retention, jurisdiction, prohibited actions and exceptions.

Human-review controls

Named scientific, security, privacy and business owners; calibrated escalation; override logs; release gates; rollback; and incident command.

Additional internal context required before implementation decisions: target workflows and measurable business outcomes; existing model/tool gateways; data classifications and residency; current identity and authorization; knowledge/RAG/memory services; evaluation and observability maturity; security architecture; vendor contracts and pricing; regulatory jurisdictions; scientific/statistical ownership; human-review capacity; latency and cost budgets; privacy, labor and accessibility constraints; and incident/rollback processes.
Implementation priorities

A reversible 90-day evidence program

Days 0–15Select one sanitized software workflow and one completed non-sensitive analysis. Define owners, outcomes, equivalence margins, prohibited actions, cost scope and stop conditions.
Days 16–35Create frozen task/data sets, power analysis, paired ground truth, trace/provenance schema, normalized cost ledger and a sandbox with no production access.
Days 36–60Run full baselines. Test successive halving in replay; shadow TokenEconomizer state; reproduce the analysis with claim-level lineage and multiplicity controls.
Days 61–90Run held-out repetitions and failure injections. Review quality, uncertainty, leakage, privacy, latency, cost, review burden and rollback; advance each layer independently.

Minimum measurement set

  • Task/claim success with confidence intervals
  • Paired disagreement and equivalence margin
  • Power and minimum detectable effect
  • Hypothesis count and multiplicity correction
  • Claim-to-code/data/source lineage coverage
  • External validation and negative-control behavior
  • Token, cache, tool and human-review cost
  • Cost-estimation bias and budget overrun
  • Memory contradiction, expiry and poisoning events
  • Rare-tool recall after pruning
  • Unauthorized egress and identity violations
  • p50/p95/p99 latency and operator workload

Portfolio stop conditions

  • Any pilot can reach production, patient, customer or regulated actions.
  • Power is insufficient to resolve the predeclared business margin.
  • A material claim lacks traceable data, code, source or test lineage.
  • Repeated hypotheses are evaluated without a declared correction or holdout.
  • Learned context crosses repository/tenant boundaries, remains unreviewably stale, or can be poisoned.
  • Cost savings coincide with a critical quality/security regression or reviewer burden erases the benefit.
  • “Air-gapped” operation has undeclared model, package, telemetry or tool egress.
Knowledge reinforcement

What to remember tomorrow

  1. Non-rejection is not equivalence. An unresolved leaderboard gap may still be operationally large.
  2. Evaluation and deployment budgets differ. How much you spend choosing is separate from what the chosen system costs to run.
  3. Accuracy sampling can bias cost. Discriminating tasks may not represent full-workload spend.
  4. Experience is governed data. Memory needs scope, provenance, contradiction, expiry and deletion.
  5. Scientific iteration creates multiplicity. More hypotheses without correction can create persuasive false discoveries.
  6. Local is end-to-end. A local wrapper is not air-gapped if inference, packages, telemetry or tools egress.
  7. Case studies show possibility, not prevalence. Featured successes do not estimate arbitrary-task reliability.
  8. Human authority remains part of the system. Expert review is not a caveat to subtract; it is a measured component of readiness.

    Runtime, model inference, artifacts, packages, tools and telemetry must all remain inside the controlled boundary.
  4. What is OpenScientist ready for?
    Contained, expert-supervised analysis assistance—not autonomous scientific or clinical decisions.
